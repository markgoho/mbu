{{< drg/requirement number="15d" >}}
Identify how you might use the skills and knowledge in the field of emergency medical services to pursue a personal hobby and/or healthy lifestyle. Research the additional training required, expenses, and affiliation with organizations that would help you maximize the enjoyment and benefit you might gain from it. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and share what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursued this.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Not every use of first-aid and EMS knowledge leads to a career. Some of the most valuable applications are personal: the hiker who can manage a wilderness injury, the swim coach who keeps a pool safe, the volunteer firefighter who serves their community on weekends. This requirement asks you to look at your own interests and think honestly about how emergency-care knowledge could make you more capable, more confident, and more useful in the activities you already care about.

## Step 1 — Connect EMS Skills to Something You Care About

Start by identifying a hobby, sport, activity, or lifestyle goal that could benefit from first-aid or EMS knowledge. Consider:

**Outdoor activities:**
- Hiking, backpacking, or mountaineering — wilderness first aid and patient assessment
- Kayaking, canoeing, or whitewater rafting — water rescue, hypothermia management
- Rock climbing or caving — trauma care, rope-based rescue techniques
- Mountain biking or trail running — wound care, fracture management

**Sports and fitness:**
- Coaching youth sports — CPR, bleeding control, concussion recognition
- Lifeguarding — water rescue, CPR, AED use, spinal precautions
- Competitive athletics — sports medicine, injury prevention, sideline first aid
- Martial arts — managing bleeding, soft tissue injuries

**Volunteer and community roles:**
- Volunteer fire or EMS service — full emergency response training
- Community Emergency Response Team (CERT) — disaster first aid, search and rescue basics
- Search and rescue volunteer — patient packaging, wilderness evacuation, navigation
- Lifeguard or swim instructor — water safety and emergency response

**Personal readiness:**
- Preparedness for natural disasters or emergencies at home
- Carrying a personal trauma kit and knowing how to use it
- CPR and AED certification as a personal commitment

## Step 2 — Research the Path

Once you have identified one connection, research it in these areas:

### Additional Training Required

- What first-aid or EMS training builds on the First Aid merit badge for your activity?
- Options include: Wilderness First Responder (WFR), Wilderness First Aid (WFA), WEMT, lifeguard certification, CERT training, CPR/AED instructor certification, sports first aid courses.
- What is the length of the program and how is it taught (in-person, online, hybrid)?

### Expenses

- What do the training courses cost?
- Is there equipment you would need to purchase (first-aid kit, AED, rescue bag)?
- Are there ongoing costs (certification renewal, organization dues, equipment maintenance)?

### Organizational Affiliations

- What organizations support or certify practitioners in your area of interest?
  - **NOLS Wilderness Medicine** — wilderness first aid, WFR, WEMT
  - **American Red Cross** — lifeguard certification, first aid, CPR, AED
  - **NASAR** — search and rescue training and membership
  - **FEMA CERT** — community emergency response team program
  - **Local volunteer fire and EMS departments** — entry-level volunteer training
  - **USA Swimming or similar sports governing bodies** — coaching and safety certifications

## Step 3 — Set Short-Term and Long-Term Goals

Concrete goals make this requirement meaningful. Think through two time horizons:

**Short-term goals (next 6–12 months):**
- A specific course you want to take
- A certification you want to earn
- A volunteer role you want to apply for
- Equipment you want to assemble

**Long-term goals (2–5 years and beyond):**
- A skill level you want to reach (Wilderness First Responder, certified lifeguard, CERT team member)
- A role in your community you want to fill
- How this connects to a possible career direction or sustained volunteer commitment

{{< drg/tip >}}
Goals are most useful when they are specific. "Get better at first aid" is not a goal. "Complete a Wilderness First Aid course before next summer's backpacking trip" is a goal. Write yours with enough detail that you will know when you have achieved them.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Discussing with Your Counselor

Your counselor will want to hear your thinking, not just your research findings. Be ready to:

- Explain the hobby or lifestyle connection you chose and why it matters to you
- Describe the additional training you researched — what it involves and what it costs
- Name the organizations relevant to your interest
- Share your short-term and long-term goals
- Reflect on whether this feels like a direction you want to pursue and why

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Many Scouts who earn the First Aid merit badge go on to become lifeguards, CERT volunteers, youth sports coaches, or wilderness first-aid certified hikers. The skills from this badge do not expire when you turn in the signed card — they stay with you and grow whenever you choose to build on them.
