{{< drg/requirement number="15b" >}}
Interview an emergency medical services professional about their work. Learn about how they chose this career and about their duties. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and whether you might be interested in this career.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

An interview is a focused, intentional conversation — different from a casual chat on a tour or a ride-along. You come prepared with specific questions, you listen carefully, and you leave with a real understanding of how one person built a career in emergency medical services, what the work demands, and what it gives back. The best interviews feel like a conversation, not a questionnaire.

## Who to Interview

An EMS professional can be anyone whose work centers on emergency medical care:

- **EMT (Emergency Medical Technician)** — provides basic life support and transport
- **Paramedic** — provides advanced life support, including medications and advanced airway management
- **Flight medic or flight nurse** — provides care on air medical transport (helicopter or fixed-wing)
- **Emergency room nurse or physician**
- **Emergency medical dispatcher** — the 911 operator who coordinates response
- **Wilderness EMT or search-and-rescue medic**
- **Community paramedic** — works in a non-emergency, preventive-care role

Ask your Scoutmaster, parents, or counselor if they know someone in any of these roles. Many Scouts find a willing interviewee through a parent's workplace, a family connection, or their local fire station.

## Preparing Your Questions

Write your questions before the interview. Aim for open-ended questions that invite the person to share their experience — not yes/no questions that end the conversation.

{{< drg/checklist title="Interview Questions" subtitle="Choose the most relevant and add your own" >}}
- How did you first become interested in EMS or emergency medicine?
- What steps did you take to get into this field — certifications, school, training programs?
- What does a typical shift look like for you?
- What kinds of calls do you find most challenging?
- What do you find most rewarding about the work?
- What surprised you most about the job once you started doing it?
- How has the field changed since you began?
- What skills — beyond medical knowledge — do you think are most important in this job?
- What do you wish someone had told you before you started this career?
- What would you recommend for a young person who wants to explore a career in EMS?
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Conducting the Interview

- Start by thanking the person for their time and briefly explaining the requirement.
- Ask one question at a time and listen to the full answer before asking the next one.
- Follow up on interesting answers — "Can you tell me more about that?" or "What happened after that?"
- Take brief notes on specific details you want to remember (certifications, job titles, a memorable story they shared).
- Close by thanking them and asking if they have any questions for you.

{{< drg/tip >}}
The interview can happen in person, by phone, or by video call. An in-person interview is usually richer, but a phone or video interview with the right person is far more valuable than a face-to-face interview with someone who is a poor fit for the requirement.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## After the Interview

Write a brief summary covering:

- The person's name, role, and EMS setting
- How they got into the field and the path they took
- What their daily duties involve
- One or two things that stood out from the conversation
- Whether anything they shared made this career more or less interesting to you

## Discussing with Your Counselor

Be ready to share what you learned in a real conversation. Your counselor will ask:

- Who you interviewed and in what EMS role
- How the person chose this career
- What their daily responsibilities look like
- Whether anything they shared surprised you
- Whether you could see yourself in this kind of work, and why or why not
