{{< drg/requirement number="15c" >}}
Identify three career opportunities that would use skills and knowledge in emergency medical services. Pick one and research the training, education, certification requirements, experience, and expenses associated with entering the field. Research the prospects for employment, starting salary, advancement opportunities, and career goals associated with this career. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and whether you might be interested in this career.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This requirement is about career research — real, organized investigation into what it takes to enter a specific field and what you can expect once you are in it. It is less about the emergency skills themselves and more about understanding the professional pathway: how you get there, what it costs, and where it can take you.

## Step 1 — Identify Three Career Opportunities

Start by identifying three different careers that use EMS skills and knowledge. Think broadly — not just EMT or paramedic. Consider roles that use emergency care knowledge in different settings:

| Career | Setting | Key EMS skills used |
|---|---|---|
| EMT (Emergency Medical Technician) | Ambulance, fire station | Basic life support, patient assessment, transport |
| Paramedic | Ambulance, fire-based EMS, critical care transport | Advanced life support, medications, intubation |
| Flight medic / flight nurse | Air ambulance (helicopter or fixed-wing) | Advanced ALS in austere, moving environment |
| Emergency room nurse | Hospital emergency department | Patient assessment, procedures, trauma care |
| Emergency physician | Hospital emergency department | Full medical diagnosis and treatment |
| Firefighter/EMT | Fire station | Combined fire suppression and EMS response |
| Search and rescue medic | Wilderness or technical rescue | Wilderness medicine, patient packaging, evacuation |
| Military medic or corpsman | Military branches (Army, Navy, Marines) | Tactical combat casualty care |
| Emergency medical dispatcher | 911 center | Call intake, pre-arrival instructions, resource coordination |
| Community paramedic | Mobile integrated health programs | Preventive care, chronic disease management, community outreach |

Select three from this list (or others you research) and write a brief one-paragraph summary of each.

## Step 2 — Research One Career in Depth

Pick the career that interests you most and research the following areas:

### Training and Education

- What is the minimum education required to enter this field?
- What formal training programs are required (EMT course, paramedic program, nursing school, medical school)?
- What is the typical length of the training or degree program?
- Are apprenticeships or clinical internship hours required?
- Where can you take the required training in your area?

### Certification and Licensing Requirements

- What national certifications are required (NREMT, ACLS, PALS, others)?
- What state licensing is required, and how does it differ from state to state?
- How often must certifications be renewed, and what does renewal require?
- Are there additional specialty certifications that improve advancement (CFRN, FP-C, WEMT)?

### Experience Requirements

- How much field or clinical experience is typically required before hire?
- Do employers prefer or require volunteer experience?
- Are there entry-level positions that allow you to build toward a higher-level role?

### Expenses

- What does the training program cost (tuition, fees, equipment)?
- What licensing exam fees are involved?
- What ongoing continuing education costs are associated with certification maintenance?

### Employment Outlook

- Is demand for this role growing, stable, or declining?
- What geographic areas have the most opportunities?
- Are there more opportunities in urban, suburban, or rural settings?

### Salary

- What is the typical starting salary for someone entering this field?
- What is the median salary for experienced workers?
- Do salaries vary significantly by region, employer type (public vs. private), or specialization?

### Advancement Opportunities and Career Goals

- What positions can someone advance into from this starting point?
- Are there supervisory, management, or administrative paths in this field?
- What does long-term career growth look like (field supervisor, program director, educator)?

{{< drg/checklist title="Research Checklist" subtitle="Confirm you have covered each area before meeting with your counselor" >}}
- Named three careers that use EMS skills and knowledge.
- Selected one career to research in depth.
- Documented the training and education path.
- Documented certification and licensing requirements.
- Documented experience requirements for entry-level positions.
- Estimated typical expenses for training and certification.
- Researched employment outlook and salary range.
- Identified at least one advancement opportunity beyond the entry-level role.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Discussing with Your Counselor

Your counselor will want a real conversation — not a recitation of your notes. Be ready to:

- Name the three career opportunities you identified and briefly describe each
- Walk through your in-depth research on the career you chose
- Share what surprised you about the training path, expenses, or job outlook
- Reflect honestly on whether this career interests you and why or why not

{{< drg/tip >}}
Use reliable sources: the Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook (bls.gov), the National Registry of EMTs (nremt.org), state health department licensing pages, and CAAHEP-accredited program directories. Wikipedia and general blog posts are fine for initial orientation but should not be your primary sources for specific data.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
