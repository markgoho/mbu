{{< drg/requirement number="14" >}}
With guidance from your counselor, develop a plan to teach a first-aid skill or topic using the EDGE method. Discuss your skill, topic, and plan with your counselor, and then teach your skill or topic to your family or to one or more Scouts.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You have spent this entire merit badge building a toolkit of skills and knowledge. Now you put it to use in a different way: by teaching someone else. This requirement is not a performance review of your own first-aid technique — it is a test of whether you can break a skill down, explain it clearly, demonstrate it well, and guide another person through it until they can do it on their own.

## What EDGE Means

EDGE is a four-step teaching method used throughout Scouting:

| Step | What you do | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **E — Explain** | Describe the skill in plain language | Gives the learner a mental picture of what they are about to do |
| **D — Demonstrate** | Show the skill at normal speed, then slowly if needed | Lets the learner see what success looks like |
| **G — Guide** | Coach the learner as they try it themselves | Provides support and correction while the learner builds confidence |
| **E — Enable** | Step back and let the learner do it on their own | Confirms the skill has been transferred |

Teaching is complete when the learner can perform the skill or explain the topic without your help. The final "Enable" step is your evidence that the teaching worked.

## Choosing Your Skill or Topic

Choose something you know well enough to teach confidently. Good options include:

- **Hands-only CPR** — A high-impact skill with a clear, demonstrable technique
- **Controlling severe bleeding** — Direct pressure, wound packing, or tourniquet application
- **Recognition and response to choking** — Abdominal thrusts for a conscious adult
- **The BE-FAST stroke recognition acronym** — Easy to teach and immediately practical
- **Treating heat exhaustion** — Describe the signs, then walk through the cooling steps
- **Applying a bandage or splint** — Physical, demonstrable, and practical for any Scout

{{< drg/tip >}}
Pick a topic you have already practiced or demonstrated during this merit badge — not one you are still working to understand yourself. The best teachers are confident in the material before they stand in front of a learner.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Building Your Teaching Plan

Before you meet with your counselor to discuss your plan, prepare answers to these questions:

{{< drg/checklist title="Teaching Plan Checklist" subtitle="Work through these before your counselor meeting" >}}
- What skill or topic have I chosen, and why is it worth knowing?
- Who is my audience — family members, younger Scouts, a patrol?
- What does my audience already know about this topic?
- What is the key takeaway I want them to leave with?
- What materials or props do I need (bandage, dummy, diagrams)?
- How will I Explain it — what are the key facts in plain language?
- What will I Demonstrate — what does the correct technique look like?
- How will I Guide them — what will I watch for and correct?
- How will I Enable them — what task will show me they can do it independently?
- How long will the lesson take?
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Discussing Your Plan with Your Counselor

Your counselor will review your plan before you teach. Be prepared to:

- Describe the skill or topic and explain why you chose it
- Walk through your EDGE plan step by step
- Explain who you will be teaching and what they are likely to know coming in
- Answer questions about the first-aid content itself — your counselor may ask you to demonstrate your own understanding of the skill

Make adjustments based on your counselor's feedback before you proceed to teaching.

## Teaching the Lesson

When you are in front of your audience, move through the four steps deliberately:

**Explain:** Start by naming the skill and explaining its purpose. "Today I'm going to teach you how to control severe bleeding. This matters because bleeding control is one of the most important things a bystander can do before EMS arrives."

**Demonstrate:** Show the skill yourself, clearly and at a pace your audience can follow. Narrate what you are doing as you do it. Repeat the demonstration if your audience looks confused.

**Guide:** Invite your learner to try it while you watch. Give specific feedback — not just "good job" but "your hand position is right; press a little firmer." Be encouraging and patient.

**Enable:** Step back. Have the learner perform the skill or explain the topic without your help or prompting. Watch without jumping in. If they succeed, the skill has been transferred.

## After the Lesson

Report back to your counselor. Be ready to describe:

- What went well
- What was harder than you expected
- Whether your audience reached the "Enable" stage
- What you would do differently next time
