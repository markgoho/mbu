{{< drg/requirement number="15a" >}}
Visit an emergency medical station house or training center in person. From the medical first responders that you meet during your visit, learn about how they serve their community and about their careers. Discuss with your counselor what you learned during your tour and interviews.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

An in-person visit puts you in the same spaces where emergency responders train and prepare to serve. You see the equipment up close, hear directly from the people who use it, and begin to understand what a career in emergency medical services actually looks like day to day. This is not a passive field trip — you need to come prepared, pay attention, and have real conversations.

## Planning Your Visit

Before you go, work with your Scoutmaster, parent, or counselor to:

1. **Identify a location.** Options include a fire station that runs EMS, a standalone EMS station, an ambulance service depot, a community paramedic training center, or a hospital emergency medicine training facility. Many stations welcome Scout visits — call ahead to find out.
2. **Schedule in advance.** Contact the station or center and explain that you are working on the First Aid merit badge requirement. Ask if a responder or coordinator would be willing to spend time with you.
3. **Get any permissions needed.** Your council or unit may require a permission form for this kind of trip.

## What to Observe During the Visit

{{< drg/checklist title="During the Visit" subtitle="Pay attention to all of these" >}}
- The types of vehicles and equipment on site (ambulances, response vehicles, AED stations).
- How the station is organized — dispatch area, equipment storage, crew quarters.
- The training spaces and simulators, if it is a training center.
- How crews are deployed and what their shift schedule looks like.
- The types of calls the station typically responds to in the community.
- How the responders interact with each other and talk about their work.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Questions to Ask the Responders

Come with questions written down. You are expected to have real conversations — not just nod while someone gives you a tour. Good questions to ask:

**About their community service:**
- What kinds of emergencies do you respond to most often?
- What is the most challenging type of call for your team?
- How does your station work with other agencies (fire, police, hospitals)?
- What does your station do to prepare the community for emergencies?

**About their careers:**
- How did you decide to go into emergency medical services?
- What training or certifications did you need to get this job?
- What does a typical shift look like?
- What do you find most rewarding about this work?
- What would you tell a young person who is considering this career?

{{< drg/tip >}}
Bring a small notebook or use your phone to jot down answers — not to record the entire conversation, but to capture specific details (certifications mentioned, job titles, equipment names) that you will want to reference when you discuss the visit with your counselor.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## After the Visit

Take a few minutes to write down:

- The names and roles of the people you spoke with
- Three things you learned about how EMS serves the community
- Three things you learned about EMS careers
- One thing that surprised you or that you had not expected
- Whether any part of this work interests you as a possible direction

## Discussing with Your Counselor

Your counselor will want a real conversation about what you observed and learned — not a summary you read from notes. Be ready to:

- Describe the facility and what you saw there
- Share specific things you heard from the responders
- Reflect on whether any career paths you learned about interest you
- Connect what you observed to first-aid skills you practiced during this merit badge
