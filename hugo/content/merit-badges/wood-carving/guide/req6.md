{{< drg/requirement number="6" >}}
Plan your own or select a project from the Wood Carving merit badge pamphlet and complete a simple carving in the round.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Carving in the round means shaping a piece that has real form and volume, not just a decorated surface. That could be a simple neckerchief slide, a small animal, a walking-stick top, or another modest 3-D project. The word **simple** matters. This requirement is about finishing a clean beginner project, not attempting the hardest design you can imagine.

### Start with the plan

Before you carve, answer three questions:

1. **What am I making?**
2. **Why is this a beginner-level project for me?**
3. **What wood and tools fit it best?**

The pamphlet's advice is sensible: keep the project small, think it through before cutting, and practice on scrap wood first. Some Scouts even sketch the shape from more than one side so they can picture what is closer, farther back, thicker, or thinner.

### A smart project choice

A good in-the-round project has:

- one clear main shape
- limited tiny details
- wood thick enough to hold safely
- no thin parts that will snap easily
- a size you can realistically finish

{{< drg/checklist title="Signs you picked the right project" subtitle="Use this before you begin carving" >}}

- **I can describe the sequence**: rough shape first, then refine, then detail.
- **The project matches my skill level**: no tiny faces or ultra-thin fragile pieces.
- **My wood choice makes sense**: soft enough to carve, strong enough for the shape.
- **I have practice material**: scrap wood for testing cuts before touching the project.
  {{< /drg/checklist >}}

### A practical carving sequence

**Lay out the design.** Mark important lines and shapes on the wood before cutting.

**Rough out the form.** Remove the biggest extra wood first. This is where your [basic cuts and push cuts](/merit-badges/wood-carving/guide/req4/) do much of the work.

**Refine the shape.** Start rounding, slimming, or separating major forms.

**Add controlled detail.** Use stop cuts, V-cuts, and small paring cuts where the shape needs definition.

**Smooth and finish.** Clean fuzzy areas, remove rough spots, and prepare the project for the finish your counselor approves.

{{< drg/image src="images/in-the-round-progress-sequence.avif" alt="Four-stage sequence showing a simple neckerchief slide or small figure from rough block to finished rounded carving" >}}

{{< drg/be-prepared title="If your project starts going wrong" >}}

- **Stop cutting immediately**: Do not try to "save it" with faster cuts.
- **Look at the grain and shape**: Did the wood tear because you cut in the wrong direction?
- **Simplify the design**: Remove less detail or change the shape to something sturdier.
- **Ask early**: A counselor can often save a project with one good suggestion.
  {{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

### What your counselor will want to hear

You should be ready to explain:

- why you chose this project
- why your wood choice fit the project
- which tools you used most
- where you had to slow down and work carefully
- what you learned from mistakes or practice cuts

That explanation connects this requirement back to [Req 3](/merit-badges/wood-carving/guide/req3/) and [Req 5](/merit-badges/wood-carving/guide/req5/). A finished carving matters, but so does the thinking behind it.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Finishing a simple project cleanly teaches more than abandoning an ambitious one halfway through.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
During rough shaping, it is tempting to remove too much wood too fast. Keep cuts short and controlled, especially when the project starts getting smaller and your hands move closer to the blade.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

Once you can make a simple three-dimensional project, you are ready to compare two flat-surface styles: low-relief and chip carving.
