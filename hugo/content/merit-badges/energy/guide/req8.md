The energy industry is one of the largest and fastest-changing sectors of the global economy. Whether you are drawn to engineering, environmental science, business, policy, or hands-on technical work, there are careers in energy that match your interests. The U.S. Department of Energy estimates that clean energy jobs alone grew to over 3.3 million workers — and the sector is projected to keep expanding.

### Three Career Categories to Explore

You need to identify three career opportunities. Here are several possibilities organized by category to help you find ones that appeal to you:

**Engineering and Technical Careers**
- **Solar panel installer** — Installs and maintains residential and commercial solar systems.
- **Wind turbine technician** — Maintains and repairs wind turbines, often working hundreds of feet in the air.
- **Nuclear engineer** — Designs, operates, or maintains nuclear power systems.
- **Electrical engineer (power systems)** — Designs and manages electrical grids, substations, and distribution networks.
- **Energy storage engineer** — Develops battery systems and other storage technologies.

**Science and Research Careers**
- **Energy analyst** — Studies energy markets, forecasts demand, and evaluates policy impacts.
- **Environmental scientist** — Assesses the environmental impact of energy projects and monitors compliance.
- **Geothermal scientist** — Studies underground heat resources and identifies sites for geothermal development.
- **Materials scientist** — Develops new materials for solar cells, batteries, fuel cells, and other energy technologies.

**Business, Policy, and Consulting Careers**
- **Energy auditor** — Inspects buildings and recommends efficiency improvements (similar to what you did in [Req 4](/merit-badges/energy/guide/req4a/)).
- **Sustainability consultant** — Helps companies reduce energy use and environmental impact.
- **Energy policy analyst** — Works for government agencies, think tanks, or advocacy organizations on energy legislation and regulation.
- **Utility manager** — Oversees operations at electric, gas, or water utilities.

**Skilled Trades**
- **Electrician** — Installs and maintains wiring, including solar and EV charging systems. Growing demand for electricians with renewable energy training.
- **HVAC technician** — Installs and maintains heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems. High-efficiency and heat pump systems are driving new demand.
- **Pipefitter/Steamfitter** — Works on piping systems in power plants, refineries, and industrial facilities.

### Researching Your Chosen Career

Once you pick one career to research in depth, here is a framework for organizing your findings:

{{< drg/checklist title="Career Research Framework" subtitle="What to investigate for your chosen energy career" >}}
- **Education required**: High school diploma? Associate degree? Bachelor's degree? Graduate degree? What subjects to study?
- **Certifications**: Are there industry certifications? (BPI for auditors, NABCEP for solar, OSHA safety certifications)
- **Training pathway**: Apprenticeship? Internship? On-the-job training? How long does it take to become qualified?
- **Experience**: How many years before you are fully independent? What entry-level positions lead to this career?
- **Expenses**: Cost of education or certification programs. Are scholarships or employer-paid training available?
- **Starting salary**: What does an entry-level worker earn? (Check the Bureau of Labor Statistics for data)
- **Advancement**: What does a 5-year, 10-year, 20-year career path look like? Can you move into management, start a business, or specialize?
- **Job outlook**: Is demand growing, stable, or declining? Where are the best job markets geographically?
- **Day-to-day work**: What does a typical workday look like? Desk work? Field work? Lab work? Travel?
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

### Spotlight: Wind Turbine Technician

To give you a sense of what a career deep-dive looks like, here is a brief example:

**Wind turbine technicians** (often called "windtechs") inspect, maintain, and repair wind turbines. They climb towers that can be 300+ feet tall, work in all weather conditions, and troubleshoot complex mechanical and electrical systems.

- **Education**: Most employers require a certificate or associate degree from a wind energy technology program (1-2 years). Some accept related experience in electrical or mechanical fields.
- **Median salary**: About $61,000 per year (2023 BLS data), with experienced techs earning $75,000+.
- **Job outlook**: Projected to grow 45% from 2022-2032 — among the fastest-growing occupations in the country.
- **Trade-off**: The work is physically demanding and involves heights, extreme weather, and travel to remote sites. But it offers strong pay without a four-year degree and the satisfaction of working directly in renewable energy.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Wind turbine technician has been ranked as one of the fastest-growing jobs in America for several years running. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects 45% growth from 2022 to 2032 — compared to an average of 3% for all occupations. The expansion of offshore wind is expected to create thousands more positions in coastal states.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
If possible, try to talk to someone who actually works in the energy career you are researching. A 15-minute conversation with a real professional will teach you things that no website can — like what they love about the job, what they wish they had known before starting, and what a typical Monday looks like. Your counselor, a parent, or a troop leader may be able to help you make a connection.
