{{< drg/requirement number="7" >}}
Tell what is being done to make FIVE of the following energy systems produce more usable energy. In your explanation, describe the technology, cost, environmental impacts, and safety concerns.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You must choose exactly **five** of the nine options in this requirement. This page is here to help you decide which five will give you the strongest comparison and the most interesting discussion with your counselor.

## Your Options

- **[Req 7a — Biomass & Waste-to-Energy](/merit-badges/energy/guide/req7a/)**: Learn how plants, food waste, and garbage can become fuel, heat, or electricity — and why pollution control matters.
- **[Req 7b — Combined Heat and Power](/merit-badges/energy/guide/req7b/)**: See how one fuel source can produce electricity and useful heat at the same time for greater overall efficiency.
- **[Req 7c — Modern Fossil Fuel Plants](/merit-badges/energy/guide/req7c/)**: Explore how engineers squeeze more efficiency out of coal and natural gas systems while trying to reduce emissions.
- **[Req 7d — Fuel Cells](/merit-badges/energy/guide/req7d/)**: Study a technology that makes electricity electrochemically instead of by burning fuel.
- **[Req 7e — Geothermal Power](/merit-badges/energy/guide/req7e/)**: Investigate how Earth’s internal heat can be turned into useful power.
- **[Req 7f — Nuclear Power](/merit-badges/energy/guide/req7f/)**: Compare how advanced reactor designs aim for high output with strong safety systems.
- **[Req 7g — Solar Power Systems](/merit-badges/energy/guide/req7g/)**: Look at how panels, tracking systems, storage, and improved materials help capture more sunlight.
- **[Req 7h — Ocean Energy Systems](/merit-badges/energy/guide/req7h/)**: Explore how tides, waves, and ocean temperature differences can be used — and why the ocean is such a tough engineering environment.
- **[Req 7i — Wind Turbines](/merit-badges/energy/guide/req7i/)**: Learn how larger rotors, better controls, and smarter siting help turbines capture more energy from moving air.

## How to Choose

{{< drg/checklist title="Choosing your five" subtitle="Compare the options before you commit" >}}
- **Time and familiarity**: Solar, wind, fossil fuel plants, and nuclear often have lots of easy-to-find background information.
- **Most different from each other**: Choosing a mix like solar, nuclear, biomass, fuel cells, and ocean energy gives you stronger comparison points than choosing five very similar systems.
- **What you will gain**: Some options teach grid-scale power generation, some teach waste reduction, and some teach cutting-edge technology.
- **Where trade-offs stand out**: Nuclear, fossil fuels, biomass, and ocean energy are especially strong if you want rich discussions about environment, safety, and cost.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
If you are unsure, start with one familiar system, one renewable system, one nonrenewable system, one emerging technology, and one “wild card.” That gives you the clearest contrasts.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### A balanced set many Scouts could use

One strong five-option mix is:

- solar power systems
- wind turbines
- nuclear power plants
- combined heat and power
- fuel cells

That group gives you a nice spread across mature systems, newer systems, renewable and nonrenewable sources, and very different engineering approaches.

You have your menu of options. Next comes the first one: how biomass digesters and waste-to-energy plants try to turn materials people often throw away into useful power.
