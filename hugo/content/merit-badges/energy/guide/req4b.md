Your home energy audit in [Req 4a](/merit-badges/energy/guide/req4a/) gave you the raw data. Now it is time to figure out what to do with it. This requirement is about turning knowledge into action — finding specific, practical ways to cut energy waste in your daily life.

### Daily Energy Habits: Where the Waste Hides

Most energy waste is not dramatic. It is not a furnace blasting at full power with the windows open. It is dozens of small, invisible habits that add up over weeks and months. Here are the biggest areas to examine:

**Heating and Cooling (largest home energy use — about 50%)**
- Every degree you lower the thermostat in winter saves about 3% on heating costs.
- Close curtains at night in winter to insulate windows. Open south-facing curtains during the day to let solar heat in.
- Clean or replace HVAC filters monthly — a dirty filter forces the system to work harder.
- Use a programmable thermostat to lower heat while you sleep and when no one is home.

**Water Heating (about 18% of home energy)**
- A 10-minute shower uses roughly 20 gallons of hot water. Cutting it to 5 minutes saves both water and the energy to heat it.
- Washing clothes in cold water instead of hot can save $50–100 per year.
- A water heater set above 120°F wastes energy and risks scalding.

**Lighting (about 10%)**
- Switching from incandescent to LED bulbs cuts lighting energy by up to 80%. An LED bulb that replaces a 60-watt incandescent uses only 8-10 watts.
- Turn off lights when you leave a room. It sounds obvious, but a single 60-watt bulb left on for 8 extra hours a day costs about $18 per year.

**Electronics and Appliances**
- "Phantom load" or "vampire power" is the electricity devices draw when turned off but still plugged in. Chargers, game consoles, TVs, and cable boxes can waste 5-10% of a household's electricity.
- Use power strips and switch them off when devices are not in use.
- Run dishwashers and washing machines with full loads rather than multiple small loads.

### What Are Sustainable Energy Sources?

A **sustainable energy source** is one that meets today's needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs. The concept goes beyond just "renewable." A truly sustainable energy source must be:

1. **Renewable** — It replenishes naturally faster than we use it (solar, wind, hydro, geothermal).
2. **Low environmental impact** — It does not cause unacceptable pollution, habitat destruction, or ecological harm.
3. **Reliably available** — It can consistently meet demand, or be paired with storage technology.
4. **Accessible** — It can be deployed at a scale and cost that communities can actually use.

Solar and wind energy are the most rapidly growing sustainable sources. They produce no emissions during operation, their "fuel" is free, and their costs have dropped dramatically — solar panel costs have fallen by over 90% since 2010.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
In 2023, renewable energy sources provided about 21% of all U.S. electricity generation. Wind and solar alone accounted for more than 15%. That share is growing every year as costs continue to fall and battery storage becomes more practical.

### Reuse and Recycling: The Energy Connection

You might not think of recycling as an energy issue, but it absolutely is. Manufacturing products from raw materials takes enormous amounts of energy. Recycling uses far less:

- **Aluminum**: Recycling uses 95% less energy than making aluminum from raw bauxite ore.
- **Steel**: Recycling uses about 74% less energy.
- **Paper**: Recycling uses about 60% less energy.
- **Glass**: Recycling uses about 30% less energy.
- **Plastic**: Recycling uses about 70% less energy.

**Reuse** goes even further — using a product again without reprocessing it requires almost no new energy. Refilling a water bottle, donating used clothing, and repurposing containers all save the energy that would have been used to manufacture a replacement.
