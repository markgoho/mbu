To reach the rank of Eagle Scout, you need to earn at least **21 merit badges**. That includes **14 specific Eagle-required badges** and **7 elective badges**.

Earning 21 badges takes planning and steady progress. If you wait until your last year before turning 18, you can get trapped by badges that require 3 months of daily tracking. Planning ahead helps you earn your badges with confidence and zero stress.

## The 21 Merit Badges You Need for Eagle

Every Eagle Scout earns 14 Eagle-required badges and 7 electives across their Scouting journey:

| Rank You Are Working On | Eagle-Required Badges Needed | Electives Needed | Total Badges for This Rank |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| {{< cross-link rank="star" text="Star Rank" />}} | 4 Eagle-Required | 2 Electives | 6 Total Badges |
| {{< cross-link rank="life" text="Life Rank" />}} | 3 more (7 total) | 2 more (4 total) | 11 Total Badges |
| {{< cross-link rank="eagle" text="Eagle Scout" />}} | 7 more (14 total) | 3 more (7 total) | 21 Total Badges |

### The 14 Eagle-Required Badges

| Badge | What You Learn | Where Most Scouts Earn It | What Makes It Challenging |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| [First Aid](/merit-badges/first-aid/) | Handling serious trauma & medical emergencies | Troop meetings or clinics | Hands-on CPR, bandaging, and splinting demonstrations. |
| [Citizenship in Society](/merit-badges/citizenship-in-society/) | Inclusion, ethical leadership, and civil dialogue | Small-group discussions | Guided reflection, case studies, and group conversations. |
| [Citizenship in the Community](/merit-badges/citizenship-in-the-community/)| Local town government and civic service | On your own in your hometown | Attending a real city or town council meeting. |
| [Citizenship in the Nation](/merit-badges/citizenship-in-the-nation/) | Federal government, Constitution, and history | Civics class or summer camp | Visiting a federal facility or historic landmark. |
| [Citizenship in the World](/merit-badges/citizenship-in-the-world/) | International relations and global trade | Merit badge college or clinic | Learning about international treaties and world organizations. |
| [Communication](/merit-badges/communication/) | Public speaking, interviews, and writing | Troop meetings or speech class | Giving a 5-minute speech and running a court of honor. |
| [Cooking](/merit-badges/cooking/) | Nutrition, camp stoves, Dutch ovens, home meals | Troop campouts + home kitchen | Cooking 10 meals across camp, trail, and home. |
| [Camping](/merit-badges/camping/) | Wilderness camping and Leave No Trace | Troop campouts over 2+ years | Logging 20 days and 20 nights in tents. |
| [Personal Fitness](/merit-badges/personal-fitness/) *(Time-Lock)* | Fitness routines, health, and conditioning | On your own with a counselor | 12-week workout program (3 workouts per week). |
| [Family Life](/merit-badges/family-life/) *(Time-Lock)* | Family duties, chores, and home projects | At home with your family | 90-day daily chore log and 2 family meetings. |
| [Personal Management](/merit-badges/personal-management/) *(Time-Lock)*| Money, budgets, finance, and time management | On your own with a counselor | 13-week budget and expense tracking log. |
| Choice 1: [Emerg. Prep](/merit-badges/emergency-preparedness/) *OR* [Lifesaving](/merit-badges/lifesaving/) | Disaster response or water rescue | Troop clinic or Summer Camp | Lifesaving requires strong swimming stamina. |
| Choice 2: [Env. Science](/merit-badges/environmental-science/) *OR* [Sustainability](/merit-badges/sustainability/) | Ecology experiments or green living | Summer Camp or home research | Env Science requires outdoor nature study plots. |
| Choice 3: [Swimming](/merit-badges/swimming/) *OR* [Hiking](/merit-badges/hiking/) *OR* [Cycling](/merit-badges/cycling/) | Outdoor travel and physical endurance | Summer camp or weekend trips | Hiking includes a 20-mile hike; Cycling includes 50 miles. |

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
If you earn more than one badge in a choice group (like earning both *Swimming* and *Hiking*), one counts as Eagle-required and the second automatically counts as one of your 7 elective badges!
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

## The 4 Badges That Take 90+ Days

These four "time-lock" badges cannot be finished in a single weekend or summer camp week. Their requirements mandate months of continuous tracking:

| Badge | How Long It Takes | What You Must Track | When to Start | Best Advice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| [Personal Management](/merit-badges/personal-management/) | **13 weeks** (Req 2) | Write down every dollar you make and spend | As soon as you reach Star rank | Use a simple notebook or spreadsheet. Log allowances, gifts, and chores every weekend. |
| [Family Life](/merit-badges/family-life/) | **90 days** (Req 3) | Do your chores daily and lead 2 family meetings | Star rank (at the same time as Personal Mgmt) | Put a chore chart on the fridge. Have a parent initial it weekly so you don't forget. |
| [Personal Fitness](/merit-badges/personal-fitness/) | **12 weeks** (Reqs 7 & 8) | Baseline test, 3 workouts/week, final test | Life rank (or during a school sports season) | Match this with your track, soccer, cross country, or swim season to make logging easy. |
| [Camping](/merit-badges/camping/) | **20 days & nights** (Req 9) | 1 summer camp (max 6 nights) + 14 weekend nights | Start counting from your first campout! | Keep a camp logbook from day one with dates, locations, and weather. |

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Don't Wait Until Age 17.5" >}}
Every year, Scouts turn 17.5 years old and realize they haven't started Personal Management or Personal Fitness. Because these badges take at least 90 to 100 days to log plus counselor review time, starting them less than 4 months before your 18th birthday puts your Eagle rank at serious risk.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

## How to Pick Your Choice Badges

For the three option groups, pick the badge that fits your skills, interests, and schedule:

| Choice Group | Option A | Option B | Option C | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Group 1: Emergency & Rescue | [Emergency Preparedness](/merit-badges/emergency-preparedness/) (Disaster planning, tabletop scenarios) | [Lifesaving](/merit-badges/lifesaving/) (Water rescues, 10-lb brick dive, conscious/unconscious tows) | — | Take *Lifesaving* if you are a strong swimmer at summer camp; otherwise take *Emergency Preparedness*. |
| Group 2: Environment & Science | [Environmental Science](/merit-badges/environmental-science/) (Nature plots, ecology experiments, pollution studies) | [Sustainability](/merit-badges/sustainability/) (Home energy audits, waste reduction, community design) | — | Take *Environmental Science* at summer camp; take *Sustainability* if you prefer working at home. |
| Group 3: Outdoor Travel | [Swimming](/merit-badges/swimming/) (Finished in 1 week at summer camp aquatics dock) | [Hiking](/merit-badges/hiking/) (One 5-mile, three 10-mile, one 15-mile, one 20-mile hike) | [Cycling](/merit-badges/cycling/) (Two 10-mile, two 15-mile, two 25-mile, one 50-mile bike ride) | *Swimming* is by far the fastest. *Hiking* and *Cycling* require great endurance and multiple full days on trails. |

## How to Start and Finish a Merit Badge (The Blue Card)

The Blue Card (or its digital Scoutbook record) is your official proof that you earned a badge. Follow these 5 simple steps:

| Step | What You Do | Who Is Involved | Helpful Tip |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Step 1: Talk to Your Scoutmaster | Ask for a Blue Card and counselor recommendation | You & Scoutmaster | Make sure you have your Scoutmaster's approval before contacting a counselor. |
| Step 2: Contact the Counselor | Reach out with your buddy to introduce yourself | You, Buddy & Counselor | Confirm you are using the newest badge requirements. |
| Step 3: Complete Requirements | Do the activities, demonstrations, and logs | You & Buddy | Keep neat, dated notes so you can easily show your work. |
| Step 4: Review with Counselor | Show your completed work and get signed off | You, Buddy & Counselor | Make sure the counselor signs all sections of the card. |
| Step 5: Turn in Your Card | Give the signed card to your troop advancement chair | You & Advancement Chair | Keep your signed "Applicant" stub safe in your binder forever! |

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
**Always Use the Buddy System**:
Whenever meeting with a Merit Badge Counselor—in person, over Zoom, or by email—you must **always** have a buddy with you. This can be another Scout, a parent, or an adult leader. One-on-one contact with an adult is never allowed under Scouting America Youth Protection rules.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Eagle Merit Badge Checklist

{{< drg/checklist title="Merit Badge Game Plan Checklist" subtitle="Use these steps to stay ahead on your trail to Eagle" >}}
- Check your advancement record in Scoutbook with your Troop Advancement Chair.
- Count your total camping nights to see how close you are to 20 nights for Camping merit badge.
- Choose which option badges you want to take (Lifesaving vs Emerg Prep; Env Sci vs Sustainability; Swimming vs Hiking vs Cycling).
- Get a signed Blue Card from your Scoutmaster before starting any new badge.
- Start your 13-week budget log for Personal Management as a Star Scout.
- Put up your 90-day chore chart for Family Life.
- Do your baseline fitness test with your Personal Fitness counselor.
- Sign up for summer camp badges that need camp gear (like swimming, boating, shooting sports).
- Put your signed Blue Card stubs in a plastic page protector in your binder.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}
