Option D—Earth History. Do ALL of the following:
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This option is the best fit for Scouts who want the biggest story geology can tell. You will move through geologic time, plate tectonics, fossilization, ancient environments, fossils as clues, and a final visit-or-presentation path tied to real fossil evidence.

## What You'll Complete

- **[Req 4d1 — Geologic Time in Your Region](/merit-badges/geology/guide/req4d1/)**: Build a chart of eras and periods and connect it to rocks in your region. You will gain a timeline for Earth's history.
- **[Req 4d2 — Plate Tectonics](/merit-badges/geology/guide/req4d2/)**: Explain how plates move and why earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain belts cluster where they do. You will gain the main engine of large-scale geologic change.
- **[Req 4d3 — Burial, Fossils, and Extinction](/merit-badges/geology/guide/req4d3/)**: Learn how fossils form and why many species disappear from the record. You will gain the basics of preservation and loss.
- **[Req 4d4 — Fossils as Ancient Clues](/merit-badges/geology/guide/req4d4/)**: Use fossils to infer environment, climate, geography, and food-gathering habitats. You will gain practice reconstructing ancient worlds.
- **[Req 4d5 — Collect or Identify Fossils](/merit-badges/geology/guide/req4d5/)**: Work directly with fossil specimens. You will gain hands-on observation and classification experience.
- **[Req 4d6 — Visit, Observe, or Present Fossils](/merit-badges/geology/guide/req4d6/)**: Finish with an approved visit or research-based presentation. You will gain a real-world connection to fossil interpretation.

## How to Work Through This Option

Keep a notebook with three kinds of pages: one for timeline notes, one for habitat terms, and one for fossil observations. That way you will not have to hunt for information when you discuss the option with your counselor.

{{< drg/tip >}}
If the long geologic timescale feels overwhelming, do not try to memorize every period at once. Focus first on the major sequence from oldest to youngest and where your local rocks fit in.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

You are about to begin geology's biggest challenge: turning scattered clues into a story that spans millions of years.
