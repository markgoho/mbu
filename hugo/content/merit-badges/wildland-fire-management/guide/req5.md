## Requirement 5a: The Fire Environment Triangle (Fuel, Weather, Topography)

{{< drg/requirement number="5a" >}}
Discuss with your counselor how the fire environment affects wildland fire behavior. Include examples of the influences of weather, topography, and fuel.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The **Fire Environment Triangle** consists of three environmental factors that control fire behavior:

```
                  [ Weather ]
                 /           \
                /             \
      [ Topography ] -------- [ Fuel ]
```

1. **Weather**:
   - **Wind**: The most variable and dangerous factor. Wind supplies fresh oxygen, pushes flames toward unburned fuel, and carries spot embers far ahead of the main fire front.
   - **Relative Humidity & Temperature**: As temperature rises, relative humidity drops. Dry air absorbs moisture from dead forest fuels, making them far more flammable.
2. **Topography**:
   - **Slope**: Fire travels **much faster uphill** because rising heat preheats the vegetation above the fire front.
   - **Aspect**: South- and west-facing slopes receive more direct sunlight, making their fuels drier and warmer than north-facing slopes.
   - **Chutes & Canyons**: Narrow canyons create a chimney effect, funneling wind and rapidly accelerating fire spread.
3. **Fuel**:
   - **Fuel Moisture**: Dry fuels burn rapidly and intensely; moist fuels require more heat to ignite.
   - **Fuel Size**: Fine fuels (grass, leaves, pine needles) ignite instantly, while heavy fuels (logs, stumps) burn longer at higher temperatures.
   - **Fuel Continuity**: Continuous fuel beds allow fires to spread unchecked; breaks (rivers, roads, rock outcrops) disrupt fire spread.

## Requirement 5b: The Fire Triangle & Suppression Principles

{{< drg/requirement number="5b" >}}
Explain how a wildfire can be suppressed by removing heat, fuel, or oxygen.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

All fires require three elements to exist, known as the **Fire Triangle**: **Heat**, **Fuel**, and **Oxygen**. Removing any single leg of the triangle immediately extinguishes the fire.

```
       [ HEAT ]  +  [ FUEL ]  +  [ OXYGEN ]  =  FIRE
```

- **Removing Heat**: Applying water or chemical fire retardant lowers the fuel temperature below its ignition point.
- **Removing Fuel**: Digging a mineral soil fireline with hand tools or using a bulldozer removes all burnable vegetation ahead of the fire front. Backfiring also burns out fuel before the main fire arrives.
- **Removing Oxygen**: Smothering embers with mineral dirt using a Pulaski shovel, or dropping retardant foam, starves the fire of air.

## Requirement 5c: Anatomy & Parts of a Wildfire

{{< drg/requirement number="5c" >}}
Draw a diagram to illustrate the parts of a wildfire.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

To complete this requirement, draw a clear diagram illustrating the key parts of a wildfire:

### Key Parts of a Wildfire

- **Point of Origin**: The exact location where the fire started.
- **Head**: The fastest-moving edge of the fire, traveling with the prevailing wind or burning uphill.
- **Flanks**: The sides of the fire parallel to the main direction of travel (Left Flank and Right Flank).
- **Rear / Heel**: The slow-moving edge burning against the wind or downhill.
- **Finger**: A long, narrow strip of fire extending out from the main body.
- **Pocket**: An unburned area of land enclosed between the head and a finger.
- **Island**: An unburned patch of forest completely surrounded by fire inside the perimeter.
- **Spot Fire**: A new fire ignited outside the main fire perimeter by airborne embers.
