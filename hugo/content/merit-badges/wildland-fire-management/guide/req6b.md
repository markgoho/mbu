## Requirement 6b1: 10 Standard Firefighting Orders

{{< drg/requirement number="6b1" >}}
10 Standard Firefighting Orders
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The **10 Standard Firefighting Orders** were established in 1957 following tragic fireline fatalities. They are mandatory safety rules that every wildland firefighter must memorize and obey.

{{< drg/checklist title="The 10 Standard Firefighting Orders" subtitle="Non-negotiable fireline safety rules" >}}
- **1. Keep informed on fire weather conditions and forecasts.**
- **2. Know what your fire is doing at all times.**
- **3. Base all actions on current and expected behavior of the fire.**
- **4. Identify escape routes and safety zones, and make them known to all crew members.**
- **5. Post lookouts when there is possible danger.**
- **6. Be alert, keep calm, think clearly, act decisively.**
- **7. Maintain prompt communications with your forces, your boss, and adjoining forces.**
- **8. Give clear instructions and be sure they are understood.**
- **9. Maintain control of your forces at all times.**
- **10. Fight fire aggressively, having provided for safety first.**
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Requirement 6b2: 18 Watch Out Situations

{{< drg/requirement number="6b2" >}}
18 Watch Out Situations
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The **18 Watch Out Situations** describe specific hazardous conditions that warn firefighters of potential danger. Key examples include:

- Building firelines downhill toward a fire.
- Wind blowing fire toward your position.
- Unburned fuel between you and the fire.
- Cannot see the main fire and not in contact with anyone who can.
- Weather getting hotter and drier.
- Feeling sleepy or exhausted.

When a firefighter identifies a Watch Out Situation, they must pause, evaluate risks, and apply safety mitigations before proceeding.

## Requirement 6b3: LCES (Lookouts, Communication, Escape Routes, Safety Zones)

{{< drg/requirement number="6b3" >}}
Lookouts, Communication, Escape Routes, and Safety Zones (LCES)
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

**LCES** is an acronym representing the core safety system established before any firefighter enters a wildland fire area.

```
[ L ] Lookouts  +  [ C ] Communication  +  [ E ] Escape Routes  +  [ S ] Safety Zones
```

- **Lookouts**: Experienced firefighters positioned where they can observe both the fire behavior and the crew's location.
- **Communication**: Reliable radio or visual contact between lookouts, crew leaders, and adjacent forces.
- **Escape Routes**: Pre-planned, cleared paths allowing firefighters to move quickly from a danger area to a safety zone.
- **Safety Zones**: A location where a firefighter can survive a radiant heat burnover without deploying a fire shelter (such as a large rock outcrop, wide road, or burned-out area).
