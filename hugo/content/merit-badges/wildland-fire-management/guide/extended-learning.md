## Deep Dives in Wildland Fire Science

### Fire Weather and Atmospheric Dynamics

Wildfires generate their own local weather patterns. Extremely intense fires produce massive smoke and ash plumes called **pyrocumulus** (or **pyrocumulonimbus**) clouds. These towering fire clouds can trigger dry lightning, severe downdrafts, and sudden 50+ mph wind shifts that trap firefighting crews. Understanding thermal lifting, inversion layers, and humidity recovery is essential for wildland meteorology.

### Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK) & Cultural Burning

Indigenous communities across North America have practiced cultural burning for millennia. Modern fire management increasingly incorporates Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK)—partnering tribal burn masters with state and federal foresters to reintroduce low-intensity broadcast burning that restores native plants, reduces catastrophic fuel loads, and preserves cultural heritage.
