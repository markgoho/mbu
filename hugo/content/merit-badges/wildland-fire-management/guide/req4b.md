## Requirement 4b1: The Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI)

{{< drg/requirement number="4b1" >}}
Wildland-Urban Interface
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The **Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI)** (pronounced *"WOO-ee"*) is any area where human homes, buildings, and infrastructure meet or intermingle with undeveloped wildland vegetation and flammable fuels.

### Why the WUI Present Special Wildfire Challenges

- **Dual Exposure**: Structures in the WUI are vulnerable both to direct wildfire flame fronts and to wind-blown embers traveling over a mile ahead of the main fire.
- **Evacuation Bottlenecks**: Narrow mountain or rural roads can become clogged with fleeing residents, blocking incoming fire engines.
- **Resource Division**: Firefighters must divide limited crews between fighting the wildland fire front and performing structural defense on individual homes.

## Requirement 4b2: Defensible Space and Home Hardening

{{< drg/requirement number="4b2" >}}
Defensible space and how homes and communities in the wildland-urban interface can be protected from wildfire
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

**Defensible space** is a buffer area created around a building by clearing or thinning flammable vegetation to reduce fire intensity and give firefighters a safe zone to defend the structure.

{{< drg/image src="images/defensible-space-zones.avif" alt="Labeled diagram showing Zone 0 (0-5ft), Zone 1 (5-30ft), and Zone 2 (30-100ft) around a residential home" >}}

### The Three Zones of Defensible Space

```
[ Structure ] → Zone 0 (0-5 ft) Non-combustible → Zone 1 (5-30 ft) Clean & Lean → Zone 2 (30-100 ft) Reduced Fuel
```

1. **Zone 0 — Immediate Ember-Resistant Zone (0 to 5 feet)**:
   - Use non-combustible landscaping (gravel, concrete, stone).
   - Keep gutters, roofs, and decks completely free of dead leaves and pine needles.
   - Install 1/8-inch metal mesh over attic and crawlspace vents to block flying embers.
2. **Zone 1 — Lean, Clean, and Green Zone (5 to 30 feet)**:
   - Remove all dead plants, grass, and weeds.
   - Space trees so canopies maintain at least 10 feet of separation.
   - Prune tree branches up to 6–10 feet off the ground to eliminate fuel ladders.
3. **Zone 2 — Reduced Fuel Zone (30 to 100+ feet)**:
   - Cut annual grass down to a maximum height of 4 inches.
   - Remove dense underbrush and create horizontal spacing between tree clusters.
