## Requirement 8a: Wildland Hand Tools & Fireline Construction

{{< drg/requirement number="8a" >}}
Demonstrate the use of three hand tools unique to wildland fire suppression. Use one of these tools to demonstrate how to construct a fireline.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

### Three Essential Wildland Hand Tools

1. **Pulaski Tool**: A dual-purpose tool combining an axe blade on one side and a narrow adze hoe on the other. Used for chopping heavy roots, cutting brush, and digging mineral soil.
2. **McLeod Tool**: A heavy combination tool featuring a large rake on one side and a flat hoe blade on the other. Used for raking forest duff, scraping grass to bare dirt, and cutting fine roots.
3. **Rogue Hoe / Combi Tool**: A heavy steel hoe used for scraping mineral soil and cutting tough brush on steep slopes.

### Fireline Construction Technique

A **fireline** is a strip of land scraped down to bare mineral soil (completely free of burnable organic matter).

- **Width**: Typically 1.5 to 3 feet wide, cleared down to bare dirt, with brush thinned back several feet on the outside.
- **Trenching**: On steep slopes below a fire, dig a "cup trench" to catch rolling burning logs or pine cones.
- **Scraping**: Use the McLeod or Pulaski to pull all scraped vegetation away from the fire side.

## Requirement 8b: Seven Specialized Wildland Equipment & Apparatus

{{< drg/requirement number="8b" >}}
Discuss seven other tools, equipment, or apparatus that are unique to wildland fire suppression and explain how these resources are used.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

### Seven Specialized Wildland Resources

1. **Type 3 / Type 6 Wildland Fire Engines**: High-clearance, four-wheel-drive fire trucks equipped with off-road suspension, water tanks (300–500 gallons), and pump-and-roll capabilities for rugged terrain.
2. **Chainsaws (Wildland Configuration)**: Heavy-duty chainsaws used by sawyers to fell dead hazard trees (snags) and clear brush lines.
3. **Drip Torch**: A canister carrying a mixture of diesel and gasoline used to ignite controlled backfires and burnouts safely ahead of the main fire front.
4. **Backpack Water Pump (Fedco / Indian Pump)**: A 5-gallon collapsible or rigid water tank worn on a firefighter's back, equipped with a hand-operated slide pump for dousing hot spots during mop-up.
5. **Fire Dozers (Bulldozers)**: Heavy armored tractors fitted with front blades and rear rippers used to scrape wide containment lines through dense brush.
6. **Air Tankers (VLAT & SEAT)**: Fixed-wing aircraft ranging from Single-Engine Air Tankers (SEATs) to Very Large Air Tankers (VLATs, such as DC-10s) that drop thousands of gallons of red chemical retardant to pre-treat fuels ahead of the fire.
7. **Helitack Helicopters**: Helicopters equipped with underslung water buckets (Bambi buckets) to drop water directly on flare-ups, and transport rappellers or helitack crews to remote ridges.
