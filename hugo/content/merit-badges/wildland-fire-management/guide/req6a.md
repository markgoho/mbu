Wildland firefighters work in extreme heat, dense smoke, and rough terrain. Unlike structural firefighters who wear heavy turnout gear, wildland firefighters wear lightweight, breathable, flame-resistant Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) designed for long miles of hiking and handline construction.

### Essential Wildland Firefighter PPE

{{< drg/checklist title="Wildland PPE Gear List" subtitle="Personal protective equipment for the fireline" >}}
- **Flame-Resistant Clothing (Nomex® / Kevlar®)**: High-visibility yellow shirts and forest green trousers made of inherently flame-resistant aramid synthetic fibers that will not melt or ignite.
- **Hardhat (NFPA Rated)**: Heavy-duty plastic helmet with a chin strap to protect against falling tree branches and debris.
- **Eye Protection**: Shatter-resistant safety goggles or glasses to shield eyes from embers, ash, and dust.
- **Hearing Protection**: Earplugs or earmuffs used during chainsaw, heavy machinery, or helicopter operations.
- **Leather Work Gloves**: Heavy-duty, flame-resistant leather gloves to protect hands from heat, sharp tools, and blisters.
- **Heavy-Duty Leather Boots**: High-top (minimum 8-inch) leather boots with lug soles (such as Vibram) and melt-resistant stitching for traction on steep slopes.
- **Personal Fire Shelter**: A lightweight, foil-and-fiberglass deployable tent carried in a waist pack. Used strictly as a last-resort life-saving measure to reflect radiant heat if trapped by an entrapment burnover.
- **Line Gear / Web Pack**: Ergonomic pack carrying 3–5 quarts of drinking water, fireline rations, headlamp, and first-aid supplies.
