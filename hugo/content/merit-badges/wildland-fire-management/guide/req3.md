Fire is not solely a destructive force—it is a vital natural element required to maintain healthy, resilient wildland ecosystems. **Prescribed fire** (or controlled burning) is the intentional application of fire under specified weather and fuel conditions to achieve land management goals.

## Requirement 3a: Fuel Reduction

{{< drg/inherited-requirement number="3a" req_path="3.a" topic="Fuel reduction" />}}

Over time, dead leaves, pine needles, fallen branches, and dense underbrush accumulate on the forest floor, creating a dangerous "fuel ladder" that can carry surface fires up into tree canopies. Prescribed fires safely burn off surface fuels during cool, humid weather conditions, preventing future unplanned wildfires from growing into uncontrollable canopy megafires.

## Requirement 3b: Fish and Wildlife Habitat Enhancement

{{< drg/inherited-requirement number="3b" req_path="3.b" topic="Fish and wildlife habitat enhancement" />}}

Low-intensity prescribed burns clear out overgrown brush, encouraging the growth of fresh grasses, berries, and shrubs that provide essential forage for deer, elk, wild turkeys, and upland birds. Many species—such as the red-cockaded woodpecker and Gopher tortoise—depend specifically on open, fire-maintained pine savannahs for nesting and survival.

## Requirement 3c: Ecosystem Restoration

{{< drg/inherited-requirement number="3c" req_path="3.c" topic="Ecosystem restoration" />}}

Many North American biomes—such as longleaf pine forests in the Southeast and ponderosa pine woodlands in the West—are **fire-adapted ecosystems**. Reintroducing regular low-intensity fire restores historical plant diversity, removes invasive plant species that cannot tolerate fire, and maintains open canopy structures.

## Requirement 3d: Forest Regeneration

{{< drg/inherited-requirement number="3d" req_path="3.d" topic="Forest regeneration" />}}

Certain tree species, such as the Giant Sequoia and Jack Pine, produce **serotinous cones** sealed with tough resin. The high heat of a fire melts the resin, releasing millions of seeds onto a freshly cleared, mineral-rich ash bed. Fire also opens up the forest canopy, allowing sunlight to reach young seedlings on the ground.

## Requirement 3e: Insect and Disease Control

{{< drg/inherited-requirement number="3e" req_path="3.e" topic="Insect and disease control" />}}

Prescribed fire thins out overcrowded, weak trees that compete for water and nutrients, leaving healthier trees better equipped to resist insect attacks. Heat and smoke from prescribed fire also kill fungal spores (such as brown spot needle blight) and reduce populations of parasitic insects like pine bark beetles.
