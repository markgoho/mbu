Apply Your Learning to a Real Event. Research an historic catastrophic wildland fire incident that occurred in the United States. Discuss with your counselor and/or your troop or a group approved by your counselor the conditions that led to the incident, how the incident was managed, and how the incident could have been prevented. Explain what lessons were learned and how this incident affected future fire suppression policy or suppression tactics.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Studying historic wildfire disasters helps firefighters and policymakers learn vital lessons about fire behavior, safety protocols, and ecosystem management.

## Four Major Historic Wildfire Case Studies

Select one of the following historic incidents for your research:

### 1. The Tillamook Burn (Oregon, 1933)
- **Incident Summary**: In August 1933, a logging operation friction spark ignited a massive fire in the Coast Range of Oregon, burning 240,000 acres of prime virgin Douglas fir timber in ten days.
- **Lessons Learned**: Highlighted the severe risk of operating heavy machinery during extreme heat and low humidity. Led to mandatory logging shutdowns during fire weather and massive reforestation efforts.

### 2. The Mann Gulch Tragedy (Montana, 1949)
- **Incident Summary**: On August 5, 1949, a lightning-ignited fire in the Gates of the Mountains Wilderness blew up rapidly, trapping 15 smokejumpers. Foreman Wagner Dodge lit an "escape fire" to burn off grass ahead of him, but 13 smokejumpers lost their lives.
- **Lessons Learned**: Transformed firefighter safety, leading to the creation of the 10 Standard Firefighting Orders, formal fire behavior research labs, and mandatory escape route protocols.

### 3. The Yellowstone Fires of 1988 (Wyoming, 1988)
- **Incident Summary**: A historic summer drought combined with high winds resulted in fires that burned over 793,000 acres (36%) of Yellowstone National Park.
- **Lessons Learned**: Sparked intense national debate over "let-burn" natural fire management policies. Highlighted the role of extreme drought in overriding suppression efforts and demonstrated ecological recovery following fire.

### 4. Storm King Mountain / South Canyon Fire (Colorado, 1994)
- **Incident Summary**: On July 6, 1994, a sudden cold front pushed 30 mph winds across a lightning-ignited fire on Storm King Mountain, causing a rapid blowup that overran 14 firefighters on a steep slope.
- **Lessons Learned**: Reinforced mandatory adherence to LCES (Lookouts, Communication, Escape Routes, Safety Zones), downhill line construction rules, and real-time weather monitoring.
