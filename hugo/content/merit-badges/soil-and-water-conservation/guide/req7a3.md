{{< drg/requirement number="7a3" >}}
Make a trip to a wildlife refuge or a fish or game management area. Write a report of more than 500 words about the soil and water and energy conservation practices you saw.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A refuge or game area lets you see conservation from the habitat side. Soil and water management here is often aimed at supporting birds, fish, pollinators, wetlands, and other wildlife instead of maximizing crop production.

Look for restored wetlands, water-control structures, native plantings, streambank protection, invasive species control, and trails or roads designed to limit erosion.

### Report angles that work well

- how water levels are managed for habitat
- how vegetation is used to reduce erosion and improve cover
- how public access is balanced with protection
- how managers prevent sediment from damaging streams or ponds

{{< drg/be-prepared title="If You Cannot Get a Staff Tour" >}}
Many refuges still give you plenty to observe from public access areas.

- Read trail signs and kiosk maps.
- Note wetland cells, dikes, restored plantings, and protected shorelines.
- Record how roads, overlooks, and trails are designed to limit damage.
- Use the refuge website later to confirm the management goals behind what you saw.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}
