Make one or more articles of leather that use at least five of the following steps:
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You must choose exactly 5 options from this requirement. This page is here to help you decide which skills belong in your project before you start cutting leather.

## Your Options

- **[Req 3a — Patterns and Transfer](/merit-badges/leatherwork/guide/req3a/)**: Learn how to place a design on leather accurately so every later step starts in the right spot.
- **[Req 3b — Clean Cutting](/merit-badges/leatherwork/guide/req3b/)**: Shape your project with safe, controlled cuts that follow the pattern instead of wandering off line.
- **[Req 3c — Punching Accurate Holes](/merit-badges/leatherwork/guide/req3c/)**: Make holes for stitching, lacing, or hardware without tearing or stretching the leather.
- **[Req 3d — Carving and Stamping Designs](/merit-badges/leatherwork/guide/req3d/)**: Add decoration, texture, and personality to a project by tooling the surface.
- **[Req 3e — Dye, Stain, and Finish](/merit-badges/leatherwork/guide/req3e/)**: Change the look of the leather and protect it from wear, dirt, and moisture.
- **[Req 3f — Lacing and Stitching](/merit-badges/leatherwork/guide/req3f/)**: Join pieces together so the article becomes strong and usable.
- **[Req 3g — Snaps and Rivets](/merit-badges/leatherwork/guide/req3g/)**: Add metal hardware for closures, straps, and reinforcement.
- **[Req 3h — Smooth Finished Edges](/merit-badges/leatherwork/guide/req3h/)**: Make the outside edges look neat, feel smooth, and resist fraying.

## How to Choose

Some combinations happen naturally. A stamped coaster might use 3a, 3b, 3d, 3e, and 3h. A small pouch might use 3a, 3b, 3c, 3f, and 3h. A strap project with a closure might use 3a, 3b, 3c, 3g, and 3h.

{{< drg/checklist title="Choosing your five steps" subtitle="Compare time, tools, and what you will learn" >}}
- **Best for a first simple project**: 3a, 3b, 3c, 3e, and 3h keep the project manageable while teaching accurate layout and finishing.
- **Best if you enjoy decoration**: 3d adds the most artistic freedom, especially on vegetable-tanned leather.
- **Best if you want a usable pouch or case**: 3f matters because assembly turns flat pieces into real gear.
- **Best if you want hardware practice**: 3g teaches careful alignment and firm setting technique.
- **Best for building neat habits**: 3h is small but important because edge quality often separates beginner work from polished work.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
Do not choose five steps just because they sound impressive. Choose the five that fit your actual article. A strong, simple project completed neatly is better than an overcomplicated project with rushed work.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

If you are not sure what to make, start by naming the article first — wallet, bookmark, key fob, coaster set, bracelet, or pouch — then work backward and see which five skills naturally belong in that build.

The pages that follow walk through each skill one by one so you can understand what it adds to your project.
