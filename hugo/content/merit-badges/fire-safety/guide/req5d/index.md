{{< drg/requirement number="5d" >}}
Using the Home Fire Safety Checklist in the Fire Safety merit badge pamphlet or one approved by your counselor, and with the help of an adult, inspect a home (or a similar building near where you live or at a camp) for fire safety hazards. Present your completed checklist to your counselor and discuss your findings.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Inspecting your home for fire hazards teaches you to think like a fire prevention professional. You'll walk through your home systematically, checking each room and area against a checklist of known hazards. The inspection reveals risks you might not notice in daily life.

## Preparing for Your Home Inspection

### Gather Materials

**You'll need:**
- Fire Safety merit badge pamphlet checklist, OR a checklist approved by your counselor
- Notebook or printed checklist for notes
- Pen or pencil
- Measuring tape (optional, for checking distances)
- Camera or phone camera (optional, to document hazards for discussion)
- Adult supervisor (parent, guardian, or leader)

### Choose Your Inspection Location

**You can inspect:**
- Your own home (most useful—you live there)
- A relative's home (with permission)
- A Scout camp or facility
- A similar building in your neighborhood

**Ideal:** Inspect YOUR home, as the findings will help your family and the learning is most personal.

### Set Timeline and Plan

- Inspect without rushing (30-60 minutes typical)
- Plan time when household is relatively calm
- Inspect room by room
- Note hazards you find (take photos if needed)
- Discuss findings with adult afterward

## The Home Fire Safety Inspection Process

### KITCHEN — Highest Risk Area

**Stovetop and Cooking:**
- [ ] Stovetop clear of items
- [ ] Pot handles turned inward
- [ ] No flammables hanging above stove (curtains, towels, decorations)
- [ ] Grease splatter screen available
- [ ] No extension cords running to stove
- [ ] Space heater (if any) more than 3 feet away

**Electrical:**
- [ ] Outlets functioning (not loose/damaged)
- [ ] No multiple extension cords daisy-chained
- [ ] Appliance cords in good repair
- [ ] Microwave positioned safely (not near water)
- [ ] Oven functioning properly

**Fire Safety Equipment:**
- [ ] Fire extinguisher accessible
- [ ] Clear path to stovetop for evacuation
- [ ] Water or baking soda available (for oil fire response)

### BEDROOMS — Sleep Safety

**Smoke Detection:**
- [ ] Smoke alarm present in bedroom or hallway outside
- [ ] Alarm functioning (when tested)
- [ ] No blockage of alarm

**Electrical:**
- [ ] No extension cords under carpet
- [ ] Appliances (phone chargers, fans) in good repair
- [ ] No overloaded outlets

**Space Heaters:**
- [ ] At least 3 feet from bed and furniture
- [ ] At least 3 feet from curtains/hanging items
- [ ] No blockage of heater vents
- [ ] Never in use while sleeping

**Exit Routes:**
- [ ] Exit door accessible
- [ ] Window can be opened for emergency exit (not painted shut)
- [ ] No furniture blocking exit
- [ ] Clear path to door in darkness

**Smoking Safety:**
- [ ] No smoking in bedroom (if applicable)
- [ ] Ashtrays not on furniture (fire risk)

### LIVING AREAS — Common Fire Sources

**Fireplace/Woodstove:**
- [ ] Fireplace screen in place (if applicable)
- [ ] Hearth clear of clutter (1-foot clearance recommended)
- [ ] No logs/kindling stacked near fireplace
- [ ] Chimney inspected/cleaned recently (ask homeowner)
- [ ] No flammables on mantle (photos, candles close to heat?)

**Space Heaters:**
- [ ] At least 3 feet from furniture, curtains, and walls
- [ ] On hard, level surface (not carpet)
- [ ] Thermostat working (no constant running)
- [ ] Cord in good repair
- [ ] Automatic shutoff if available

**Electrical:**
- [ ] Outlets not overloaded with power strips
- [ ] Cords running along walls safely (not under rugs)
- [ ] Appliances in good repair
- [ ] Circuit breaker panel accessible

**Candles:**
- [ ] Candles supervised when burning
- [ ] Candles on heat-resistant surfaces
- [ ] Away from curtains and flammables (12+ inches)
- [ ] No candles on armrests or soft furniture

**General:**
- [ ] Clear exit routes (no blockage)
- [ ] Furniture arrangement doesn't block doors
- [ ] No clutter on stairs

### BATHROOM — Water and Electrical Hazard Mix

**Electrical Safety:**
- [ ] GFCI outlets present (special outlets that sense ground faults)
- [ ] No extension cords (water hazard)
- [ ] Hair dryer/heater kept away from water sources
- [ ] No candles/open flames in bathroom

**Water Management:**
- [ ] Water heater set to safe temperature (should be checked; test with thermometer if possible)
- [ ] Exhaust fan working (removes moisture/fire risk reduction)
- [ ] No blockage of water heater venting
- [ ] Anti-scald devices on shower/tub valves (if applicable)

### LAUNDRY AREA — Dryer Safety

**Clothes Dryer:**
- [ ] Lint screen clean before/after use
- [ ] Vent duct clear (feel for airflow at outside vent)
- [ ] Vent duct not crushed or kinked
- [ ] Dryer exterior clean of lint accumulation
- [ ] Dryer venting to outside (not into walls or attic)
- [ ] No flammables stored near dryer

**Electrical:**
- [ ] Dryer plugged directly into outlet (not extension cord)
- [ ] Outlet functioning properly
- [ ] Cord in good repair

### UTILITY/FURNACE ROOM — System Safety

**Furnace/Boiler:**
- [ ] Clear space around furnace (2-foot clearance minimum)
- [ ] No flammables stored near furnace
- [ ] Furnace filter clean
- [ ] Furnace venting properly
- [ ] No water heater next to furnace (space heaters can be fire risk)

**Electrical Panel:**
- [ ] Panel accessible
- [ ] No items blocking access
- [ ] No water/moisture around panel
- [ ] Breakers labeled

**Flammable Storage:**
- [ ] Gas can stored in shed/garage (not indoors)
- [ ] Propane BBQ tanks stored outside
- [ ] Batteries stored safely (damaged ones removed)
- [ ] Cleaning supplies stored away from heat

### HALLWAYS AND STAIRS — Escape Routes

**Exit Accessibility:**
- [ ] All doors operable
- [ ] Windows accessible (not painted shut)
- [ ] No furniture blocking hallways
- [ ] Stairs clear of clutter
- [ ] Light switches accessible (can find in darkness?)

**Safety Features:**
- [ ] Smoke alarms in hallway
- [ ] Exit signs visible (if required)
- [ ] Emergency contact info accessible

### EXTERIOR — Outside Fire Risk

**Structure:**
- [ ] Roof/gutters clear of debris/leaves (fire spreads from roof)
- [ ] Dead branches removed from trees near house
- [ ] Brush/vegetation at least 10 feet away (wildfire risk)
- [ ] Firewood stacked away from house (at least 30 feet)

**Electrical:**
- [ ] Outside outlets functioning
- [ ] No extension cords lying in yard long-term
- [ ] Generators (if present) not run indoors

**Water:**
- [ ] Garden hose accessible
- [ ] Water source accessible for fire response
- [ ] Driveway clear for fire truck access

## Conducting Your Inspection

### Room-by-Room Walkthrough

1. **Start at kitchen** (highest risk)
2. **Move to bedrooms**
3. **Check living areas**
4. **Inspect bathrooms and laundry**
5. **Check utility areas**
6. **Review hallways and exits**
7. **Walk exterior perimeter**

### Documentation

**For each hazard found:**
- Write location (e.g., "Kitchen stovetop")
- Describe hazard (e.g., "Grease splatter screen not accessible")
- Note severity: Minor, Moderate, Serious
- Suggest remedy (e.g., "Purchase splatter screen and store in cabinet")

**Take photos:**
- Hazardous situations (space heater placement, blocked exits)
- Positive findings (working smoke alarm, cleared lint screen)
- Visual evidence for counselor discussion

### Observations to Make

**Beyond the checklist, notice:**
- How quickly you could escape in darkness
- Where smoke/CO might accumulate
- Which areas are clutter-prone (fire risk)
- Behavioral patterns (is stove often left unattended?)
- Adequacy of exit routes for mobility-limited family members

## Presenting Your Findings to Your Counselor

### Prepare Your Presentation

**Organize by category:**
1. **Serious hazards** (immediate danger)
2. **Moderate hazards** (significant risk)
3. **Minor hazards** (preventable, less risk)
4. **Positive findings** (things done well)

**Be specific:**
- Location: "Front right burner on stovetop"
- Hazard: "Pot handles extend over edge, could be knocked"
- Solution: "Turn handles to point along stovetop edge"

### Discussion Points with Counselor

**Prepare to discuss:**

1. **What surprised you?** — Any hazards you didn't expect?
2. **Which hazards are most dangerous?** — Why?
3. **What can be fixed immediately?** — No-cost fixes?
4. **What needs investment?** — Space heater, new cord, repair?
5. **Who is most vulnerable?** — Children? Elderly? Why?
6. **Prevention focus** — How do your findings relate to fire causes in Req 5a-5b?
7. **Family conversation** — How will you discuss findings with family? Who should help fix things?

### Action Plan

Create a prioritized action plan:

**Immediate (this week):**
- Clear flammables from stove area
- Remove items blocking exits
- Test smoke alarms

**Short-term (this month):**
- Replace damaged cords
- Clean dryer vent
- Adjust space heater distance

**Long-term (next season):**
- Have furnace inspected
- Get fireplace chimney cleaned
- Update fire extinguishers

## Why This Inspection Matters

By inspecting your home systematically, you:

- **Develop safety awareness** — Learn what hazards look like in real homes
- **Protect your family** — Identify risks they may have missed
- **Apply knowledge** — Connect what you learned in Req 5a-5c to actual hazards
- **Practice prevention** — The best fire response is preventing fires
- **Build advocacy** — You can help your family and community reduce fire risk

{{< drg/tip >}}
Many families don't think about fire safety until after a fire. Your inspection helps your family think about it proactively. Even if your family doesn't fix every hazard immediately, awareness is the first step to prevention.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
