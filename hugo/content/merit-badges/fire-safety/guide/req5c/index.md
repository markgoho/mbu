Demonstrate how to safely light and extinguish a candle. Discuss with your counselor, then with your family, how to safely use candles.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Candles create ambiance, but they also create open flames in your home. Knowing how to light and extinguish them safely, plus understanding the risks, means you can use candles responsibly. This requirement asks you to demonstrate the skill AND discuss safety—both parts matter.

## How to Safely Light a Candle

### Preparation

Before striking a match or using a lighter, prepare your space:

**Choose a Safe Location:**
- Place candle on heat-resistant surface (metal, glass, or ceramic plate/holder)
- Never place on wood, plastic, or cardboard
- Choose stable location where candle won't be knocked over
- Away from curtains, wall hangings, or other flammables (at least 12 inches)
- Away from drafts (they make flames flicker and increase wind-blown embers)
- On a stable table or shelf, not on armrests, beds, or soft surfaces

**Remove Flammables:**
- Clear area around candle of papers, tissues, plastic bags, blankets
- Keep hair tied back if you have long hair
- Move loose sleeves away from candle before lighting
- Remove anything that could blow into flame if there's a breeze

**Have Water or Fire Extinguisher Nearby:**
- Fire extinguisher within arm's reach (or water in cup)
- Knowing help is immediately available builds confidence

### The Lighting Process

**Using a Match:**

1. **Hold the match steady** — Strike at the matchbook at a slight angle, not perpendicular
2. **Bring match to candle wick** — Position match just above the wick
3. **Light the wick** — Flame from match ignites wick; you'll see it catch and glow
4. **Hold steady for a moment** — Let wick establish flame before removing match
5. **Let match burn briefly** — Wick will now have independent flame
6. **Extinguish match safely** — Let it burn down slightly, then carefully place in water or fireproof container
7. **Never wave match to extinguish** — Waving spreads embers

**Using a Lighter:**

1. **Grip lighter firmly** — Don't let your hand slip
2. **Use ignition button or wheel** — Depending on lighter type
3. **Position flame near wick** — Wick needs direct contact with flame to light
4. **Hold steady until wick ignites** — Watch for the wick to glow independent of lighter flame
5. **Remove lighter slowly** — Don't jerk away
6. **Keep lighter at safe distance** — Away from flammables after use

**Using Taper or Long Match:**

1. **Light the taper** — Use another candle or match to light a long taper
2. **Carefully approach candle** — Use both hands for stability
3. **Position taper at wick** — Let taper flame contact wick
4. **Hold steady until wick ignites** — Watch for independent flame
5. **Withdraw taper slowly** — Don't blow on candle (can extinguish it)

### Candle Burning Safety

Once lit, keep these safety practices:

- **Never leave unattended** — Someone must be in the room while candle burns
- **Keep away from pets and children** — They may touch hot wax or knock over candle
- **Trim wick to 1/4 inch** — Long wicks smoke and create soot
- **Keep wax pool clear** — Don't let debris fall into melted wax
- **Replace when nearly gone** — Candles in metal holders can overheat bottom
- **Watch for tunneling** — If wax only melts down center, candle isn't burning properly (might be draft)

## How to Safely Extinguish a Candle

### The Wick Dip Method (Best)

This is the preferred method for snuffing candles:

1. **Prepare** — Have a candle snuffer or a long spoon ready
2. **Dip wick into wax** — Gently tip the snuffer's metal bell onto the flame to smother it
3. **Lift out** — Remove snuffer after 1-2 seconds
4. **Watch flame go out** — Flame extinguishes from lack of oxygen
5. **Don't disturb candle** — Let it cool without moving it

**Why this works:** You're using the candle's own wax to smother the flame. Smothering removes oxygen, extinguishing the fire. Much safer than blowing.

### The Snuffer Tool

If you have a candle snuffer (small bell on a handle):

1. **Hold snuffer steady** — Use both hands if needed
2. **Lower snuffer over flame** — Place metal bell gently on flame
3. **Hold 1-2 seconds** — Let oxygen deplete
4. **Lift away** — Flame should be extinguished
5. **Don't rush** — Forcing snuffer down too fast splashes wax

### What NOT to Do

**Never blow out a candle:**
- Blowing scatters embers and soot
- Can blow out matches or lighter and create danger
- Can spread wax around
- Creates risk of inhaling particles

**Never use water to extinguish:**
- Water + hot wax causes sputtering and splashing
- Water may crack candle holder if it's ceramic or glass
- Risk of getting burned by splashing wax

**Never pinch the wick:**
- Burns your fingers
- Creates smoke and soot
- Can break wick

**Never leave to self-extinguish:**
- Candle burns completely down
- Holder overheats
- Flame reaches near objects
- Risk of unattended fire

## Demonstrating Safe Candle Use to Your Counselor

Your counselor will want to see:

**Preparation Demonstration:**
- Safe location choice
- Removal of flammables
- Proper candle holder or surface
- Distance from curtains/combustibles

**Lighting Demonstration:**
- Proper match/lighter technique
- Steady hand positioning
- Watching for wick to ignite
- Safe disposal of match

**Burning Knowledge:**
- Why you never leave unattended
- What to watch for while burning
- How to keep wax pool clear
- Why wick length matters

**Extinguishing Demonstration:**
- Using snuffer or dipping method
- Never blowing
- Never using water
- Letting candle cool

**Discussion Topics:**
- Where candles are safe to burn (not near curtains, on stable surfaces)
- Why supervision is critical
- What could go wrong if candles aren't used carefully
- When to use candles vs. other lighting

## Discussing Candle Safety with Your Family

After demonstrating with your counselor, you'll discuss with your family. Here are talking points:

### Risks of Candle Use

**Fire Risk:**
- Open flame in the home
- Can ignite nearby flammables if accidentally placed too close
- Unattended candles are a common fire cause
- Children/pets may knock over candles

**Burn Risk:**
- Hot wax burns (not just the flame)
- Candle holders get extremely hot
- Melted wax can splash

**Air Quality:**
- Some candles produce soot that deposits on walls/ceiling
- Low-quality candles smoke heavily
- Scented candles release chemicals

### Safe Candle Use Rules for Your Home

Propose these rules and discuss with family:

1. **Location Rule:** Candles only on stable, heat-resistant surfaces away from flammables (draft a rule with your family)
2. **Supervision Rule:** Never leave a burning candle unattended (agree on room someone must be in)
3. **Children/Pets Rule:** Candles kept away from reach (agree on safe placement)
4. **Extinguishing Rule:** Always use snuffer or wick-dip method, never blow (discuss alternatives)
5. **No Bedroom Rule:** No candles while sleeping (discuss fire risk if candle is knocked over)
6. **No Bathroom Rule:** No candles unattended in bathrooms (risk near water, flammable items)

### Alternatives to Candles

You might discuss safer alternatives:
- **LED candles** — Realistic flicker, no real flame, no heat
- **Oil lamps** — Safer than candles but still require attention
- **Lighting fixtures** — Electric lights that don't create fire risk
- **Flashlights/lanterns** — For ambiance without fire risk

## Candle Safety Tips Summary

{{< drg/checklist title="Safe Candle Use" subtitle="Every time you light a candle" >}}
- **Preparation:** Safe location, heat-resistant holder, clear of flammables, away from drafts
- **Lighting:** Steady match/lighter technique, watch for wick ignition, safe match disposal
- **Burning:** Never unattended, away from pets/children, watch wax pool, proper wick length
- **Extinguishing:** Snuffer or dip method, never blow, let cool before moving
- **Conversation:** Family understanding of risks and rules, agreement on safe practices
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
The most important safety step is deciding in advance WHERE candles are safe in your home and WHEN someone must be nearby. Before lighting, take 10 seconds to assess: Is this location safe? Is someone going to stay with this candle? If the answer is no to either question, don't light it.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Some candles are made from soy or beeswax and produce less soot than paraffin candles. If your family uses candles regularly, higher-quality candles burn cleaner and are safer (less smoking = less soot = less air quality issues).
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}
