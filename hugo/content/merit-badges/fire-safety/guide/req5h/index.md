{{< drg/requirement number="5h" >}}
Explain what to do and what not to do if you smell natural gas and if you smell smoke.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

When danger signs appear (unusual odors, smoke, alarms), knowing what to do saves lives. What you do in the first 30 seconds determines whether you escape safely or become trapped. This requirement teaches you to recognize danger and respond correctly.

## If You Smell Natural Gas

### Why Natural Gas is Dangerous

**Natural gas in your home:**
- Powers stove, oven, furnace, water heater, dryer
- Naturally odorless (utility company adds rotten-egg smell so you detect leaks)
- Explodes if ignited (concentrations of 5-15% in air ignite)
- Can cause illness or death if inhaled (displaces oxygen)
- Heavier than air (accumulates at floor level)

**How leaks happen:**
- Broken gas line
- Loose fitting or connection
- Aging equipment failure
- Improper installation

**Why response is critical:**
- Gas leak can lead to explosion
- Gas can asphyxiate people
- Leak spreads quickly through home
- Delay of minutes can be catastrophic

### What TO DO If You Smell Natural Gas

{{< drg/be-prepared title="If You Smell Rotten Egg Smell (Natural Gas Leak)" >}}
**Immediate actions (first 30 seconds):**

1. **Don't ignore the smell** — Act immediately, don't wait to see if it goes away
2. **Alert everyone in home** — Tell family members what you detected
3. **Leave the home immediately** — Go outside, don't use phone or light switches inside
4. **Don't search for the source** — Don't look for leaking pipe or appliance
5. **Don't use any electrical switches** — Light switches, outlets, fans
6. **Don't use matches, lighters, or candles** — Any spark can ignite gas
7. **Don't start vehicle or use garage door opener** — Also can spark ignition
8. **Go to safe location** — Outside home, at least 50 feet away
9. **Call 911 from safe location** — Use phone outside or neighbor's phone
10. **Tell gas company and firefighters** — They'll investigate and clear

**What firefighters will do:**
- Investigate the leak
- Determine if ignition risk exists
- Turn off gas supply if needed
- Ventilate building
- Find and repair the leak
- Test to ensure it's safe to re-enter

{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

### What NOT TO DO If You Smell Natural Gas

**Never do these things:**

- **Don't turn lights on or off** — Light switch spark can ignite gas
- **Don't use outlets** — Plugging in/unplugging creates spark
- **Don't run appliances** — Even if you think they're safe, avoid them
- **Don't use phone inside home** — Phone spark in presence of gas can ignite
- **Don't start car** — Engine spark inside/near garage is extremely dangerous
- **Don't use matches or lighter** — Obviously, but panic people sometimes try
- **Don't open windows normally** — Move outside first, then ventilate from outside
- **Don't assume it will blow over** — Gas leaks don't fix themselves
- **Don't go to different room to check** — Get out first
- **Don't wait for other family members** — Get them as you go, but don't delay

### Signs of Natural Gas Leak

Besides the rotten-egg smell:
- **Hissing sound** near gas appliance or line
- **White condensation clouds** from gas line (frost forming)
- **Bubbles in water** if you pour water over suspected line (hiss = leak)
- **Physical symptoms** — Dizziness, nausea, headache (gas inhalation effects)
- **Damaged gas line visible** — Dent, crack, corrosion

### Special Situations with Gas

**Outdoors detecting gas smell:**
- May indicate leak at meter or outside line
- Still call utility company (don't assume minor)
- Evacuate area (50+ feet)
- Let professionals investigate

**In an apartment/condo:**
- Your gas may connect through shared walls
- Still evacuate your unit
- Alert neighbors if you think leak originates in their unit
- Don't access other units

**If gas is currently flowing:**
- You may hear hissing as gas flows
- Smell is strongest nearest the leak
- Don't try to find source
- Get everyone out

## If You Smell Smoke

### Why Early Smoke Detection is Critical

Smoke arrival usually precedes visible flames by minutes. Early detection gives maximum escape time:
- 2-3 minutes to evacuate before conditions become lethal
- Smoke spreads faster than most people expect
- Smoke carries toxic gases (not just heat and particles)
- Visibility decreases rapidly as smoke thickens

### What TO DO If You Smell Smoke

{{< drg/be-prepared title="If You Smell Smoke (Possible Fire)" >}}
**Immediate actions:**

1. **Don't hesitate** — Smoke smell means fire exists or is imminent
2. **Alert everyone** — Yell "FIRE!" or "SMOKE!" to wake/alert household
3. **Activate your alarm** — If fire is in room, press fire alarm if accessible
4. **Evacuate immediately** — Follow your fire escape plan
5. **Use primary route** — Go to your planned exit
6. **Stay low** — Crawl under smoke if possible (breathable air near floor)
7. **Feel doors** — Before opening, feel if door is hot
8. **Leave everything** — Clothes, belongings, anything else
9. **Go to meeting point** — Family gathers at designated location
10. **Call 911 from outside** — Once safe and accounted for
11. **Never go back in** — Not for people, pets, or things

**If trapped:**
- Go to room with windows
- Close door (slows fire spread)
- Block gap under door with cloth/blanket
- Signal from window (wave, flashlight)
- Call 911 if phone available
- Wait for rescue

{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

### What NOT TO DO If You Smell Smoke

**Never do these things:**

- **Don't investigate the fire** — You don't have protective equipment
- **Don't close doors behind you** — Door closes on itself; it slows your escape and traps you
- **Don't gather belongings** — Every second counts; minutes matter
- **Don't wake people individually** — Alert but move; don't wait
- **Don't try to extinguish fire** — Unless very small and exit is accessible (even then, leave instead)
- **Don't turn off lights** — It wastes time; leave them on so rescue can find people
- **Don't stop to get dressed** — Life is more important than clothes
- **Don't use elevator** — If there's smoke, take stairs
- **Don't hide in closet** — Go outside where rescuers can find you
- **Don't split up from family** — Go to meeting point together

### Recognizing Different Smoke Smells

**Different fires smell different:**

| Smell | Likely Source | Color Clue | Risk |
|-------|---------------|-----------|------|
| Electrical burning | Electrical fire, outlet, cord | Gray/light smoke | Toxic gases, spreads in walls |
| Plastic burning | Appliance, synthetic materials | Black smoke | Extremely toxic, rapid spread |
| Wood burning | Fireplace escape, wooden structure | Gray/brown smoke | Heat damage, spread potential |
| Cooking smell | Stovetop fire, oven | Tan/gray smoke | Often contained, if growing = danger |
| Chemical/acrid | Insulation, treated materials | Dark gray/black | Very toxic, evacuate faster |

**Important:** Don't try to identify—all smoke means get out.

### Smoke From Different Locations

**If smoke is where you are:**
- Evacuate immediately using primary route
- Crawl under smoke if visibility low
- Feel doors before opening (hot = fire beyond)

**If smoke visible/smelled but you don't know source:**
- Treat as real fire—don't investigate
- Alert household
- Activate your escape plan
- Get outside

**If alarm sounds (you didn't smell smoke):**
- Assume fire exists (alarm may detect smoke you can't yet smell)
- Evacuate immediately
- Don't wait for smoke to become obvious

### Smoke Inhalation Response

If you've breathed smoke:
- **Get fresh air immediately** — Leaves building
- **Call 911 if breathing difficulty** — Even if seems okay now, smoke inhalation effects can be delayed
- **Don't go back in** — Smoke continues spreading
- **Get evaluated at hospital** — Lung damage may not be obvious

## Coordinated Response: Gas AND Smoke

**If you smell both gas AND smoke:**

1. **Exit immediately** — Combination is extremely dangerous (explosion risk)
2. **Don't attempt to identify sources** — Both require professional response
3. **Go at least 100 feet away** — Distance provides protection
4. **Call 911** — Report both gas and smoke
5. **Keep others away** — Don't let neighbors re-enter

## Teaching Others

This is critical knowledge for your whole family. Discuss with your counselor:

- **Your family's action plan** — What will each person do?
- **Gas smell response** — Does everyone know to leave and call from outside?
- **Smoke response** — Does everyone know their escape route?
- **Special situations** — What if someone is sleeping?
- **Child understanding** — Can young children understand and follow plan?

## Building Safety Knowledge

Your counselor will want to discuss:

1. **Natural gas hazards** — Why immediate evacuation?
2. **Smoke detection importance** — Why every second counts?
3. **Why you don't investigate** — Professional training needed
4. **Chain of command** — Who alerts 911?
5. **Special family situations** — How do elderly/disabled respond?

{{< drg/tip >}}
If you're ever unsure whether to evacuate: **Evacuate.** It's better to leave and find it was false alarm than to stay and find it was real fire. False alarms are inconvenient; fire deaths are permanent.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Most people who die in fires die from smoke inhalation (not burns). The smoke carries toxic gases like carbon monoxide and cyanide that kill in seconds. Getting out of the smoke is your first priority—breathing smoke is almost as dangerous as flames.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}
