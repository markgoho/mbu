Camp stoves are efficient, Leave No Trace alternatives to campfires. They provide controlled heat for cooking in any weather or location. Safe operation requires understanding fuel types and proper technique.

## Types of Camp Stoves

### Canister Stoves (Most Common)

**How it works:**
- Threaded fuel canister (butane/propane mix) screws into stove
- Burner assembly heats fuel, vaporizing it
- Creates controllable flame
- Lightweight and compact

**Advantages:**
- Ultra-light (fits in backpack)
- Quick heating (water boils in minutes)
- Precise flame control
- Leave No Trace (no ashes, minimal impact)

**Disadvantages:**
- Fuel canisters are consumable (eventually empty)
- Performance decreases in cold
- Cost per use (canisters ~$5-7)

### Liquid Fuel Stoves (Backpacking)

**How it works:**
- Separate fuel bottle (white gas, kerosene, or unleaded gas)
- Pump pressurizes fuel into burner
- Creates consistent flame
- Separate bottle from burner

**Advantages:**
- Excellent cold-weather performance
- Inexpensive per use
- Canisters reusable

**Disadvantages:**
- More complex setup
- Heavier than canister
- Messier (requires priming)
- More maintenance

### Integrated Stove Systems

**What they are:**
- Canister stove integrated into pot/container system
- Compact, lightweight, convenient
- Good heat transfer efficiency

**Advantages:**
- Ultra-lightweight
- Boils water very efficiently
- Everything compact together

**Disadvantages:**
- Limited to one pot size
- Can be expensive ($30-50)

## Safe Camp Stove Operation

### Setup (Before Ignition)

1. **Choose location** — Flat, protected from wind, away from tent
2. **Clear area** — No flammables within 1 foot
3. **Inspect stove** — Check for damage, cracks, loose parts
4. **Prepare fuel** — Have canister or bottle ready
5. **Attach fuel source** — Screw on canister or connect bottle
6. **Test connection** — Ensure tight (no fuel leaks)
7. **Position pot** — Stable pot on grate; won't tip over
8. **Place lighter/matches nearby** — Ready for ignition

### Ignition (Turning On)

**Canister stove:**
1. Turn control valve slightly (don't fully open yet)
2. Wait 10-30 seconds (allows fuel to flow and vaporize)
3. Light with lighter or match
4. Adjust flame with control valve
5. Steady blue flame is normal; yellow means need more air

**Liquid fuel:**
1. Follow manufacturer priming instructions
2. Light priming fuel
3. Once hot, main burner ignites from priming
4. Open fuel valve to desired flame level

### Operation Safety Rules

**Always:**
- Operate outdoors (ventilation critical)
- Keep hands and face away from flame
- Turn off stove when leaving (even briefly)
- Use proper pot supports (don't improvise)
- Monitor pot (don't let water boil over onto burner)
- Keep flammables away (tent, sleeping bag, fuel bottles)
- Use pot lid to extinguish if needed (covers flame)

**Never:**
- Use indoors (carbon monoxide hazard)
- Adjust valve vigorously (could break connection)
- Add fuel while lit (fire hazard)
- Leave burning unattended
- Use in high winds without protection (unstable flames)
- Wear loose clothing near flame

### Cooking on Camp Stove

1. Light stove and establish flame
2. Place pot on secure grate
3. Wait for water to boil or food to heat
4. Stir frequently (especially if cooking food)
5. Adjust flame as needed (more flame = faster heat, but wastes fuel)
6. Remove pot carefully (very hot!)
7. Turn off stove when done cooking

### Extinguishing

**Proper shutdown:**
1. Turn off fuel valve completely
2. Flame should extinguish immediately
3. If flame doesn't extinguish, try again (may need more pressure on valve)
4. Wait 2-3 minutes before touching stove (cooling period)
5. Wait 5+ minutes before removing fuel canister (allows pressure to equalize)

**Cool down:**
- Don't touch burner (extremely hot immediately after use)
- Allow air to cool it
- Pot will also be very hot (use gloves when handling)

## Wind Protection

Camp stoves work poorly in strong wind (flame blows away from pot). Solutions:

- **Wind screen** — Purchase folding aluminum screen around stove
- **Natural shelter** — Position against log or rock
- **Cooking area** — Find location protected by terrain

## Fuel Canister Management

**Pressure build-up:**
- Hot canisters build internal pressure
- Keep away from direct heat
- Cool canisters before removing (pressure equalizes)
- Store upright (prevents liquid fuel from leaking)

**Storage:**
- Keep spare canisters in cool location
- Never store near heat
- Never in direct sunlight (pressure increases)
- Check for leaks (smell for fuel odor)

## When to Use Camp Stove vs. Campfire

**Use camp stove when:**
- Leave No Trace priority (no scarring)
- Weather bad (rain prevents campfire)
- Area has fire ban
- You want quick, efficient cooking
- Campfire not permitted

**Use campfire when:**
- Established fire ring available
- Extended group gathering desired
- Heat for warmth is priority
- Traditional Scouting experience valued

**Many campers use both** — Stove for cooking, campfire for warmth/gathering

## Demonstration for Counselor

Show your counselor:
- **Safe setup** (location, inspection, preparation)
- **Proper ignition** (controlled lighting)
- **Stable operation** (flame control, pot security)
- **Safety rules** (flammable clearance, never leave unattended)
- **Safe extinguishing** (complete shutdown)
- **Cooking competence** (boil water or heat food)
- **Equipment respect** (proper cool-down, storage)
