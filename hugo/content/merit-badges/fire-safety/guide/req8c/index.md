{{< drg/requirement number="8c" >}}
Explain and demonstrate making sparks with flint and steel or with a ferro rod and catching a spark to create a flame. Extinguish the flame safely using one of principles of the fire tetrahedron.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Flint and steel (or modern ferro rods) are backup fire-starting methods when matches fail. They create sparks that ignite tinder and teach fire science through practice.

## Flint and Steel vs. Ferro Rod

**Flint and Steel:**
- Traditional method, unlimited sparks
- Requires practice; slower ignition
- Works when wet

**Ferro Rod:**
- Modern method with hotter sparks (1500-2000°F)
- Easier to use; more reliable
- Wears down over time but lasts thousands of strikes

## Creating Sparks

### Technique (Both Methods)

1. **Prepare tinder bundle** — Dry material (lint, bark, char cloth)
2. **Position materials** — Flint/rod 6-8 inches from tinder
3. **Strike downward** — Create sparks flowing into bundle
4. **Watch for glow** — Sparks may create small ember
5. **Blow gently** — Oxygen encourages flame development
6. **Add kindling** — Small twigs once flame appears

### Common Mistakes

- Wrong angle (sparks fly away)
- Damp tinder (won't catch)
- Insufficient blowing (flame dies)
- Tinder bundle too small

## Fire Tetrahedron Connection

**The four elements:**
1. **Heat** — Sparks provide initial heat (400-2000°F)
2. **Fuel** — Tinder is highly flammable
3. **Oxygen** — Air within tinder and around spark
4. **Chemical reaction** — Oxidation begins

**Extinguishing using tetrahedron:**
- **Remove heat** — Blow out or immerse water
- **Remove fuel** — Remove kindling (impractical during fire)
- **Remove oxygen** — Smother with cloth or soil
- **Interrupt reaction** — Not practical once ignition starts

## Demonstration Checklist

- **Proper preparation** — Tinder ready, kindling organized
- **Spark creation** — Correct striking technique
- **Catching sparks in tinder** — Sparks reach and ignite bundle
- **Flame development** — Blowing to encourage growth
- **Safe extinguishment** — Using one tetrahedron principle
- **Explanation** — Describe spark ignition process
