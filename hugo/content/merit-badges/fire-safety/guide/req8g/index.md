{{< drg/requirement number="8g" >}}
Explain how to set up and keep a campsite safe from fire.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A safe campsite prevents accidental fires and allows quick response if fire starts. This requirement teaches you to think about camp layout, fire placement, and emergency preparedness.

## Campsite Location Selection

### Site Assessment

**Before setting up:**
- Check for fire bans or restrictions
- Evaluate fire danger rating (weather forecast)
- Note water sources (for emergency use)
- Identify evacuation routes
- Check weather forecast (wind, lightning risk)

**Choose location with:**
- Flat, stable ground
- Distance from dense vegetation
- Natural windbreaks (but adequate ventilation)
- Access to water
- Clear escape routes

### Site Layout Planning

**Establish zones:**
1. **Cooking zone** — Stove or campfire area
2. **Sleeping zone** — Tents, at least 20+ feet from fire
3. **Storage zone** — Fuel, food (away from fire)
4. **Evacuation route** — Clear path to safety

**Distances:**
- Fire to tent: 20+ feet minimum
- Fire to fuel storage: 30+ feet
- Fire to sleeping area: 20+ feet
- All away from overhanging branches

## Campfire Setup Safety

### Fire Ring Preparation

**Ideal situation:**
- Use established fire ring (don't create new)
- Clear radius of 12+ feet
- Remove flammable debris (leaves, pine needles, branches)
- Remove any low branches above fire ring
- Create bare earth ring around fire (prevents spread)

**Water preparation:**
- Keep bucket or large container filled with water nearby
- Have shovel for smothering with dirt if needed
- Know location of water source (river, stream, lake)

### Fire Management During Use

**Active monitoring:**
- Always have adult present while fire burns
- Never leave campfire unattended
- Keep children supervised (away from direct flame)
- Keep pets away from fire
- Monitor wind (if gusts increase, extinguish fire)

**Fire size control:**
- Keep fire small (easier to manage than large)
- Don't let fire grow beyond control
- Never allow flames to touch overhanging branches
- Keep firewood controlled (don't scatter around site)

## Tent and Sleeping Area Safety

### Distance and Layout

**Position tents:**
- At least 20 feet from fire (ideally 30+)
- Away from standing dead trees (could fall if wind)
- Not directly downwind of fire (smoke hazard)
- On level ground away from water runoff

**Never:**
- Place tent within 15 feet of fire ring
- Position tent where smoke blows directly into it
- Leave candle or lantern inside tent unattended
- Use open flame for tent heating

### Inside Tent Safety

**If using lantern:**
- Hang securely (won't tip)
- Keep away from fabric (tent material)
- Never leave unattended
- Better: use battery-powered light

**Tent ventilation:**
- Leave some ventilation (prevents CO buildup if using stove nearby)
- Never use camp stove inside tent (carbon monoxide hazard)

## Fuel Storage and Hazard Management

### Fuel and Equipment Storage

**Store safely:**
- Keep fuel canisters away from fire (at least 30 feet)
- Store in cool location
- Keep out of direct sunlight
- Keep upright and undamaged
- Never store near tent

**If using liquid fuel:**
- Keep bottles tightly capped
- Store away from heat
- Store away from sleeping areas
- Check for leaks periodically

### Flammable Item Management

**Keep away from fire:**
- Backpacks and gear
- Food and supplies
- Extra clothing
- Sleeping bags
- Anything that could accidentally blow into fire

**Organization:**
- Designate storage areas
- Keep organized to prevent searching in fire area
- Store properly (protected from weather and animals)

## Emergency Preparedness

### Fire Extinguishing Setup

**Always prepared:**
- Water bucket filled and nearby
- Shovel (for dirt smothering)
- Dirt or sand for emergency smothering
- Fire extinguisher (if vehicle camping)
- Know how to use each

### Escape Routes

**Before emergency occurs:**
- Identify evacuation paths
- Know location of nearest help
- Have meeting point established
- Know how to call for help (if cell service)
- Brief all campers on emergency plan

### Emergency Response

**If fire escapes control:**
1. Alert everyone at camp
2. Try to extinguish with water/dirt (if safe)
3. If can't control, evacuate camp
4. Move to established meeting point
5. Call 911 from safe location
6. Never go back into fire

## Lightning Safety

**If thunderstorm threatening:**
- Move away from tall, isolated trees
- Avoid open areas
- Don't cook on stove during lightning storm
- Leave tent and move to lowest spot if lightning very close
- Wait 30 minutes after last thunder before resuming activities

## Weather Monitoring

**Check conditions:**
- Daily weather forecast before setting out
- Watch sky for storm development
- High wind increases fire danger (extinguish fire)
- Fire bans may be issued (stop all fires immediately)
- Red Flag Warnings mean extreme fire danger (no campfires)

## Typical Campsite Fire Safety Layout

```
            [Tents]
            (20+ feet from fire)


[Fuel Storage]  [FIRE RING]  [Water bucket]
(30+ feet away) (Clear 12-ft) (Nearby)
                  radius


                [Cooking area]
                (Camp stove)
```

## Demonstration for Counselor

Explain to your counselor:
- **Site selection criteria** (what makes good/bad location)
- **Zone layout** (cooking, sleeping, storage arrangement)
- **Fire ring safety** (clearing, water availability)
- **Distance requirements** (why 20+ feet matters)
- **Emergency preparedness** (what you'd do if fire escaped)
- **Weather monitoring** (how to respond to conditions)
- **Wind management** (when to extinguish, why)
