{{< drg/requirement number="5b" >}}
Describe how to prevent frequent causes of seasonal and holiday-related home fires, including fires related to home heating, holiday trees, candles, and fireworks.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Different seasons bring different fire hazards. Winter heating, holiday decorations, and summer fireworks create seasonal peaks in residential fire deaths. By understanding what makes each season dangerous, you can prepare your home.

## Winter Heating Fires

Winter is peak fire season because most homes rely on heat sources that create fire risk. More fires occur in January-February than any other months.

### Why Winter Heating Causes Fires

**Space Heaters:**
- Generate intense heat (often 1000-1500 watts)
- Hot surfaces reach 200-400°F
- Can ignite nearby combustibles in seconds
- Tip-over ignites surrounding materials
- Cheap heaters may not have safety shutoff

**Fireplaces and Woodstoves:**
- Extremely hot surfaces (500-900°F at firebox)
- Chimney creosote builds up from wood burning
- Creosote ignites causing chimney fires
- Sparks escape through cracks in chimney
- Ash contains hot coals that ignite materials

**Central Heating System Issues:**
- Furnaces with worn components overheat
- Clogged filters restrict airflow
- Broken heat exchangers create fire/CO hazards
- Improper venting allows combustion byproducts inside home
- Ductwork with gaps allows flames/heat to escape

**Extension Cord Overload:**
- Space heater plugged into extension cord that overheats
- Multiple devices on circuit during peak heating usage
- Older homes with insufficient electrical capacity
- Extension cord meant for temporary use becomes permanent

### Prevention Strategies for Heating Fires

**Space Heater Safety:**

- **Placement:** Keep at least 3 feet away from anything flammable (bed, curtains, furniture, walls)
- **Flooring:** Place on hard, level surface—not on carpet (blocks cooling)
- **Supervision:** Never leave running heater unattended
- **Powered off:** Turn off when leaving room
- **Tip-over protection:** Use heaters with automatic shutoff if tipped
- **Inspection:** Look for cracks, damage, or discoloration (signs of overheating)
- **Children/pets:** Keep away from hot surfaces
- **Cord safety:** Plug directly into outlet, not extension cord

**Fireplace and Woodstove Safety:**

- **Chimney cleaning:** Professional chimney sweep annually before heating season
- **Creosote removal:** Burning hardwood (not softwood) reduces creosote, but still requires cleaning
- **Spark protection:** Use fireplace screen or glass doors to contain sparks
- **Ash management:** Remove ash carefully (can contain hot coals); place in metal container outside
- **Distance:** No furniture or combustibles within 3 feet of fireplace opening
- **Damper:** Ensure damper is open (prevents dangerous gas backup)
- **Inspection:** Have chimney inspected for cracks, separation, or creosote buildup
- **Never:** Don't burn garbage, treated wood, or anything that produces toxic fumes

**Central Heating System:**

- **Annual inspection:** Have furnace professionally inspected before heating season
- **Filter changes:** Replace furnace filters monthly during heating season
- **Chimney/vent:** Keep vent to outside clear and undamaged
- **CO detection:** Install CO detectors near bedrooms
- **Professional maintenance:** Have HVAC technician service furnace if making unusual noises
- **Age consideration:** Furnaces over 15-20 years old should be professionally evaluated

**Electrical Load Management:**

- **Understand circuits:** Know which circuit powers space heater
- **Don't combine:** Never plug space heater and other high-draw device on same circuit
- **Dedicated outlet:** If possible, use dedicated circuit for heater
- **Avoid extension cords:** Plug directly into wall outlet
- **Surge protectors:** Avoid using for heaters (they may overheat)

## Holiday Tree Fires

Christmas tree fires, while less common than heating fires, are extremely severe. A tree fire can grow from small flame to fully involved in 60-90 seconds.

### Why Holiday Trees Catch Fire

**Dry Tree:**
- Cut trees dry out quickly indoors
- Dried needles and wood are highly flammable
- Moisture is critical to fire prevention
- Tree left without water becomes dangerous within days

**Electrical Hazards:**
- Damaged light strands short-circuit
- Loose connections create sparks
- Too many lights draw excessive power
- Water near electrical connections causes shock/fire risk

**Ignition Sources Near Tree:**
- Candles placed in/near tree
- Fireplace or heater too close to tree
- Smoking near tree
- Decorative items with open flames

**Tree Placement:**
- Placed near heat source (heater, fireplace)
- Blocking exit route if fire occurs
- In corner where fire spreads quickly
- Away from water source if fire starts

### Prevention Strategies for Holiday Trees

**Tree Selection and Maintenance:**

- **Live tree:** Choose fresh, not dried-out tree (needles shouldn't fall when shaken)
- **Water supply:** Keep tree in water bucket; add water daily (tree drinks 1 quart per day)
- **Needle test:** If needles turn brown and fall, tree is too dry to keep
- **Storage:** Keep tree cool and away from heater/fireplace
- **Placement:** Position tree in location away from heat sources, exits, and seating

**Electrical Safety:**

- **Inspect lights:** Check for damaged cords, loose connections, burnt-out sections
- **Test before using:** Plug in lights on separate circuit before installing on tree
- **Light limits:** Check light strand packaging for maximum number of strands to chain together
- **Water protection:** Never use watered trees with lights—water and electricity don't mix
- **Outlet choice:** Use outlet on different circuit from heater/high-draw devices
- **Turn off at night:** Unplug lights when not home and while sleeping

**Candles and Open Flames:**

- **Never place candles in/near tree:** Dried needles ignite instantly
- **No smoking near tree:** Ash ignites needles
- **Keep fireplace damper:** Ensure heater and fireplace don't draw branch too close
- **Distance:** Keep tree at least 3 feet from fireplace, heater, and other heat sources

**Emergency Preparedness:**

- **Fire extinguisher nearby:** Keep ABC extinguisher within reach
- **Water source:** Have bucket of water or hose ready
- **Exit route:** Ensure tree doesn't block exits
- **Know escape:** Have exit route if fire spreads quickly

## Holiday Decoration Fires

Beyond the tree, holiday decorations create fire hazards when not properly managed.

### Common Holiday Fire Causes

**Decorative Items:**
- Inflatable decorations with internal electrical components fail
- Light-up decorations overheat
- Damaged decorations used year after year
- Decorations too close to heat sources

**Display Setup:**
- Decorations block exits
- Cords run under rugs
- Multiple extension cords daisy-chained
- Decorations touching curtains or walls

**Candles:**
- Scented candles left burning unattended
- Candles in decorative holders that melt or ignite
- Candles near tree or other decorations
- Multiple candles in close proximity

### Prevention Strategies for Holiday Decorations

- Inspect all decorations before using (check for damage)
- Follow manufacturer instructions for display and use
- Don't block exits with large decorations
- Keep decorations away from heat sources
- Plug decorations into separate circuit
- Never leave decorative candles unattended
- Turn off lights and decorations when sleeping or away
- Replace any decorations that show signs of wear

## Fireworks-Related Fires

Fireworks cause burns and injuries, but also structure fires when sparks ignite nearby materials or when fireworks are mishandled.

### Why Fireworks Are Dangerous

**Heat and Sparks:**
- Fireworks reach temperatures of 1000°F+
- Sparks can travel 20+ feet
- Spark lands on dry grass, leaves, or structures
- Fire starts far from apparent source

**User Error:**
- People use fireworks indoors (windows, garages)
- Fireworks lit near structures or vehicles
- Debris left in yard after display
- Improper storage of fireworks (heat, humidity sensitivity)

**Legal Issues:**
- Many areas ban consumer fireworks
- Illegal fireworks lack safety features
- Insurance won't cover firework-related fires
- Criminal liability possible

### Prevention Strategies for Fireworks

- **Check local laws:** Many areas ban consumer fireworks; use public displays instead
- **Location:** If legal and allowed, use in open area away from structures, vehicles, dry grass
- **Supervision:** Never leave fireworks unattended
- **Water nearby:** Have bucket of water or hose available
- **After use:** Collect and dispose of debris properly
- **Professional shows:** Attend public fireworks displays instead of setting off personal fireworks
- **Storage:** Keep in cool, dry location (heat and moisture degrade them)
- **Never:** Don't use indoors, near buildings, or in high-wind conditions

## Seasonal Fire Causes Summary

{{< drg/checklist title="Seasonal Fire Prevention" subtitle="By season" >}}
- **Winter:** Space heaters at safe distance, fireplaces/woodstoves maintained, furnace inspected, heating load managed
- **Fall/Winter:** Trees properly watered, lights inspected, electrical load managed, trees away from heat sources
- **Holiday:** Decorations inspected, candles supervised, exits not blocked, decorations away from heat
- **Summer:** Fireworks used legally and safely, debris cleared, dry conditions recognized
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Cross-Seasonal Awareness

Some fire risks span multiple seasons:

**Candles:** Used year-round (holidays, ambiance, power outages)
- Never leave unattended
- Keep away from flammable items
- Use candle holders designed for purpose
- Trim wicks to prevent smoking

**Smoking:** Year-round hazard
- Never in bed (true every season)
- Never near seasonal decorations
- Properly dispose of butts

**Electrical:** Year-round concern
- Amplified during winter (heating load)
- Amplified during holidays (decoration load)
- Circuit capacity is finite; overload increases fire risk

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Most holiday tree fires occur 3+ weeks after Christmas when trees have dried considerably. If keeping a tree past mid-January, accept that fire risk rises daily without water. The driest trees are the most dangerous.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}
