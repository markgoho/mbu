{{< drg/requirement number="8e" >}}
Describe three common campsite fire lays. Collect, prepare, and set up tinder, kindling, and fuel for a campfire following the Leave No Trace Seven Principles. Ignite your fire lay and safely extinguish your fire.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A "fire lay" is the arrangement of tinder, kindling, and fuel that makes a fire possible. Different lays serve different purposes. Understanding them helps you build efficient campfires.

## Three Common Fire Lays

### 1. Teepee (Cone) Lay

**Structure:**
- Tinder in center
- Kindling arranged in teepee shape around tinder
- Fuel wood leans against kindling sides
- Creates peaked roof shape

**Advantages:**
- Good airflow (tinder draws oxygen upward)
- Self-supporting (tighter as fire burns)
- Good for quick, hot fire
- Visually appealing

**Best for:**
- Quick cooking
- Warming
- Beautiful campfire

**Disadvantages:**
- Collapses if burning unevenly
- Harder to add large logs
- More wood-intensive

### 2. Log Cabin (Parallel) Lay

**Structure:**
- Tinder in center
- Two parallel kindling pieces on opposite sides
- Two more kindling pieces perpendicular
- Creates square/cabin shape
- Fuel wood stacked alternating directions on top

**Advantages:**
- Excellent airflow (gaps between pieces)
- Very stable (won't collapse)
- Easy to feed (can keep adding wood)
- Good for long-burning fire

**Best for:**
- Extended campfires
- Group gathering
- Heating/warmth
- Cooking longer meals

**Disadvantages:**
- Takes more setup time
- Requires more wood
- Larger base footprint

### 3. Star Lay

**Structure:**
- Tinder in center
- Kindling radiates from center like star points (5-6 pieces)
- Fuel wood extends from kindling ends
- Create "arms" extending outward

**Advantages:**
- Easy wood management (just push logs in as they burn)
- Conserves wood (can feed only what burns)
- Stable (wide base)
- Good for camping multiple days

**Best for:**
- Extended camps
- Heat management (reduce heat by not feeding wood)
- Fuel conservation
- Group warming

**Disadvantages:**
- Takes practice to manage
- Not good for quick hot fire
- Less common (unfamiliar to some)

## Collecting and Preparing Materials

### Tinder Selection

**Good tinder:**
- Very dry material that catches easily
- Examples: dry leaves, inner bark, small twigs, lint
- Collect into ball-sized bundle
- Leave No Trace: Use natural materials found at site

**Collection:**
- Look under trees (protected from moisture)
- Deadwood sheathed in bark (inside is dry)
- Gather from ground (not from living plants)

### Kindling Selection

**Good kindling:**
- Pencil-thickness to thumb-thickness wood
- Completely dry
- Gather 2-3 armful for complete fire startup
- Cut to manageable lengths (12-18 inches)

**Collection:**
- Gather dead branches from ground
- Break by hand if thin enough
- Look for standing dead wood (often very dry)
- Never cut live branches

### Fuel Wood

**Good fuel:**
- Wrist-thickness to thicker
- Dry wood (dry wood burns hot and clean)
- Gather more than you think needed (usually underestimate)
- Prepare by cutting to fireplace length (burns better)

**Collection:**
- Fallen dead trees
- Standing dead wood (careful felling)
- Downed branches
- Break/cut to size

**Leave No Trace: Only dead wood; only what you'll burn; scatter unused wood**

## Fire Lay Setup Process

### For Teepee Lay

1. Create tinder nest
2. Arrange kindling in teepee: 4-6 pieces leaning inward
3. Leave opening for ignition (one side slightly open)
4. Arrange fuel wood nearby (not yet on fire)
5. Prepare to light tinder
6. Once tinder catches, add small kindling pieces
7. Gradually add larger kindling as fire grows
8. Add fuel wood once flames established

### For Log Cabin Lay

1. Create tinder nest
2. Lay two parallel kindling pieces (2 inches apart)
3. Lay two perpendicular pieces on top
4. Place tinder in center of square
5. Prepare kindling above (between cabin pieces)
6. Stack fuel wood on top (perpendicular to kindling below)
7. Leave gaps for airflow
8. Light tinder; feed kindling through gaps
9. Stack fuel on top as fire develops

### For Star Lay

1. Create tinder nest in center
2. Arrange 5-6 fuel wood pieces radiating from center (like star points)
3. Arrange kindling along fuel wood (between center and ends)
4. Leave tinder accessible in center for ignition
5. Light tinder
6. Build fire upward toward center
7. Push logs inward as they burn

## Igniting the Fire

**Using any lay:**
1. Light tinder at center
2. Add small kindling pieces as tinder catches
3. Gradually increase wood size
4. Build upward from tinder
5. Add fuel wood once flames established
6. Allow fire to stabilize before leaving

## Safely Extinguishing

**Proper extinguishing:**
1. Stop feeding wood
2. Allow fire to burn down
3. Completely extinguish remaining coals:
   - Pour water on coals (completely douse)
   - Stir ashes (ensures all embers dead)
   - Feel with hand (should be cool)
4. Scatter cold ashes (Leave No Trace)
5. Disassemble fire ring if newly built

**Test:** Water on ashes should not sizzle (indicates fire is out)

## Demonstration Checklist

Show your counselor:
- **Three fire lays explained** (structure and advantages)
- **Materials collected** (tinder, kindling, fuel prepared)
- **Fire lay setup** (properly arranged before lighting)
- **Leave No Trace practiced** (only dead wood, proper amount, careful site)
- **Fire ignition** (successful lighting and growth)
- **Safe extinguishment** (complete with ash scatter)
