This requirement gives you two options. Choose the one that interests you most.

## Option A: Visit a Fire Station

**What you'll do:**
- Visit a fire station in person
- Meet firefighters and talk with them
- Learn about their vehicles and equipment
- Understand fire prevention activities in your community
- Ask about their careers

**How to arrange:**
1. Call your local fire station (non-emergency number)
2. Ask if they can accommodate a Scout visit
3. Ask about the best time to visit
4. Show up ready to listen and learn
5. Bring a parent or guardian
6. Come with prepared questions

**Questions to ask:**
- How long have you been a firefighter?
- What does a typical day look like?
- What training did you need?
- What equipment do we have and what does it do?
- What are the most common fires you respond to?
- What's the most challenging part of the job?
- How do you stay in shape for the job?
- What fire prevention activities does the community do?
- Have you ever saved a life?

**What you'll learn:**
- Different types of fire service vehicles
- Fire prevention programs in your community
- What firefighters actually do (more than fighting fires)
- Career pathways in fire service
- Personal stories from working firefighters

**After the visit:**
Discuss with your counselor what you learned. Be prepared to explain the fire prevention activities you discovered and what interested you about the firefighters' careers.

## Option B: Research Fire Safety Careers

**What you'll do:**
- Identify three career opportunities in fire safety
- Choose one career for deeper research
- Research training, education, and certification requirements
- Research employment prospects and salary
- Discuss findings with your counselor

**Three career options to research:**

**1. Firefighter**
- Local, state, or federal positions
- Wildland firefighting or structural firefighting
- Specialized roles (hazmat, rescue, arson investigation)

**2. Fire Safety Inspector**
- Building code enforcement
- Inspection of schools, businesses, apartments
- Ensuring compliance with fire codes

**3. Fire Investigator**
- Determine cause of fires
- Collect evidence at fire scenes
- Work with law enforcement on arson cases

**Other fire safety careers:**
- Fire marshal (administration and leadership)
- Wildland fire ecologist
- Fire apparatus engineer/mechanic
- Emergency dispatcher
- Fire safety educator/outreach
- Arson investigator
- Insurance adjuster

**Research what you need to know:**
- **Education:** High school? College? Specialized training?
- **Certification:** Specific certifications required?
- **Experience:** Years of experience needed?
- **Physical requirements:** Fitness standards?
- **Testing:** Written exams, physical tests?
- **Salary:** Entry-level and experienced pay?
- **Job growth:** Career growing or declining?
- **Work environment:** Shifts? On-call? Desk or field work?
- **Stress level:** Why is this job stressful or rewarding?

**Where to research:**
- Bureau of Labor Statistics (bls.gov)
- Fire service websites (city/state fire departments)
- Career exploration sites
- Informational interviews
- YouTube videos of people in the job
- Job postings

**Your research project:**
Create a document with:
1. **Three careers identified** (brief description)
2. **One career chosen** (explain why)
3. **Education requirements** (schooling/training)
4. **Certification/experience** (credentials needed)
5. **Employment prospects** (job availability)
6. **Salary data** (starting and experienced pay)
7. **Your interest level** (would you want this career?)
8. **Skills you have/need** (what do you bring? What would you learn?)

**After your research:**
Discuss findings with your counselor. Be prepared to explain what you learned and whether this career interests you.

## Option Summary

| Aspect | Option A | Option B |
|--------|----------|----------|
| **Format** | In-person experience | Research and analysis |
| **Time commitment** | 1-2 hours for visit | Several hours for research |
| **Social interaction** | Direct conversation with firefighters | Potentially one interview |
| **Flexibility** | Depends on station availability | Can do on your schedule |
| **Output** | Counselor discussion | Research presentation |
| **Best if you** | Like meeting people; want hands-on | Like research; prefer independent work |

## Fire Service Careers Are Rewarding

**Regardless of your choice, understand:**
- Fire service is rewarding work with real community impact
- Multiple pathways exist (not just firefighting)
- Training and education are crucial
- Physical fitness is important for many roles
- Fire safety professionals save lives daily
