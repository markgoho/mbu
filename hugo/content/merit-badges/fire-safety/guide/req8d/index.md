Demonstrate the safe way to start a charcoal fire.

Charcoal fires are common for grilling and outdoor cooking. They burn hotter and longer than wood, making them efficient for cooking. Starting them safely requires proper technique and preparation.

## Types of Charcoal

**Briquettes:** Uniform, compressed charcoal; consistent burn
**Lump charcoal:** Irregular chunks; burns hotter, faster
**Choice:** Both are acceptable; briquettes easier for beginners

## Safe Charcoal Fire Starting

### Preparation

1. **Fire container** — Metal grill, fireplace, or safe container
2. **Clear area** — 10+ feet from flammables
3. **Arrange charcoal** — Pile in container
4. **Prepare lighter fluid** — Only if needed; use sparingly
5. **Have water nearby** — For emergencies

### The Process

**Method 1: Charcoal Lighter**
1. Arrange charcoal in pyramid
2. Spray lighter fluid on charcoal (follow label directions)
3. Wait 1-2 minutes (allows fluid to soak)
4. Light with match or lighter
5. Don't re-light if flame goes out; wait and try again
6. Charcoal ready when covered in ash (20-30 minutes)

**Method 2: Charcoal Chimney** (No lighter fluid needed)
1. Fill metal chimney starter 3/4 full with charcoal
2. Stuff newspaper under chimney
3. Light newspaper
4. Fire rises through charcoal
5. Dump charcoal into grill when ready (very hot—use tongs)

**Method 3: Electric Starter**
1. Nestle coil in charcoal
2. Plug in (heats to ignition temperature)
3. Charcoal catches fire from coil heat
4. Unplug and remove when charcoal lights

### Safety Rules

**Always:**
- Use charcoal only in open air (ventilation critical)
- Never use charcoal indoors (carbon monoxide hazard)
- Keep lighter fluid bottle away from heat
- Never pour lighter fluid on already-lit charcoal (explosion risk)
- Use long-handled tongs for safety
- Wear closed-toe shoes (protect feet from coals)

**Never:**
- Use gasoline (extremely dangerous)
- Close lid while lighting (builds heat/pressure)
- Refill lighter fluid bottle over lit fire
- Leave charcoal unattended while lighting
- Touch charcoal with hands (very hot)

## Readiness Indicators

Charcoal is ready when:
- Most charcoal covered in gray ash
- Glowing hot (visible heat shimmer)
- Temperature stable (no flaming)
- Typically 20-30 minutes after lighting

## Extinguishing Charcoal

**After cooking:**
1. Let charcoal burn down (use all heat)
2. Don't douse with water (creates steam, splatters ash)
3. Let cool in grill (12+ hours)
4. Remove cold ash carefully
5. Dispose in ash can or trash

## Demonstration for Counselor

Show your counselor:
- Safe charcoal arrangement
- Proper lighter fluid use (or chimney use)
- Patient waiting (don't rush)
- Safe handling with tongs
- Readiness assessment
