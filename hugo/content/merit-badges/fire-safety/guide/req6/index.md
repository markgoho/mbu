{{< drg/requirement number="6" >}}
Explain how to safely store, handle, and dispose of flammable and combustible liquids.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Flammable liquids are everywhere in homes—gasoline, kerosene, paint thinner, acetone, and many solvents. These substances are essential for many tasks but extremely dangerous if mishandled. Understanding safe storage and handling prevents explosions and fires.

## Understanding Flammable vs. Combustible Liquids

**Flammable liquids** (flash point below 100°F):
- Ignite easily at room temperature
- Include: gasoline, paint thinner, acetone, some alcohol-based products
- Most dangerous category

**Combustible liquids** (flash point 100-200°F):
- Ignite at higher temperatures
- Include: diesel, kerosene, fuel oil, many paints
- Still dangerous but less volatile than flammable

**Flash point:** Temperature at which liquid gives off enough vapor to ignite when exposed to flame or spark

## Safe Storage

### Storage Containers

**Use proper containers:**
- Designed for flammable storage (amber glass, metal)
- Marked with warning labels
- Sealed to prevent evaporation
- Never store in glass except original containers
- Never store in food containers (confusion risk)

**Capacity limits:**
- Most homes can safely store limited quantities (2-5 gallons total)
- Larger quantities require special storage
- Keep in smallest quantity needed

### Storage Location

**Where to store:**
- Outside building (shed, detached garage)
- Away from ignition sources (heaters, electrical outlets, pilot lights)
- Away from direct sunlight
- In cool location (heat increases vapor pressure)
- Elevated (above floor level, in case of spill)
- Away from children and pets
- Clearly labeled and locked

**Where NOT to store:**
- Inside home or basement
- Near furnaces, water heaters, or laundry appliances
- In attached garage (fumes can enter home)
- Under stairs (traps fumes)
- Near chemicals that could react
- In vehicles with passengers

### Ventilation and Sealing

- **Containers must have tight-fitting caps**
- **Spill containment:** Store in secondary container (catches spills)
- **Ventilation:** Storage area should have air circulation
- **Temperature:** Keep below 80°F if possible

## Safe Handling

### Before Use

- **Read label** — Understand product and hazards
- **Use in ventilated area** — Outdoors preferred
- **Tie back hair** — Prevents contact with flames/heat
- **Remove ignition sources** — No smoking, no open flames
- **Have water available** — For cleanup or emergency

### During Use

- **Wear protective gear** — Gloves, eye protection, clothing that covers skin
- **Never smoke while using** — Vapors can ignite from cigarette
- **Keep away from heat** — Stoves, heaters, sunlight
- **Don't pour down drains** — Contaminates water, hazardous to ecology
- **Minimize spill** — Pour carefully, use funnel
- **Cap immediately** — Reduce vapor release
- **Never use near children or pets**

### Spill Cleanup

**Small spill (less than 1 quart):**
1. Ensure ventilation
2. Don't allow spill near drains
3. Use absorbent material (sawdust, absorbent towels)
4. Let dry, then sweep
5. Place in sealed container for disposal

**Large spill (more than 1 quart):**
1. Evacuate area (vapors are dangerous)
2. Call fire department (non-emergency)
3. Don't attempt to clean
4. Professionals handle hazardous cleanup

**Never use water to clean up:**
- Water and oil don't mix
- Makes spill larger
- Creates hazardous wastewater

## Safe Disposal

### Why Proper Disposal Matters

- **Environmental:** Protects groundwater, soil, ecosystems
- **Safety:** Prevents fires and explosions
- **Legal:** Illegal dumping carries fines
- **Responsibility:** You created the waste; proper disposal is your duty

### Disposal Methods

**Original containers:**
- If completely empty, may be recycled (follow local rules)
- Rinse thoroughly before recycling

**Partially full containers:**
- Do NOT pour down drain
- Do NOT dump in yard
- Do NOT throw in trash
- Must be disposed as hazardous waste

**Finding disposal:**
- Contact local hazardous waste facility
- Many communities have collection days
- Automotive shops accept used oil/fluids
- Hardware stores may accept paint/solvents
- Contact your city's solid waste department

**Process:**
- Take container to authorized disposal facility
- They'll handle safely
- Usually free or low cost
- Protects environment and prevents fires

## Refueling Safely

**For equipment that uses gasoline:**

- **Engine off and cool:** Never refuel running equipment
- **Outside only:** No indoors or in garage
- **On ground:** Don't refuel in vehicle or elevated position
- **Spill prevention:** Use funnel to minimize spillage
- **Cap immediately:** After fueling, cap both container and equipment
- **Move away:** Before starting equipment
- **Keep away from ignition:** No sparks, flames, or hot surfaces during refueling

## Fire Extinguishers and Flammable Liquids

**Special requirements:**
- Class B extinguishers for flammable liquid fires (foam, CO₂, dry powder)
- Water extinguishers DO NOT work (water floats on oil)
- Keep extinguisher accessible near storage area
- Know how to use PASS technique before you need it

## Special Considerations

**Paint and finishes:**
- Oil-based paints contain flammable solvents
- Water-based paints less flammable but still hazardous
- Store in original containers
- Dispose through hazardous waste

**Solvents and thinners:**
- Acetone, lacquer thinner extremely flammable
- Turpentine, mineral spirits also hazardous
- Follow same storage/handling as gasoline
- Keep away from any ignition source

**Oily rags and materials:**
- Rags saturated with oil can spontaneously ignite if piled hot
- Hang to dry outdoors
- Never store in sealed container
- Dispose properly when completely dry

## Common Mistakes

- Storing gasoline indoors (extreme danger)
- Using gas as cleaner (highly flammable and toxic)
- Leaving containers open (vapor release)
- Storing near water heater (ignition source)
- Mixing with other chemicals
- Overfilling containers
- Reusing food containers

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Flammable liquid fires burn at extremely hot temperatures and spread rapidly. Improper storage or handling can cause explosions that destroy property, cause severe burns, and kill people. Treat these liquids with extreme respect.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}
