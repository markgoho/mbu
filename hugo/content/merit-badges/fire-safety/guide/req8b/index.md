{{< drg/requirement number="8b" >}}
Demonstrate lighting a match safely, the proper way to extinguish it, and to dispose of it. Explain the similarities and differences between safety matches and strike-anywhere matches.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Matches are the simplest, most reliable way to start a fire in the field. Understanding match types and proper technique ensures safe, successful fire starting.

## Types of Matches

### Safety Matches

**How they work:**
- Match head contains phosphorus sesquisulfide and other chemicals
- Strike box contains red phosphorus
- Must strike on the box to ignite
- Can't accidentally ignite in your pack

**Advantages:**
- Safer (can't spontaneously ignite)
- More reliable (consistent burn)
- Commonly available
- Easier to use

**Disadvantages:**
- Require strike box (must carry both)
- Harder to light in wind/wet conditions
- Less versatile

### Strike-Anywhere Matches

**How they work:**
- Match head contains phosphorus sesquisulfide (complete ignition chemical)
- Can strike on any rough surface (rock, boot sole, etc.)
- Ignite from friction alone

**Advantages:**
- No strike box needed
- Can ignite on any surface
- Single item (lighter to carry)
- More versatile in field

**Disadvantages:**
- Can accidentally ignite in pack (more dangerous)
- Slightly more expensive
- Harder to control intensity of flame

### Comparison Table

| Feature | Safety Matches | Strike-Anywhere |
|---------|---|---|
| **Requires strike box** | Yes | No |
| **Can ignite accidentally** | No | Possibly |
| **Reliability** | High (consistent ignition) | High |
| **Cost** | Cheaper | Slightly more |
| **Best for camping** | Either; choose based on preference | Better (less to carry) |

## Demonstrating Safe Match Lighting

### Preparation

1. **Choose tinder** — Dry material that catches easily
2. **Prepare kindling** — Small sticks ready to add
3. **Clear area** — No loose materials near strike point
4. **Position yourself** — Stable stance, away from face
5. **Hold box steady** — One hand holds box, one strikes match

### Lighting a Safety Match

1. **Hold match firmly** — Between thumb and forefinger, about 1 inch from head
2. **Strike firmly** — Press match head against strike box at 45-degree angle
3. **Drag smoothly** — Slow, steady motion (not jerky)
4. **Watch ignition** — Match head will ignite with small burst
5. **Let flame stabilize** — Flame creeps down stick for 1-2 seconds
6. **Apply to tinder** — Touch flame to prepared tinder
7. **Hold steady** — Wait for tinder to catch (2-3 seconds)
8. **Release match** — Once tinder burns, drop match

### Lighting a Strike-Anywhere Match

**Similar process, but:**
1. **Strike on any rough surface** — Rock, wood, boot sole
2. **Use firm pressure** — Strike firmly for ignition
3. **Short striking motion** — Quick downward stroke
4. **Same application to tinder** — Once lit, apply to dry tinder

### Common Mistakes

- **Striking too lightly** — Match won't ignite
- **Striking too hard** — Match breaks
- **Holding match upside down** — Flame creeps toward hand
- **Rushing to tinder** — Match extinguishes before reaching tinder
- **Applying to damp material** — Won't catch; wast match

## Safely Extinguishing a Match

### The Proper Method

**After using match for fire:**
1. **Wait for flame to stabilize** — 5-10 seconds
2. **Blow gently** — Extinguish flame from the side
3. **Or wave gently** — Move through air to cool and extinguish
4. **Check it's cool** — Don't assume it's out; wait briefly
5. **Feel the stick** — Should not be hot to touch
6. **Dispose safely** — Place in ash or carry out

### What NOT to Do

- **Don't pinch with fingers** — Burns you and creates soot
- **Don't plunge in water** — If possible, blow out first
- **Don't leave burning** — Obvious hazard
- **Don't reuse partially burned matches** — Unreliable

## Disposing of Used Matches

### In Camp

**If campfire present:**
- Add spent matches to fire
- They'll burn completely with wood

**If no fire:**
- Place in ash pile or trash
- Don't leave scattered around camp
- Leave No Trace: pack out if no established fire ring

**Never leave at campsite** — Littering, hazard

### At Home/Permanent Location

- Place in trash
- Don't flush (can clog plumbing)
- If many matches, burn in safe container before trashing

## Advantages and Disadvantages Summary

**Choose based on your situation:**

**Safety matches best when:**
- You're in group (less worry about loss)
- You want safest storage (won't spontaneously ignite)
- You plan multiple fires (bring extra strike boxes)

**Strike-anywhere best when:**
- Solo camping (less to carry)
- Backpacking (weight matters)
- You want maximum versatility
- You're confident in fire-starting skills

**Many campers carry both** — Safety matches for reliability, strike-anywhere for backup

## Demonstration Checklist

When showing your counselor, demonstrate:

{{< drg/checklist title="Match Safety Demonstration" subtitle="Show your counselor each step" >}}
- **Lighting safety match** (proper strike, controlled ignition, application to tinder)
- **Lighting strike-anywhere match** (different striking surface, same control)
- **Extinguishing properly** (blow out or wave, check coolness)
- **Safe disposal** (matches placed in fire or trash safely)
- **Explain differences** (types of matches, why each exists, when to use)
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
In an emergency, strike-anywhere matches can start fires where safety matches couldn't (no box available). In normal conditions, safety matches are perfectly adequate and safer to carry. Neither is inherently "better"—they're tools for different situations.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
