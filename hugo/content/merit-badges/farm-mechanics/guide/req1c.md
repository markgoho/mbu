Find all the universal warning and safety symbols on a piece of equipment and explain what they mean.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Every piece of farm equipment arrives with a language of symbols — little pictures that warn you away from danger. These symbols are universal, which means the same symbol means the same thing whether you are working on a John Deere in Iowa or a CLAAS combine in Germany. Learn to read them, and you will instantly understand hazards you might otherwise miss.

### Symbol Colors and Their Meanings

Manufacturers use colors to signal danger levels:

**Red** = Danger or prohibition. Red means "STOP" or "DO NOT DO THIS." Red symbols mark controls that shut things down or indicate immediate hazards.

**Yellow** = Caution or warning. Yellow means "Be careful" or "Pay attention." Yellow symbols alert you to potential hazards that require safe practices.

**Orange** = Danger (used on some equipment). Orange marks critical hazards like hot surfaces or crushing points.

**Blue** = Information or instruction. Blue symbols provide guidance on how to use equipment correctly or where to find safety information.

**Green** = Safety (exit, first aid, safety equipment). Green marks emergency exits, first-aid kits, and safety showers.

### Common Farm Equipment Warning Symbols

As you inspect farm equipment, you will see these symbols repeatedly:

**Hot Surface**

A hand pulling away from flames or a steaming object. You will find this on radiators, engine blocks, and mufflers. The hazard: touching hot metal causes instant severe burns.

**Rotating Shaft or Pinch Point**

Often shown as a hand or hair being caught in a spinning wheel or gap. Dangers include:
- Hair, loose clothing, or jewelry caught and wrapped around a shaft
- Hands or fingers crushed in the gap between two rotating parts
- Rings, bracelets, or watches torn off

**Hydraulic Pressure**

Usually depicted as a jet of fluid spraying. Hydraulic systems operate at 2,000–5,000 psi. A pinhole leak under pressure can inject fluid deep into your hand, causing serious tissue damage. Never use your bare hand to locate leaks — use a piece of paper or cardboard.

**Electrical Hazard**

A lightning bolt, usually yellow and black. Electrical hazards include:
- Electrocution from touching live wires
- Arc flash from faulty connections
- Electrical shock from wet hands on electrical equipment

**Toxic Fumes or Gas**

A skull and crossbones or a stylized face with fumes. Engine exhaust, refrigerant vapors, and hydraulic fluid mist are all toxic. This symbol warns you to ensure proper ventilation.

**Explosive or Flammable Material**

A flame or explosion symbol, usually red or orange. Diesel fuel, gasoline, hydraulic fluid, and grease are all flammable. Do not smoke, grind, or weld near these materials. Keep them away from ignition sources.

**Crushing Hazard**

A hand or object being crushed between two surfaces. This warns of equipment that can:
- Trap limbs or body parts
- Lower suddenly and crush anything beneath
- Close and pinch

**Chemical Splash or Caustic Material**

Droplets eating through material or a hand showing damage. Many engine coolants, solvents, and degreasers are caustic. They burn skin and destroy eyes instantly. Never splash these chemicals around; always pour carefully.

**Eye Hazard**

An eye with an arrow or particle aimed at it. This warns of operations that throw particles: grinding wheels, wire brushes, chipping, or high-pressure washing.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Different equipment manufacturers sometimes position symbols differently, but the symbol itself is standardized. So a rotating shaft symbol on a John Deere looks identical to one on a Case IH or AGCO machine. This consistency means you can instantly recognize the same hazard across brands.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

### How to Find Symbols on Equipment

Symbols are printed, stenciled, or affixed as decals in specific places:

1. **On the equipment itself** — near the hazardous area. A rotating shaft symbol will be positioned right at the entrance to that spinning component.

2. **On covers and guards** — warning you away before you remove them. If you see a symbol on a cover, there is a hazard underneath.

3. **In operator manuals** — a legend showing all symbols and their meanings (in multiple languages).

4. **On fluid containers** — engine oil cans, hydraulic fluid drums, and coolant containers all have symbols warning of hazards.

### Symbols vs. Decals: Durability Matters

Symbols fade in sun and weather. A mechanic occasionally finds a worn or missing decal on used equipment. If you see an area where a symbol used to be (you can see the outline), that indicates a known hazard — treat it as if the symbol is still there. Do not skip safety because a decal faded.

Conversely, new equipment sometimes has symbols in unexpected places. Always read symbols before you work, even on familiar equipment.

### Reading the Full Warning

Symbols are shorthand, but many include text underneath:

- **"DANGER"** = immediate life threat
- **"WARNING"** = serious injury possible
- **"CAUTION"** = minor injury possible
- **"NOTICE"** = equipment or data loss (not a physical hazard)

A complete warning combines symbol + color + text + location. They work together to communicate: "There is a hot surface here; touching it will cause burns; keep away."

### Your Assignment: Symbol Inventory

For this requirement, find a real piece of farm equipment (a tractor, baler, grain dryer, or other machinery). Walk around it slowly and:

1. **Locate every symbol** (yes, every single one). Note its location.
2. **Identify each symbol** using the categories above or a manual.
3. **Explain the hazard** it warns about. What could happen if you ignored it?
4. **Describe the safe practice** — what should you do instead?

Write this up or discuss it with your counselor. This exercise trains your eye to see hazards before they become injuries.
