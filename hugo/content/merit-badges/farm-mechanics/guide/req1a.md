A loose shoelace pulled into a spinning PTO shaft. A metal chip from a chisel flying into an unprotected eye. A chemical burn from spilled battery acid soaking through a cotton shirt. Every one of these injuries has happened on a farm, and every one was preventable with the right safety equipment and clothing. Before you pick up a single wrench, you need to know what to wear and what to use to protect yourself.

## Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

PPE is the gear that stands between you and injury. Different tasks require different protection, but some items should be worn every time you step into the shop.

### Eye Protection

Safety glasses or goggles are the single most important piece of PPE in a farm shop. Grinding, filing, hammering, and working under equipment all create flying debris. Standard eyeglasses are not safety glasses — they lack side shields and impact-rated lenses.

- **Safety glasses** with side shields: Wear these for general shop work, light grinding, and any task involving hand tools.
- **Safety goggles**: Use these when working with chemicals, using a wire brush wheel, or doing heavy grinding. Goggles seal against your face, preventing particles from entering around the edges.
- **Face shield**: For heavy grinding, cutting, or welding tasks, a full face shield provides additional protection.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Eye injuries are the most common preventable shop injury. Always put on safety glasses before you start working — not after the first spark flies. Keep a pair hanging by the shop door so you never forget.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

### Hearing Protection

Farm shop tools generate noise levels that can permanently damage your hearing. An impact wrench, angle grinder, or running tractor engine in an enclosed shop can exceed 90 decibels — the threshold where hearing damage begins with prolonged exposure.

- **Foam earplugs**: Inexpensive, disposable, and effective. Roll them between your fingers, insert into the ear canal, and let them expand.
- **Earmuffs**: Easier to put on and remove quickly. Good for intermittent noise like starting an engine.

### Hand Protection

Your hands are your most valuable tools, so protect them.

- **Leather work gloves**: Wear them when handling rough or sharp materials, moving equipment, or working with hot surfaces.
- **Chemical-resistant gloves (nitrile or neoprene)**: Required when handling fuel, solvents, coolant, hydraulic fluid, or other chemicals.
- **Never wear gloves near rotating machinery.** A glove caught in a spinning shaft, belt, or gear can pull your entire hand into the machine.

### Respiratory Protection

Dust, fumes, and chemical vapors in the shop can damage your lungs over time.

- **Dust mask (N95)**: Wear when sanding, grinding, or sweeping dusty areas.
- **Respirator with chemical cartridges**: Required when painting, using solvents, or working in poorly ventilated areas with chemical fumes.

## Clothing for the Shop

What you wear matters as much as the PPE you put on over it.

{{< drg/checklist title="Shop Clothing Checklist" subtitle="Dress right before you start work" >}}
- Close-toed leather boots or steel-toed work boots: Protect your feet from dropped tools and heavy parts.
- Long pants (denim or heavy cotton): Shield your legs from sparks, sharp edges, and hot surfaces.
- Short-sleeved or close-fitting long-sleeved shirt: Loose sleeves catch on rotating parts. Tuck in your shirt.
- No jewelry: Remove rings, watches, bracelets, and necklaces. Metal jewelry conducts electricity and catches on moving parts.
- Tie back long hair: Hair can get caught in rotating machinery just like loose clothing.
- No open-toed shoes or sandals: Ever. Not even "just for a minute."
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Essential Safety Tools

Beyond what you wear, certain tools in the shop exist purely for safety.

**Fire extinguisher** — Every farm shop should have at least one ABC-rated fire extinguisher mounted near the exit. Know where it is and how to use it (Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep — PASS).

**First aid kit** — A well-stocked kit should be within arm's reach of the work area. Stock it with bandages, antiseptic, eye wash, burn cream, and a tourniquet.

**Jack stands** — Never work under a vehicle or implement supported only by a hydraulic jack. Jacks can fail. Jack stands do not.

{{< drg/be-prepared title="A Hydraulic Jack Fails" >}}
You are changing oil on a tractor and using a floor jack to lift the front end. Halfway through the job, you hear a hissing sound — the jack is slowly losing pressure.

- **If you used jack stands:** The tractor settles onto the stands. You are safe. You can fix or replace the jack and continue.
- **If you did not use jack stands:** The tractor drops. If any part of your body is underneath, you face a crush injury.

Always place jack stands under the frame immediately after lifting. Never trust a jack alone.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}
