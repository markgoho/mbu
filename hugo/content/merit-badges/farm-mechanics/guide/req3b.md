{{< drg/requirement number="3b" >}}
Organize a tool rack or a storage system for nails, bolts, nuts, and washers.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A disorganized farm shop wastes time and money. You search for a 3/8" bolt for 20 minutes, interrupt your work, and lose focus. An organized storage system makes maintenance faster and prevents purchasing duplicate hardware because you forgot you already had it.

### Types of Storage Systems

Choose one of these approaches (or combine them):

**Hardware Bins or Drawer Organizers**

Plastic bins with compartments hold small items. Label each compartment clearly. A drawer with dividers can hold nails, screws, fasteners, and small parts. This works well if you have multiple drawers.

**Pegboard Tool Rack**

Pegboard mounted on a wall with hooks and holders keeps tools visible and accessible. Each tool has a specific location, making it obvious when something is missing. This also works for small items stored in hanging containers.

**Shelving with Clear Containers**

Tall shelves with labeled clear containers let you see what is inside without opening them. Stack different sizes of bolts, nuts, and washers in separate containers.

**Small Parts Cabinet**

A multi-drawer cabinet (like a vintage machinist's cabinet) provides excellent organization. Each small drawer holds one type or size of fastener. Drawers are usually labeled.

**Custom-Built Storage**

Build your own storage using scrap wood, PVC pipe, or hardware. Cut wood blocks with holes (1/2" holes hold different bolt sizes), or build a frame to hold containers at convenient heights.

### Planning Your System

Before you build or install anything, plan:

**What are you storing?**

- **Nails**: Various sizes and types (roofing, galvanized, finishing). Nails are often stored by size (1", 2", 3", etc.).
- **Bolts**: Various diameters and lengths. Organize by size (1/4", 3/8", 1/2", etc.) and length.
- **Nuts**: Match bolt sizes. A 3/8" bolt needs 3/8" nuts.
- **Washers**: Flat and lock washers; organize by bolt size.
- **Screws**: Wood screws, machine screws, different head types. Can organize by type or size.

**How much of each do you have?**

Count or estimate quantities. A small drawer might hold 100 nails, while you need space for 500. Plan container sizes accordingly.

**Where will it go?**

Locate it near where you work most often. A pegboard next to your workbench is more useful than one in the far corner. Make sure the location is not in the way of equipment or people moving through the shop.

**What is your budget?**

DIY solutions using scrap materials are free. Commercial storage ranges from $20 (plastic bins) to $200+ (multi-drawer cabinets).

### Building a Simple Bin System

**Materials:**

- 4–10 clear plastic bins (various sizes, from 1-liter to 5-liter)
- Permanent marker or label maker
- Shelf or drawer
- Optional: dividers or smaller containers to fit inside the bins

**Steps:**

1. **Sort your fasteners** by type and size. Create piles of all 1" nails, all 2" nails, all 1/4" bolts, etc.

2. **Count quantities** to determine bin sizes. A 5-liter bin holds about 500 small nails or 100 large bolts.

3. **Label each bin clearly**. Examples: "Nails 2" Galvanized" or "Bolts 3/8" × 2"". Include a count so you know when to reorder.

4. **Fill bins** carefully, avoiding overfilling. Bins should close easily.

5. **Arrange by frequency of use**. Put your most-used items in the most accessible location (eye level, nearest to the work area).

6. **Store on a shelf** or in a cabinet, with all labels facing forward so you can read them easily.

### Building a Pegboard Rack

**Materials:**

- 1/4" pegboard (4' × 8' sheets available at hardware stores)
- 2" × 4" lumber for backing
- Pegboard hooks and holders (various sizes)
- Nails or screws
- Wooden or plastic containers for small items
- Paint (optional, for finish)

**Steps:**

1. **Mount the pegboard** to the wall over studs (for strength). Use 2×4 backing pieces to hold it away from the wall slightly, so hooks can slide in easily.

2. **Screw the pegboard** to the backing with 1.5" screws spaced 16" apart. Use washers to prevent screw heads from punching through the pegboard.

3. **Arrange hooks** in logical groups: hammers together, wrenches together, pliers together.

4. **Add containers** for small items. Pegboard-mounted bins can hold nails, bolts, and washers.

5. **Paint the pegboard** (optional) for a finished look and to protect the wood.

6. **Label the pegboard** above or below each section: "Hammers," "Wrenches," "Pliers," etc.

### Organizing by Size: The Bolt Standard

Bolts follow a standard sizing system. Organizing by this system makes bolts easy to find:

**Diameter**: Measured in fractions of an inch (1/4", 5/16", 3/8", 1/2", etc.)

**Length**: Measured in inches, from the bottom of the head to the tip (1/2", 3/4", 1", 1.5", 2", 3", etc.)

**Grade**: Indicates strength (Grade 2, Grade 5, Grade 8). Higher grades have more strength but cost more.

A complete organization might look like:

| Container | Contents |
|-----------|----------|
| Bin 1 | 1/4" Bolts × 3/4" |
| Bin 2 | 1/4" Bolts × 1" |
| Bin 3 | 1/4" Bolts × 1.5" |
| Bin 4 | 3/8" Bolts × 1" |
| Bin 5 | 3/8" Bolts × 2" |
| Bin 6 | 1/2" Bolts × 1.5" |
| Bin 7 | 1/4" Nuts |
| Bin 8 | 3/8" Nuts |
| Bin 9 | 1/2" Nuts |
| Bin 10 | Washers (flat & lock, mixed) |

This approach lets you find exactly what you need without rummaging.

Take a photo of your organized system and label it. Show your counselor the before (if you have a disorganized shop) and after. Walk them through your organization logic: how you decided to group items, why you chose that system, and how it has made your work faster.

### Maintenance of Your System

Once organized, keep it that way:

- **Return items to the correct bin** after each use
- **Restock periodically** — keep common items available
- **Update labels** if quantities change dramatically or you add new items
- **Clean out old items** that are no longer used (rusty bolts, broken nails)
- **Replenish supplies** before you run out, so you are never searching for a critical fastener

### Inventory Management

For a large system, keep an inventory list:

| Item | Bin | Quantity | Reorder At | Last Restocked |
|------|-----|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Nails 2" Galvanized | 1 | 300 | 100 | 3/5/2024 |
| Bolts 1/4" × 1" | 4 | 75 | 25 | 2/20/2024 |

An inventory helps you budget for supplies and prevents running out of frequently used items.

### Completing the Requirement

Document your work:

1. What items did you organize?
2. What system did you choose (bins, pegboard, custom)?
3. How did you label and arrange items?
4. How much space does it take up?
5. How has it improved your shop efficiency?

Discuss with your counselor how your system works and any improvements you might make for larger quantities or different types of tools.
