{{< drg/requirement number="1d" >}}
Describe what a safety data sheet (SDS) is and tell why it is used. Obtain the SDS for any engine coolant, oil, grease, fuel, hydraulic or transmission fluid, or other flammable or hazardous materials you use in meeting the requirements for this merit badge.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

If a warning symbol is the quick visual alert, a Safety Data Sheet (SDS) is the complete instruction manual for a hazardous chemical. Every chemical used in a farm shop — from engine oil to brake fluid to welding gas — has an SDS. It is the most important document you will read while working with these materials.

### What Is a Safety Data Sheet?

A Safety Data Sheet is a standardized document (usually 1–16 pages) that communicates the hazards of a chemical and how to work with it safely. Every manufacturer is legally required to provide an SDS for every hazardous product they sell. It is free — you just have to ask for it.

The SDS format is standardized internationally (called GHS — Globally Harmonized System) so that whether you are working with a German hydraulic fluid or an American engine oil, the information layout is identical. This consistency means you can find critical information quickly, even in an unfamiliar product's SDS.

### The 16 Sections of an SDS

Every SDS contains these sections (though not all apply to every product):

**Section 1: Product Identification**

Product name, brand, manufacturer contact information, and product code. If something goes wrong and you need to reach the manufacturer immediately, this is where to find them.

**Section 2: Hazards Identification**

The "quick summary" of danger. This includes:
- **Hazard symbols** (the same universal symbols you learned in Req 1c)
- **Hazard statements** ("Fatal if swallowed," "Causes severe skin burns," "May cause respiratory irritation")
- **Precautionary statements** ("Avoid contact with eyes," "Use with adequate ventilation")

This section tells you the most dangerous ways this chemical can hurt you.

**Section 3: Composition/Information on Ingredients**

What is in this product? For a simple product like diesel fuel, it might just be "petroleum distillate." For complex products like hydraulic fluid, it might list a base oil, anti-wear additives, corrosion inhibitors, etc. This section helps you understand what you are handling.

**Section 4: First-Aid Measures**

What to do if you are exposed:
- **Inhalation**: "Move to fresh air. If breathing is difficult, seek medical attention."
- **Skin contact**: "Wash with soap and water for at least 15 minutes."
- **Eye contact**: "Rinse cautiously with water for at least 15 minutes. Call poison control if vision changes."
- **Ingestion**: "DO NOT induce vomiting. Call poison control immediately."

If something goes wrong, this section tells you the first response.

**Section 5: Fire-Fighting Measures**

How to fight a fire involving this chemical:
- **Suitable extinguishing media**: "Use foam, CO₂, dry powder, or water spray."
- **Unsuitable media**: "Do NOT use straight streams of water" (for flammable liquids, water can make it worse).
- **Hazards from combustion**: "May produce toxic fumes of carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide."

If a hydraulic fluid catches fire, using the wrong extinguisher could make it worse.

**Section 6: Accidental Release Measures**

How to clean up a spill:
- **For small spills**: "Absorb with inert material (sand, sawdust, absorbent pads). Sweep into container for disposal."
- **For large spills**: "Contain with dikes. Prevent entry into waterways."
- **Special precautions**: Some chemicals require specific handling (e.g., avoid high heat, do not use certain materials).

Every farm shop needs this information before a spill happens, not after.

**Section 7: Handling and Storage**

How to safely handle and store the chemical:
- **Handling**: "Use in well-ventilated area. Avoid contact with skin and eyes."
- **Storage**: "Store in a cool, well-ventilated area. Keep container tightly closed. Store away from incompatible materials."
- **Incompatibilities**: "Keep away from strong oxidizing agents" (to prevent fire).

This tells you where to store it, how to keep it safe, and what NOT to store it near.

**Section 8: Exposure Controls/Personal Protective Equipment**

The PPE you need:
- **Engineering controls**: "Ensure adequate ventilation (at least X air changes per hour)."
- **PPE**: "Safety glasses, chemical-resistant gloves (nitrile recommended), long sleeves, closed-toe shoes."

This section confirms whether your safety practices are adequate.

**Section 9: Physical and Chemical Properties**

Appearance, odor, pH, boiling point, flash point, etc. This helps you identify the chemical (you need to know if that clear liquid is water or solvent), understand its behavior (a low flash point means it is highly flammable), and avoid unsafe conditions.

**Section 10: Stability and Reactivity**

Is this chemical stable, or does it degrade or react dangerously?
- **Stability**: "Stable under normal conditions."
- **Conditions to avoid**: "Temperatures above 50°C. Direct sunlight."
- **Incompatible materials**: "Avoid contact with strong bases or oxidizing agents."
- **Hazardous decomposition**: "May produce carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide if heated to decomposition."

This tells you what could go wrong if conditions change.

**Section 11: Toxicological Information**

Health effects of exposure:
- **Acute toxicity** (short-term exposure): "Harmful if swallowed. Skin irritant. Eye irritant."
- **Chronic toxicity** (long-term exposure): "Repeated or prolonged exposure may cause organ damage."
- **LD50 / LC50**: Lethal doses in animals (a technical benchmark for hazard level).

This section quantifies danger for different exposure routes.

**Section 12: Ecological Information**

Environmental hazards:
- "Toxic to aquatic organisms. Do not discharge into waterways."
- "Not readily biodegradable."

This is why spill kits exist and why used oil must be collected, not poured down the drain.

**Section 13: Disposal Considerations**

How to safely dispose of the product and its containers:
- **Used product**: "Recycle or incinerate in an approved facility."
- **Empty containers**: "Rinse three times. May be recycled or disposed of as regular waste."

Your farm shop should have a waste disposal contract. This section tells you how to prepare materials for pickup.

**Section 14: Transport Information**

How to ship the chemical (not usually relevant for on-farm use, but included for completeness).

**Section 15: Regulatory Information**

Laws and standards that apply. This includes OSHA regulations, EPA classifications, etc.

**Section 16: Other Information**

Revision date, preparer contact info, and any additional hazards not covered above.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Print or save the SDS for every hazardous material in your farm shop. Create a binder or digital folder labeled "Safety Data Sheets." Keep it in an accessible location and review it before you use a product for the first time. Your farm shop may keep this information digitally on a tablet or computer — that is fine too, as long as you can access it quickly.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### Why SDS Matters

An SDS is not just bureaucratic paperwork. It is the difference between a safe operation and a tragic accident:

1. **Prevents exposure**. Before you handle a chemical, you know what PPE to wear and what ventilation you need.

2. **Guides emergency response**. If someone is exposed, the SDS tells first-aiders exactly what to do — and what NOT to do.

3. **Protects your shop**. Incompatible chemicals stored together can react violently. An SDS tells you what to keep separate.

4. **Documents hazards**. If something goes wrong, the SDS proves you followed the manufacturer's recommendations.

### Obtaining an SDS

SDS documents are free and easy to get:

**From the manufacturer**: Visit the company website (John Deere, Shell, Mobil, etc.) and search "Safety Data Sheets" or "SDS." Most have a download center.

**From the supplier**: Ask the person who sold you the product. They are legally required to provide it.

**From online databases**: The OSHA SDS database or SafetyDataSheet.com can help you find obscure products.

**From the product container**: Many containers have a QR code linking to the SDS online.

### Reading an SDS: A Practical Scenario

You are about to change hydraulic fluid in a tractor. You have a container of Shell Tellus hydraulic oil. Here is what you do:

1. **Get the SDS** for Shell Tellus hydraulic oil (search online or ask the dealer).
2. **Read Section 2** (Hazards). You learn it is harmful if swallowed and causes skin irritation.
3. **Read Section 4** (First Aid). If it gets on your skin, wash with soap and water for 15 minutes.
4. **Read Section 8** (PPE). You need chemical-resistant gloves, safety glasses, and a long-sleeved shirt.
5. **Read Section 6** (Spill). If you spill some, absorb it with pads and dispose per Section 13.
6. **Read Section 7** (Storage). Store in a cool area, keep the container tightly closed, and keep away from water.

Now you are ready to work safely.
