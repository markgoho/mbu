{{< drg/requirement number="5" >}}
Visit an implement dealer. Interview the dealer technician or service manager for hints on good preventive maintenance. Ask why it is important, the costs, and what causes wear or damage. Report what you learn.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Dealer technicians see hundreds of machines a year — machines that have been well maintained and machines that have been neglected. They know exactly what shortcuts lead to expensive failures and what simple habits extend a machine's life by thousands of hours. This visit gives you access to their hard-won expertise.

## Preparing for Your Visit

A productive visit starts with preparation. Dealer service managers are busy professionals, especially during planting and harvest seasons. Contacting them in advance shows respect for their time and ensures you get a thorough conversation.

{{< drg/checklist title="Pre-Visit Preparation" subtitle="Steps to arrange and prepare for your dealer visit" >}}
- Identify local implement dealers: John Deere, Case IH, Kubota, AGCO/Massey Ferguson, New Holland — find the ones nearest you.
- Call the service department and ask to speak with the service manager. Explain you are a Scout working on the Farm Mechanics merit badge and ask for a 20–30 minute visit.
- Schedule during a slow period if possible: mid-winter or mid-summer are typically less hectic than spring planting or fall harvest.
- Prepare your questions in advance (see the list below).
- Bring a notebook and pen to record answers.
- Dress neatly and arrive on time.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Questions to Ask

The requirement specifies three topics: preventive maintenance importance, costs, and causes of wear or damage. Use these as your starting framework, then let the conversation go deeper.

### Why Preventive Maintenance Matters

- What is the single most important maintenance task farmers should never skip?
- What happens when a farmer ignores the recommended maintenance schedule?
- How much can regular maintenance extend a machine's lifespan?
- What percentage of the breakdowns you see in the shop were preventable?

### The Cost of Neglect

- What is the average cost of a major engine repair? A hydraulic system failure?
- How does the cost of a preventive oil change compare to the cost of an engine rebuild?
- What is the most expensive repair you have seen that was caused by skipped maintenance?
- How much does downtime during harvest cost a farmer per day?

### What Causes Wear and Damage

- What is the number one cause of premature engine failure?
- What destroys hydraulic systems faster than anything else?
- How does operating in dusty conditions affect equipment life?
- What common operator mistakes lead to transmission or drivetrain damage?

{{< drg/tip >}}
Ask the technician to show you an example of a worn-out part next to a new one. Seeing a clogged air filter beside a clean one, or a scored hydraulic cylinder next to a smooth one, makes the lesson concrete and memorable. Take photos if they allow it.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## What You Will Likely Hear

While every technician has their own stories, certain themes come up consistently across the industry.

**Contamination is the enemy.** Whether it is dirt in the fuel, water in the hydraulic fluid, or dust past a clogged air filter, contamination causes the majority of premature failures. Keeping fluids clean and filters fresh is the single most effective thing a farmer can do.

**Grease is cheap; bearings are not.** A $0.50 shot of grease takes 10 seconds. A replacement bearing costs $50–$200 and takes hours to install. Skipping a single grease point can destroy a bearing in one season.

**Small leaks become big problems.** A drip of hydraulic fluid from a loose fitting means the system is losing pressure and fluid. Left unaddressed, the pump works harder, overheats, and eventually fails — a repair that can exceed $5,000.

**Operator error is real.** Overloading, excessive speed, ignoring warning lights, and operating with worn tires or loose belts all cause damage that no amount of maintenance can undo.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
A single tablespoon of dirt in a diesel fuel system can destroy injectors that cost $300–$500 each to replace. Modern diesel injectors operate with internal clearances measured in microns (thousandths of a millimeter) — even particles invisible to the naked eye can score the precision surfaces and cause failures. That is why diesel fuel filters are so important.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

## After Your Visit

Write up what you learned in a brief report (one to two pages). Organize it around the three topics: importance, costs, and causes. Include any specific numbers, stories, or examples the technician shared — those details make your report come alive.

Be prepared to discuss your findings with your counselor. The best reports go beyond listing facts and include your own reflections: What surprised you? What will you do differently when working on equipment? How does what you learned connect to the maintenance work you did in [Req 4](/merit-badges/farm-mechanics/guide/req4/)?
