A well-designed farm shop is organized, efficient, and — most importantly — safe. Every zone serves a purpose, and every safety device is there because someone learned a hard lesson. Think of your shop as a classroom where the equipment teaches respect.

### Zones in a Farm Shop

A professional farm shop divides work into distinct zones, each with different requirements:

**The Engine Work Area**

This is where engines are diagnosed, tuned, and rebuilt. It needs bright lighting, a workbench with a vise, engine hoist space, and good ventilation. Diesel exhaust is toxic, so if you are running an engine indoors, install a flexible exhaust hose to direct fumes outside.

**The Hydraulic Work Area**

Hydraulic systems operate under thousands of pounds per square inch. This work requires a clean surface (even tiny dirt particles can block hydraulic lines), pressure gauges, proper torque wrenches, and absorbent materials to contain fluid spills. Keep a hydraulic fluid collection container nearby — used fluid is hazardous waste and cannot go down the drain.

**The Welding & Cutting Station**

Welding and cutting generate sparks, smoke, and intense heat. This area must be isolated from flammable materials, have good ventilation, and be far away from fuel and hydraulic fluid storage. A welding station needs a concrete or fire-resistant floor, a nearby fire extinguisher, and clear space around the work.

**Tool & Parts Storage**

Organized storage prevents injuries and lost time. Keep frequently used hand tools within arm's reach on a pegboard or wall-mounted rack. Store heavy items on lower shelves, light items above. Label everything. Hazardous materials (solvents, greases, fuels) go in a locked cabinet away from food or areas where someone might accidentally ingest them.

**The Cleaning & Inspection Area**

Before you work on equipment, you need to see what you are doing. A solvent parts washer (a tank where you dip dirty parts in degreaser) lets you clean grease and buildup without using a high-pressure hose. Nearby, a workbench with good lighting and magnifying lamps is perfect for detailed inspection.

### Mandatory Safety Devices

Every professional farm shop has these non-negotiable features:

**Fire Extinguishers**

At least one Type ABC multipurpose extinguisher should hang on a wall near the welding station, another near fuel storage, and one near the main exit. They must be clearly marked, regularly inspected, and mounted where anyone can grab them quickly.

**First-Aid Kit**

A locked cabinet with bandages, antiseptic, burn cream, gauze pads, and a triangular sling should be easily accessible. Include emergency contact information and a log where users record what they used. Replenish it regularly.

**Eye Wash Station**

If chemicals or hydraulic fluid could splash into eyes, install an eye wash station (a wall-mounted dispenser that floods both eyes with clean water for at least 15 minutes). Many shops mount it near the hydraulic work area.

**Safety Shower**

For larger shops where workers might get doused with chemicals, a safety shower (similar to eye wash but covers the whole body) is important. It should spray water for at least 15 minutes continuously.

**Ventilation System**

Diesel exhaust, welding fumes, and solvent vapors are all toxic. A good ventilation system removes them. Options include:
- **Natural ventilation**: Open doors and windows (not always reliable).
- **Exhaust hose for the engine**: Flexible hose running from the engine to outdoors.
- **Fume hood over the parts washer**: Captures solvent vapors before they enter the air.
- **Ceiling-mounted fans**: Move air continuously (but do not seal the exhaust — it must exit outside).

**Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter (GFCI) Outlets**

Electricity and moisture (from washing equipment) are a deadly combination. GFCI outlets instantly cut power if they detect a leak, preventing electrocution. Install them in all work areas, especially near wash stations.

**Proper Lighting**

Shadows cause mistakes. Every work area needs at least 50 foot-candles of light (roughly equivalent to 500 lux). Overhead LED lights, task lighting on workbenches, and flashlights all contribute. Avoid harsh shadows.

**Spill Kit**

Hydraulic fluid, engine oil, and fuel spills happen. A spill kit contains absorbent material (sand, oil dry, or absorbent pads), brooms, and containers for disposal. Keep it accessible but separate from the work areas.

### Shop Layout Principles

When you draw your plan, keep these principles in mind:

1. **Zoning prevents cross-contamination**: Welding sparks should not reach flammable storage. Hydraulic work should not happen near grinding wheels that throw sparks.

2. **Traffic flow matters**: A clear path from the main entrance to each work area reduces accidents. Avoid creating "dead ends" where equipment is hard to retrieve.

3. **Gravity helps**: Place grease, oil, and waste disposal at the bottom of a slope (if your floor slopes) so they drain toward collection containers, not across the floor.

4. **Access to equipment**: Machines need space to move in and out. A tractor should fit through the door; a hydraulic press should not block the fire extinguisher.

5. **Emergency exits**: Design at least two routes out of the shop in case the main door is blocked.

### Creating Your Diagram

For this requirement, you will draw a to-scale plan of a farm shop. You can use graph paper, a computer drawing program, or even a sketch — as long as it shows:

- The overall floor dimensions
- Each work zone (labeled)
- The location of every mandatory safety device and feature
- Key furniture and fixed equipment (workbenches, storage cabinets, machines)
- Electrical outlets (especially GFCI outlets)
- Emergency exits
- A legend or labels identifying each safety feature

Your diagram does not have to be artistic, but it should be clear and organized. A good practice: color-code different zones (blue for hydraulics, red for welding, green for general work, etc.) to make the layout easier to understand at a glance.
