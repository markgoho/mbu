Do TWO of the following:

This requirement asks you to complete two hands-on tasks that build fundamental maintenance skills. You will work with hand tools, fix damaged tools, organize equipment, or restore worn implements. These are the tasks that separate someone who owns tools from someone who can maintain and care for them.

### Your Options

You have four options. Choose any two:

**Option A: Replace the Handle of Any Farm Tool**

Find a tool with a damaged handle — a hammer with a cracked wooden handle, a hoe with a bent shaft, a shovel with a broken grip. Learn how to safely remove the old handle, prepare the new one, and secure it so it will not wiggle or fall off. This is practical, useful work.

→ [See Req 3a for details](/merit-badges/farm-mechanics/guide/req3a/)

**Option B: Organize a Tool Rack or Storage System**

Every efficient farm shop has organized storage. Create a system for storing nails, bolts, nuts, washers, and other hardware. Design a tool rack that makes frequently used tools easy to grab. This teaches organization, which prevents lost time and lost tools.

→ [See Req 3b for details](/merit-badges/farm-mechanics/guide/req3b/)

**Option C: Dress a Chisel or Punch With a Hand File**

Sometimes a chisel or punch develops a mushroomed head — metal that is "peened over" from repeated striking. Using a hand file, you carefully wear away that deformed metal to restore the chisel to its proper shape. This develops precision and introduces you to hand tool technique.

→ [See Req 3c for details](/merit-badges/farm-mechanics/guide/req3c/)

**Option D: Dress a Screwdriver Tip With a Hand File**

A screwdriver tip can become rounded, bent, or beveled from years of use. Using a hand file, you restore it to sharp, flat edges so it grips screw slots properly. Like chiseling, this teaches precision and hand tool control.

→ [See Req 3d for details](/merit-badges/farm-mechanics/guide/req3d/)

### Why Tool Maintenance Matters

A dull tool is frustrating and dangerous. A dull chisel bounces off the material instead of cutting, risking your hand. A rounded screwdriver slips in the slot and damages screw heads. A tool with a damaged handle can splinter or break during use. Learning to maintain and restore tools teaches respect for equipment and develops skills that last a lifetime.

Professional mechanics keep their tools in perfect condition. A electrician's screwdriver is sharp. A carpenter's hammer has a solid handle. A machinist's chisels are properly dressed. These professionals do not accept damaged tools — they either repair them or replace them.

### Difficulty and Time

Each option requires different skills and takes varying amounts of time:

- **Handle replacement**: 1–2 hours, depends on how firmly the old handle is stuck
- **Tool organization**: 2–4 hours, depending on the scope (small rack or entire storage system)
- **Dressing a chisel or punch**: 1–2 hours, requires hand filing technique and patience
- **Dressing a screwdriver**: 30 minutes to 1 hour, relatively quick

### Safety While Working

Wear appropriate PPE throughout:
- Safety glasses (metal filings can fly when filing)
- Work gloves (splinters from wooden handles, or sharp metal edges)
- Hearing protection if using power tools
- Steel-toe boots (dropped tools hurt)

Keep your workspace clear of clutter. Organize materials before starting. If something does not feel right, stop and ask your counselor.
