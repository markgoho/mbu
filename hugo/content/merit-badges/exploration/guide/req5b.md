{{< drg/requirement number="5b" >}}
With permission and approval, visit either in person or via the internet a science lab, astronomical observatory, medical research facility, or similar site. Learn what exploration is done in this facility.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This option lets you see where exploration actually happens — in labs, observatories, and research centers where scientists are asking questions and conducting experiments right now.

## Types of Facilities to Visit

### University Research Laboratories

Most universities have science laboratories where researchers explore biology, chemistry, physics, and other sciences.

**What to look for:**
- What research questions are they investigating?
- What equipment do they use?
- How many people work on each project?
- How long do typical projects last?

**How to arrange a visit:**
- Contact the university's science department
- Ask if they offer lab tours for community groups
- Many universities are happy to host visits if you schedule in advance

### Medical Research Facilities

Hospitals and medical centers often have research departments investigating disease and treatments.

**What to look for:**
- What diseases are they researching?
- Are they studying human patients or cell cultures?
- What stage are their treatments at (basic research, animal testing, human trials)?

**Example research areas:**
- Cancer research
- Alzheimer's disease
- Heart disease
- Genetic disorders

### Astronomical Observatories

Observatories use telescopes to explore the universe.

**What to look for:**
- What objects are they observing (planets, galaxies, exoplanets)?
- How large is their telescope?
- What discoveries have they made?
- Can you look through the telescope?

**Public observatories:**
- Many universities run observatories open to the public
- Local science museums may have observatories
- Some planetariums have educational programs

### Marine Research Facilities

Aquariums and marine science centers conduct ocean research.

**What to look for:**
- What species are they studying?
- Are they doing conservation work?
- What partnerships do they have with field explorers?

**Example facilities:**
- Monterey Bay Aquarium (California)
- Georgia Aquarium (Atlanta)
- Local university marine science labs

### Geological Survey & Environmental Facilities

Government agencies and universities study geology, weather, and climate.

**What to look for:**
- What geological or environmental questions are they investigating?
- What equipment do they use?
- How do their findings affect policy or conservation?

## How to Visit Online

If in-person visits are not possible, many facilities offer online content:

1. **Virtual lab tours** — Search for "[facility name] virtual tour"
2. **Educational videos** — Many research centers post videos about their work
3. **Websites** — Explore research facility websites to learn about current projects
4. **Social media** — Research centers often post updates and discoveries on Twitter, YouTube, or Instagram
5. **Online databases** — Many research facilities make data publicly available

## What to Find Out

During your visit, gather information about:

1. **What is the facility?** What is its mission?

2. **What types of exploration happen here?** What questions are researchers asking?

3. **Who works here?** How many scientists? What are their backgrounds?

4. **What equipment or technology do they use?** What makes their exploration possible?

5. **What have they discovered recently?** Ask about current projects or recent findings.

6. **How do they share their findings?** Do they publish papers? Give talks? Work with other researchers?

7. **What makes this exploration difficult or interesting?** What are the biggest challenges?

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
If you visit a research facility in person, you will be asked to follow safety rules. Research labs have hazardous materials and equipment. Listen carefully to safety instructions and follow them exactly. This is how real researchers stay safe while exploring.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Sample Visits

### Visiting a University Physics Lab

**What you might learn:**
- The lab is investigating how particles behave under extreme conditions
- Researchers use specialized equipment costing millions of dollars
- The team includes graduate students and postdoctoral researchers
- Results are published in peer-reviewed journals
- One recent finding is being prepared for publication

### Visiting a Marine Science Facility

**What you might learn:**
- They are studying coral bleaching due to ocean warming
- They keep coral colonies alive in controlled tanks while varying temperature and water chemistry
- They document how corals respond to stress
- Their findings inform conservation efforts
- They collaborate with field explorers in tropical oceans

### Visiting an Astronomical Observatory

**What you might learn:**
- The telescope can detect light from billions of light-years away
- Researchers are searching for exoplanets around distant stars
- Data is collected automatically and analyzed by computers
- Discoveries are shared with other astronomers worldwide
- Amateur astronomers also contribute to discoveries

---

## Report Back to Your Counselor

Be prepared to share:

1. **Which facility you visited and its mission**

2. **What types of exploration happen there** — What questions are researchers asking?

3. **The tools and methods they use** — What equipment or techniques make their exploration possible?

4. **A current or recent research project** — What are they investigating? What have they discovered?

5. **How the lab's work connects to field exploration** — How do laboratory findings apply to real-world exploration?

6. **Your reflection** — What surprised you? What would you like to explore further?
