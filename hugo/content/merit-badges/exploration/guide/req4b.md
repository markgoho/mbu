This option asks you to research a major scientific expedition — a space mission, an oceanographic cruise, a paleontology field study, or any organized scientific exploration. You will learn how science works at scale and how expeditions produce real discoveries.

## How to Approach This

### Step 1: Pick an Expedition

Choose an expedition that excites you. Some famous scientific expeditions currently active or recently completed:

**Space Missions:**
- **Mars 2020 Perseverance Rover** (NASA) — Searching for signs of ancient microbial life on Mars
- **International Space Station Research** (ongoing, international) — Long-term scientific experiments in orbit
- **James Webb Space Telescope** (NASA, launched 2021) — Observing the earliest galaxies and distant planets

**Ocean Exploration:**
- **Titanic Exploration** (Robert Ballard, 1985) — Locating and documenting the wreck
- **Hydrothermal Vent Research** (ongoing, multiple institutions) — Studying extreme-environment life and mineral deposits
- **Coral Reef Monitoring Expeditions** (ongoing) — Understanding coral bleaching and restoration

**Paleontology & Archaeology:**
- **Dinosaur Fossil Expeditions** (ongoing, various locations) — Finding and analyzing dinosaur remains
- **Lucy Fossil Site** (Ethiopia) — Study of human ancestor fossils
- **Amazon Expedition** (National Geographic) — Discovering new species and indigenous cultures

**Climate & Earth Science:**
- **Greenland Ice Core Research** (ongoing) — Extracting ancient ice to study climate history
- **Arctic Permafrost Study** (ongoing) — Understanding thawing and its impacts
- **Ocean Acidification Research** (ongoing) — Measuring how climate change affects ocean chemistry

### Step 2: Research Your Expedition

Gather information about:
- **Objectives:** What scientific questions is the mission designed to answer?
- **Timeline:** When did/does it happen? How long?
- **Location:** Where in the world or solar system?
- **Team:** Who is funding and leading it? What countries/institutions are involved?
- **Methods:** What equipment and techniques are they using?
- **Challenges:** What obstacles have they overcome?
- **Discoveries:** What have they found or observed?
- **Impact:** How do these findings answer scientific questions?

**Reliable sources:**
- Official mission websites (NASA, ESA, oceanographic institutions)
- Scientific journal articles
- TED talks and documentaries
- National Geographic articles
- University press releases

### Step 3: Focus on Key Discoveries

When you gather your information, emphasize:
- **What was the question?** The expedition was designed to answer something specific.
- **What did they find?** Be specific about discoveries, not vague.
- **Why does it matter?** How does this discovery answer the original question or open new questions?

**Example: Perseverance Rover & Ancient Microbial Life**

*Question:* Is there evidence that Mars once had conditions suitable for microbial life?

*Discovery:* Perseverance found organic molecules and methane variations in rocks — signs that could indicate ancient microbial life.

*Significance:* If Mars harbored life in the past, it suggests life is not rare in the universe. It also informs the search for life on other planets and moons.

### Step 4: Create Your Presentation or Report

Present your findings to your counselor and unit. Format options:
- **Written report** (2–4 pages)
- **Verbal presentation** (5–10 minutes)
- **Multimedia** (slides, video, poster)

**Structure:**
1. **Introduction:** Why you chose this expedition
2. **Background:** What scientific question does it address?
3. **The expedition:** Timeline, location, team, methods, challenges
4. **Key discoveries:** Specific findings (be detailed!)
5. **Scientific impact:** How do these discoveries answer the original questions?
6. **Your reflection:** What does this teach you about exploration?

## Notable Scientific Expeditions

### Mars 2020 Perseverance Rover (NASA, 2021–ongoing)

**Objective:** Land on Mars and search for signs of ancient microbial life; collect samples for future return to Earth

**Location:** Jezero Crater, Mars

**Why this location:** Jezero Crater once had a river delta and a standing water lake — environments where life could have existed.

**Key methods:**
- Drilling samples from rock up to 2 inches deep
- Analyzing organic chemistry with laboratory instruments
- Photographing and studying geological formations
- Recording weather, radiation, and atmospheric data

**Key discoveries:**
- Evidence of organic molecules in Martian rocks
- Variations in methane levels suggesting possible microbial metabolism
- Ancient river deltas showing water once flowed on Mars
- Seasonal carbon dioxide variations in the atmosphere

**Impact:** Perseverance is the first mission specifically equipped to search for *biosignatures* (evidence of past life). Its findings will guide future human missions to Mars and inform our search for life elsewhere.

### Expedition to the Mariana Trench (Deepest Point in the Ocean)

**Objective:** Explore the deepest part of Earth's ocean and study extreme deep-sea life

**Location:** Mariana Trench, western Pacific Ocean

**Depth:** 36,000 feet (11 kilometers) below sea level

**Key discoveries:**
- Unique species adapted to extreme pressure, cold, and darkness
- Hydrothermal activity on the ocean floor
- Evidence of chemical cycles previously unknown

**Impact:** Understanding how life thrives in the most extreme environment on Earth helps us understand the limits of life and suggests where to search for life on other worlds (like Jupiter's moon Europa, which has a deep subsurface ocean).

### Hubble Space Telescope & James Webb Space Telescope

**Objective:** Observe the universe in unprecedented detail; study distant galaxies, exoplanets, and stellar processes

**Key discoveries (Hubble, launched 1990):**
- Determined the age of the universe (approximately 13.8 billion years)
- Discovered exoplanets orbiting distant stars
- Observed the deepest, most distant galaxies ever seen
- Documented the existence of black holes

**Impact:** Space telescope observations have answered fundamental questions about the universe's age, size, and contents. They have shown that planets around other stars are common — opening the possibility of life beyond Earth.

## Key Questions to Answer

When you present, address:

1. **What was the central scientific question?** (Not "What is this expedition?" but "What are we trying to find out?")

2. **What unexpected discoveries were made?** (Curiosity rover finding methane variations on Mars was a surprise that launched new investigations.)

3. **How did the discoveries answer the original question?** (Or open new questions?)

4. **What makes this a significant exploration achievement?** (First to go somewhere? First to measure something? Changed our understanding of something?)

5. **What do we still not know?** (Good explorations raise new questions.)
