Every successful expedition starts with a clear, specific objective. You cannot plan a journey without knowing where you are going or why. This first requirement asks you to establish your exploration goals.

## What Is an Exploration Objective?

An **objective** is a specific, measurable goal for your exploration. It answers the question: "What do we hope to discover or learn?"

**Weak objective:** "Go somewhere cool and see what we find."

**Strong objective:** "Document native plant species and their elevations in the ridge trail system west of town, with the goal of understanding how elevation affects plant distribution."

The strong objective is specific, measurable, and has a purpose.

## Characteristics of Good Exploration Objectives

### Clear & Specific

"Learn about geology" is too vague. "Identify three different types of rock formations in the canyon and collect samples to analyze" is specific.

### Achievable in Your Timeline

Your objective should be realistic for the time and resources you have. "Document all bird species in North America" is not achievable. "Document 10 bird species in our local park" is.

### Based on Genuine Curiosity

Choose something *you* are interested in. Exploration is hard work — you need real curiosity to push through challenges.

### Observable & Measurable

Your objective should result in concrete observations: specimens collected, measurements taken, photographs documented, data recorded. Vague goals like "experience nature" are not exploration objectives.

### Connected to Your Community or Interests

Choose an exploration that matters to you and potentially benefits your community. Maybe you want to understand a local stream ecosystem. Maybe you want to catalog wildflower species in your region. Maybe you want to explore a cave system no one has fully mapped.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Ask yourself: "If I spend a week exploring X, what specific knowledge or discoveries would I come home with?" If you cannot answer that clearly, your objective is not specific enough yet.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Types of Exploration Objectives

### Geographic / Ecological Objectives

"Map the changes in vegetation as you ascend this mountain."

"Catalog aquatic insect species and water quality at different points along this stream."

"Document how the local canyon habitat differs from similar canyons in neighboring regions."

### Archaeological / Historical Objectives

"Search for and document archaeological evidence from historic settlements in the area."

"Research and map historical sites mentioned in local historical records."

### Scientific Research Objectives

"Measure how temperature and light affect moss growth in different microhabitats."

"Conduct a citizen science project documenting bird nesting behavior."

"Collect water samples from a local watershed and analyze for pH, dissolved oxygen, and invertebrate species."

### Conservation Objectives

"Document invasive species in a local park and develop a removal plan."

"Study how a local habitat has changed in response to restoration efforts."

"Assess the health of a local wetland ecosystem."

## Examples of Strong Exploration Objectives

### Example 1: Ecological Survey

**Objective:** Document the diversity of macro-invertebrates (visible insects and related creatures) in three different stream habitats: shallow riffles, deep pools, and vegetated banks.

**Why it is strong:**
- Specific (three stream habitats, macro-invertebrates)
- Measurable (number of species, abundance)
- Achievable (can be done in one expedition)
- Has purpose (understanding habitat diversity informs conservation)

### Example 2: Archaeological Investigation

**Objective:** Search for and document artifacts from a homestead mentioned in county historical records from the 1890s, including photographs, measurements, and artifact collection.

**Why it is strong:**
- Specific (particular homestead, particular time period)
- Measurable (artifacts documented, photographed, measured)
- Achievable (local area, defined location)
- Has purpose (understanding local history)

### Example 3: Species Documentation

**Objective:** Catalog lichen species growing on different rock types (granite, sandstone, limestone) on north-facing and south-facing slopes, with the goal of understanding how geology and sun exposure affect lichen distribution.

**Why it is strong:**
- Specific (lichen species, rock types, slope exposure)
- Measurable (species identified, locations recorded)
- Achievable (local area)
- Has purpose (understanding ecological relationships)

## Setting Your Objectives

Work through these questions:

1. **What am I genuinely curious about?** What would I enjoy researching and thinking about for weeks?

2. **What would I like to explore in my region?** What natural areas, ecosystems, or historical sites are nearby?

3. **What specific question could I answer through exploration?** (Not "What is out there?" but "What is the relationship between X and Y?" or "What species live in this habitat?" or "How has this place changed?")

4. **How would I measure success?** What concrete outputs would indicate a successful exploration? (Specimens collected, measurements taken, species identified, artifacts documented)

5. **How long would it take?** Is this something you could accomplish in one weekend? One week? Multiple trips over a month?

6. **What resources would I need?** What equipment, permissions, or expertise?

Your objectives might evolve as you plan (which is fine!), but start with a clear answer to these questions.
