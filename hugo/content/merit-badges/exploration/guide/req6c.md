{{< drg/requirement number="6c" >}}
Budget and plan for adequate financial resources. Estimate costs for travel, equipment, accommodations, meals, permits or licenses, and other expedition expenses.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Every expedition — from a local creek survey to a deep-ocean dive — has costs. Professional expeditions can run into millions of dollars, but even a Scout-scale expedition requires careful financial planning. A budget that is too low means you leave out something critical. A budget that is realistic shows your counselor that you have thought through every detail.

## Building Your Expedition Budget

Break your budget into categories. Even if some categories end up at $0, listing them shows thorough planning.

### Travel

How will you get to your expedition site? Consider fuel costs, tolls, parking fees, or public transportation fares. If your expedition involves multiple sites, calculate travel between them.

### Equipment

What do you already own, and what do you need to buy, borrow, or rent? A water quality test kit costs money. So does a field guide, a topographic map, or replacement batteries. List every item and note whether you own it, can borrow it, or need to purchase it.

### Accommodations

If your expedition extends overnight, where will you stay? Campground fees, tent site reservations, or lodging costs should be estimated. Many Scout-scale expeditions are day trips with no accommodation costs — that is fine, but account for it explicitly.

### Meals

Food and water for everyone on the expedition. For a day trip, this might be a packed lunch and snacks. For longer expeditions, meal planning becomes more significant. Do not forget water — especially if you will be in an area without reliable water sources.

### Permits and Licenses

Some permits are free; others cost money. Research permit fees for your specific site. If you need a fishing license, a park entry pass, or a research collection permit, include those costs.

### Other Expenses

This catch-all category covers things like printing costs for data sheets, postage for mailing permit applications, photo printing for your report, and any other miscellaneous expenses.

## Sample Budget

Here is a sample budget for a one-day stream ecology expedition:

| Category | Item | Cost |
|----------|------|------|
| Travel | Gas (round trip, 60 miles) | $15.00 |
| Travel | Park entrance fee | $5.00 |
| Equipment | Water quality test kit | $25.00 (purchase) |
| Equipment | Field notebook | $0.00 (already owned) |
| Equipment | Insect collection net | $0.00 (borrowed from school) |
| Equipment | Batteries for GPS | $6.00 |
| Meals | Lunch and snacks (3 people) | $15.00 |
| Meals | Water bottles (3 people) | $0.00 (brought from home) |
| Permits | State park research permit | $0.00 (free for educational use) |
| Other | Printing data sheets | $2.00 |
| Other | Photo prints for report | $5.00 |
| | **Total** | **$73.00** |

{{< drg/tip >}}
Add a **contingency fund** of 10–15% on top of your estimated total. Unexpected expenses always arise — a forgotten piece of gear, an extra meal, a replacement battery. For the sample budget above, a 10% contingency would add $7.30, bringing the total to about $80.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Funding Your Expedition

Where does the money come from? For a Scout-scale expedition, you have several options:

- **Personal funds** — Your own savings or contributions from family.
- **Troop support** — Your troop may have funds available for merit badge activities.
- **Shared costs** — If you are going with a team, split costs fairly.
- **Borrowed equipment** — Schools, nature centers, and libraries sometimes lend scientific equipment. Borrowing saves money and reduces waste.
- **Sponsorship** — Some local businesses or organizations support Scout activities. It never hurts to ask.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Major exploration expeditions have always relied on sponsors. Christopher Columbus was funded by the Spanish crown. Ernest Shackleton sold naming rights to features he planned to discover. Today, organizations like National Geographic and The Explorers Club offer grants to fund scientific expeditions — including some for students and young researchers.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

## Presenting Your Budget

When you discuss your budget with your counselor, be prepared to explain:

- **Why you chose each item** — What purpose does it serve for your objectives?
- **How you estimated costs** — Did you look up prices online, call a store, or check with the land manager for permit fees?
- **What you can borrow vs. what you must buy** — This shows resourcefulness.
- **Your contingency plan** — What happens if costs are higher than expected?
