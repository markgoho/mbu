Explore how you could use knowledge and skills from this merit badge to pursue a hobby. Research any training needed, expenses, and organizations that promote or support it. Discuss with your counselor what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursued this.

This option asks you to think about exploration as a *hobby* — something you could do throughout your life for enjoyment, personal growth, and contribution to science. Many of the world's greatest discoveries come from hobbyists with passion and curiosity.

## Exploration Hobbies

### Birdwatching (Birding)

**What:** Identify and observe birds in nature

**Training needed:** Field guide skills, binoculars use, bird identification

**Cost:** Low to moderate
- Binoculars: $100–$500
- Field guides: $20–$40
- Club membership: free–$50/year

**Organizations:**
- National Audubon Society
- eBird (Cornell Lab of Ornithology)
- Local birding clubs

**Short-term goals:**
- Identify 20 species in your area
- Keep a birding journal

**Long-term goals:**
- Identify 100+ species
- Contribute to citizen science projects
- Lead birding walks for others
- Travel to see new species

### Citizen Science

**What:** Contribute to real scientific research by collecting data

**Examples:**
- iNaturalist (photographing organisms and submitting locations)
- Zooniverse (analyzing scientific images online)
- BirdCount (tracking bird populations)
- Stream monitoring (water quality testing)

**Training needed:** Varies by project; mostly learn as you go

**Cost:** Low to free
- iNaturalist: free
- Training: online or local workshops ($0–$50)
- Equipment: varies (binoculars, water testing kit, etc.)

**Organizations:** Varies by project

**Short-term goals:**
- Submit 50 observations to iNaturalist
- Complete one citizen science project

**Long-term goals:**
- Contribute thousands of observations
- Have findings used in real research
- Lead local citizen science efforts

### Hiking & Backpacking

**What:** Explore new trails, peaks, and wilderness areas

**Training needed:** Navigation, wilderness skills, safety

**Cost:** Moderate to high
- Equipment (tent, pack, etc.): $500–$2000
- Guidebooks: $15–$30
- Park fees: $5–$20 per visit

**Organizations:**
- American Alpine Club
- Appalachian Trail Conservancy
- Local hiking clubs
- Sierra Club

**Short-term goals:**
- Hike 10 new trails
- Complete a multi-day backpacking trip

**Long-term goals:**
- Hike 100+ trails
- Backpack in multiple regions
- Lead hikes for others
- Explore major peaks or trail systems

### Scuba Diving / Snorkeling

**What:** Explore underwater ecosystems

**Training needed:** SCUBA certification (3–4 day course)

**Cost:** Moderate to high
- Initial certification: $300–$500
- Equipment rental: $50–$100 per dive
- Equipment purchase: $1000–$3000

**Organizations:**
- PADI (Professional Association of Diving Instructors)
- Local dive shops
- Coral Reef Alliance

**Short-term goals:**
- Get SCUBA certification
- Complete 10 dives
- Identify 30 underwater species

**Long-term goals:**
- Log 100+ dives
- Explore multiple dive sites
- Contribute to coral reef monitoring
- Become a dive master or instructor

### Geology / Rockhounding

**What:** Collect and study rocks and minerals

**Training needed:** Rock and mineral identification

**Cost:** Low to moderate
- Collecting tools: $20–$50
- Field guides: $15–$30
- Membership in rockhounding club: $20–$50/year

**Organizations:**
- American Federation of Mineralogical Societies
- Local rock clubs
- Museums with geology collections

**Short-term goals:**
- Identify 30 rock and mineral types
- Build a collection

**Long-term goals:**
- Specialize in one type (gems, fossils, etc.)
- Display your collection
- Contribute findings to museums
- Lead rockhounding trips for others

### Kayaking / Canoeing Exploration

**What:** Explore rivers, lakes, and coastal areas by water

**Training needed:** Paddling skills, water safety, navigation

**Cost:** Moderate to high
- Kayak/canoe: $500–$2000
- Paddles, life jacket: $100–$300
- Training course: $100–$200

**Organizations:**
- American Kayaking Association
- American Canoeing Association
- Local paddling clubs

**Short-term goals:**
- Take a paddling course
- Paddle 3 new waterways

**Long-term goals:**
- Paddle 20+ waterways
- Multi-day paddling trips
- Explore coastal or remote areas
- Lead paddling adventures

## Research Your Hobby

Choose one hobby and research:

1. **Training required** — What skills do you need? How long to learn?

2. **Equipment costs** — What do you need to buy? (list with prices)

3. **Organizations** — Who promotes this hobby? What do they offer?

4. **Accessibility** — Where can you pursue this in your region?

5. **Growth potential** — How deep can you go? (casual to expert level)

6. **Community** — Are there groups of people you could join?

---

## Setting Goals

**Short-term (1 year):**
- Specific, achievable milestones
- Build foundational skills
- Complete basic training/certification if needed
- Experience the hobby a few times

**Long-term (5–10 years):**
- More ambitious exploration
- Deeper expertise
- Possible contribution to science or conservation
- Leadership or mentoring others

**Example goal progression for birding:**

*Year 1:*
- Buy binoculars and field guide
- Join local birding club
- Identify 30 species in my area
- Keep a detailed birding journal

*Years 2–5:*
- Identify 100+ species
- Contribute to eBird
- Lead monthly bird walks at local park
- Travel to new regions to see new species

*10-year vision:*
- Expert birder with extensive knowledge
- Mentoring younger birders
- Contributing meaningfully to conservation
- Possibly publishing articles on local birds

---

## Discussion with Your Counselor

When you meet with your counselor, discuss:

1. **Hobby you chose** — What appeals to you about it?

2. **Training & equipment** — What's required to start?

3. **Cost analysis** — Is it affordable for you?

4. **Accessibility** — Can you pursue this where you live?

5. **Short-term goals** — What would you accomplish in the first year?

6. **Long-term vision** — How might this grow over your lifetime?

7. **Community** — Would you enjoy doing this with others?

---

Exploration does not have to be a career. It can be a joyful, lifelong hobby that keeps you curious, active, and connected to nature and discovery. Some of the greatest discoveries come from passionate hobbyists!
