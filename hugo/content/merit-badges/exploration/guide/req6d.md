{{< drg/requirement number="6d" >}}
Determine equipment and supplies required for personal and mission needs for the length of the expedition.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The difference between a well-equipped expedition and a scrambling mess often comes down to one forgotten item — the extra batteries you did not bring, the rain jacket you left in the car, or the data sheets you forgot to print. Smart packing starts by separating your needs into two categories: personal gear and mission gear.

## Personal Gear

Personal gear keeps you safe, comfortable, and functional throughout the expedition. This is the gear you would bring on any outdoor activity, regardless of your research objectives.

### The Essentials

{{< drg/checklist title="Personal Gear Checklist" subtitle="Adjust based on weather, terrain, and expedition length" >}}
- Appropriate footwear (hiking boots or sturdy shoes for the terrain).
- Weather-appropriate clothing (layers, rain gear, sun protection).
- Water — enough for the full day plus extra.
- Food and snacks — calorie-dense, easy to eat in the field.
- Sun protection — hat, sunscreen, sunglasses.
- Insect repellent (if applicable to your area).
- Personal first-aid kit.
- Whistle and signaling device.
- Headlamp or flashlight with fresh batteries.
- Pocket knife or multi-tool.
- Personal medications.
- Identification and emergency contact information.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Never cut corners on personal safety gear to save weight or money. A first-aid kit, adequate water, and rain gear are non-negotiable, even on a short day trip. Conditions can change rapidly, and being unprepared puts your entire team at risk.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Mission Gear

Mission gear is everything specific to your expedition objectives. This is what separates your gear list from a standard day hike packing list. The right mission gear depends entirely on what you are studying.

### Examples by Expedition Type

**Ecological Survey:**
- Field guide(s) for the species you expect to encounter
- Binoculars or magnifying lens
- Collection containers or specimen bags (if permitted)
- Camera with charged battery and spare memory card
- Water quality test kit (for aquatic surveys)
- Measuring tape or ruler
- Thermometer

**Geological Exploration:**
- Rock hammer and safety goggles
- Hand lens (10x magnification)
- Geological field guide
- Sample bags with labels
- Acid bottle (for carbonate testing — with adult supervision)
- GPS device or smartphone with GPS app

**Historical/Archaeological Investigation:**
- Camera with multiple batteries
- Measuring tape
- Graph paper for sketching
- Reference materials about the site
- Gloves (for handling artifacts, if applicable)
- Ziplock bags and labels for any authorized collections

**General for All Expeditions:**
- Field notebook and pencils (not pens — pencils work in rain)
- Data recording sheets (pre-printed for your specific objectives)
- GPS device or smartphone with mapping app
- Camera or phone camera
- Extra batteries or portable charger
- Clipboard (keeps data sheets organized and provides a writing surface)
- Permanent markers and labels for any samples

{{< drg/tip >}}
Make two lists: "must have" and "nice to have." Pack everything on the "must have" list first. If you have room and weight capacity, add "nice to have" items. A spotting scope is nice to have for birding, but binoculars are a must have. Do not let luxury items crowd out essentials.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## How to Justify Your Gear

Your counselor will ask why you chose each item. Every piece of equipment should connect directly to either personal safety or your expedition objectives. If you cannot explain why you need it, leave it behind.

Here is a framework:

| Item | Justification |
|------|--------------|
| Water quality test kit | Needed to measure pH and dissolved oxygen — primary objective data |
| Binoculars | Required for identifying bird species from survey distance |
| Rain jacket | Personal safety — weather forecast shows 30% chance of afternoon rain |
| Extra batteries | GPS and camera are critical for data collection and navigation |
| Field notebook | Primary documentation tool — all observations recorded here |

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
The first ascent of Mount Everest in 1953 required over 7 tons of equipment and supplies, carried by 362 porters. Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay carried only 30 pounds each for the summit push — every ounce carefully chosen. Great expeditions are defined as much by what you leave behind as by what you bring.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}
