{{< drg/requirement number="6h" >}}
Establish detailed recordkeeping (documentation) procedures. Plan the interpretation and sharing of information at the conclusion of the expedition.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Documentation is what transforms your expedition from a personal experience into exploration. Without records, your discoveries are just stories. This requirement asks you to plan how you will document everything and share it afterward.

## Why Document Everything?

**Verification:** Other people can check your work and verify your findings.

**Completeness:** Written records catch details that memory misses.

**Impact:** Shared findings can inform conservation, science, or public knowledge.

**Personal growth:** Reviewing what you found helps you understand deeper lessons.

---

## What to Document

### Field Observations

**Daily notebook entries:**
- Date, time, location (GPS coordinates if possible)
- Weather conditions
- What you observed
- Measurements taken
- Species identified (with photos)
- Problems encountered and how you solved them
- Team morale and physical condition

**Structure for entries:**

```
DATE: July 15, 2024
TIME: 9:00 AM – 2:00 PM
LOCATION: Stream Section 3 (41.2345° N, 105.6789° W)
WEATHER: 72°F, partly cloudy, light breeze

OBSERVATIONS:
- Water clarity: Good, can see 2 feet down
- Water temperature: 58°F
- Macro-invertebrates found:
  - Mayfly nymphs (approx. 30)
  - Caddisfly larvae (approx. 15)
  - Freshwater snails (approx. 8)
  - Stonefly nymphs (approx. 5)

PHOTOGRAPHS TAKEN:
- Macro-invertebrate habitat (5 photos)
- Water sampling site (2 photos)

CHALLENGES:
- Water murky in one pool; moved to clearer section
- Lost one collection net; used backup

TEAM NOTES:
- All team members engaged and enthusiastic
- No injuries or issues
```

### Photography & Video

- High-quality photos of discoveries, team, landscape
- Video documentation of techniques or processes
- Labeled or dated for context
- Backup copies (cloud storage, external drive)

### Specimen Collection

If you collect specimens (plants, rocks, insects, etc.):
- Label with location, date, collector name
- Preserve properly (dried, in alcohol, frozen, etc.)
- Create catalog or database

### Data Recording

If measuring (water quality, temperature, species counts):
- Use standardized forms
- Record units (feet, pounds, Celsius, etc.)
- Double-check calculations
- Note any anomalies

## Documentation Tools

### Field Notebook

**What:** Waterproof or regular notebook with pencil
**When:** Daily, in the field
**Content:** Observations, sketches, measurements

**Format:**
- Use standard pages (numbered)
- Permanent ink or pencil (not pen, which runs in water)
- Leave margin for notes
- One entry per day

### Camera or Smartphone

**What:** Digital camera or smartphone camera
**When:** Throughout expedition
**Content:** Photos of discoveries, team, landscape, techniques

**Best practices:**
- Take lots of photos
- Include scale (ruler, person) in photos of objects
- Label or organize photos by date/location
- Back up to cloud storage daily

### Audio Recorder

**What:** Smartphone voice memo or digital recorder
**When:** During observations
**Content:** Verbal descriptions while in the field, interview quotes, ambient sounds

**Advantages:**
- Faster than writing
- Captures emotions and first impressions
- Can transcribe later

### Video Camera

**What:** Smartphone or action camera
**When:** For key moments and demonstrations
**Content:** How you perform techniques, discoveries, team interaction

**Keep clips short:** 30 seconds – 2 minutes each

### Database or Spreadsheet

**What:** Computer spreadsheet or database program
**When:** After returning from field
**Content:** Organized data for analysis

**Columns might include:**
- Date
- Location/GPS
- Species
- Count
- Water temperature
- pH
- Photo reference
- Notes

## Planning Documentation

Create a documentation plan before the expedition:

**1. Roles:**
- [ ] Primary photographer: _____
- [ ] Primary note-taker: _____
- [ ] Data recorder: _____
- [ ] Video documentarian: _____

**2. Tools:**
- [ ] Field notebooks (how many?)
- [ ] Cameras (film and backup)
- [ ] Recording devices
- [ ] Collection containers & labels
- [ ] Backup storage (external drive, cloud)

**3. Standards:**
- [ ] What goes in daily notebook?
- [ ] Photo labeling system?
- [ ] Specimen labeling system?
- [ ] Measurement recording format?

**4. Schedule:**
- [ ] When do you record observations? (In field? Each evening?)
- [ ] Who processes photos? (Daily? After expedition?)
- [ ] When do you organize data?

## Sharing Your Findings

After your expedition, how will you share what you discovered?

### Presentations to Counselor & Unit

**Verbal presentation:** 10–15 minutes to your Scout unit
- What was your objective?
- Where did you go?
- What did you find?
- What was the most interesting discovery?

**Multimedia presentation:** Slides with photos and data
- Title slide: Your name, objective, location, dates
- Method slides: How you explored, techniques used
- Finding slides: Photos of discoveries with explanations
- Conclusion slide: What you learned

### Written Report

**Length:** 2–5 pages
**Content:**
- Executive summary (half page)
- Objective and methods
- Findings with photos/data
- Conclusion
- Appendix (full data, map, specimen list)

### Social Media or Blog

Share your journey:
- Daily expedition photos and updates
- Post-expedition summary
- Key discoveries and learning

### Scientific Contribution

If your findings are significant:
- Submit data to citizen science platforms (iNaturalist, Zooniverse)
- Send findings to relevant agency (park, university, conservation group)
- Offer your report to local libraries or schools

## Example Documentation Plan

**Local Ecology Survey**

**Before expedition:**
- Notebook prepared with date, location column headers
- Camera and smartphone charged
- Data collection sheet printed
- Specimen labels prepared

**During expedition:**
- Notebook entries each evening (5–10 minutes)
- Photos of each survey location and significant findings
- Water samples collected and labeled
- Data recorded on printed sheets

**After expedition:**
- Photos downloaded, organized by location
- Spreadsheet created with summary data
- Report written (2 pages + photos)
- Presentation prepared for unit
