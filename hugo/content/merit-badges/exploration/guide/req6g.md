Exploration is a team effort. The right people, with the right skills, make the difference between success and failure. This requirement asks you to think about who should be on your team and what skills the leader needs.

## Roles on an Exploration Team

### Expedition Leader

The leader is responsible for overall safety, decision-making, and success of the mission.

**Skills required:**
- Experience with the terrain or activity (hiking, climbing, kayaking, etc.)
- Leadership and decision-making ability
- First aid or wilderness medicine training
- Communication and conflict resolution
- Calm under pressure
- Knowledge of the specific area or environment
- Physical fitness appropriate to the expedition

### Technical Specialists

Depending on your exploration objective, you may need specialists:

- **Scientist/researcher:** Knows how to conduct the investigation, identify species, analyze samples
- **Naturalist:** Expert in local flora/fauna
- **Geologist:** For rock/mineral investigations
- **Biologist:** For ecological surveys
- **Medical person:** First aid/EMT trained
- **Navigator:** Experienced with maps, GPS, route finding

### Support Team Members

General Scouts who contribute to the mission:

- **Helpers:** Assist with data collection
- **Equipment managers:** Keep gear organized and functioning
- **Documenters:** Take photos, write observations, collect data
- **Logistics coordinator:** Handle food, water, camp setup

## Team Composition

**Minimum team size:**
- At least 3 people (so if one gets injured, one can stay with them while one gets help)
- Ideally 4–6 for a backcountry expedition

**Maximum team size:**
- Depends on your objective, but larger teams are harder to manage
- 8–10 is typically a manageable size

**Skills inventory:**
Create a table showing who has what skills:

| Name | Leadership | First Aid | Identification | Navigation | Strength | Experience |
|------|-----------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|----------|------------|
| You | High | CPR trained | Moderate | Moderate | Good | Hiking |
| Friend A | Moderate | Basic | High | Excellent | Excellent | Climbing |
| Friend B | Low | Low | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate | Hiking |
| Adult leader | High | WFA trained | High | High | Moderate | Multiple |

**Identify gaps:** Do you need someone with a specific skill? Can someone learn before the expedition?

## Skills Essential for the Expedition Leader

### Planning & Organization

- Create detailed expedition schedule
- Budget and cost management
- Obtain permits
- Arrange logistics (transportation, accommodations, meals)
- Create contingency plans

### Technical Leadership

- Navigate with map and compass (and GPS backup)
- Know the terrain or activity (climbing, kayaking, etc.)
- Recognize and respond to hazards
- Make sound decisions under stress

### People Management

- Communicate clearly with team
- Listen to team input
- Make decisions fairly
- Motivate team members
- Manage conflict
- Ensure everyone is physically and mentally safe

### Safety & Medical

- First aid training (ideally WFA or higher)
- Know when to evacuate
- Manage emergencies calmly
- Know limits of team and terrain

### Environmental/Scientific Knowledge

- Understand your objective (what you are trying to discover)
- Know the area (plants, animals, geology, history)
- Be able to teach team members
- Recognize significant findings

## Selection Process

How do you choose who goes?

**Consider:**

1. **Volunteer interest** — Who actually wants to go?
2. **Physical fitness** — Can they handle the physical demands?
3. **Skill set** — Do they bring skills you need?
4. **Reliability** — Will they show up prepared and on time?
5. **Team compatibility** — Will they work well with others?
6. **Commitment** — Will they help with planning and preparation?

**Be honest:**

- If someone is not physically fit enough, work with them to get fit, or choose someone else
- If someone is unreliable, do not put them in a critical role
- If someone lacks necessary skills, provide training before the expedition

## Preparing Your Team

**Before the expedition:**

1. **Hold planning meetings** — Get team input on plans
2. **Do practice activities** — Hike together, practice rope skills, etc.
3. **Training** — Teach skills team members lack (map reading, first aid, species ID)
4. **Clear communication** — Review the safety plan, roles, and expectations
5. **Physical conditioning** — Train together if needed
6. **Trial runs** — Do a shorter, similar expedition first if possible

**Sample preparation schedule:**

- **8 weeks out:** First planning meeting, define objective
- **6 weeks out:** Finalize permits, equipment, budget
- **4 weeks out:** Physical training begins, skill training
- **2 weeks out:** Final planning meeting, review safety procedures
- **1 week out:** Equipment check, final confirmations
- **Day before:** Final briefing, gather at starting point

## Leadership Quality Checklist

Is your leader prepared?

- [ ] Has done similar expeditions before
- [ ] Has first aid/WFA certification
- [ ] Can navigate with map and compass
- [ ] Is physically fit for the terrain
- [ ] Communicates clearly with team
- [ ] Has contingency plans for emergencies
- [ ] Has written detailed expedition plan
- [ ] Has counselor approval for the plan
- [ ] Knows how to use all technical equipment
- [ ] Has experience making decisions under stress
