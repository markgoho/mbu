{{< drg/requirement number="5a" >}}
With your parent or guardian's permission and counselor's approval, visit either in person or via the internet an exploration sponsoring organization (such as The Explorers Club, National Geographic Society, Smithsonian Institution, American Alpine Club, World Wildlife Fund, or similar organization). Find out what type(s) of exploration the organization supports.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Organizations around the world work to support, fund, and conduct exploration. This requirement asks you to visit one and learn how they operate.

## Organizations That Support Exploration

### The Explorers Club (explorers.org)

**Mission:** Support geographic, scientific, and technical exploration

**Founded:** 1904

**What they do:**
- Fund exploration expeditions worldwide
- Provide grants and sponsorships for explorers
- Publish exploration research
- Host lectures and events featuring explorers

**Types of exploration they support:** Mountain climbing, polar exploration, geographic discovery, scientific research expeditions, underwater exploration

### National Geographic Society (nationalgeographic.org/society)

**Mission:** Inspire people to care about Earth

**Founded:** 1888

**What they do:**
- Fund exploration and research projects
- Produce documentaries and magazines about exploration
- Support conservation expeditions
- Run education programs

**Types of exploration they support:** Geographic exploration, wildlife research, archaeology, marine exploration, climate science, conservation

### Smithsonian Institution (si.edu)

**Mission:** Increase and diffuse knowledge

**Founded:** 1846

**What they do:**
- Operate 19 museums and 21 research centers
- Conduct scientific research expeditions
- Preserve specimens and artifacts
- Support scientific researchers

**Types of exploration they support:** Archaeology, paleontology, natural history, cultural research, anthropology, zoology

### World Wildlife Fund (worldwildlife.org)

**Mission:** Conserve nature and reduce threats to wildlife

**Founded:** 1961

**What they do:**
- Fund conservation expeditions studying endangered species
- Protect habitats
- Research wildlife populations
- Work with indigenous communities

**Types of exploration they support:** Wildlife research, conservation biology, habitat assessment, species discovery, climate impact studies

### American Alpine Club (americanalpineclub.org)

**Mission:** Promote knowledge of mountains, climbing, and the mountain environment

**Founded:** 1902

**What they do:**
- Give grants to mountain explorers and climbers
- Document climbing history and research
- Support mountaineering education
- Advocate for conservation of mountain environments

**Types of exploration they support:** Mountain exploration, climbing expeditions, geological research in mountain regions, mountaineering education

## How to Visit (Online or In Person)

### Online Visit

Most organizations maintain websites with information about their missions, projects, and impact. To conduct an online visit:

1. **Go to their website** and explore their expedition/research section
2. **Look for active projects** — What expeditions are they currently supporting?
3. **Read about recent discoveries** — What have explorers funded by this organization discovered recently?
4. **Look for educational resources** — Many have documentaries, articles, or videos you can watch
5. **Look at their funding process** — How do they decide which explorers to support?

### In-Person Visit

If you can visit in person:
- **Museums** (Smithsonian, natural history museums) often have exhibits about exploration and expeditions
- **Headquarters visits** — Some organizations allow groups to tour their facilities. Call ahead to ask.
- **Public lectures** — Many organizations host talks by explorers. Check their events calendar.

## What to Find Out

When you visit, gather information about:

1. **What is the organization's mission?** What do they care about?

2. **What types of exploration do they support?** (Geographic, scientific, conservation, etc.)

3. **How do they choose which explorers to fund?** Do they have a grant process? What are they looking for?

4. **What recent expeditions have they supported?** Pick one and learn details about it.

5. **How do they share what explorers discover?** (Documentaries, magazines, educational programs, etc.)

6. **What impact do their explorations have?** Have any discoveries led to conservation efforts? Policy changes? Scientific breakthroughs?

## Sample Organizations & What to Look For

**If you visit The Explorers Club:**
- Read about their recent grant recipients — what expeditions did they fund?
- Look at their archives — they have records of explorations dating back to 1904
- Find an expedition that interests you and learn the details

**If you visit National Geographic:**
- Browse their Explorers program — see who they are currently funding
- Watch one of their documentaries about an exploration project
- Read articles about recent discoveries

**If you visit the Smithsonian:**
- Tour a museum related to exploration (Natural History, Air & Space, American History)
- Look at exhibits about paleontology, archaeology, or natural history expeditions
- Learn how they fund scientific research

**If you visit World Wildlife Fund:**
- Look at their conservation projects around the world
- Find information about endangered species research
- Learn about a habitat conservation expedition

---

## Report Back to Your Counselor

Be prepared to share:

1. **Which organization you visited and why you chose it**

2. **What you learned about their mission and work**

3. **A specific example of an exploration project** they support — Give details about what explorers did and what they discovered

4. **How their work impacts the world** — Does it lead to conservation? Scientific knowledge? Policy change?

5. **Your reflection** — What impressed you about their work? Would you be interested in supporting similar exploration?
