{{< drg/requirement number="8" >}}
Go on an Expedition. Complete the following:

a. With your parent or guardian's permission and under the supervision of your counselor or a counselor-approved qualified person, use the planning steps you learned in requirement 6 and the preparations you completed in requirement 7 to personally undertake an actual expedition to an area you have not previously explored.

b. Discuss with your counselor about the vital role of outdoor ethics in exploration, explaining how they promote responsible and mindful adventuring. Explain how you applied the outdoor code, Leave No Trace and Tread Lightly! principles during your expedition.

c. After you return, compile a report on the results of your expedition and how you accomplished your objective(s). Include a statement of the objectives, note your findings and observations, include photos, note any discoveries, report any problems or adverse events, and have a conclusion (whether you reached your objective or not). The post-expedition report must be at least one page and no more than three; one page can be photos, graphs, or figures.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This is it — the main event. You have planned and prepared. Now you go out into the world, conduct your exploration, document what you discover, and return with a report on what you learned.

## During the Expedition

### Day-to-Day Operations

**Each morning:**
- Team briefing on day's objectives and safety
- Assign documentation roles
- Verify all equipment is functional
- Review route and safety protocols

**During field work:**
- Follow your planned schedule
- Maintain safety procedures
- Document observations in notebooks
- Take photographs
- Collect data as planned
- Stay together as a team
- Maintain communication

**Each evening:**
- Return to camp safely
- Process specimens/samples
- Enter data into spreadsheet
- Download photos and back up
- Document any problems or adjustments
- Team briefing on next day
- Rest and prepare for tomorrow

### Handling Challenges

**Equipment fails:**
- Use backup or repair
- Adapt and continue
- Document the problem and solution

**Weather turns bad:**
- Execute safety protocol
- Consider sheltering or turning back
- Prioritize safety over objectives

**Someone gets injured:**
- Provide first aid
- Evaluate severity
- Consider evacuation if needed
- Document incident

**Objective harder than expected:**
- Adjust scope if needed
- Change focus to achievable goals
- Document modified objectives

**Unexpected discovery:**
- Record it thoroughly
- Take photos
- Collect evidence if appropriate
- Share with team

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Safety is more important than reaching your objective. If conditions become unsafe, turn back or shelter in place. A successful expedition returns everyone safely, even if you did not accomplish everything planned.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Outdoor Ethics During Your Expedition

Before you go, understand outdoor ethics. These principles guide responsible exploration.

### Leave No Trace

The Leave No Trace principles are:

1. **Plan Ahead and Prepare:** Minimize your impact by planning routes, camping away from water sources, and using established campsites when possible.

2. **Travel and Camp on Durable Surfaces:** Stay on trails. Camp on established sites or bare ground. Avoid damaging vegetation.

3. **Dispose of Waste Properly:** Pack out all trash. Use established toilets. Bury or pack out human waste following local regulations.

4. **Leave What You Find:** Do not take rocks, plants, or artifacts unless permitted by law. Do not build cairns or modify natural features.

5. **Minimize Campfire Impacts:** Use a camp stove instead of open fires when possible. If you must use fire, use established fire rings. Keep fires small.

6. **Respect Wildlife:** Observe animals from a distance. Do not feed wildlife. Store food properly to prevent wildlife attraction.

7. **Be Considerate of Others:** Keep noise low. Respect other visitors' experiences. Yield on trails.

### Outdoor Code (BSA)

As a Scout, you already live by this code:

**"As an American, I will do my best to be physically strong, mentally awake, and morally straight, and to be respect for others as people deserve, to be responsible for my own well-being and actions, to seek wisdom and self-improvement, to practice good citizenship, and to care for, protect, and improve the world around me and understand my role as a global citizen."**

In exploration, this means:
- Respect the land and the life it supports
- Leave no damage or litter
- Respect local people and cultures if exploring in inhabited areas
- Use your skills responsibly
- Share your knowledge responsibly

### Tread Lightly!

The Tread Lightly! principles apply especially to off-trail exploration:

1. **Travel and camp responsibly:** Avoid damaging vegetation, soil, and water sources.
2. **Respect wildlife:** Do not disrupt animals or habitats.
3. **Respect others:** Respect private property and other users.
4. **Educate yourself:** Know regulations and best practices for your specific area.
5. **Plan ahead:** Proper planning reduces environmental impact.

## How You Applied Ethics

Think about how you followed these principles:

**Example reflection:**

*"During our stream ecology survey, we applied Leave No Trace by camping 200 feet from the stream (as required), using a camp stove instead of a fire, and packing out all waste. We accessed the stream only at established crossing points to minimize streambank erosion. We took photographs of specimens instead of collecting them unnecessarily, only gathering a few for identification. We left all rocks and plants undisturbed. We observed wildlife (deer, birds) from a distance without disturbing them. We left our camp area cleaner than we found it."*

## Post-Expedition Report

After returning, compile a report on your expedition:

### Report Structure

**Title:** Name of expedition and date

**Objective Statement:** (1 paragraph)
- What you hoped to discover or accomplish
- Where you went
- When you went

**Methods:** (1 paragraph)
- How you explored
- Techniques used
- Team members

**Findings:** (1–2 pages)
- What you discovered
- Data collected
- Species identified
- Observations made
- Photos and figures

**Discoveries:** (short section)
- The most interesting/significant finding
- Unexpected discoveries
- New questions raised

**Problems or Adverse Events:** (if applicable)
- Equipment issues resolved
- Safety incidents and how you handled them
- Weather challenges
- Modifications to your plan

**Conclusion:** (1 paragraph)
- Did you accomplish your objectives? Why or why not?
- What did you learn about exploration?
- What would you do differently next time?
- How will you share your findings?

### Report Format

- **Length:** 1–3 pages (plus one page of photos/figures)
- **Written neatly** or typed
- **Photos included** (at least 5–10 high-quality images)
- **Data presented** (maps, graphs, tables, if applicable)
- **Professional presentation** (as if reporting to your community)

### Sample Report Outline

```
TITLE: Macro-Invertebrate Survey of Bear Creek, July 15–17, 2024

OBJECTIVE
In July 2024, we conducted an ecological survey of Bear Creek to
catalog macro-invertebrate species and assess water quality at three
survey sites. We aimed to determine whether water quality supports
healthy aquatic communities.

METHODS
We selected three sites: upstream (above town), midstream (town area),
and downstream (below town). At each site, we collected samples using
nets and measured water temperature, pH, and dissolved oxygen.

FINDINGS
[Two paragraphs describing findings, supported by data table and photos]

DISCOVERIES
The most significant finding was a high count of mayfly nymphs
upstream but low counts downstream, suggesting pollution from town
affects aquatic communities.

CONCLUSION
We successfully completed our objective. We documented a clear decline
in aquatic health moving downstream, likely due to pollution. These
findings align with ongoing concerns about stream quality in the area.
```
