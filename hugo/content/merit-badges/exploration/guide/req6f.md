{{< drg/requirement number="6f" >}}
Establish safety and first aid procedures (including planning for medical evacuation). Identify the hazards that explorers could encounter on the expedition, and establish procedures to prevent or avoid those hazards.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Safety is non-negotiable. Before you explore, you must identify potential hazards and have plans to prevent or respond to them. This requirement is about staying alive and healthy.

## Identify Hazards Specific to Your Expedition

Every environment has different hazards. Think systematically:

### Terrain Hazards

- **Steep slopes:** Fall risk, scrambling difficulty
- **Water:** Drowning, hypothermia, currents
- **Caves:** Getting lost, low oxygen, hypothermia, falling rocks
- **Desert:** Dehydration, heat exhaustion, lack of water sources
- **Dense vegetation:** Getting disoriented, cuts from thorns/plants, insects

### Weather Hazards

- **Cold:** Hypothermia (occurs faster than you think)
- **Heat:** Heat exhaustion, dehydration, sun exposure
- **Lightning:** Strike risk (especially on exposed ridges)
- **Rain:** Flash floods (especially in canyons), hypothermia, slippery surfaces
- **Wind:** Blown off cliffs, knocked down, difficulty with balance

### Wildlife Hazards

- **Large animals:** Bears, mountain lions, moose, wild horses
- **Venomous animals:** Snakes, scorpions, spiders (region-dependent)
- **Insects:** Ticks (Lyme disease risk), mosquitoes (disease vectors), wasps, ants
- **Disease-carrying organisms:** Water-borne pathogens, parasites

### Human Factors

- **Inexperience:** Not knowing proper technique
- **Fatigue:** Poor decisions when tired
- **Separation from group:** Getting lost, isolated
- **Equipment failure:** Rope breaks, map lost, headlamp dies
- **Miscommunication:** Team doesn't understand safety plan

## Hazard-Specific Prevention Plans

### Water Exploration (stream, lake, ocean)

**Hazards:** Drowning, hypothermia, currents, slippery surfaces, dehydration

**Prevention:**
- Everyone wears a life jacket or PFD
- No one swims alone
- Know how to swim or have strong swimmers leading
- Do not enter water if tired or cold
- Check weather for flash flood risk
- Bring extra clothes (hypothermia happens even in summer)
- Establish a "stay together" protocol
- Never enter water in high water/flood conditions

**First aid:** CPR training for all team members

### Backcountry/Wilderness Exploration

**Hazards:** Hypothermia, dehydration, injury far from help, getting lost, wildlife encounters

**Prevention:**
- Carry map and compass (not just phone GPS)
- Stay on established routes or mark your route clearly
- Carry 3L+ water per person
- Bring extra insulating layers (even in summer)
- Establish a "turn-back time" — if you have not reached a destination by X time, you turn back
- Know wildlife in the area and carry bear spray if appropriate
- Never hike alone; stay with your group
- Carry a first aid kit and emergency shelter

**First aid:** Blister treatment, wound care, hypothermia response

### Rock/Mountain Exploration

**Hazards:** Falls, loose rock, lightning, hypothermia, disorientation

**Prevention:**
- Only climb terrain appropriate to your skill level
- Wear helmet if climbing or scrambling
- Use ropes if there is fall risk
- Turn back if weather deteriorates
- Do not climb during thunderstorms
- Establish anchor points if climbing
- Have a turnaround time

**First aid:** Fracture stabilization, spinal injury awareness, descent planning for injured member

### Cave Exploration

**Hazards:** Getting lost, low oxygen, hypothermia, falling, water (floods or hypothermia)

**Prevention:**
- NEVER enter a cave alone
- Mark your route with chalk or tape
- Carry at least 3 headlamps per person
- Know the cave layout (map or guide)
- Check for flooding risk
- Stay together as a group
- Do not enter if tired or ill-prepared
- Hire a cave guide for unfamiliar caves

**First aid:** Hypothermia response, wound care, evacuation plan

### Research/Environmental Hazards

**Hazards:** Contamination, chemical exposure, biological hazards

**Prevention:**
- Wear gloves when handling samples
- Use hand sanitizer after contact with water/soil
- Know what pathogens may be present (waterborne illness, parasites)
- Follow biohazard protocols for biological samples
- Assume all water sources are contaminated; treat or filter
- Boil or filter drinking water

**First aid:** Wound care, poison control access

## Medical Evacuation Plan

What if someone is seriously injured or ill and cannot walk out?

**Establish before the expedition:**

1. **Where is the nearest road?** How far away?
2. **Can a vehicle reach your site?** (Road/trail accessibility)
3. **Nearest medical facility:** Hospital location and distance
4. **Emergency contacts:** Names and numbers for local rescue
5. **Helicopter evacuation possible?** (Only in serious emergencies; very expensive)
6. **Cell phone coverage?** Will 911 work?
7. **Evacuation route:** Most direct way out

**Pre-trip checklist:**
- [ ] Know location of nearest trailhead/road
- [ ] Know distance to nearest medical facility
- [ ] Have emergency numbers written down
- [ ] Inform rescue services of your planned area (if backcountry)
- [ ] Have satellite communicator or PLB if truly remote
- [ ] Practice evacuation procedure with team

**In an emergency:**
- Call 911 immediately if possible
- Provide exact location (GPS coordinates, landmarks, or map references)
- Describe the emergency clearly
- Do NOT move injured person unless there is immediate danger
- Keep injured person warm and calm
- Prepare for a long wait; rescuers may take hours to arrive

## First Aid Kit Essentials

Your first aid kit should include:

**Basic wound care:**
- Antibiotic ointment
- Bandages (variety of sizes)
- Gauze pads
- Adhesive tape
- Elastic bandage (for sprains)

**Pain & sickness:**
- Pain reliever (ibuprofen, acetaminophen)
- Anti-nausea medication
- Anti-diarrhea medication
- Acid reflux medication (if any team members need it)

**Specialty for your environment:**
- Blister treatment (Moleskin, antibiotic cream)
- Insect repellent
- Sunscreen
- Lip balm
- Tweezers (splinters, tick removal)
- Antihistamine (allergic reactions)

**Tools:**
- Scissors
- Thermometer
- CPR barrier device
- Tweezers
- Gloves

**Mental health:**
- Know team members' medications (asthma inhaler, EpiPen, etc.)
- Keep medications accessible

**Wilderness-specific:**
- Emergency blanket (mylar, retains heat)
- Tourniquet (for severe bleeding)
- Trauma dressing (large wound coverage)

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Ideally, at least one team member should have Wilderness First Aid certification (16-hour course). This is more thorough than basic CPR and covers wilderness-specific injuries.

- [ ] Identified all potential hazards
- [ ] Created prevention plan for each hazard
- [ ] All team members trained on safety procedures
- [ ] First aid kit assembled and checked
- [ ] Medical information for all team members (allergies, medications)
- [ ] Emergency contacts documented
- [ ] Evacuation route identified
- [ ] Communication plan in place
- [ ] Permission from parents/guardians
- [ ] Counselor has reviewed safety plan
