{{< drg/requirement number="6f" >}}
Establish safety and first aid procedures (including planning for medical evacuation). Identify the hazards that explorers could encounter on the expedition, and establish procedures to prevent or avoid those hazards.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

In 1996, eight climbers died on Mount Everest in a single storm — not because they lacked skill, but because their safety plans did not account for deteriorating conditions and rigid turnaround times were ignored. You do not have to be on Everest for safety planning to matter. A twisted ankle on a creek bank two miles from the car is a serious problem if no one knows how to stabilize it or carry the injured person out.

## Hazard Identification

The first step in safety planning is identifying what could go wrong. Every environment has specific hazards, and your job is to think through each one before you encounter it.

### Common Expedition Hazards

**Environmental hazards:**
- Weather changes (sudden storms, temperature drops, heat waves)
- Terrain risks (slippery rocks, steep slopes, unstable ground, water crossings)
- Water hazards (flash floods, swift currents, deep water)
- Wildlife encounters (venomous snakes, stinging insects, bears, territorial animals)
- Plant hazards (poison ivy, poison oak, thorny vegetation)

**Human hazards:**
- Dehydration and heat illness
- Hypothermia
- Allergic reactions (insect stings, food allergies)
- Cuts, sprains, fractures from slips and falls
- Getting lost or separated from the group
- Fatigue and poor decision-making from exhaustion

**Equipment hazards:**
- Tool-related injuries (rock hammers, collection knives)
- GPS or communication device failure
- Inadequate lighting after dark

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Identify every team member's medical conditions, allergies, and medications before the expedition. Keep this information written down (with privacy protections) and accessible to the expedition leader. An allergic reaction in the field is an emergency — knowing about it in advance could save a life.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Prevention Procedures

For each hazard, establish a prevention procedure. The goal is to avoid the hazard entirely whenever possible.

| Hazard | Prevention |
|--------|-----------|
| Heat illness | Carry adequate water, take breaks in shade, wear sun protection, know the signs of heat exhaustion |
| Getting lost | Carry map and compass, use GPS with backup, stay on planned route, use buddy system |
| Wildlife encounter | Make noise on trail, store food properly, know local species and behaviors |
| Slip/fall injury | Wear appropriate footwear, use trekking poles on steep terrain, avoid wet rocks |
| Severe weather | Check forecast before departure, set turnaround time, identify shelter locations on route |
| Allergic reaction | Carry EpiPens for known allergies, carry antihistamines, know locations of nearest medical facilities |

## First Aid Procedures

Your expedition must have a first-aid plan that covers three scenarios:

### Minor Injuries

Blisters, small cuts, insect bites, mild scrapes. Treat on-site with your first-aid kit and continue the expedition if the person can safely do so. Cross-reference the skills you learned in the [First Aid](/merit-badges/first-aid/) merit badge — they apply directly here.

### Moderate Injuries

Sprains, deeper cuts, mild heat exhaustion, twisted ankles. Stabilize the injury, assess whether the person can walk out, and decide whether to continue or abort the expedition. A moderate injury should trigger a team discussion about next steps.

### Serious Injuries or Emergencies

Fractures, severe bleeding, anaphylaxis, heat stroke, loss of consciousness. Activate your emergency plan immediately: call 911 (or use your satellite communicator), provide first aid within your training, and prepare for evacuation.

## Medical Evacuation Planning

Before you leave, know the answers to these questions:

{{< drg/checklist title="Evacuation Planning" subtitle="Know these before you step into the field" >}}
- Where is the nearest hospital or urgent care facility?
- What is the fastest route from your expedition site to that facility?
- Where is the nearest road accessible by ambulance?
- What is the phone number for the local ranger station or land management office?
- Do you have cell service at your site, or do you need a satellite communicator?
- Can an injured person be walked out, or would they need to be carried?
- Who on your team has first-aid training?
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
Walk through a hypothetical emergency with your team before the expedition. "If someone breaks an ankle at Site 2, what do we do?" Rehearsing the response makes everyone calmer and more effective if a real emergency occurs.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## The SAFE Checklist

Scouting America provides a SAFE Checklist that you will use in Requirement 7c for your pre-expedition check. SAFE stands for:

- **S** — Is the activity **S**upervised by a qualified adult?
- **A** — Is the activity **A**ge-appropriate for all participants?
- **F** — Does the activity meet Scouting America's safety guidelines (**F**itness)?
- **E** — Does the setting meet environmental and safety standards (**E**quipment and environment)?

You will walk through this checklist with your counselor before your expedition.
