{{< drg/requirement number="4a" >}}
Learn about a living explorer. Create a short report or presentation (verbal, written, or multimedia slide presentation) on this individual's objectives and the achievements of one of the explorer's expeditions. Share what you have learned with your counselor and unit.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This option asks you to pick a real, living explorer; research their life and work; and share what you learn. Your presentation should focus on one specific expedition, not their entire life story.

## How to Approach This

### Step 1: Pick an Explorer

Choose an explorer whose work interests you. Here are some living explorers actively exploring today:

**Geographic & Adventure Explorers:**
- **Levison Wood** — British explorer famous for "walking nations." He walked the entire length of the Nile River (4,650 miles), the Himalayas, the Americas, and Arabia. His expeditions combine tourism, cultural immersion, and documentary filmmaking.
- **Kilian Jornet** — Spanish mountaineer and ski mountaineer who holds the speed record for climbing Mount Everest (without supplemental oxygen) and is exploring vertical speed mountaineering.

**Ocean & Marine Explorers:**
- **Sylvia Earle** — Marine biologist and explorer with over 7,000 hours of diving. She holds the record for deepest solo ocean dive (1,000 meters/3,280 feet). She is exploring deep-sea ecosystems and advocating for ocean protection.
- **Robert Ballard** — Legendary oceanographer who discovered the Titanic, explored hydrothermal vents, and continues exploring submerged shipwrecks.

**Wildlife & Conservation Explorers:**
- **Jane Goodall** — Primatologist who revolutionized our understanding of chimpanzees through decades of observation in Gombe, Tanzania. At 90, still actively advocating for conservation and animal welfare.
- **Steve Backshall** — British naturalist and explorer who leads expeditions to understand and protect endangered species. Known for climbing the "volcanic seven summits."

**Cave & Underground Explorers:**
- **Bill Stone** — American cave explorer and engineer. He has pushed the deepest underwater cave diving boundaries, exploring the Pozo Azul in Mexico and Bushman's Hole in South Africa.

**Polar Explorers:**
- **Ranulph Fiennes** — British explorer considered the "greatest living explorer." Completed the first pole-to-pole expedition, multiple Antarctic crossings, and continues expeditions into his 80s.

### Step 2: Research Your Explorer

Find credible sources about your explorer:

- **Documentaries and videos** — National Geographic, TED talks, YouTube interviews
- **Books and autobiographies** — Many explorers write detailed accounts of their expeditions
- **Official websites and social media** — Many explorers maintain websites or social media with expedition updates
- **News articles and interviews** — Recent interviews, magazine profiles, news coverage of their expeditions
- **Scientific publications** — If your explorer is also a scientist, they may have published research papers

{{< drg/tip >}}
Look for **recent expeditions** — your explorer's current or very recent work. Older expeditions are fine, but something from the last 5–10 years shows what explorers are investigating today and what methods they are using now.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### Step 3: Focus on ONE Expedition

Do not try to cover your explorer's entire life. Instead, pick **one expedition** and go deep:

**Information to gather:**
- **What was the objective?** What question was the explorer trying to answer? What did they hope to discover?
- **Where and when?** Where did the expedition take place? How long did it last?
- **Methods and challenges:** How did they travel? What obstacles did they face? What equipment did they use?
- **Key discoveries:** What did they find or observe? What was surprising?
- **Impact:** How did this expedition contribute to science, conservation, or human knowledge?

**Example research structure for Jane Goodall's Gombe Chimpanzee Research:**
- **Objective:** Understand chimpanzee behavior in the wild and challenge assumptions about them
- **Location/Time:** Gombe Stream National Park, Tanzania (beginning 1960)
- **Methods:** Long-term field observation, careful note-taking, habituating wild chimpanzees to human presence
- **Key discoveries:** Chimpanzees use tools, have complex social structures, experience emotions, engage in warfare
- **Impact:** Revolutionized primatology; changed legal protections for animals; influenced conservation

### Step 4: Create Your Presentation

Your presentation can be:
- **Written report** (2–4 pages, clearly organized)
- **Verbal presentation** (5–10 minutes, speaking to your counselor and/or unit)
- **Multimedia presentation** (slides, video, infographic)

**What to include:**
1. **Introduction:** Who is your explorer and why did you choose them?
2. **The expedition:** What was the objective? When/where did it happen?
3. **Methods & challenges:** How did they explore? What made it difficult?
4. **Discoveries:** What did they find? What was most interesting?
5. **Impact:** How did this expedition matter? What did it teach us?
6. **Reflection:** What did you learn about exploration from studying this person?

**Example opening:**
"I chose to research Levison Wood because he has walked the length of the Nile River, the Himalayas, and the Americas — all on foot. I want to tell you about his Nile expedition, which took him across 11 countries from the source to the mouth of the river."

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Many explorers' expeditions have generated documentaries. If your explorer has a documentary about them, watching it is an excellent research method. Netflix, National Geographic, and YouTube have many explorer documentaries.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

## Sample Explorers & Expeditions

### Jane Goodall — Gombe Chimpanzee Research

**Objective:** Understand how chimpanzees behave in their natural habitat

**Location/Timeline:** Gombe Stream, Tanzania (1960–present, but initial 10-year study was transformative)

**Key discovery:** Chimpanzees use tools (stripping bark from twigs to fish for termites). This shattered the assumption that tool use was unique to humans.

**Why it mattered:** This discovery forced scientists to rethink what makes us human. If chimpanzees use tools, are they less different from us than we thought? The research sparked decades of animal behavior research and changed how we view animal intelligence.

### Ranulph Fiennes — The Pole-to-Pole Expedition

**Objective:** First uninterrupted expedition from North Pole to South Pole

**Location/Timeline:** North Pole → South Pole (1979–1982, approximately 35,000 miles)

**Key achievement:** Successfully traversed the longest North-South route on Earth, crossing the Equator, the Arctic, and Antarctica.

**Why it mattered:** Demonstrated extraordinary human endurance and logistical planning. Proved that a small team could cross the globe by land, ship, and ice.

{{< drg/video
    title="The World's Greatest Living Explorer — Ranulph Fiennes"
    url="https://www.youtube.com/embed/ePOa2OpRQ2g" >}}

### Levison Wood — Walking the Nile

**Objective:** Walk the entire length of the Nile River from source to mouth

**Location/Timeline:** Burundi (source) to Egypt (mouth), 4,650 miles on foot (2014–2015, about 9 months)

**Key challenge:** Walking through 11 countries, navigating remote terrain, dealing with wildlife, conflict zones, and changing geography as he descended from highlands to lowlands.

**Why it mattered:** Documented the Nile's ecosystems, met diverse cultures along the river, and highlighted the Nile's vulnerability to climate change and human use. Created awareness of the river's importance to Africa.

    url="https://www.youtube.com/embed/MkOV5Ex6puA" >}}

### Sylvia Earle — Deep Ocean Solo Dive

**Objective:** Dive deeper into the ocean solo than anyone had before

**Location/Timeline:** Gulf of Mexico, 1979

**Key achievement:** Solo dive to 1,000 meters (3,280 feet) in a specialized submersible, the Deepstar 4000

**Observation:** Observed bioluminescent organisms, strange deep-sea creatures, and geological features never before seen in detail.

**Why it mattered:** Proved that humans could safely explore deeper parts of the ocean. Sylvia's observations contributed to understanding deep-sea ecosystems and inspired decades of subsequent deep-ocean exploration.

## Before You Share

Review your presentation for:

✓ Clear research — You have facts and can cite your sources

✓ Focus — You concentrate on one expedition, not your explorer's entire life

✓ Specifics — You include dates, locations, objectives, and discoveries

✓ Clarity — Someone listening/reading could understand what the explorer did and why it mattered

✓ Your reflection — You include your own thoughts on what this teaches about exploration

Good luck with your research!
