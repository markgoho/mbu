{{< drg/requirement number="9a" >}}
Explore careers related to this merit badge. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. Your research methods may include—with your parent or guardian's permission—an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Discuss with your counselor both your findings and what about this profession might make it an interesting career.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This option asks you to pick *one* career related to exploration and research it thoroughly. You are looking for realistic information about training, costs, salary, and whether it might be right for you.

## Picking a Career

Choose a career that genuinely interests you. Here are some examples:

### Biologist/Ecologist

**What they do:** Study organisms and ecosystems, often in the field

**Education:** Bachelor's degree (4 years) in biology; many have master's or Ph.D.

**Cost:** Tuition at state university ~$80k–$120k for bachelor's; scholarships available

**Salary:** $40k–$80k starting; more with advanced degrees

**Job duties:** Field research, data collection, analyzing results, writing papers, teaching

**Career advancement:** More education = higher salary and leadership roles

### Field Geologist

**What they do:** Study rocks, minerals, and Earth structure; often working outdoors

**Education:** Bachelor's in geology (4 years); many pursue master's

**Cost:** Similar to biology ~$80k–$120k

**Salary:** $45k–$85k starting; mining companies pay more

**Job duties:** Mapping geology, collecting samples, analyzing minerals, consulting with mining/construction companies

**Career advancement:** Advanced degrees, certifications, moving to leadership roles

### Park Ranger / Wildlife Officer

**What they do:** Manage natural areas, educate public, protect wildlife

**Education:** Bachelor's degree in natural resource management or biology

**Cost:** ~$80k–$120k

**Salary:** $30k–$50k starting (government job); stable benefits

**Job duties:** Patrol areas, give educational programs, protect wildlife, manage trails

**Career advancement:** Supervisory roles, higher salaries with experience

### Archaeologist

**What they do:** Excavate and study artifacts from past human settlements

**Education:** Bachelor's in archaeology (4 years); most have master's or Ph.D.

**Cost:** ~$80k–$150k (Ph.D. programs often have funding)

**Salary:** $35k–$65k starting; varies by employer

**Job duties:** Excavation, artifact analysis, writing research papers, publishing findings

**Career advancement:** Advanced education, publishing research, leading expeditions

### Mountain Guide / Expedition Leader

**What they do:** Lead outdoor expeditions, teach outdoor skills

**Education:** Training and certifications (varies; no standard degree required)

**Cost:** $2k–$10k for certifications

**Salary:** $40k–$70k; varies widely

**Job duties:** Plan and lead trips, teach skills, manage group dynamics, ensure safety

**Career advancement:** Reputation building, specialty certifications, owning your own guide service

## Research Methods

### Internet Research

Search for:
- Career descriptions on Bureau of Labor Statistics (bls.gov)
- Company websites showing job postings and requirements
- University degree program descriptions
- Professional associations (National Association of Geoscientists, Society of American Foresters, etc.)
- Salary databases (Glassdoor, Payscale, Indeed)

### Library Research

- Career reference books
- Educational guidebooks
- Professional journals and magazines
- Newspaper articles about professionals

### Interview with a Professional

Call or email someone in the field and ask:
- "How did you get into this career?"
- "What education was required?"
- "What is a typical day like?"
- "What is the salary range?"
- "What do you enjoy most? Least?"
- "What advice would you give someone starting out?"

### Visit a Workplace

Contact employers and ask to:
- Tour a research facility
- Observe work in progress
- Talk with professionals
- Learn about hiring requirements

## Research Template

Organize your research:

**Career:** _____________

**Primary employer types:** (government, university, private company, etc.)

**Education required:**
- Bachelor's degree in __________
- Master's/Ph.D.? (yes/no, common/rare)
- Other certifications?
- Cost estimate: __________

**Salary:**
- Entry level: __________
- With 10 years experience: __________
- Highest potential: __________

**Job duties:**
- Main activities
- Work environment (office, field, or both)
- Physical demands

**Job prospects:**
- Growing field? Shrinking?
- Expected job openings in next 10 years
- Geographic locations (concentrated or widespread)

**Career advancement:**
- How do people move up?
- What experience leads to higher positions?
- Training or education needed for advancement?

**Interesting aspects:**
- What appeals to you about this career?
- What concerns do you have?
- Would this be a good fit for you? Why or why not?

---

## Discussion with Your Counselor

When you meet with your counselor, discuss:

1. **Why you chose this career** — What interests you about it?

2. **What you learned** — Be ready to share facts about education, salary, job duties

3. **Realistic assessment** — Do you have the aptitude and interest for this path?

4. **Next steps** — What would you need to do to pursue this career? (College preparation, volunteering, etc.)

5. **Alternatives** — Are there related careers that might interest you?

6. **Your reaction** — After researching, is this still appealing to you?
