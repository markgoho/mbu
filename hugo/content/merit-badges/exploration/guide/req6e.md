{{< drg/requirement number="6e" >}}
Determine communication and transportation needs. Plan how to keep in contact with your base or the outside world, and determine how you will communicate with each other on-site.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Staying connected is critical for safety. This requirement asks you to plan two types of communication: (1) **external communication** with people outside your expedition, and (2) **internal communication** among your team.

## External Communication

**Why it matters:** If something goes wrong, someone needs to know where you are and be able to help. Your family and counselor need to know you are safe.

### Communication Methods

**Cell phones:**
- Reliable if you have signal
- Plan for limited or no signal
- Keep spare battery or portable charger
- Have a backup communication method

**Satellite messengers:**
- inReach device or similar
- Send messages when no cell signal
- Cost: $200+ for device + monthly plan
- Good for remote expeditions

**Traditional radio:**
- Handheld walkie-talkies (limited range, 1–10 miles)
- Two-way radios for team communication
- No network required

**Check-in schedule:**
- Daily call/text at a set time
- Emergency contact information with family
- Itinerary left with counselor

### External Communication Plan

Create a communication plan documenting:

1. **Primary method:** Cell phone, satellite messenger, or scheduled check-ins
2. **Backup method:** If primary fails
3. **Check-in schedule:** How often and when (e.g., "Call home at 6 PM each evening")
4. **Emergency contacts:** Phone numbers and email addresses
5. **What information to share:** "We are at location X, all safe, see you at Y time"
6. **How to signal for help:** Who to call, what to say, emergency numbers

**Example Communication Plan:**

- **Primary:** Cell phone check-in daily at 7 PM
- **Backup:** Scheduled satellite text if no cell signal
- **Extreme emergency:** Call 911
- **Emergency contacts:** Mom (555-0100), Dad (555-0101), Counselor (555-0102)
- **Message:** "All safe at [location]. See you [return time]."

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Always leave a detailed itinerary with someone at home. Include: exact location, dates, expected return time, emergency contact plan, and medical information. If you do not check in by a certain time, they should contact authorities.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Internal Communication (On-Site)

How will your team communicate while exploring?

### Short Range

**Walkie-talkies / Two-way radios:**
- Range: 1–10 miles depending on terrain
- Cost: $20–$50 per unit
- Reliable, no network needed
- Good for teams spread out in the field

**Whistles:**
- Universal signal (three blasts = emergency)
- No battery required
- Effective in wilderness

**Hand signals:**
- Visible signals for team spread across an area
- Useful for silent observation (wildlife study)

### Longer Range

**Cell phones:** If you have signal, use them

**Satellite messengers:** Send messages across terrain

### Communication Protocols

Establish clear signals:

- **One whistle blast:** "Check in / answer back"
- **Three whistle blasts:** "Emergency / serious problem"
- **Verbal signal:** "Ready to move?" "All set!" "Returning to camp"

## Transportation Plan

**Getting to and from your site:**

### Local Expeditions (Less than 1 hour away)

- **Parent/adult driver** to transport team
- **Personal vehicles** if team members can drive
- **Scout troop transportation** if available
- **Public transportation** (bus, train)

**Plan:**
- Who drives?
- When do they pick up/drop off?
- What if a vehicle breaks down?
- Backup transportation if primary plan fails

### Remote Expeditions (Several hours away)

- **Extended vehicle trip** (8+ hours)
- **Fly or take a train**
- **Guided service** (outfitter provides transport)

**Plan:**
- Detailed driving directions
- Vehicle maintenance before the trip
- Rest stops and driver rotation
- Fuel and food budget
- Communication of ETA

### On-Site Transportation

How do you move around your exploration area?

- **On foot:** Hiking trails, backpacking
- **By canoe/kayak:** Water-based exploration
- **By mountain bike:** Trail exploration
- **Climbing/scrambling:** Vertical exploration (caves, cliffs)

**Plan each activity:**
- Routes and distances
- Estimated time for each leg
- Difficulty level
- Equipment needed (rope for climbing, paddles for kayaking, etc.)

## Sample Communication & Transportation Plan

**Local Ecology Survey**

**External Communication:**
- Method: Cell phone check-in
- Schedule: Call home at 6 PM each day
- Emergency: 911 for serious issues
- Counselor copy of itinerary

**Internal Communication:**
- Method: Cell phones and whistles (if spread out)
- Signals: 1 whistle = check in, 3 = emergency
- Daily briefing: 7 AM before field work

**Transportation:**
- To site: Parents drive (45 minutes)
- On site: Walking between survey areas (estimated 2 miles/day)
- Return: Parents pick up at 5 PM
