{{< drg/requirement number="7a" >}}
Make your preparations under the supervision of a trained expedition leader, expedition planner, or other qualified adult experienced in exploration (such as a school science teacher, museum representative, or qualified instructor).
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You cannot prepare for an expedition alone. This requirement ensures that an adult with experience oversees your preparation and keeps you on track.

## Who Can Supervise?

**Qualified adults include:**

- **Expedition leader:** Someone who has led similar expeditions before
- **Merit badge counselor:** Experienced in Exploration badge
- **School science teacher:** Especially if your exploration is science-based
- **Museum or research center representative:** If exploring archaeology, paleontology, etc.
- **Outdoor recreation professional:** Hiking guide, climbing instructor, kayak guide
- **University researcher:** If studying ecology or environmental science
- **Scout leader or adult mentor:** Experienced in outdoor activities

**Required qualifications:**
- Experience with the type of exploration you are planning
- First aid or wilderness medicine training (ideally)
- Understanding of safety and risk management
- Ability to teach and guide you
- Willingness to review and approve your plans

---

## What Supervision Looks Like

### Planning Phase

Your supervisor should:

1. **Review your objective** — Is it specific and achievable?
2. **Check your route/location** — Is it appropriate for your skill level?
3. **Approve your permits** — Do you have all necessary permissions?
4. **Review your schedule** — Is the timeline realistic?
5. **Check your budget** — Is it reasonable and complete?
6. **Verify your team** — Are team members capable and prepared?
7. **Review safety plan** — Are hazards identified and mitigated?
8. **Approve equipment list** — Do you have everything needed?
9. **Verify documentation plan** — Will you record findings properly?

### Preparation Phase

Your supervisor should:

1. **Help with logistics** — Contacting agencies, obtaining permits
2. **Guide training** — Ensure team members learn necessary skills
3. **Review final preparations** — Check that you are ready
4. **Do a final briefing** — Review safety procedures one last time
5. **Provide contact info** — You have supervisor's phone number for emergencies

### Expedition Phase

Your supervisor might:

1. **Accompany you** (if appropriate)
2. **Be available by phone** for questions or emergencies
3. **Receive check-in reports** from you
4. **Be ready to help with evacuation** if needed

## Finding a Supervisor

### Ask Your Merit Badge Counselor

Start here. Your counselor knows exploration and may be willing to supervise.

### Contact Your Scout Council

They often have lists of qualified adults experienced in various activities.

### Local Organizations

- University biology/geology/archaeology department
- Local naturalist society
- Museum or nature center
- Parks and recreation department
- Outdoor recreation shops
- Guide companies (rock climbing, kayaking, hiking)

### Interview Process

When you find a potential supervisor, ask:

1. **What expedition experience do you have?**
2. **Have you done this type of exploration before?**
3. **Are you willing to review and approve my plans?**
4. **Will you be available during my expedition?**
5. **What is your background in safety/first aid?**

---

## Working with Your Supervisor

**Be respectful of their time.** They are volunteering their expertise.

**Come prepared to meetings.** Have your plans written down. Ask specific questions.

**Accept their input.** They have experience you lack. If they suggest changes, listen.

**Keep them updated.** Let them know progress on permits, training, logistics.

**Follow their advice.** If they say something is unsafe, trust their judgment.
