{{< drg/requirement number="5" >}}
Exploration in Lab and Field. Do ONE of the following, and share what you learn with your counselor:
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

For this requirement, you choose **one** of two options. Both involve visiting an organization or facility — either in person or online — and learning about the exploration work they do. You need your parent or guardian's permission and your counselor's approval before your visit.

## Option A: Visit an Exploration Organization

Visit (in person or via the internet) an organization that sponsors exploration. Find out what type(s) of exploration the organization supports.

### Organizations to Consider

The requirement suggests several organizations, and there are many more worth exploring:

**The Explorers Club** — Founded in 1904, this is one of the world's most prestigious exploration organizations. Members have included the first people to reach both poles, the summit of Everest, the deepest point in the ocean, and the surface of the Moon. The club funds grants for field research and hosts lectures on expeditions worldwide.

**National Geographic Society** — Funds thousands of scientific research, exploration, and conservation projects every year. Their Explorers program supports people working in every environment on Earth — from deep-sea divers to archaeologists to wildlife biologists.

**Smithsonian Institution** — The world's largest museum, education, and research complex, with 21 museums, the National Zoo, and nine research facilities. Smithsonian scientists conduct exploration in fields ranging from astrophysics to zoology.

**World Wildlife Fund (WWF)** — While primarily a conservation organization, WWF funds and supports exploration that identifies endangered ecosystems, discovers new species, and monitors wildlife populations in remote areas.

**American Alpine Club** — Supports mountaineering expeditions, climbing research, and conservation of mountain environments. Their grant programs fund expeditions that advance climbing knowledge and alpine science.

{{< drg/tip >}}
An online visit can be surprisingly rich. Many of these organizations offer virtual tours, recorded expedition talks, live-streamed research, and detailed project descriptions on their websites. Take notes as if you were physically there — your counselor will want specifics.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### What to Learn During Your Visit

{{< drg/checklist title="Organization Visit Guide" subtitle="Questions to answer during your visit" >}}
- What is the organization's mission?
- What types of exploration does it fund or conduct?
- What environments do its explorers work in?
- How does someone get involved with the organization?
- What is one recent expedition or project the organization supported?
- How does the organization share its findings with the public?
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Option B: Visit a Science Lab or Research Facility

Visit (in person or via the internet) a science lab, astronomical observatory, medical research facility, or similar site. Learn what exploration is done in this facility.

### Facilities to Consider

- **Local university research labs** — Most universities have researchers conducting original investigation. Contact the science department to ask about tours or open-house events.
- **Natural history museums** — Many have research labs behind the public galleries where scientists study specimens, analyze fossils, and classify species.
- **Astronomical observatories** — Facilities like Lick Observatory, Lowell Observatory, and many university observatories offer public programs and virtual tours.
- **National labs** — Facilities like Fermilab, Oak Ridge, and Sandia National Laboratories offer educational programs and virtual tours.
- **Medical research centers** — Hospitals and medical schools often have research facilities that welcome student visitors for educational programs.
- **NASA centers** — Johnson Space Center, Kennedy Space Center, and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory all offer tours and extensive online resources.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
NOAA's Okeanos Explorer livestreams its deep-sea ROV dives to the public in real time. You can watch as scientists discover new species on the ocean floor — from your own home. Viewers can even submit questions that the shipboard scientists answer live during the dive.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

### Making the Most of a Virtual Visit

If you are visiting online, go deeper than just browsing the homepage. Look for:

- **Virtual lab tours** — Many facilities have 360-degree tours or video walkthroughs
- **Published research** — Read about recent projects and what they discovered
- **Researcher profiles** — Learn about the people doing the work and their career paths
- **Live events** — Check for webinars, livestreams, or Q&A sessions

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Presenting Your Visit to Your Counselor" >}}
After your visit, prepare to discuss:

- **What facility or organization you visited** and how you accessed it
- **What exploration work they do** — be specific about projects, not just mission statements
- **What surprised you** — what did you learn that you did not expect?
- **How their work connects to the exploration concepts** you learned in Requirements 1–3
- **Whether their work is more like field exploration or lab exploration** — and why
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}
