{{< drg/requirement number="4" >}}
Real-Life Exploration. Do ONE of the following:

a. Learn about a living explorer. Create a short report or presentation (verbal, written, or multimedia slide presentation) on this individual's objectives and the achievements of one of the explorer's expeditions. Share what you have learned with your counselor and unit.

b. Learn about an actual scientific exploration expedition. Gather information about the mission objectives and the expedition's most interesting or important discoveries. Share what you have learned with your counselor and unit. Tell how the information gained from this expedition helped scientists answer important questions.

c. Learn about types of exploration that may take place in a laboratory or scientific research facility (medicine, biology, chemistry, physics, astronomy, etc.). Explain to your counselor how laboratory research and exploration are similar to field research and exploration.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This requirement asks you to move from theory to reality. You have learned what exploration is and why it matters. Now it is time to study actual explorers and expeditions — real people asking real questions and making real discoveries. Choose **one** of the three options below and dive deep.

## Option Overview

### Option A: Profile a Living Explorer

Choose a real explorer working today, research their objectives and accomplishments, and create a presentation (written, verbal, or multimedia) on their work. This option teaches you about individual explorers and gives you practice in communicating what you learn.

**Why choose this option:** If you want to learn about the personality, methods, and philosophy of a single explorer. You will discover what drives them, how they plan expeditions, and what challenges they face.

**Who are some living explorers to research?**
- **Jane Goodall** — Primatologist who pioneered the study of wild chimpanzees in Africa. Now 90 years old and still advocating for conservation.
- **Ranulph Fiennes** — British explorer who has completed the first pole-to-pole expedition, crossed the Antarctic unsupported, and summited the highest mountains on every continent.
- **Levison Wood** — British explorer known for walking entire nations (walked the length of the Nile, the Himalayas, and the Americas).
- **Sylvia Earle** — Marine biologist who holds the record for solo deepest ocean dive and is a world-leading voice on ocean conservation.
- **Bear Grylls** — Mountaineer and survival expert who has summited Mount Everest and leads exploration and outdoor survival expeditions.
- **David Attenborough** — Naturalist and broadcaster whose life has been dedicated to exploring and documenting Earth's biodiversity.

[Continue to Option A page for detailed guidance.](req4a)

### Option B: Study a Scientific Expedition

Research a real exploration mission — a paleontology expedition, a space mission, an oceanographic cruise, a climate science campaign. Learn about the objectives, methods, and discoveries. Share what you learned with your counselor.

**Why choose this option:** If you want to understand how large-scale exploration works and how specific discoveries advance science. You will learn about the planning required, the challenges faced, and the impact of real findings.

**What are some famous scientific expeditions to research?**
- **Mars 2020 Perseverance Rover** — NASA's ongoing mission to search for signs of ancient microbial life on Mars and collect samples.
- **Titanic Wreck Exploration** — Robert Ballard's 1985 expedition to locate and explore the wreck of RMS Titanic.
- **Hadron Collider Experiments** — CERN's ongoing physics research smashing particles together to discover fundamental physics.
- **Amazon Expedition** — National Geographic expeditions documenting new species and indigenous cultures in the Amazon.
- **Arctic Climate Research** — International expeditions studying melting glaciers, permafrost thaw, and Arctic ecosystems.

[Continue to Option B page for detailed guidance.](req4b)

### Option C: Explore Laboratory Research

Visit a research laboratory (in person or virtually) or research what laboratory exploration looks like. Understand the techniques, questions, and methods. Compare laboratory research to field research.

**Why choose this option:** If you want to understand that exploration is not limited to wilderness adventures. Laboratory research is systematic, rigorous exploration happening in universities, hospitals, and research institutes worldwide.

**What kinds of labs conduct exploration?**
- **Genetics laboratories** — Sequencing genomes, understanding heredity and disease.
- **Astronomy observatories** — Observing distant galaxies, searching for exoplanets.
- **Medical research facilities** — Developing new treatments and understanding disease mechanisms.
- **Chemical research labs** — Creating new materials, understanding molecular behavior.
- **Neuroscience labs** — Studying the brain and nervous system.

[Continue to Option C page for detailed guidance.](req4c)

### How to Choose

Ask yourself:
- **Do I want to focus on a person?** → Choose Option A
- **Do I want to learn about a specific mission or discovery?** → Choose Option B
- **Do I want to understand how research works in a controlled lab setting?** → Choose Option C

There is no wrong choice. Each option teaches you something different about how exploration works in the modern world. Pick the one that most excites you.
