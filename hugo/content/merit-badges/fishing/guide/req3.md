{{< drg/requirement number="3" >}}
Demonstrate the proper use of two different types of fishing equipment.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Knowing the parts of a fishing outfit is only the beginning. To actually catch fish, you need to master the techniques of using different types of equipment. This requirement asks you to demonstrate proficiency with two different types of fishing equipment — whether that is two different rod-and-reel combinations or different specialized equipment like casting nets or fly rods.

## Spinning Outfit Technique

The spinning outfit is the most popular and most forgiving type of fishing equipment. It is an excellent choice for beginners to learn and demonstrate.

### Setting Up a Spinning Outfit

1. **Attach the reel to the rod** at the reel seat, with the reel hanging below the rod (handle on your preferred side)
2. **Spool the reel with line** — Load 100–150 yards of line onto the spool
3. **Thread the line through each guide**, starting with the guide closest to the reel and working toward the rod tip
4. **Attach your bait or lure** using a secure knot (see Req 4 for knot tying)
5. **Adjust the drag** — Set it so the line will slip if a heavy fish pulls (usually a moderate setting; test by pulling the line by hand)

### Casting with a Spinning Outfit

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Casting with a Spinning Reel" >}}
You are ready to make your first cast with a spinning outfit.

**Step 1: Hold the rod.** Grip the handle with your dominant hand, with your index finger able to reach the line. Your other hand rests on the handle for support.

**Step 2: Open the bail.** Use your other hand to flip the bail open (the wire arm swings away from the rod).

**Step 3: Position your finger on the line.** Hook the fishing line with the index finger of your rod hand. This finger will release the line to cast.

**Step 4: Bring the rod to your ear.** Raise the rod tip to about ear level (roughly a 2 o'clock position). The rod should be smooth and relaxed.

**Step 5: Accelerate smoothly.** Using your forearm and a bit of wrist motion, accelerate the rod tip forward smoothly. Do not jab or punch — smooth acceleration generates distance and accuracy.

**Step 6: Release the line.** As the rod reaches the 10 o'clock position (forward), release the line from your finger. The momentum of the rod carries the bait or lure toward the target.

**Step 7: Close the bail.** Use your other hand to flip the bail closed, or turn the handle and the bail closes automatically.

**Step 8: Retrieve.** Turn the reel handle steadily to retrieve line and your bait.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

### Retrieving with a Spinning Outfit

Once your bait or lure is in the water, you need to retrieve it to reach different depths, cover more water, and trigger bites.

**Types of retrieves:**
- **Steady retrieve** — Turn the handle at a constant speed to cover water methodically
- **Stop-and-go** — Retrieve several turns, then pause and let the bait sink or hover
- **Twitching** — Sharp flicks of the rod tip make the bait jerk and appear alive
- **Jigging** — Lift and drop the rod tip to make the bait move up and down

### Setting the Hook

When a fish bites, you need to "set the hook" — pull sharply to drive the hook point into the fish's mouth.

1. **Feel the bite** — The fish will pull on the line or create a tug
2. **Set immediately** — Quickly raise the rod tip above your head with a strong upward motion
3. **Keep tension** — Maintain pressure on the line to keep the hook in the fish's mouth
4. **Reel steadily** — Begin retrieving line smoothly and consistently

## Fly Fishing Technique

Fly fishing is fundamentally different from spinning or baitcasting. Instead of casting a heavy sinker, you cast the lightweight fly line itself.

### Setting Up a Fly Outfit

1. **Attach the reel** to the rod (usually with the reel below the rod)
2. **Spool the reel** with backing line (thin, strong line that fills the reel), then fly line on top
3. **Attach the leader** (tapered line) to the fly line using a reliable knot
4. **Tie on the fly** using a small, secure knot
5. **Leave 18–24 inches of leader** between the fly line and the fly for natural presentation

### The Fly Casting Motion

Fly casting is an art form. The motion is smooth and controlled, using the rod's flexibility to load and unload energy.

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Making a Basic Fly Cast" >}}
You have a fly outfit set up and you are standing near the water, ready to cast.

**Step 1: Establish a grip.** Hold the rod with your thumb on top of the handle, your fingers wrapped below. Your other hand holds the line lightly.

**Step 2: Start in the ready position.** The rod tip should be just above the water's surface.

**Step 3: Accelerate smoothly backward.** Bring the rod tip up to about 1 o'clock (behind you) using a smooth acceleration. This is the "backcast" — it loads energy into the rod.

**Step 4: Pause at the top.** Stop briefly at the 1 o'clock position. This allows the rod to release its energy and the line to travel backward (you will feel the line pull the rod).

**Step 5: Accelerate forward.** Bring the rod tip forward toward 10 o'clock (forward and up). This "forward cast" sends the fly toward the target.

**Step 6: Follow through.** As the line extends forward, lower the rod tip to the water's surface to prepare for the next cast or to begin your retrieve.

**Step 7: Control with your other hand.** Your non-rod hand manages the fly line, taking in slack or releasing more line as needed.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

### Fly Fishing Presentation

In fly fishing, how you present the fly to the fish is critical.

**Common presentations:**
- **Dry fly** — Float the fly on the surface to imitate insects landing on the water
- **Nymph** — Fish the fly beneath the surface to imitate aquatic insect larvae
- **Streamer** — Use larger, flashy flies to imitate baitfish
- **Dead drift** — Allow the fly to float naturally with the current, mimicking a real insect

## Baitcasting Technique (Brief Overview)

Baitcasting is similar to spinning in many ways but requires more skill to avoid backlashes (tangled line on the spool).

**Key differences:**
- The reel is on top of the rod rather than below
- You control the spool rotation with your thumb to prevent backlashes
- Casting requires a quick release rather than finger control
- Baitcasting allows more precise control and distance with heavier lures

## Demonstrating Equipment Proficiency

When demonstrating your equipment use to your counselor, make sure to show:

{{< drg/checklist title="Equipment Demonstration Checklist" subtitle="Show your counselor that you can:" >}}
- Properly assemble the equipment (attach reel, thread line through guides, attach bait/fly)
- Execute a smooth, controlled cast
- Demonstrate proper grip and stance
- Execute at least two different types of retrieves appropriate to the equipment
- Set the hook properly when a fish bites
- Handle the equipment safely (no wild casts near people, keep hooks under control)
- Care for the equipment properly after use
- Explain what you are doing and why as you demonstrate
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Practice Tips

You do not have to catch fish to demonstrate proper technique. Many anglers practice casting without even going to the water. You can:

- **Practice casting in a yard** — Use a length of rope or yarn tied to your lure instead of an actual hook (safer and just as good for practice)
- **Practice at a park** — Find an open area and practice your casting motion without water
- **Fish at a local pond** — Many ponds are stocked with willing fish that help beginners learn
- **Learn from experienced anglers** — Visit a fishing buddy or local angler and ask to watch and learn

{{< drg/tip >}}
The best way to learn fishing techniques is by doing them repeatedly. Do not expect perfection your first time. Every cast makes you better. Experienced anglers have cast thousands of times — that is why they are skilled.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
