This requirement covers two essential aspects of responsible fishing: understanding Leave No Trace principles and how they protect fishing resources, and mastering the technique of catch-and-release.

## 6a: Leave No Trace & Outdoor Code

{{< drg/requirement number="6a" >}}
Explain the importance of practicing Leave No Trace Seven Principles and the Outdoor Code. Discuss the positive effects of Leave No Trace Seven Principle and the Outdoor Code on fishing resources.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Leave No Trace and the Scout Outdoor Code are not just nice ideas — they are essential to preserving wild places and the fish populations that live in them. Every angler who practices these principles contributes to healthier fisheries for future generations.

### The Seven Leave No Trace Principles

**1. Plan Ahead and Prepare**

When you plan your fishing trip thoughtfully, you minimize your impact on the environment and improve your experience.

- Scout your location in advance so you know where you will fish
- Check regulations and obtain necessary licenses
- Bring maps and know the area so you will not get lost
- Pack all the supplies you need so you will not have to improvise or leave things behind

**Impact on fishing:** Anglers who plan ahead do not unnecessarily trample vegetation that fish depend on for shelter. They do not accidentally destroy nesting areas. Their presence is less disruptive.

**2. Travel and Camp on Durable Surfaces**

Use established campsites and trails rather than creating new impacts.

- Stick to existing trails and paths
- Camp on mineral soil, rock, or established sites rather than on vegetation
- Avoid walking through shallow water where you might damage aquatic plants
- When fishing from shore, use established access points rather than forcing your way through dense vegetation

**Impact on fishing:** Protecting riparian vegetation (plants along the water's edge) preserves fish habitat. Trees and bushes provide shade that keeps water cool, shade for fish, and root systems that stabilize banks and prevent erosion.

**3. Dispose of Waste Properly**

Pack out everything you pack in — and pick up any trash you find.

- Carry a trash bag and remove all your waste
- Do not bury garbage — animals dig it up
- Pack out old fishing line, broken lures, and other fishing waste
- Never pour fish guts or waste back into the water
- Pee and poop away from water sources (at least 200 feet)

**Impact on fishing:** Fishing line in the water tangles and kills fish and other wildlife. Litter degrades water quality and habitat. Waste contaminating water makes it unsafe for humans and fish.

**4. Leave What You Find**

Do not collect natural objects or disturb living things beyond what you catch.

- Do not pick wildflowers or plants
- Do not move rocks unless you are fishing underneath them, and replace them carefully
- Do not disturb bird nests, turtle eggs, or other wildlife
- Do not remove historical or archaeological artifacts

**Impact on fishing:** When you protect plants and the broader ecosystem, you protect the entire food web that fish depend on. The insects, crustaceans, and smaller fish that larger fish hunt all depend on a healthy ecosystem.

**5. Minimize Campfire Impacts**

Keep fires small, use established fire rings, and dispose of ashes properly.

- Use only dead, downed wood
- Keep fires small and manageable
- Never leave a fire unattended
- Fully extinguish fires before moving on
- Scatter cold ashes away from camp

**Impact on fishing:** Campfires near water can contaminate it with ash. Fires require clearing vegetation and creating scars that take years to heal. Where they are necessary, proper fire management is essential.

**6. Respect Wildlife**

Observe animals from a distance and do not feed or harass them.

- Keep a safe distance from waterfowl and other wildlife
- Never attempt to hand-feed ducks, geese, or other animals
- Store food properly so animals do not become dependent on human food
- Respect nesting areas and breeding seasons

**Impact on fishing:** Wildlife around water — otters, mink, herons, cormorants — depend on healthy fish populations. Respecting their habitat means respecting fish habitat.

**7. Be Considerate of Others**

Share the resource and minimize impacts on other people's experience.

- Keep noise levels down
- Yield good fishing spots to other anglers
- Do not cast toward other people
- Pack out and camp responsibly so the area is pleasant for the next person

**Impact on fishing:** When fishing areas are managed thoughtfully and respected, they remain available and healthy for everyone. Overcrowding and disrespect damage the resource and drive away both anglers and fish.

### The Scout Outdoor Code

The Scout Outdoor Code and Leave No Trace are deeply compatible. The code calls for:

- **Respect the rights and property of all persons.** Do not trespass or damage property while accessing fishing areas.
- **Realize that the outdoors is a destination.** Treat it as a special place, not just a backdrop for fishing.
- **Practice good outdoor skills.** Fish skillfully and ethically.
- **Appreciate the beauty of creation.** Fishing in wild places connects us to nature's beauty.
- **Walk softly.** Minimize your impact — fish quietly, move carefully, leave no trace.

## 6b: Catch-and-Release

{{< drg/requirement number="6b" >}}
Discuss the meaning and importance of catch and release. Describe how to properly release a fish safely to the water.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Catch-and-release fishing allows anglers to enjoy the thrill of the catch while letting the fish swim free. When done properly, catch-and-release minimizes harm to fish and helps preserve populations for future fishing opportunities.

### Why Catch-and-Release Matters

**Population Conservation:** Many fish populations are stressed by overfishing. Catch-and-release allows fishing to continue without depleting wild populations.

**Research and Education:** Catch-and-release anglers can research fish populations and behavior. Scientists catch, measure, tag, and release fish to understand populations and migration patterns.

**Enjoyment:** Many anglers find the challenge and skill of catch-and-release fishing more rewarding than keeping fish. The focus is on the experience and connection to nature, not on the harvest.

**Flexibility:** Catch-and-release allows fisheries to remain open to anglers even when populations are delicate. Anglers can practice their skills without damaging the resource.

**Let's Fish Forever:** The philosophy is simple — catch fish today, so fish will be here to catch tomorrow.

### How to Properly Release a Fish

Improper release can injure or kill fish even if you throw them back. Follow these steps to ensure the fish survives and is able to continue its life in the water.

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Properly Releasing a Fish" >}}
You have just caught a beautiful trout and decided to release it so it can be caught again by another angler.

**Step 1: Minimize air exposure.** Keep the fish in water as much as possible. Fish depend on water flowing through their gills to breathe. The moment it comes out of water, it starts to suffocate.

**Step 2: Wet your hands.** Dry hands damage the protective slime coating on a fish's skin. This coating protects against disease and parasites. Wet hands, a wet towel, or a fish net minimizes damage.

**Step 3: Remove the hook carefully.**
- If the hook is shallow and easy to remove, use pliers or your fingers to back it out gently.
- If the hook is deep in the throat, it may be better to cut the line close to the mouth and leave the hook. Fish can often expel or dissolve deeply embedded hooks over time.
- Do not yank or jerk — steady, gentle removal minimizes injury.

**Step 4: Support the fish properly.**
- Hold the fish upright along its belly, not by the gills or eyes.
- Keep it facing the current (if in a river) so water flows through its gills as you hold it.
- Never squeeze or grip tightly — support it gently.

**Step 5: Revive if necessary.** If the fish appears exhausted or has been out of water, place it back in the water facing upstream. Move it gently forward and backward to push water through its gills. This "revives" the fish by getting water flowing through its respiratory system.

**Step 6: Release slowly.** Do not throw the fish back into the water or release it roughly. Gently lower it into the water and let it swim away on its own. A fish released roughly may be injured or disoriented.

**Step 7: Watch it swim away.** Make sure the fish swims away strongly and is able to recover. If it seems lethargic or unwell, you may need to revive it further.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

### Handling Considerations

**Different fish types:** Some fish are hardier than others. Trout are very sensitive to handling and must be treated delicately. Bass are tougher but still benefit from gentle, minimal-contact release. Research your target species.

**Barbless hooks:** Using barbless hooks or crushing the barbs on your hooks makes removal faster and causes less injury. Many catch-and-release anglers fish exclusively with barbless hooks.

**Air exposure time:** Different species tolerate air exposure differently. Trout can be damaged in minutes. Bass can last longer. As a rule, minimize air exposure for all fish.

**Water temperature:** Fish in cold water may recover more slowly. Fish in warm water (above 75°F) experience stress more quickly. Adjust your handling time accordingly — fast and gentle in warm water.

### The Ethics of Catch-and-Release

Catch-and-release is not harm-free — studies show that fish caught and released experience some stress and may suffer minor injuries. However, when done properly, most fish survive and recover fully.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Research suggests that 85–95% of properly released fish survive, depending on the species and handling technique. This means catch-and-release fishing allows sustainable angling that preserves fish populations.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

**Ethical considerations:**
- Is catch-and-release fishing ethical if it causes some harm? Many anglers and conservationists believe it is, because it preserves fish populations and wild places for future generations.
- Some believe catch-and-release is fairer to the fish than keeping and killing it.
- Others argue that fishing for food is ethical if done sustainably and respectfully.

The key principle is **respect for the resource** — whether you keep fish or release them, treat them humanely and fish within regulations designed to protect populations.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
If you are catch-and-release fishing, always carry tools to safely remove hooks (pliers, forceps, or a hook removal tool). Attempting to remove a hook barehanded can result in a hook to your finger, which you now know how to handle — but prevention is better than treatment.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Catch-and-Keep Fishing

While catch-and-release is emphasized in conservation, there is nothing wrong with keeping fish if you follow regulations and plan to eat them. Many Scouts enjoy fishing partly because they can cook and eat their catch.

**If you keep fish:**
- Follow all size and bag limits (see Req 7 — Fishing Regulations)
- Keep only fish you plan to eat
- Dispatch fish humanely with a quick, painless method
- Clean and cook them promptly
- Do not waste — use the entire fish or share with others

**Sustainable fishing:** Keeping fish is sustainable if everyone follows regulations designed by fish biologists to maintain healthy populations.
