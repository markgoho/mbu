{{< drg/requirement number="9" >}}
Catch one fish and identify it.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

All the knowledge in the world means nothing until you actually go fishing and catch a fish. This is the heart of the Fishing merit badge — putting your skills into practice and bringing home your first catch (or your hundredth).

## Preparing for Your Fishing Trip

Before you head to the water, take time to prepare. Success in fishing comes from planning and practice.

### Choose Your Fishing Location

**Where to fish:**
- **Local ponds and lakes** — Often stocked and accessible, great for beginners
- **Small streams** — Beautiful and often productive for trout
- **Rivers** — Larger than streams, home to bass, catfish, and pike
- **Coastal areas** — Saltwater fishing if you live near the coast

**How to choose:**
- Ask local anglers at a tackle shop where they recommend fishing
- Check your state's website for stocked waters (many states list them)
- Visit different locations to find one you like
- Ask permission before fishing private property
- Look for signs of fish: structure (rocks, logs, vegetation), depth changes, moving water

### Choose Your Target Species

Pick a species that is:
- **Abundant in your area** — Common species are more likely to bite
- **Appropriate for your skill level** — Start with panfish or bass, not pike or tarpon
- **Legally fishable** — Check season dates and regulations
- **Available in season** — Do not try to catch summer species in winter

**Good beginner species:**
- Sunfish (bluegill, pumpkinseed) — Easy to catch, forgiving
- Largemouth bass — Abundant, aggressive, fun to catch
- Catfish — Not picky eaters, fight hard
- Trout (in cold-water areas) — Beautiful, skilled challenge

### Gather Your Equipment

You should have:
- **Rod and reel** — Spinning outfit is best for beginners
- **Fishing line** — Fresh monofilament line (6–12 lb test for beginners)
- **Hooks or lures** — Appropriate for your target species
- **Terminal tackle** — Sinkers, bobbers, swivels
- **Net** (optional but helpful) — Makes landing fish easier
- **Pliers or forceps** — For removing hooks
- **Measuring device** — Ensures you keep legal-sized fish
- **First-aid kit** — Always have one nearby
- **Sunscreen and insect repellent** — Protection
- **Food and water** — For your trip
- **Fishing license** — Required in most places

### Practice Your Skills

Before you go fishing:
- Practice casting in your yard with a rope lure
- Tie your knots repeatedly until you can do them quickly
- Review your target species' behavior and habitat

## Catching Your Fish

When you are on the water, remember these principles:

### Fish the Right Way

{{< drg/be-prepared title="A Successful Fishing Trip" >}}
You are at a small lake on a calm morning, ready to catch your first fish.

**Step 1: Get Set Up.** Choose a location where fish are likely to be — near structure (fallen trees, rocks, vegetation). Set up with your rod ready and tackle organized.

**Step 2: Make Your Presentation.** Cast your bait or lure toward the structure. Let it settle and present it naturally. If using bait, be patient — fish take time to find and bite.

**Step 3: Be Alert.** Watch your line or bobber for any sign of a bite. A bite might be a tug, a pull, or a gentle nudge. Stay alert and ready.

**Step 4: Set the Hook.** The moment you feel a bite, raise your rod sharply to drive the hook into the fish's mouth. Do not jerk too hard — you just need to set it firmly.

**Step 5: Play the Fish.** Once hooked, keep tension on the line while you reel. Do not let the line go slack — a slack line allows the fish to spit the hook. Reel and gain line smoothly. If the fish runs, let it take line (let the drag slip) rather than risk breaking your line.

**Step 6: Bring It In.** As the fish gets closer, keep reeling. Keep the rod tip up (high) to maintain pressure. Do not lower the rod tip suddenly or the fish might escape. When the fish is close, you can guide it toward you.

**Step 7: Land It.** Use a net if you have one — it makes landing much easier and reduces damage to the fish. If no net, prepare to hand-land the fish by supporting it firmly under the belly and gills. Be ready — a hooked fish can thrash.

**Step 8: Secure Your Prize.** The moment it is in your net or hands, you have successfully caught a fish. Now comes identification.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

### Be Patient

The biggest mistake beginning anglers make is changing locations or lures too quickly.

- **Stay patient.** Fish do not bite immediately. Spend at least 15–20 minutes at a location before moving.
- **Match the hatch.** Use bait or lures that match what fish naturally eat in that water.
- **Fish the right time.** Early morning (first hour after sunrise) and evening (last hour before sunset) are often most productive.
- **Fish the right weather.** Overcast days are often better than bright sunny days. Fish feed more actively before storms.

## Identifying Your Fish

Once you have caught a fish, you need to identify it correctly. Fish identification is a skill — different species can look similar, especially if you are new to fishing.

### Basic Fish Anatomy for Identification

All fish have similar structures, but proportions and colors vary between species.

**Key identification features:**
- **Body shape** — Elongated, deep-bodied, flat-sided?
- **Color and markings** — Solid color, stripes, spots, blotches?
- **Mouth position** — Terminal (pointing forward), superior (top), inferior (bottom)?
- **Fin shape and position** — Sharp spines, soft rays, distinctive patterns?
- **Scales** — Large, small, shiny, dull?
- **Distinctive features** — Barbels (whiskers), adipose fin, spots, stripes?

### Common Freshwater Fish of North America

Here are some of the most common fish you might catch:

**Largemouth Bass**
- **Color:** Green to dark green on back, white belly, dark stripe along side
- **Identifying features:** Large mouth extends past the eye, horizontal dark stripe
- **Size:** 2–15 pounds typical (can be larger)
- **Habitat:** Ponds, lakes, rivers with vegetation

**Smallmouth Bass**
- **Color:** Brownish or bronze with dark vertical bars
- **Identifying features:** Smaller mouth than largemouth, vertical bars rather than horizontal stripe
- **Size:** 1–5 pounds typical
- **Habitat:** Rocky streams, lakes, rivers

**Sunfish (Bluegill)**
- **Color:** Blue and orange with dark wavy lines on cheek
- **Identifying features:** Small mouth, deep compressed body, distinctive face markings
- **Size:** 4–12 inches typical
- **Habitat:** Ponds, lakes, quiet backwater areas

**Rainbow Trout**
- **Color:** Silver with rainbow-colored stripe (especially males in spawning colors)
- **Identifying features:** Spots on entire body including tail, adipose fin, white gum line around mouth
- **Size:** 1–3 pounds typical (can be larger)
- **Habitat:** Cold, clear streams and lakes

**Brown Trout**
- **Color:** Golden-brown with dark spots and red/orange spots with halos
- **Identifying features:** Adipose fin, no white gum line (unlike rainbow), dark spots with lighter halos
- **Size:** 1–5 pounds typical
- **Habitat:** Cold streams, deep lakes, spring-fed waters

**Channel Catfish**
- **Color:** Gray to dark brown on back, light belly, dark spots
- **Identifying features:** Barbels (whiskers) around mouth, adipose fin, forked tail, smooth skin (no scales)
- **Size:** 2–10 pounds typical
- **Habitat:** Rivers, ponds, lakes, murky water

**Walleye**
- **Color:** Golden-green with dark blotches, white belly
- **Identifying features:** Large eye with reflective coating, sharp teeth visible in mouth
- **Size:** 2–8 pounds typical
- **Habitat:** Deep lakes, murky rivers, cool water

**Northern Pike**
- **Color:** Green with light spots, white belly
- **Identifying features:** Elongated body, large mouth with sharp teeth, spots on sides and fins
- **Size:** 3–15 pounds typical
- **Habitat:** Weedy lakes, rivers, murky water

### Using Field Guides

**Best practices for identification:**
- **Take a photo** of your catch before release
- **Consult field guides** at home to confirm identification
- **Check multiple features** — do not identify based on one characteristic
- **Note the location** — the species found in your area helps identification
- **Ask experienced anglers** — locals know what fish are in their waters

### Identifying to Your Counselor

When you show your catch to your merit badge counselor:

1. **Have the fish fresh** — either bring it in a cooler or take a clear photo before releasing
2. **Be ready to describe it** — tell them the color, size, unique markings
3. **Explain your identification process** — "I knew it was a largemouth bass because of the large mouth that extends past the eye and the dark horizontal stripe"
4. **Reference a field guide** — show them the guide you used to identify it
5. **Know where you caught it** — this helps confirm identification (some species only exist in certain areas)

{{< drg/tip >}}
Many beginning anglers worry they will misidentify their fish. If you are not 100% sure, ask your counselor or another experienced angler. Learning to identify fish accurately is part of the badge — there is no shame in getting help.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Next Steps After Your Catch

Once you have caught and identified your fish, you have two options:

**Release it:** Carefully remove the hook and return it to the water using the catch-and-release technique you learned in Req 6.

**Keep it:** If the fish meets size requirements and you want to cook it, proceed to Requirement 10.
