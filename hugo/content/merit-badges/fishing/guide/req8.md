Explain what good outdoor sportsmanlike behavior is and how it relates to anglers. Tell how the Leave No Trace Seven Principles and the Outdoor Code relate to a fishing sports enthusiast, including the aspects of littering, trespassing, courteous behavior, and obeying fishing regulations.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Fishing is a sport. Like all sports, it has rules, ethics, and a code of conduct. An ethical angler is not just someone who catches fish — it is someone who respects the resource, respects other people, and follows the principles that keep fishing vibrant and accessible for everyone.

## What Is Outdoor Sportsmanlike Behavior?

**Sportsmanship** is more than following written rules. It is a commitment to playing fairly, treating others with respect, and maintaining integrity even when no one is watching.

**Core principles of outdoor sportsmanship:**

### Fair Chase

**What it means:** You give the fish a reasonable chance to escape. You do not use unfair advantages or illegal methods that remove all challenge and skill.

**In fishing:**
- Use appropriate tackle for the species (not overkill)
- Do not fish with dynamite, poison, electricity, or nets
- Do not use spear guns (in most waters)
- Follow gear restrictions designed to maintain fair chase
- Do not feed fish or chum excessively just to concentrate them into an unfair advantage

**Why it matters:** If fishing had no challenge or skill, it would lose its meaning. The struggle between angler and fish is what makes fishing rewarding. An angler who resorts to unsporting methods cheapens the experience for everyone.

### Respect for the Resource

**What it means:** You treat fish and habitat with respect, understanding that the resource is finite and shared.

**In fishing:**
- Keep only fish you will eat (when keeping is legal)
- Release fish humanely and carefully
- Protect fish habitat by following Leave No Trace principles
- Do not damage vegetation or disturb nesting areas
- Support conservation efforts and habitat restoration

**Why it matters:** Without respect for the resource, it disappears. Disrespectful anglers who trash habitats, over-harvest, and fish illegally have destroyed fisheries throughout history.

### Integrity

**What it means:** You do the right thing even when no one is watching.

**In fishing:**
- Report your catch honestly to your counselor
- Follow regulations even when enforcement is unlikely
- Do not fish closed areas or out of season just because you think you will not get caught
- Admit mistakes (like accidentally keeping an undersized fish) and correct them

**Why it matters:** If everyone cheated when they thought they could get away with it, regulations would become meaningless and fisheries would collapse. Your integrity protects the resource for everyone.

## How Leave No Trace Principles Relate to Fishing

You learned about Leave No Trace in Requirement 6. Here is how each principle applies specifically to fishing and anglers:

### 1. Plan Ahead and Prepare

**For fishermen:**
- Research your target species, water, and regulations before you go
- Bring all equipment you need so you do not have to improvise
- Know what season and hours are legal
- Plan your route so you do not get lost and have to bushwhack back to civilization

**Sportsmanship connection:** A prepared angler respects both the resource and other people. You do not create damage through careless planning.

### 2. Travel and Camp on Durable Surfaces

**For fishermen:**
- Use established trails to fishing spots rather than forcing your way through vegetation
- Fish from established access points on shore
- Use established campsites, never trample new areas
- Do not wade unnecessarily through vegetation or spawning areas
- Stay on banks rather than in the water when possible

**Sportsmanship connection:** Protecting habitat means protecting fish's homes and the ecosystems they depend on.

### 3. Dispose of Waste Properly

**For fishermen:** This is where fishing is particularly problematic if done carelessly.

- Pack out all trash, including empty bait containers and broken lures
- Never leave fishing line in the water — it tangles and kills wildlife
- Do not throw fish guts back into the water (proper disposal: bury away from water or pack out)
- Pack out old hooks, sinkers, and lead weights (lead especially is toxic)
- Do not urinate or defecate near the water
- Properly dispose of unused bait

**Sportsmanship connection:** An angler's waste kills fish and wildlife. Proper waste management is a basic sportsmanship responsibility.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Fishing line is one of the most dangerous forms of litter. Birds, turtles, and fish become tangled in discarded line and die. Always pack out your line or safely store it until you get home. Never leave it at the fishing spot.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

### 4. Leave What You Find

**For fishermen:**
- Do not collect plants or disturb wildlife beyond the fish you catch
- Do not remove rocks unless you are flipping them for bait
- Replace rocks gently after checking underneath
- Do not nest-hop or disturb waterfowl
- Do not pick wildflowers or vegetation

**Sportsmanship connection:** The ecosystem surrounding the fish is the foundation of the fishery. Respect it.

### 5. Minimize Campfire Impacts

**For fishermen:**
- Keep fires small and only where permitted
- Use dead, downed wood only
- Never leave fires unattended
- Fully extinguish fires — soak with water and stir the ashes
- Do not build fires near vegetation or where they scar the ground

**Sportsmanship connection:** Fires near water can contaminate it. Careless fires harm habitat.

### 6. Respect Wildlife

**For fishermen:**
- Keep distance from waterfowl and other wildlife
- Do not feed ducks or geese — it makes them dependent on humans
- Store food properly so bears and other animals do not become habituated
- Do not disturb nesting areas (keep out during nesting season)
- Understand that you are visiting the fish's home

**Sportsmanship connection:** Fish and fishermen share the aquatic ecosystem. Respecting other wildlife means respecting the fish's habitat.

### 7. Be Considerate of Others

**For fishermen:**
- Keep noise down — loud talking and radios spook fish
- Yield good spots to anglers who were there first
- Do not cast toward other people
- Fish early or evening to avoid crowding busy times
- Share information and be friendly to other anglers
- Do not crowd other fishermen on small waters

**Sportsmanship connection:** Other anglers share the resource. Courtesy ensures everyone has a good experience.

## The Scout Outdoor Code

The Scout Outdoor Code complements Leave No Trace perfectly:

### As an outdoor enthusiast, I will:

**Respect the rights and property of all persons.** Do not trespass. Ask permission before fishing private property. Respect other users of public lands — hikers, campers, and anglers all share the space.

**Realize that the outdoors is a destination, not a means to an end.** Slow down. Do not rush through your fishing trip. Appreciate the sunrise, the sound of the water, the smell of the forest. Fishing is not just about catching fish.

**Practice good outdoor skills.** Fish skillfully and ethically. Learn to cast well, tie knots correctly, and handle fish properly. Respect the craft.

**Appreciate the beauty of creation.** The outdoors is beautiful. Take time to notice it. A Scout who appreciates beauty is more likely to protect it.

**Walk softly and leave no sign of my passing.** This is the essence of Leave No Trace and outdoor sportsmanship — leave places as you found them or better.

## Specific Sportsmanship Challenges

### Littering

**Sportsmanship perspective:** Littering is disrespect — to the resource, to other people, and to the future.

**Common fishing litter:**
- Fishing line and broken lures
- Plastic bags and food packaging
- Aluminum cans and bottles
- Old hooks and sinkers (especially lead, which is toxic)
- Broken rod pieces and reel parts

**Sportsmanlike behavior:**
- Pack out every piece of trash
- Do not leave anything behind
- If you find litter at your fishing spot, pick it up and pack it out
- Educate other anglers — if you see someone leaving trash, politely mention the importance of Leave No Trace

### Trespassing

**Sportsmanship perspective:** Trespassing is theft — you are taking the landowner's permission without asking.

**Where not to fish:**
- Private property without permission
- Posted lands (even if no sign is visible, assume private property is off-limits)
- Restricted areas near dams, military installations, or other sensitive locations
- Waters closed to fishing

**Sportsmanlike behavior:**
- Always ask permission before fishing private property
- If permission is granted, follow any conditions the owner sets
- Leave property cleaner than you found it
- Respect posted areas immediately without question
- Respect seasonal closures and restricted areas

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Many of the best fishing spots are on private property. Landowners who see responsible, respectful anglers are more likely to grant permission to future anglers. One careless angler can ruin access for everyone by trespassing or leaving trash.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

### Courteous Behavior

**Sportsmanship perspective:** Courtesy is respect in action.

**Courteous fishing practices:**
- Keep noise down — loud talking spooks fish and ruins others' experience
- Do not cast toward other anglers (keep 50+ feet away)
- If you hook someone else's line, help untangle it
- Yield good spots to anglers who are already there
- Share information (where fish are biting, what lure works) with other anglers
- If you make a mistake that affects someone else, apologize and make it right

**Uncouth behavior to avoid:**
- Yelling and loud music near fishing areas
- Crowding other anglers on small waters
- Stealing someone else's fishing spot
- Refusing to help if another angler is in trouble
- Boasting about your catch to discourage others

### Obeying Fishing Regulations

**Sportsmanship perspective:** Regulations are not restrictions — they are the rules of the game. Playing by the rules is fundamental to sportsmanship.

**Sportsmanlike regulation compliance:**
- Obtain a fishing license before you fish
- Fish only during legal seasons
- Keep only fish that meet size requirements
- Do not exceed bag limits
- Follow gear restrictions and allowed-bait rules
- Report illegal fishing if you witness it
- If you accidentally break a rule (like keeping an undersized fish), acknowledge it and do better

**Why this matters:** Anglers who break regulations are cheaters. They gain unfair advantage and damage the resource for everyone. True sportsmanship means following rules even when enforcement is unlikely.

## Being a Fishing Ambassador

The most important role you can play is being a positive example. When young anglers see you practicing Leave No Trace, respecting regulations, and treating the resource with care, they learn that this is how fishing is done. When they see an older angler ignoring rules and littering, they learn a different lesson.

{{< drg/checklist title="Fishing Ambassador Checklist" subtitle="How to model good sportsmanship" >}}
- Always pack out your trash and pick up litter you find
- Treat other anglers with courtesy and respect
- Fish during legal seasons with a valid license
- Follow size and bag limits exactly
- Release fish humanely and carefully
- Help educate other anglers about Leave No Trace
- Respect private property and closed areas
- Share your love of fishing and conservation with younger Scouts
