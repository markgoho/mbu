{{< drg/requirement number="1c" >}}
Tell some specific characteristics of seven breeds of dogs (one from each major group), OR give a short history of one breed.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You have two paths for this requirement: describe one breed from each of the seven AKC groups, or do a deep dive into the history of a single breed. Both options build your breed knowledge -- you just need to pick the approach that interests you most.

### Option A: One Breed from Each Group

For this option, select a breed from each of the seven groups you learned about in Req 1b. For each breed, be ready to discuss physical characteristics (size, coat, build), temperament, original purpose, and any special care needs. Here are sample profiles to show you the level of detail your counselor will expect -- but pick breeds that genuinely interest *you*.

**Labrador Retriever (Sporting)**
- **Size:** 55--80 lbs, 21.5--24.5 inches tall
- **Coat:** Short, dense, water-resistant double coat in black, yellow, or chocolate
- **Temperament:** Outgoing, even-tempered, gentle. Consistently the most popular breed in America
- **Special care:** Prone to obesity -- portion control and daily exercise are essential. Hip and elbow dysplasia are common health concerns

**Beagle (Hound)**
- **Size:** 20--30 lbs, 13--15 inches tall
- **Coat:** Short, dense, weather-resistant coat in tricolor (black, white, tan) or lemon and white
- **Temperament:** Curious, merry, friendly. Follows their nose relentlessly
- **Special care:** Beagles are escape artists -- a securely fenced yard is a must. They howl when lonely or bored

**Siberian Husky (Working)**
- **Size:** 35--60 lbs, 20--23.5 inches tall
- **Coat:** Thick double coat with a dense undercoat -- sheds heavily twice a year ("blowing coat")
- **Temperament:** Friendly, mischievous, outgoing. Bred for endurance, not obedience -- they can be independent
- **Special care:** Need heavy exercise (running, hiking, sledding). Not suited to hot climates. Known escape artists who can jump fences and dig under them

**Airedale Terrier (Terrier)**
- **Size:** 50--70 lbs, about 23 inches tall -- the largest of all terrier breeds
- **Coat:** Dense, wiry coat in tan with a black saddle. Requires hand-stripping or professional grooming
- **Temperament:** Confident, intelligent, courageous. Called the "King of Terriers"
- **Special care:** Needs mental stimulation as much as physical exercise. Can be stubborn if training is boring or repetitive

**Cavalier King Charles Spaniel (Toy)**
- **Size:** 13--18 lbs, 12--13 inches tall
- **Coat:** Silky, medium-length coat in four color patterns: Blenheim, tricolor, black and tan, ruby
- **Temperament:** Gentle, affectionate, adaptable. Thrives on human companionship
- **Special care:** Prone to heart conditions (mitral valve disease). Needs regular cardiac checkups. Not an outdoor-only dog

**German Shepherd (Herding)**
- **Size:** 50--90 lbs, 22--26 inches tall
- **Coat:** Medium-length double coat, typically black and tan
- **Temperament:** Confident, courageous, smart. Loyal to family but reserved with strangers
- **Special care:** Requires consistent training and socialization starting young. Prone to hip dysplasia. Needs a job or structured activities to stay happy

**Poodle (Non-Sporting)**
- **Size:** Standard: 40--70 lbs, over 15 inches tall (also comes in Miniature and Toy sizes)
- **Coat:** Dense, curly, single-layer coat that barely sheds -- considered hypoallergenic (though no dog is truly allergen-free)
- **Temperament:** Exceptionally intelligent, athletic, eager to learn. Far more active and athletic than their fancy haircuts suggest
- **Special care:** Coat requires professional grooming every 4--6 weeks. Without regular grooming, the coat mats painfully

{{< drg/tip >}}
When presenting your seven breeds to your counselor, use a format that covers the same categories for each breed -- size, coat, temperament, original purpose, and special care needs. This makes your presentation organized and thorough.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### Option B: History of One Breed

If you choose this path, pick a breed and trace its story from origin to the present. Here is an example using the Labrador Retriever to show the kind of depth your counselor will expect:

**The Labrador Retriever: From Newfoundland Fishing Boats to America's Favorite Dog**

Despite the name, the Labrador Retriever did not originate in Labrador. The breed began in Newfoundland, Canada, in the early 1800s, where fishermen bred small water dogs (called St. John's Water Dogs) to haul nets, retrieve fish that slipped off hooks, and swim lines between boats. English sportsmen visiting Newfoundland noticed these tireless water dogs and brought them back to England in the 1830s.

In England, aristocrats like the Earl of Malmesbury bred these dogs for upland bird hunting, valuing their soft mouths (the ability to carry game without damaging it), their love of water, and their trainability. The Earl named them "Labrador dogs," and the breed was officially recognized by the English Kennel Club in 1903.

Labs arrived in the United States in the early 1900s and were recognized by the AKC in 1917. Their versatility made them favorites for hunting, but their gentle temperament soon made them the country's most popular family dog -- a title they held for 31 consecutive years (1991--2022).

Today, Labs serve as guide dogs for the blind, search-and-rescue dogs, detection dogs (drugs, explosives, even diseases), and therapy dogs. Their combination of intelligence, trainability, and friendliness makes them one of the most versatile breeds ever developed.
