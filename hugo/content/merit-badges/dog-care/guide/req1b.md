{{< drg/requirement number="1b" >}}
Describe some common characteristics of the dogs that make up each of the seven major dog groups.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The American Kennel Club (AKC) organizes over 200 recognized breeds into seven groups based on what the dogs were originally bred to do. Knowing these groups helps you predict a dog's energy level, temperament, grooming needs, and training style -- all critical knowledge for responsible dog care.

### Sporting Group

**Original purpose:** Assist hunters by finding, flushing, and retrieving game birds.

**Common characteristics:**
- High energy and stamina -- these dogs need daily vigorous exercise
- Eager to please, making them highly trainable
- Love water (many have water-resistant coats)
- Friendly and social with people and other dogs
- Dense, weather-resistant coats that need regular grooming

**Popular breeds:** Labrador Retriever, Golden Retriever, English Springer Spaniel, Cocker Spaniel, Vizsla

{{< drg/tip >}}
Sporting dogs that do not get enough exercise often develop destructive behaviors like chewing furniture or digging up the yard. If your family is considering a sporting breed, plan for at least 60 minutes of active exercise per day.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### Hound Group

**Original purpose:** Track and chase game by scent or sight.

**Common characteristics:**
- **Scent hounds** (Beagles, Bloodhounds, Basset Hounds) have extraordinary noses and tend to follow their nose wherever it leads -- sometimes ignoring you entirely
- **Sight hounds** (Greyhounds, Whippets, Afghan Hounds) are built for speed with lean, aerodynamic bodies
- Independent thinkers -- hounds were bred to work ahead of the hunter, making decisions on their own
- Many have a distinctive howl or bay
- Scent hounds tend to be laid-back indoors; sight hounds are sprinters who alternate between bursts of energy and long naps

**Popular breeds:** Beagle, Bloodhound, Greyhound, Dachshund, Rhodesian Ridgeback

### Working Group

**Original purpose:** Guard property, pull sleds, perform water rescues, and other heavy-duty tasks.

**Common characteristics:**
- Large to giant in size, powerful and muscular
- Intelligent and quick learners
- Naturally protective of their family and territory
- Need early socialization and firm, consistent training
- Many have thick double coats suited to cold climates
- Strong-willed -- not ideal for first-time dog owners without guidance

**Popular breeds:** Siberian Husky, Boxer, Great Dane, Rottweiler, Doberman Pinscher, Saint Bernard

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Working breeds are powerful dogs that can weigh over 100 pounds. They need an owner who establishes leadership through consistent training -- not through force or intimidation. An untrained working dog can be difficult and even dangerous to manage. If your family is considering a working breed, research the breed's specific needs thoroughly and consider working with a professional trainer.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

### Terrier Group

**Original purpose:** Hunt and kill vermin (rats, foxes, badgers, and other burrowing animals).

**Common characteristics:**
- Bold, feisty, and energetic -- terriers have big personalities in every size
- Tenacious and determined (the word "terrier" comes from Latin *terra*, meaning earth -- they were bred to dig)
- Can be scrappy with other dogs, especially those of the same sex
- Wiry or rough coats that often require hand-stripping instead of standard clipping
- Vocal -- many terriers bark readily
- Love to dig

**Popular breeds:** Jack Russell Terrier, Bull Terrier, Airedale Terrier, Scottish Terrier, West Highland White Terrier

### Toy Group

**Original purpose:** Companionship (though many have working-dog ancestors that were bred down in size).

**Common characteristics:**
- Small in size, usually under 10 pounds
- Bred for affection and close human contact
- Adaptable to apartment living and smaller spaces
- Can be fragile -- rough handling or falls from furniture can cause serious injury
- Often long-lived compared to larger breeds
- Some are surprisingly energetic despite their size

**Popular breeds:** Chihuahua, Pomeranian, Shih Tzu, Yorkshire Terrier, Cavalier King Charles Spaniel

### Herding Group

**Original purpose:** Control and move livestock (sheep, cattle, goats, reindeer).

**Common characteristics:**
- Extremely intelligent and responsive to training
- Strong instinct to herd -- they may try to "herd" children, other pets, or even you by nipping at heels
- High energy and need both physical exercise and mental stimulation
- Form strong bonds with their owners
- Alert and watchful -- they notice everything
- Excel at obedience, agility, and other canine sports

**Popular breeds:** Border Collie, German Shepherd, Australian Shepherd, Pembroke Welsh Corgi, Shetland Sheepdog

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Border Collies are widely considered the most intelligent dog breed. A Border Collie named Chaser learned the names of over 1,000 individual toys and could retrieve them by name -- more vocabulary than any other non-human animal ever tested.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

### Non-Sporting Group

**Original purpose:** Varies widely (this is the "everything else" group).

**Common characteristics:**
- The most diverse group -- sizes range from the small Bichon Frise to the large Dalmatian
- Temperaments, coat types, and energy levels vary enormously
- Many were once classified in other groups but no longer fit the working definition
- Each breed's characteristics must be researched individually
- Includes some of the most popular family dogs

**Popular breeds:** Bulldog, Poodle (Standard), Dalmatian, Chow Chow, French Bulldog, Shiba Inu
