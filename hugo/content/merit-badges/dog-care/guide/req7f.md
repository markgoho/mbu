{{< drg/requirement number="7f" >}}
List the things needed in every dog owner's first-aid kit.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A canine first-aid kit is not a luxury -- it is a necessity that sits alongside your family's human first-aid kit. When your dog cuts a paw at 10 p.m. on a Sunday, you do not want to be driving to a 24-hour pet store. Having the right supplies already organized and accessible means you can act immediately.

### Essential Supplies

{{< drg/checklist title="Canine First-Aid Kit" subtitle="Stock these items and check expiration dates every 6 months" >}}
- Gauze rolls (2-inch and 4-inch): For wrapping wounds and making emergency muzzles
- Non-stick pads (Telfa): For covering wounds without sticking to tissue
- Self-adhesive wrap (Vetrap or coflex): Stretchy bandage that sticks to itself, not to fur
- Roll cotton or cast padding: For cushioning under bandages
- Medical tape (1-inch): To secure bandage edges to fur
- Blunt-tipped scissors: For cutting bandages and trimming fur around wounds
- Tweezers or hemostats: For removing splinters, ticks, and debris
- Styptic powder or pencil: Stops bleeding from nail trims that cut the quick
- Digital thermometer: Normal canine temperature is 101--102.5 degrees Fahrenheit (use rectally with lubricant)
- Hydrogen peroxide (3%): Used ONLY to induce vomiting if directed by a vet or poison control -- never for wound cleaning
- Triple antibiotic ointment: For minor cuts and scrapes
- Saline solution (sterile): For flushing wounds and eyes
- Diphenhydramine (Benadryl): For mild allergic reactions -- dose is 1 mg per pound of body weight, but always confirm with your vet first
- Latex or nitrile gloves: To protect yourself and keep wounds clean
- Emergency blanket (Mylar): For warmth and shock prevention
- Muzzle or material for an emergency muzzle: Gauze roll serves double duty
- Slip leash: For restraining a stray or loose injured dog
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

### Additional Items for Active Dogs

If you hike, camp, or travel with your dog, add these:

- **Paw booties** -- Protect cut or burned paw pads during the walk back to the car
- **Electrolyte solution** -- For rehydration after vomiting or diarrhea
- **Instant cold pack** -- For reducing swelling from sprains or insect stings
- **Eye wash** -- For flushing debris, dust, or plant material from eyes
- **Tick removal tool** -- Easier and more reliable than tweezers for tick removal
- **Towel** -- For drying, restraining, or improvising a stretcher

### Information to Include

Your kit should also contain:

- **Your vet's phone number and address**
- **Nearest 24-hour emergency vet clinic phone number and address**
- **ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center: (888) 426-4435** (there is a consultation fee, but they are available 24/7)
- **Your dog's medical records** -- vaccination history, allergies, current medications, and microchip number
- **A photo of your dog** -- In case the dog gets lost during an emergency

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
The ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center handles over 250,000 calls per year about pets exposed to toxic substances. The top five toxins they receive calls about are human medications (especially ibuprofen and acetaminophen), chocolate, insecticides, rodent poison, and dietary supplements. Having their number in your kit could save your dog's life.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

### Where to Keep Your Kit

{{< drg/tip >}}
Keep one kit at home in an accessible location (not buried in a closet) and a smaller travel kit in your car or backpack. Pre-packaged pet first-aid kits are available from the American Red Cross and pet retailers, but building your own ensures you know exactly what is inside and how to use each item.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### Practice Using Your Kit

A kit is only useful if you know how to use everything in it. Schedule a practice session:

1. Take the dog's temperature (with a helper holding the dog)
2. Practice applying a non-stick pad and wrapping a paw (on a healthy, cooperative dog)
3. Practice making an emergency muzzle from the gauze roll
4. Review how to read and use each item

This hands-on practice reinforces the bandaging skills from [Req 7d](/merit-badges/dog-care/guide/req7d/) and the muzzle technique from [Req 7b](/merit-badges/dog-care/guide/req7b/).
