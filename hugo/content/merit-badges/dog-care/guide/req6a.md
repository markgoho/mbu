{{< drg/requirement number="6a" >}}
Discuss the proper vaccination schedule for a dog in your area from puppyhood through adulthood.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Vaccines are your dog's invisible armor against diseases that can kill in hours. Before vaccines, parvovirus, distemper, and rabies claimed millions of dogs every year. Today, a simple series of shots -- given on the right schedule -- can prevent nearly all of these illnesses. Understanding the schedule helps you keep your dog protected and prepares you for an informed conversation with your counselor.

### Core Vaccines

Core vaccines are recommended for every dog regardless of location, breed, or lifestyle. They protect against the most dangerous and widespread diseases:

| Vaccine | What It Prevents | Severity |
|---------|-----------------|----------|
| **Rabies** | Fatal viral infection of the nervous system; transmissible to humans | Always fatal once symptoms appear |
| **Distemper** | Highly contagious virus attacking respiratory, GI, and nervous systems | Often fatal; survivors may have permanent damage |
| **Parvovirus** | Extremely contagious virus causing severe vomiting and bloody diarrhea | Up to 90% fatal in untreated puppies |
| **Adenovirus (Hepatitis)** | Virus causing liver infection, fever, and organ damage | Can be fatal in young dogs |

These four are often combined into a single injection called **DA2PP** or **DHPP** (Distemper, Adenovirus, Parainfluenza, Parvovirus).

### Non-Core Vaccines

Non-core vaccines are recommended based on your dog's risk factors -- where you live, whether the dog goes to boarding or dog parks, and what diseases are common in your area:

- **Bordetella (Kennel Cough)** -- Required by most boarding facilities, doggy daycares, and groomers. Protects against a highly contagious respiratory infection.
- **Leptospirosis** -- A bacterial infection spread through contaminated water or wildlife urine. Important if your dog swims in ponds, drinks from puddles, or encounters wildlife.
- **Lyme Disease** -- Spread by deer ticks. Recommended if you live in or travel to tick-endemic areas (Northeast, Upper Midwest, Pacific Coast).
- **Canine Influenza (Dog Flu)** -- Increasingly common, especially in areas with high dog-to-dog contact.

{{< drg/tip >}}
When discussing this requirement with your counselor, research which non-core vaccines are recommended in *your specific area*. Call your veterinarian or check your state veterinary association's website. Your counselor will be impressed that you tailored the information to your location.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### Puppy Vaccination Schedule

Puppies receive a series of vaccinations because their mother's antibodies (received through nursing) gradually fade over the first few months. Multiple doses ensure the puppy builds its own immunity as maternal protection decreases.

| Age | Vaccines |
|-----|----------|
| 6--8 weeks | DA2PP (first dose) |
| 10--12 weeks | DA2PP (second dose), Bordetella, Leptospirosis (if recommended) |
| 14--16 weeks | DA2PP (third dose), Rabies (required by law in most states) |
| 12--16 months | DA2PP booster, Rabies booster |

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Until a puppy has completed its full vaccination series (around 16 weeks), it is vulnerable to diseases like parvovirus. Avoid dog parks, pet stores, and areas where unvaccinated dogs may have been. Socialization during this period should be limited to known, vaccinated dogs in clean environments.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

### Adult Vaccination Schedule

After the puppy series, adult dogs need periodic boosters:

| Vaccine | Booster Frequency |
|---------|-------------------|
| Rabies | Every 1--3 years (varies by state law) |
| DA2PP | Every 3 years after the 1-year booster |
| Bordetella | Annually (or every 6 months if high-risk) |
| Leptospirosis | Annually |
| Lyme Disease | Annually (if recommended for your area) |

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Rabies vaccination is the only vaccine required by law in every U.S. state. The reason is public health -- rabies is nearly 100% fatal in humans once symptoms appear, and dogs are one of the primary transmission risks. The legal requirement protects both your dog and your community.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}
