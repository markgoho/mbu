{{< drg/requirement number="6d" >}}
Discuss the benefits of grooming your dog's coat and nails on a regular basis.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Grooming is not about making your dog look pretty for Instagram. It is a health practice -- a hands-on inspection that catches problems early, prevents painful conditions, and keeps your dog comfortable. Every grooming session is also a chance to check for lumps, parasites, skin irritations, and injuries that might otherwise go unnoticed.

### Coat Care

**Why regular brushing matters:**
- Removes dead hair and distributes natural skin oils, keeping the coat healthy and shiny
- Prevents mats and tangles -- matted fur pulls painfully on the skin and traps moisture, creating a breeding ground for bacteria and fungal infections
- Reduces shedding in your home (it does not eliminate shedding, but it controls where the hair ends up)
- Gives you the chance to feel for lumps, bumps, ticks, fleas, or skin problems hidden beneath the fur

**Brushing frequency by coat type:**

| Coat Type | How Often | Best Tool |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| Short, smooth (Beagle, Boxer) | Weekly | Rubber curry brush or bristle brush |
| Double coat (Lab, Husky, Shepherd) | 2--3 times per week (daily during shedding season) | Undercoat rake and slicker brush |
| Long, silky (Yorkie, Afghan) | Daily | Pin brush and metal comb |
| Wiry (Terriers) | 2--3 times per week | Slicker brush; hand-stripping for show dogs |
| Curly (Poodle, Bichon) | Daily to prevent mats | Slicker brush and metal comb |

**Bathing:**
- Most dogs need a bath every 4--8 weeks, unless they get visibly dirty or smelly
- Use a dog-specific shampoo -- human shampoo has the wrong pH for dog skin and can cause dryness and irritation
- Overbathing strips natural oils and can cause dry, flaky skin
- Always rinse thoroughly -- leftover shampoo irritates the skin

### Nail Care

Long nails are not just a cosmetic issue -- they change how your dog walks and can cause real pain.

**Why nail trimming matters:**
- When nails get too long, they push the toes backward with every step, forcing the dog to walk on the backs of its feet
- Over time, this abnormal gait stresses the joints and tendons, leading to arthritis and chronic pain
- Extremely long nails can curl and grow into the paw pads, causing infection
- Long nails are more likely to snag and tear, which is painful and can bleed profusely

**How to trim:**
- Use dog nail clippers (guillotine-style or scissor-style) or a rotary grinding tool (like a Dremel)
- Trim just the tip of the nail, avoiding the **quick** -- the blood vessel and nerve inside the nail. In light-colored nails, the quick is visible as a pink area. In dark nails, trim small amounts and stop when you see a pale circle appear in the center of the cut surface
- If you accidentally cut the quick, apply styptic powder or cornstarch to stop the bleeding. It hurts, but it is not a medical emergency
- Trim every 2--4 weeks, or whenever you can hear nails clicking on hard floors

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Your Dog Hates Nail Trims" >}}
Many dogs resist nail trimming because of a past painful experience. Here is how to rebuild their tolerance:

- **Start by just touching the paws** -- reward with treats. Do this for several days before introducing clippers.
- **Let the dog sniff the clippers** -- reward. Open and close the clippers near (not on) the nails -- reward.
- **Trim one nail per session** if needed. One nail and a jackpot of treats is better than a traumatic full trim.
- **Use a grinding tool** if your dog tolerates the vibration better than the pressure of clippers.
- **Seek help** from a vet or groomer if your dog's anxiety is severe. There is no shame in getting professional help.

### Ear Care

Check your dog's ears weekly. Healthy ears are pale pink inside with no odor. Red, swollen, or smelly ears may indicate an infection.

- Dogs with floppy ears (Cocker Spaniels, Basset Hounds) are more prone to ear infections because air circulation is limited
- Clean ears gently with a vet-approved ear cleaner and cotton balls -- never insert anything into the ear canal
- After swimming or bathing, dry the ears thoroughly

### Eye Care

- Wipe away any discharge or "eye boogers" with a damp, soft cloth
- Watch for excessive tearing, redness, squinting, or cloudiness -- these may indicate an infection or injury
- Breeds with flat faces (Pugs, Bulldogs) are more prone to eye problems due to their shallow eye sockets

{{< drg/tip >}}
Turn grooming into a bonding ritual, not a chore. Start young, go slowly, and pair every grooming session with treats and calm praise. A dog that enjoys being groomed is a dog that lets you catch health problems early, which connects directly to the responsible ownership you learned about in [Req 3b](/merit-badges/dog-care/guide/req3b/).
