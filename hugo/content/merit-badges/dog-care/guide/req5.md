{{< drg/requirement number="5" >}}
Explain the correct way to obedience train a dog and what equipment you would need. Show with your dog any three of these commands: "come," "sit," "down," "heel," "stay," "fetch," "get it," "drop it."
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A 70-pound dog pulling you down the street is not just embarrassing -- it is dangerous. Obedience training gives you control, but more importantly, it gives your dog clarity. A dog that understands what you are asking is a confident, relaxed dog. A dog that never knows what to expect is anxious and unpredictable.

### The Right Approach: Positive Reinforcement

Modern dog training is based on **positive reinforcement** -- rewarding the behaviors you want rather than punishing the ones you do not. This method is backed by decades of animal behavior research and is recommended by the American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior (AVSAB).

**How it works:**
1. The dog performs the desired behavior (sits, for example)
2. You immediately (within 1--2 seconds) deliver a reward -- a treat, praise, or play
3. The dog learns to repeat the behavior because it produces good outcomes

**Why punishment-based methods fail:**
- They damage the trust between you and your dog
- They can cause fear, anxiety, and aggression
- They suppress behavior without teaching an alternative -- the dog learns what *not* to do but never learns what *to* do
- They require escalation over time as the dog becomes desensitized

### Equipment You Need

{{< drg/checklist title="Basic Training Equipment" subtitle="Gather these before you start" >}}
- Flat collar or harness: A well-fitted collar with a buckle or snap. Avoid choke chains and prong collars -- they cause pain and can injure the neck.
- Six-foot leash: A standard nylon or leather leash. Avoid retractable leashes for training -- they teach the dog that pulling extends the leash.
- Treat pouch: Worn on your belt or waistband so treats are instantly accessible. Timing is everything.
- Training treats: Small, soft, high-value treats the dog loves. Cut treats into pea-sized pieces so you can reward frequently without overfeeding. Pieces of cooked chicken, cheese, or commercial training treats work well.
- Long line (15--30 feet): For practicing recall ("come") at a distance before the dog is reliable off-leash.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
Train before meals, not after. A slightly hungry dog is a motivated dog. Keep sessions short -- 5 to 10 minutes, two to three times per day. End every session on a success, even if you have to make the exercise easier.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### The Commands

You need to demonstrate three of the following eight commands. Here is how to teach each one:

#### "Sit"

This is usually the first command a dog learns because it is the easiest.

1. Hold a treat in front of your dog's nose
2. Slowly move the treat up and slightly back over the dog's head
3. As the dog's nose follows the treat upward, its rear end will naturally lower to the ground
4. The instant the rear touches the ground, say "sit," give the treat, and praise
5. Repeat until the dog responds to the verbal cue alone

#### "Down"

1. Start with the dog in a "sit" position
2. Hold a treat in front of the dog's nose and slowly lower it straight down to the ground
3. As the dog follows the treat down, slide it slightly forward along the ground
4. When the dog's elbows touch the ground, say "down," give the treat, and praise
5. If the dog stands up instead of lying down, start over -- do not push the dog into position

#### "Stay"

1. Put the dog in a "sit" or "down" position
2. Hold your hand flat, palm facing the dog, and say "stay"
3. Take one step back. If the dog stays, return immediately, treat, and praise
4. Gradually increase the distance and duration over many sessions
5. Add distractions only after the dog is reliable at short distances

#### "Come" (Recall)

This is the most important safety command. A reliable recall can save your dog's life.

1. Start indoors or in a fenced area with minimal distractions
2. Show the dog a treat, then back away a few steps
3. In an excited, happy voice, say "come!" and encourage the dog toward you
4. When the dog reaches you, treat and praise enthusiastically -- make coming to you the best thing that happens all day
5. Gradually increase distance using a long line before practicing off-leash

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Never call your dog to you and then do something unpleasant (bath, nail trimming, crate time). If "come" sometimes leads to bad outcomes, the dog will stop coming. Instead, go get the dog for unpleasant tasks so "come" always means something great is about to happen.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

#### "Heel"

1. With the dog on your left side and the leash relaxed, hold a treat in your left hand at your hip
2. Say "heel" and start walking. Let the dog nibble the treat as it walks beside you
3. If the dog pulls ahead, stop walking immediately. Do not move until the dog returns to your side
4. When the dog walks next to you with a loose leash, treat and praise
5. Start with short distances (10--20 steps) and build gradually

#### "Fetch"

1. Start with a toy the dog already enjoys. Toss it a short distance
2. When the dog picks it up, encourage it to return with an excited voice
3. When the dog brings the toy back, trade it for a treat
4. Add the verbal cue "fetch" once the dog consistently chases and retrieves

#### "Get It" and "Drop It"

These two commands work as a pair:

**"Get it"** -- Point to or toss an object and say "get it." Reward the dog when it picks up the item. Start with the dog's favorite toy.

**"Drop it"** -- When the dog has something in its mouth, hold a treat near the dog's nose. As the dog opens its mouth to take the treat, say "drop it." Reward immediately. Never chase a dog to take something away -- that turns it into a game of keep-away.

Dogs can learn an average of 89 words, but some exceptional dogs have learned over 200. A Border Collie named Rico could learn the name of a new toy after hearing it just once and still remember it four weeks later -- a learning ability previously thought to be unique to human children.

### Common Training Mistakes

- **Repeating commands** -- If you say "sit, sit, sit, SIT," the dog learns that "sit" does not mean anything until you raise your voice. Say the command once, then help the dog do it.
- **Training too long** -- A tired, frustrated dog will not learn. Keep sessions short and fun.
- **Inconsistency** -- Everyone in the family must use the same commands and the same rules. If you say "down" but your parent says "lie down," the dog hears two different things.
- **Late timing** -- If you reward three seconds after the behavior, the dog may not connect the two. A clicker can help mark the exact moment the dog does the right thing.
