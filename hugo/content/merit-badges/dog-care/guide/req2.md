{{< drg/requirement number="2" >}}
Point out on a dog or a sketch at least 10 body parts. Give the correct name of each one.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Knowing the correct names for a dog's body parts is not just trivia -- it is practical vocabulary you will use when talking to veterinarians, groomers, trainers, and other dog professionals. If your vet says "there is a lump on the hock," you need to know that means the ankle area of the back leg, not the hip. This requirement builds the language of dog care.

### Head and Face

- **Stop** -- The indentation between the skull and the muzzle, located between the eyes. Some breeds (like Bulldogs) have a very pronounced stop; others (like Collies) have almost none.
- **Muzzle** -- The projecting part of the face that includes the nose, mouth, and jaws. Also called the snout.
- **Flew** -- The hanging part of the upper lip. Breeds like Bloodhounds and Saint Bernards have prominent flews.
- **Occiput** -- The bony bump at the top back of the skull. You can feel it easily on many breeds.
- **Leather** -- The ear flap itself. Ears come in many shapes: erect (German Shepherds), pendant/drooping (Beagles), rose (Greyhounds), and button (Fox Terriers).

### Body

- **Withers** -- The highest point of the shoulder blades, located just behind the base of the neck. This is where a dog's height is measured.
- **Chest** -- The front of the body between the forelegs. Also called the brisket in some breed standards.
- **Loin** -- The area of the back between the last rib and the pelvis. A strong loin is important for agility and power.
- **Croup** -- The area from the top of the hip bones to the base of the tail. The angle of the croup affects how a dog moves.
- **Flank** -- The side of the body between the last rib and the hip. This is the area that rises and falls when a dog breathes -- useful for counting respiratory rate.
- **Tuck-up** -- The area where the belly curves upward toward the hind legs. Sight hounds like Greyhounds have a dramatic tuck-up.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Dogs have about 320 bones in their body (the exact number varies by tail length), compared to 206 in an adult human. A dog also has 42 teeth -- 10 more than you do.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

### Legs and Feet

- **Shoulder** -- The joint where the front leg meets the body. A well-angulated shoulder allows smooth, efficient movement.
- **Pastern** -- The area between the wrist (carpus) and the toes on the front leg. Think of it as the dog's equivalent of the back of your hand.
- **Hock** -- The joint on the back leg that bends backward, roughly equivalent to the human ankle. This is the most common site for ligament injuries.
- **Stifle** -- The knee joint of the back leg. Torn cruciate ligaments in the stifle are one of the most common surgical issues in dogs.
- **Dewclaw** -- An extra claw located on the inside of the leg, higher up than the other toes. Most dogs have dewclaws on the front legs; some breeds also have them on the back legs.
- **Pads** -- The tough, cushioned undersides of the feet that protect the bones and joints from impact. Pads can be burned on hot pavement or cut on sharp objects.

### Tail and Rear

- **Tail set** -- Where the tail attaches to the body. It can be high-set (carried above the topline) or low-set (carried below).
- **Feathering** -- Longer hair on the ears, legs, chest, belly, or tail. Common in breeds like Golden Retrievers and setters.

### How to Practice

The best way to prepare for this requirement is to practice on a real dog:

{{< drg/checklist title="Body Part Practice" subtitle="Work through these steps with a patient dog" >}}
- Start at the head: Find the stop, muzzle, occiput, and flew.
- Move to the shoulder and withers: Feel where the shoulder blade meets the spine.
- Run your hand along the topline from withers to croup.
- Identify the loin and flank areas.
- Lift a front paw: Find the pastern, pads, and dewclaw.
- Lift a back paw: Find the hock and stifle.
- Check the tail set and note any feathering.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
Make flashcards with body part names on one side and descriptions on the other. Quiz yourself until you can point to each part on your dog (or a diagram) without hesitation. Your counselor will want you to identify at least 10, so learn more than the minimum.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
