{{< drg/requirement number="6b" >}}
Discuss the control methods for preventing fleas, ticks, heartworms, and intestinal parasites (worms) for a dog in your area from puppyhood through adulthood.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Parasites are not just gross -- they steal your dog's nutrition, transmit deadly diseases, and in severe cases can kill. The good news is that modern preventive medications make controlling parasites straightforward, as long as you use them consistently.

### Fleas

A single female flea can lay up to 50 eggs per day. Within weeks, a small infestation becomes a full-blown household crisis -- fleas live not just on your dog but in your carpet, furniture, and bedding.

**Problems fleas cause:**
- Intense itching and skin irritation
- Flea allergy dermatitis (an allergic reaction to flea saliva -- the most common skin disease in dogs)
- Tapeworm transmission (dogs swallow fleas while grooming, and fleas carry tapeworm larvae)
- Anemia in puppies or small dogs with heavy infestations

**Prevention methods:**
- **Monthly topical treatments** -- Applied between the shoulder blades. Products like those containing fipronil or imidacloprid kill fleas on contact.
- **Monthly oral tablets** -- Chewable tablets that work systemically, killing fleas when they bite. Many also prevent ticks.
- **Flea collars** -- Newer-generation collars release active ingredients over several months. Older-style collars are less effective.
- **Environmental control** -- Vacuum frequently, wash pet bedding weekly in hot water, and treat the yard if fleas persist.

### Ticks

Ticks are not just blood-sucking pests -- they are disease delivery systems. A single tick bite can transmit Lyme disease, ehrlichiosis, anaplasmosis, or Rocky Mountain spotted fever.

**Prevention methods:**
- **Monthly oral or topical preventives** -- Many flea products also repel or kill ticks. Ask your vet which product is best for the tick species in your area.
- **Tick checks** -- After every outdoor adventure, run your hands over your dog's entire body, paying special attention to the ears, armpits, groin, and between the toes. Remove ticks with fine-tipped tweezers by grasping close to the skin and pulling straight out with steady pressure.
- **Yard management** -- Keep grass short, remove leaf litter, and create a 3-foot gravel barrier between wooded areas and your lawn.

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Removing a Tick" >}}
If you find a tick attached to your dog:

- **Use fine-tipped tweezers** (not your fingers, not a match, not petroleum jelly).
- **Grasp the tick as close to the skin as possible** and pull straight out with steady, even pressure. Do not twist or jerk.
- **Clean the bite area** with rubbing alcohol or soap and water.
- **Save the tick** in a sealed bag or jar with the date written on it. If your dog shows signs of illness in the next few weeks, the tick can be identified and tested.
- **Monitor the bite site** for redness, swelling, or a bull's-eye rash (rare in dogs but possible).
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

### Heartworms

Heartworm disease is one of the most serious threats to dogs in the United States. It is caused by foot-long worms (*Dirofilaria immitis*) that live in the heart, lungs, and blood vessels.

**How dogs get heartworms:**
- Mosquitoes transmit heartworm larvae from infected animals to your dog through a single bite. There is no way to prevent mosquito bites entirely, so prevention medication is essential.

**Why prevention matters:**
- Heartworm treatment is expensive ($1,000--$3,000+), painful, and risky -- it involves injecting arsenic-based drugs to kill adult worms in the heart
- Treatment requires weeks of strict activity restriction to prevent dead worm fragments from causing blood clots
- Prevention costs about $5--$15 per month

**Prevention methods:**
- **Monthly oral preventives** -- Chewable tablets (ivermectin or milbemycin-based) that kill heartworm larvae before they mature. These must be given every 30 days, year-round, without missing a dose.
- **Monthly topical preventives** -- Applied to the skin.
- **Injectable preventive** -- A veterinarian-administered injection that provides 6 or 12 months of protection.
- **Annual testing** -- Even dogs on preventives should be tested for heartworms every year, because no preventive is 100% effective if a dose is missed or vomited.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Heartworm prevention must be given year-round in most areas, not just during "mosquito season." Mosquitoes can survive indoors through the winter, and heartworm larvae can remain dormant in a dog's system for months. Missing even one or two doses can leave your dog unprotected.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

### Intestinal Parasites (Worms)

Several types of worms can infect a dog's digestive system:

| Parasite | How Dogs Get It | Signs |
|----------|----------------|-------|
| **Roundworms** | Ingesting eggs from contaminated soil; puppies can get them from their mother | Pot-bellied appearance, vomiting, diarrhea, visible worms in stool |
| **Hookworms** | Larvae penetrate skin (paw pads) or are ingested | Anemia, weight loss, bloody diarrhea |
| **Whipworms** | Ingesting eggs from contaminated soil | Chronic diarrhea, weight loss |
| **Tapeworms** | Swallowing infected fleas or eating raw prey | Visible rice-like segments near the tail or in stool |

**Prevention and control:**
- Many monthly heartworm preventives also control roundworms, hookworms, and whipworms
- Regular flea prevention controls tapeworms (by eliminating the flea vector)
- Annual fecal exams at the vet detect infections early
- Pick up dog waste promptly to prevent environmental contamination
- Deworm puppies starting at 2 weeks of age per your vet's protocol

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Roundworms and hookworms are zoonotic -- they can infect humans, too. Children who play in contaminated soil or sandboxes are at highest risk. Roundworm larvae can migrate through human tissue and, in rare cases, cause vision damage. This is another reason to pick up your dog's waste promptly and wash your hands after handling soil.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}
