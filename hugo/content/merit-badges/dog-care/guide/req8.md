{{< drg/requirement number="8" >}}
Visit a veterinary hospital or an animal shelter and give a report about your visit to your counselor.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Textbooks teach you concepts. A visit to a real veterinary hospital or animal shelter puts those concepts into action -- you see the equipment, meet the professionals, observe the daily operations, and understand what happens behind the scenes when a dog needs care.

### Option A: Veterinary Hospital Visit

A veterinary hospital (or clinic) is where dogs receive everything from routine checkups to emergency surgery. Here is what to look for and ask about during your visit:

**Areas to observe:**
- **Reception and waiting area** -- How is the space organized? Are dogs and cats separated?
- **Exam rooms** -- What equipment is present (stethoscope, otoscope, ophthalmoscope, scale)?
- **Treatment area** -- Where medications are administered, blood draws happen, and minor procedures are performed
- **Surgery suite** -- If you are allowed to observe, notice the anesthesia equipment, monitoring machines, and sterile setup
- **Radiology/imaging** -- X-ray machines, ultrasound equipment
- **Laboratory** -- Where blood, urine, and fecal samples are analyzed
- **Pharmacy** -- Medication storage and dispensing area
- **Kennel/recovery area** -- Where dogs stay before and after surgery or during hospitalization

{{< drg/checklist title="Questions to Ask at the Vet Hospital" subtitle="Prepare these before your visit" >}}
- What is a typical day like for a veterinarian?
- What are the most common reasons dogs come in?
- What is the most challenging part of the job?
- What training and education did you need?
- How has veterinary medicine changed in recent years?
- What is the most rewarding part of working with animals?
- What preventive care do you recommend for every dog owner?
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

### Option B: Animal Shelter Visit

An animal shelter is a facility that takes in stray, abandoned, or surrendered animals and works to find them new homes. Shelters range from small, volunteer-run operations to large county-funded facilities.

**Areas to observe:**
- **Intake area** -- Where new animals arrive and are evaluated
- **Dog housing** -- Note kennel sizes, enrichment activities (toys, beds), and exercise areas
- **Medical area** -- Where incoming animals are vaccinated, spayed/neutered, and treated for illness
- **Adoption area** -- How dogs are presented to potential adopters
- **Volunteer and staff areas** -- Where training, coordination, and administration happen

{{< drg/checklist title="Questions to Ask at the Shelter" subtitle="Show genuine interest in their mission" >}}
- How many dogs do you take in each month? How many are adopted?
- What happens to dogs that are not adopted?
- What are the most common reasons people surrender their dogs?
- What does the adoption process look like? How do you screen adopters?
- How do volunteers help? What roles are available?
- What is the biggest challenge the shelter faces?
- What can the community do to help reduce the number of dogs in shelters?
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

### Writing Your Report

Your counselor will want more than "I visited a vet clinic." Prepare a thoughtful report that covers:

1. **Where you went** -- Name, location, type of facility
2. **Who you talked to** -- Names and roles of staff you interacted with
3. **What you saw** -- Specific areas, equipment, procedures, and animals
4. **What you learned** -- New information that surprised or impressed you
5. **Your reflections** -- How the visit changed or deepened your understanding of dog care
6. **Connections to requirements** -- How what you saw relates to topics you studied (vaccinations, first aid, responsible ownership)

{{< drg/tip >}}
Call ahead to schedule your visit. Most veterinary hospitals and shelters are happy to host Scouts working on merit badges, but they need advance notice to arrange a tour and designate staff to guide you. Explain that you are working on the Dog Care merit badge and ask if there is a time when they can accommodate a 30--60 minute visit.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
The first animal shelter in the United States was founded in 1866 by the American Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (ASPCA) in New York City. Today there are roughly 3,500 animal shelters operating across the country, and shelter adoption has become one of the most popular ways to find a new pet.
