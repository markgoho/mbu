This requirement gives you two options. Read both carefully and choose the one that fits your interests. Option A focuses on careers -- jobs you could do for a living. Option B focuses on hobbies and personal enrichment -- activities you pursue because you enjoy them.

### Option A: Dog-Related Careers

{{< drg/requirement number="10a" >}}
Identify three career opportunities that would use skills and knowledge in working with dogs. Pick one and research the training, education, certification requirements, experience, and expenses associated with entering the field. Research the prospects for employment, starting salary, advancement opportunities and career goals associated with this career. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and whether you might be interested in this career.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The world of dog-related careers is much broader than "veterinarian." Here are some paths to explore:

**Veterinarian**
- **Education:** 4-year bachelor's degree + 4-year Doctor of Veterinary Medicine (DVM) program
- **Licensing:** Must pass the NAVLE (North American Veterinary Licensing Examination)
- **Starting salary:** $90,000--$110,000
- **What you do:** Diagnose and treat illnesses, perform surgery, prescribe medications, provide preventive care

**Veterinary Technician**
- **Education:** 2-year associate's degree in veterinary technology (some states accept 4-year bachelor's)
- **Certification:** Must pass the VTNE (Veterinary Technician National Examination) in most states
- **Starting salary:** $35,000--$45,000
- **What you do:** Assist veterinarians with exams and surgery, take X-rays, draw blood, administer medications, educate pet owners

**Professional Dog Trainer**
- **Education:** No degree required, but certifications are strongly recommended (CPDT-KA, KPA CTP)
- **Certification cost:** $500--$3,000 for coursework and exams
- **Starting salary:** $30,000--$45,000 (varies widely; many are self-employed)
- **What you do:** Teach dogs obedience, solve behavior problems, prepare dogs for therapy or service work

**Dog Groomer**
- **Education:** Grooming school (3--6 months) or apprenticeship
- **Certification:** Optional but valued (National Dog Groomers Association of America)
- **Starting salary:** $25,000--$40,000 (experienced groomers and shop owners earn significantly more)
- **What you do:** Bathe, brush, trim, and style dogs' coats; trim nails; clean ears; identify skin conditions

**Canine Physical Therapist / Rehabilitation Specialist**
- **Education:** Veterinary or human physical therapy degree + CCRT or CCRP certification
- **Starting salary:** $50,000--$70,000
- **What you do:** Help dogs recover from surgery, injuries, and age-related conditions using exercises, hydrotherapy, and massage

**Search and Rescue Dog Handler**
- **Education:** Extensive volunteer training (1--3 years to become deployment-ready)
- **Salary:** Most SAR handlers are volunteers; some are paid by law enforcement or military
- **What you do:** Train and handle dogs that locate missing persons in wilderness, disaster, and urban settings

{{< drg/tip >}}
For your counselor meeting, pick the career that genuinely interests you most and go deep. Research specific schools, certification programs, job listings, and salary ranges in your area. Contact a professional in that field if possible -- most people are happy to answer questions from a Scout working on a merit badge.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### Option B: Dog-Related Hobbies

{{< drg/requirement number="10b" >}}
Identify how you might use the skills and knowledge in working with dogs to pursue a personal hobby and/or healthy lifestyle. Research the additional training required, expenses, and affiliation with organizations that would help you maximize the enjoyment and benefit you might gain from it. Discuss what you learned with your counselor and share what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursued this.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Dogs open the door to hobbies that combine physical activity, competition, and the joy of working with an animal partner:

**Dog Agility**
- Dogs navigate obstacle courses (jumps, tunnels, weave poles, A-frames) at speed while you direct them
- Organizations: AKC, USDAA (United States Dog Agility Association), NADAC
- Startup costs: $100--$300 for beginner classes; $500+ for home equipment
- Great for high-energy dogs and active owners

**Conformation (Dog Shows)**
- Dogs are judged against their breed standard for structure, movement, and temperament
- Organizations: AKC, UKC (United Kennel Club)
- Startup costs: $200--$500 per show (entry fees, travel, grooming supplies)
- Best for purebred dogs that meet their breed standard closely

**Canine Freestyle (Dancing with Dogs)**
- You and your dog perform choreographed routines set to music
- Organizations: World Canine Freestyle Organization, Canine Freestyle Federation
- Startup costs: Minimal (training classes, music)
- Any breed can participate

**Hiking and Backpacking with Dogs**
- Trail hiking with your dog, including multi-day backpacking trips where the dog carries its own gear
- Organizations: Local hiking clubs, AKC Trail Dog program
- Startup costs: $50--$200 (dog backpack, portable water bowl, leash/harness)
- Connects to other Scouting activities like the [Hiking merit badge](/merit-badges/hiking/)

**Therapy Dog Work**
- You and your dog visit hospitals, nursing homes, schools, and disaster sites to provide comfort
- Organizations: Alliance of Therapy Dogs, Pet Partners, Therapy Dogs International
- Requirements: Your dog must pass a temperament test and handler training ($50--$200)
- Deeply rewarding volunteer work

**Rally Obedience**
- A more relaxed form of competitive obedience where you and your dog navigate a course of signs, each indicating a specific obedience exercise
- Organizations: AKC Rally, UKC Rally
- Great stepping stone from basic obedience to competitive dog sports

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
The AKC Canine Good Citizen (CGC) test is the gateway certification for most therapy dog organizations. It tests 10 skills including accepting a friendly stranger, walking on a loose leash, sitting politely for petting, and reacting calmly to distractions. The test costs about $20 and can be taken at any AKC-certified evaluator.
