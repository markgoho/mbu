{{< drg/requirement number="6c" option="Attend an Esports Event" >}}
Attend an esports tournament, convention, or community event that includes a competitive gaming activity or demonstration. Attend with another Scout, troop member, or family member. Afterward, share with your counselor what you learned about the atmosphere of live esports events and how the experience changed your understanding of playing games competitively in front of an audience.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A screen can show you the gameplay, but it can't show you what it feels like to stand in a crowd that erupts the instant a match-winning play happens. This option asks you to experience that atmosphere directly, alongside someone else.

{{< drg/checklist title="Where to Look for Events" subtitle="Options exist at every scale" >}}
- Local game stores and community centers often host smaller tournaments or LAN events for popular titles.
- School or college esports programs sometimes hold open matches or showcase events.
- Gaming conventions in your region may include competitive stages or demo tournaments alongside exhibits.
- Larger regional or national tournaments, if one happens to be within reach, offer the full professional-event experience.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
Bring a specific question you're curious about — like how players warm up before a match, or how the crowd reacts differently to a close game versus a blowout. Having something specific to look for makes the experience easier to describe afterward.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

After the event, think about how the experience compares to playing the same game alone at home. Did the pressure of an audience change how you'd expect a player to perform? Did watching people compete in person shift how you think about your own relationship to competitive gaming?

## What to Bring

There's no official resource link for this option. Bring a short account of the event — what you saw, who you attended with, and how the live atmosphere compared to watching or playing on a screen — to share with your counselor.

You've completed Requirement 6. Requirement 7 shifts from playing and watching to thinking about competitive gaming as a career path.
