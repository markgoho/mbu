Understanding where video games and esports came from helps you appreciate the technology, design, and competitive structure of modern games. This requirement explores six decades of gaming history, major game genres, the distinction between casual gaming and esports, and key industry milestones.

## Requirement 1a: Video Game Evolution Since 1958

{{< drg/requirement number="1a" >}}
Create a timeline showing how video gaming has evolved since 1958. Include at least one significant event from each decade.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

To complete this requirement, you will create a timeline featuring key milestones across every decade from the 1950s to the present day.

### Decades of Gaming Evolution

- **1950s (1958)**: Physicist William Higinbotham creates *Tennis for Two* on an analog computer oscilloscope at Brookhaven National Laboratory—widely considered one of the earliest interactive electronic games.
- **1960s (1962)**: MIT students led by Steve Russell develop *Spacewar!* on a PDP-1 mainframe computer, demonstrating early digital computer graphics and two-player combat.
- **1970s (1972)**: Nolan Bushnell and Al Alcorn release *Pong* under Atari, launching the commercial arcade industry. Magnavox releases the Odyssey, the first home video game console.
- **1980s (1985)**: Nintendo releases the Nintendo Entertainment System (NES) in North America, reviving the video game industry following the crash of 1983 with iconic franchises like *Super Mario Bros.*
- **1990s (1995–1997)**: Sony launches the PlayStation, introducing 3D polygonal graphics to mainstream gaming. Early online multiplayer titles like *Quake* and *StarCraft* establish competitive PC gaming networks.
- **2000s (2002–2006)**: Broadband internet enables online console gaming via Xbox Live. Live streaming begins to take root, setting the stage for global esports broadcasting.
- **2010s (2011)**: Twitch launches, revolutionizing game broadcasting. Games like *League of Legends* and *Counter-Strike* fill physical arenas for world championships.
- **2020s (2020s)**: Cross-platform play becomes standard across console, PC, and mobile platforms, bringing global player bases together regardless of device.

## Requirement 1b: Major Video Game Genres

{{< drg/requirement number="1b" >}}
Identify and define five major video game genres. Provide an example for each.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A **genre** is a category of games defined by shared gameplay mechanics, control schemes, and victory conditions rather than visual style alone.

### Five Major Video Game Genres

1. **Multiplayer Online Battle Arena (MOBA)**: A strategy subgenre where two teams of players control individual characters ("champions" or "heroes") on a map, working together to destroy the opponent's main structure. *Example: League of Legends.*
2. **First-Person / Tactical Shooter (FPS)**: Games played from the direct perspective of the character, focusing on weapon handling, spatial awareness, and team tactics. *Example: Counter-Strike 2 or Valorant.*
3. **Sports & Racing Simulators**: Games that emulate real-world athletic events or vehicular racing, testing timing, tactical decision-making, and physical precision. *Example: Rocket League or EA Sports FC.*
4. **Real-Time Strategy (RTS)**: Strategy games where players gather resources, construct bases, and command armies simultaneously in real time rather than taking turns. *Example: StarCraft II.*
5. **Fighting Games**: One-on-one combat games focused on close-quarters martial arts or superpowers, requiring precise control inputs and frame-accurate timing. *Example: Street Fighter 6 or Super Smash Bros. Ultimate.*

## Requirement 1c: Video Gaming vs. Esports

{{< drg/requirement number="1c" >}}
Explain the difference between video gaming and esports. Include how esports incorporates elements of organized competition such as teams, tournaments, rules, coaching, and spectatorship. Provide two examples of games that are commonly played casually as video games but are also used in organized esports competition.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

While every esports match is played using a video game, not all video gaming is an esport.

{{< drg/image src="images/esports-vs-casual-gaming.avif" alt="Split-frame comparison of casual couch gaming versus an organized esports tournament stage with headsets, referee, and spectators" >}}

- **Casual Video Gaming**: Playing a game primarily for personal recreation, relaxation, or narrative enjoyment, without formal tournament structures, strict external rules, or spectators.
- **Esports (Electronic Sports)**: Organized, competitive video gaming where individuals or teams compete against one another under standardized rulebooks, officiated by referees, guided by coaches, and watched by spectators.

| Mode | Focus | Structure & Rules | Environment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Casual Gaming** | Recreation & Fun | Flexible, self-paced | Personal / casual play |
| **Esports** | Competition & Skill | Standardized rules, coaches & teams | Spectators & organized leagues |

### Key Elements of Organized Esports

- **Teams & Roster Positions**: Players fulfill specialized roles (e.g., captain, caller, support, entry) and train together regularly.
- **Tournaments & Leagues**: Structured brackets (single-elimination, double-elimination, round-robin) leading to season championships.
- **Rules & Referees**: Standardized game settings, banned items, anti-cheat enforcement, and match marshals.
- **Coaching & Analysis**: Coaches review match VODs (video on demand), develop strategic plays, and manage team communication.
- **Spectatorship & Broadcasting**: Matches are streamed globally with professional shoutcasters and analysts explaining play-by-play action.

### Dual-Purpose Game Examples

1. ***Rocket League***: Played casually by millions of players at home driving rocket-powered cars to hit a giant soccer ball. In organized esports (RLCS), professional teams execute high-speed aerial passes and rotational defense under strict tournament rules.
2. ***Super Smash Bros. Ultimate***: Played casually as a fun party game with items turned on. In esports tournaments, items and hazardous stages are disabled to create a pure skill-based 1v1 competition.

## Requirement 1d: Major Esports Milestones Since 1990

{{< drg/requirement number="1d" >}}
Identify and define five major moments in the development of esports as an industry since 1990. Include key tournaments, technological advances, and organizations that helped shape competitive gaming globally. Discuss your findings with your counselor.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Select five milestones that shaped esports into a global industry:

1. **1997 — Nintendo World Championships & Red Annihilation**: Dennis "Thresh" Fong wins John Carmack's Ferrari 328 GTS in the *Quake* Red Annihilation tournament, widely recognized as the first major professional gaming tournament.
2. **2000 — Formation of the Cyberathlete Professional League (CPL) & Korean e-Sports Association (KeSPA)**: KeSPA establishes government-supported esports infrastructure and television broadcasting for *StarCraft* in South Korea.
3. **2011 — Launch of Twitch.tv**: Justin.tv launches Twitch, giving gamers a dedicated live-streaming platform that transforms how esports tournaments reach global audiences.
4. **2013 — League of Legends Season 3 World Championship at Staples Center**: Riot Games sells out the Los Angeles Staples Center in minutes, establishing esports as a mainstream arena-filling spectator event.
5. **2018 — High School & Varsity Collegiate Esports Boom**: Organizations like NACE (National Association of Collegiate Esports) and PlayVS establish official high school and college esports leagues offering varsity scholarships.
