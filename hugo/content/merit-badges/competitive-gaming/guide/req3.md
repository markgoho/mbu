## Requirement 3a: Comparing Positive and Negative Research

{{< drg/requirement number="3a" >}}
Read two credible articles describing positive aspects of video gaming and two describing negative aspects. Create a chart showing where the articles agree or disagree.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

To complete this requirement, read two articles highlighting the positive effects of gaming and two articles covering potential risks. Then organize your findings into a comparison chart.

### Sample Comparison Chart Structure

| Topic / Metric | Positive Research Findings | Negative Research Findings | Area of Agreement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cognitive Skills** | Improves spatial reasoning, quick problem-solving, and hand-eye coordination. | Excessive session lengths can reduce attention span for non-interactive tasks. | Moderate gaming sharpens mental reflexes, but length matters. |
| **Social Connection** | Builds strong teamwork, communication, and global peer friendships. | Online spaces can foster toxicity, social isolation, or cyberbullying. | Social outcomes depend heavily on community norms and player behavior. |
| **Physical Impact** | Active VR/motion games promote movement and hand dexterity. | Sedentary play can contribute to poor posture, eye strain, and sleep disruption. | Extended inactive screen time requires intentional physical breaks. |

## Requirement 3b: Two-Week Gaming Log

{{< drg/requirement number="3b" >}}
Track the amount of gaming you do each day for two weeks. Record and summarize your results.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Keep a accurate log of every gaming session over a 14-day period. Record the game played, start time, end time, and total daily hours.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Be honest with your log! The goal of this exercise is not to achieve a "perfect" score, but to gather clear personal data so you can understand your daily habits.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Requirement 3c: Two-Week Physical Activity Log & CDC Comparison

{{< drg/requirement number="3c" >}}
Track your daily physical activity for the same two weeks. Compare your results to the CDC recommendation of one hour of moderate-to-vigorous activity per day for youth ages 6-17.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

During the exact same 14 days, record your daily physical activity (e.g., hiking, troop sports, bicycling, running, swimming, or yard work).

- **CDC Guideline**: **At least 60 minutes (1 hour)** of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity daily for youth ages 6–17.
- **Comparison**: Compare your daily logged minutes against the 60-minute target. Note which days met or fell below the recommendation.

## Requirement 3d: Two-Week Sleep Log & CDC Comparison

{{< drg/requirement number="3d" >}}
Track your daily sleep for the same two weeks. Compare your results to the CDC recommendation of 9-12 hours for ages 6-12 and 8-10 hours for ages 13-18.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Track the exact time you go to sleep each night and wake up each morning over the 14-day period.

- **CDC Sleep Guidelines**:
  - **Ages 6–12**: 9 to 12 hours per night.
  - **Ages 13–18**: 8 to 10 hours per night.
- **Evaluation**: Check how late-night gaming sessions affected your total sleep hours and morning alertness.

## Requirement 3e: Creating a One-Week Balanced Schedule

{{< drg/requirement number="3e" >}}
After completing 3(b), 3(c), and 3(d), create a one-week balanced schedule that includes healthy sleep, physical activity, and agreed-upon gaming time. Follow this schedule and summarize your results.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Using your 14 days of data, design a balanced 7-day schedule that allocates realistic time for:

1. **Sleep**: Guaranteeing 8–10 hours each night.
2. **Physical Activity**: 60 minutes daily of sports or exercise.
3. **School & Household Responsibilities**: Homework, chores, and family meals first.
4. **Agreed-upon Gaming Time**: Scheduled blocks of gaming after daily priorities are met.

Follow your new schedule for one week, then write a short summary describing how the schedule impacted your energy, focus, and gaming enjoyment.
