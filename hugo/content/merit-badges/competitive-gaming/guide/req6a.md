{{< drg/requirement number="6a" option="Teach Digital Citizenship" >}}
Teach your troop, patrol, or a group of younger Scouts or family members about online gaming safety or digital citizenship.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Teaching something is one of the fastest ways to discover how well you actually understand it. You already have plenty of material to draw from — Req 2's work on toxicity and the Scout Oath and Law, and Req 4's list of ten online safety strategies, both make excellent foundations for a lesson.

{{< drg/checklist title="Planning Your Lesson" subtitle="Keep it focused — 15–20 minutes is plenty" >}}
- Pick 2–3 concrete topics instead of trying to cover everything (for example: privacy settings, spotting scams, and handling toxic players).
- Use real examples, not just abstract rules — a short story about a real or hypothetical scam attempt sticks better than a list of dos and don'ts.
- Build in a moment of practice, like having your audience walk through a privacy setting on their own device.
- Leave time for questions — younger Scouts and family members often have specific situations they want advice on.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
Scouting's EDGE method — Explain, Demonstrate, Guide, Enable — works well here. Explain a safety concept, demonstrate it on a real device or screen, guide your audience through trying it themselves, then let them practice on their own.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## What to Bring

There's no official resource link for this option — instead, come prepared with a short outline of your lesson and, if possible, a device to demonstrate settings live. Bring notes on how the session went so you can discuss it with your counselor afterward: what questions came up, what surprised your audience, and what you'd change if you taught it again.
