{{< drg/requirement number="5d" >}}
Repair a leaky hose or connector.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A hose leak often looks dramatic because the water sprays in all directions, but the underlying cause is usually simple: a split hose end, worn washer, loose fitting, or damaged connector. This requirement is a good reminder that some of the best repairs start with the smallest parts.

{{< drg/video
    title="How to Repair a Garden Hose (video)"
    url="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aOmnHQMBEW4" >}}

## What to Check First

- Is the leak at the hose body or at the end fitting?
- Is a washer missing or worn out?
- Are the threads damaged or dirty?
- Is the connector tightened correctly but still leaking?

The advantage of this repair is that the system is easy to test. Turn the water on slowly and watch exactly where the leak starts.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Test outdoors or in an area where water runoff is safe. Seeing the leak clearly is easier when you are not also trying to protect nearby flooring or walls.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

This is one of the most accessible plumbing repairs in the badge because the cause-and-effect is easy to see. It is also a great first lesson in why a $1 washer can matter more than a whole new hose.
