Electricity powers civilization, and the people who design, build, maintain, and improve electrical systems are always in demand. The skills you have built during this merit badge — understanding circuits, safety, wiring, and energy — are the foundation of many rewarding careers.

## Career Paths in Electricity

Here are several career areas where electrical skills and knowledge are central. Browse through them and choose one to research in depth.

### Electrician

Electricians install, maintain, and repair electrical wiring and equipment in homes, commercial buildings, and industrial facilities. They read blueprints, pull wire through conduit, install panels and outlets, troubleshoot problems, and ensure everything meets the National Electrical Code.

- **Education:** High school diploma, then a 4–5 year apprenticeship combining classroom instruction with paid on-the-job training
- **Certification:** Must pass a state licensing exam (requirements vary by state); many also earn specialty certifications
- **Starting salary:** $40,000–$50,000/year (apprentice); $60,000–$80,000+/year (journeyman)
- **Outlook:** The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects 6% job growth through 2032 — faster than average — driven by new construction, renewable energy installations, and electric vehicle infrastructure

### Electrical Engineer

Electrical engineers design electrical systems, components, and devices — from power generation equipment to consumer electronics to medical devices. They work in offices, laboratories, and field sites.

- **Education:** Bachelor's degree in electrical engineering (4 years); many pursue a master's degree
- **Certification:** Professional Engineer (PE) license available after passing the FE and PE exams
- **Starting salary:** $70,000–$85,000/year; experienced engineers earn $100,000–$150,000+
- **Outlook:** Strong demand in renewable energy, autonomous vehicles, telecommunications, and semiconductor design

### Renewable Energy Technician

These technicians install, maintain, and repair solar panels, wind turbines, and battery storage systems. As the energy grid transitions toward renewables, demand for these specialists is surging.

- **Education:** Associate degree or technical certificate in renewable energy technology; some employers accept on-the-job training
- **Certification:** NABCEP (North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners) for solar; manufacturer certifications for wind
- **Starting salary:** $40,000–$55,000/year; experienced technicians with certifications earn $65,000–$80,000+
- **Outlook:** Wind turbine technician is one of the fastest-growing occupations in the country, with projected growth over 44% through 2032

Search for the job title and you will find detailed information about duties, education requirements, salary data, and job outlook — all the information your counselor will want to discuss.

### Power Plant Operator

Power plant operators control the equipment that generates electricity. They monitor instruments, adjust controls, and manage the systems that produce power from coal, natural gas, nuclear, hydroelectric, or other sources.

- **Education:** High school diploma plus extensive on-the-job training (1–4 years); nuclear plant operators require NRC licensing
- **Starting salary:** $50,000–$65,000/year; experienced operators earn $85,000–$100,000+
- **Outlook:** Stable demand as experienced operators retire; nuclear plant operators are especially difficult to replace

### Lineworker (Line Technician)

Lineworkers build and maintain the power lines that carry electricity from plants to homes. They work outdoors — often at heights of 100+ feet — in all weather conditions. The work is physically demanding and requires comfort with heights and high voltage.

- **Education:** Associate degree, technical certificate, or apprenticeship
- **Starting salary:** $50,000–$60,000/year; experienced lineworkers earn $75,000–$100,000+
- **Outlook:** Strong demand driven by grid modernization and storm damage repair

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Lineworkers are often the first responders after major storms, working around the clock to restore power. After hurricanes, utility companies send mutual aid crews from across the country — sometimes thousands of lineworkers driving hundreds of miles to help communities get their lights back on.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

## Researching Your Career

For your counselor discussion, gather information on:

{{< drg/checklist title="Career Research Checklist" subtitle="Topics to cover for your chosen career" >}}
- Job duties and daily responsibilities.
- Education or training required (degree, apprenticeship, technical school).
- Certifications or licenses needed and how to obtain them.
- Estimated cost of education or training.
- Starting salary and salary range for experienced workers.
- Job growth outlook and employment prospects in your area.
- Advancement opportunities (foreman, master electrician, project manager, etc.).
- What you find most and least appealing about the career.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}
