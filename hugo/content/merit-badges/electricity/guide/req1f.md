{{< drg/requirement number="1f" >}}
Explain what to do in the event of an electrical fire.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The smell of burning plastic, a wisp of smoke coming from an outlet, sparks behind a wall plate — electrical fires can start with subtle signs, but they spread fast. Electrical fires are the third leading cause of house fires in the United States, responsible for roughly 50,000 fires each year. Knowing how to respond — and how NOT to respond — can save your home and your life.

## Why Electrical Fires Are Different

An electrical fire is a **Class C fire** — a fire involving energized electrical equipment. This classification matters because it determines which type of extinguisher you can use. Throwing water on an energized electrical fire can electrocute you, because water conducts electricity. The water creates a path for current to flow from the burning equipment through the stream of water and into your body.

## What to Do

### 1. Cut the Power (If Safe)

If you can safely reach the circuit breaker panel, turn off the breaker for the affected circuit — or the main breaker if you are unsure which circuit is involved. De-energizing the circuit removes the electrical source that is fueling the fire and converts it from a Class C fire to a Class A fire (ordinary combustibles), which is easier and safer to fight.

### 2. Call 911

Even a small electrical fire warrants a 911 call. Fires inside walls can smolder for hours before breaking through, and what looks like a small flare-up at an outlet may already be burning inside the wall cavity.

### 3. Use the Right Extinguisher

If the fire is small and you have the right extinguisher, you may be able to put it out:

| Fire Extinguisher Type | Use on Electrical Fires? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Class C** (CO2 or dry chemical) | Yes | Specifically rated for energized electrical equipment |
| **ABC multipurpose** (dry chemical) | Yes | The most common home extinguisher; the "C" means it works on electrical fires |
| **Class A** (water) | Only if power is OFF | Never use water on an energized electrical fire |
| **Class B** (foam) | No | Some foams conduct electricity |

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
**Never use water on an electrical fire unless you have confirmed the power is off.** Water conducts electricity and can electrocute you. Even a damp towel thrown on a sparking outlet could transmit current through your hand. Use a Class C or ABC extinguisher, or cut the power first.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

### 4. Use the PASS Technique

If you use a fire extinguisher, remember **PASS**:

- **P** — Pull the pin
- **A** — Aim at the base of the fire (not the flames)
- **S** — Squeeze the handle
- **S** — Sweep side to side at the base of the fire

### 5. Get Out If the Fire Grows

If the fire spreads beyond a small, contained area — or if the room fills with smoke — stop trying to fight it and evacuate immediately. Close doors behind you to slow the fire's spread. Meet your family at your predetermined meeting point and wait for firefighters.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Check the fire extinguisher in your home right now. Look at the label — does it say "ABC" or "Class C"? If it only says "Class A," it is a water-based extinguisher and should NOT be used on electrical fires. Most hardware stores sell ABC multipurpose extinguishers for under $30.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Preventing Electrical Fires

Prevention is always better than response. Common causes of electrical fires include:

- **Overloaded circuits** — Too many devices plugged into one circuit (you will learn more about this in [Req 6a](/merit-badges/electricity/guide/req6a/))
- **Damaged cords** — Frayed, pinched, or cracked power cords
- **Faulty wiring** — Old aluminum wiring, loose connections, or amateur installations
- **Space heaters** — Placed too close to curtains, bedding, or furniture
- **Extension cord misuse** — Using extension cords as permanent wiring or daisy-chaining multiple cords

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Smoke from an Outlet" >}}
You notice a faint burning smell in the living room and see thin smoke rising from a wall outlet behind the couch.

- **Do not unplug anything from the smoking outlet** — the outlet itself may be the hazard.
- **Go to the breaker panel** and turn off the breaker for that room.
- **Call 911** and report an electrical fire.
- **If flames appear**, use an ABC extinguisher with the PASS technique. Aim at the base of the fire.
- **If the fire grows or smoke thickens**, evacuate and close the door behind you.
- **Do not re-enter** until firefighters say it is safe.
