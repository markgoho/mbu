{{< drg/requirement number="1a" >}}
Explain how to turn off power for a particular circuit and the whole house in the event of an emergency.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A sparking outlet, the smell of burning plastic behind a wall, water pouring onto an electrical panel — in any of these situations, the first thing you need to do is cut the power. Knowing where your electrical panel is and how to operate it before an emergency happens is like knowing where the fire extinguisher is. You do not want to be searching for it in a crisis.

## Your Home's Electrical Panel

Every home has a main electrical panel (sometimes called a breaker box or fuse box). It is usually a gray metal box mounted on a wall in a basement, garage, utility room, or on the outside of the house. Inside, you will find rows of circuit breakers (or fuses, in older homes) that control individual circuits throughout the house.

### Finding Your Panel

Walk through your home right now and locate the electrical panel. Open the door and look at what is inside. You should see:

- **Individual circuit breakers** — Each one controls a specific circuit (a group of outlets, lights, or appliances). They are labeled with toggles that say ON and OFF.
- **A main breaker** — Usually at the top of the panel and larger than the others. This one controls power to the entire house.
- **A circuit directory** — A list (often on the inside of the panel door) that identifies which breaker controls which part of the house.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Many circuit directories are incomplete or outdated. Before you need it in an emergency, take time to map your home's circuits. Turn off one breaker at a time and walk through the house to see which outlets and lights go dark. Label each breaker clearly with a permanent marker. This is a great project to do with a parent or guardian.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Turning Off Power to a Single Circuit

If you need to cut power to just one area — say, a sparking outlet in the kitchen — find the correct breaker in the panel and flip it to the OFF position. The toggle should move to the opposite side from the ON position. On most panels, OFF means the toggle points toward the outside edge of the panel.

**Before you flip the breaker:**
1. Stand on a dry surface (never in standing water).
2. Use one hand only — keep the other hand at your side or behind your back. This reduces the chance of current passing across your chest if something goes wrong.
3. Turn your face away from the panel as you flip the switch. In rare cases, a faulty breaker can arc.

## Turning Off Power to the Whole House

If the situation is more serious — flooding near the panel, a fire in the walls, or you cannot identify which circuit is the problem — use the main breaker to shut off all power to the house at once. The main breaker is almost always the largest switch in the panel, located at the top. Flip it to OFF.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
If the area around the panel is flooded or you see sparking or fire at the panel itself, **do not touch it**. Call 911 and your utility company instead. They can shut off power at the meter or the transformer. Your safety comes before any attempt to cut power.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Fuse Boxes in Older Homes

Some older homes still use fuse boxes instead of breaker panels. Instead of toggle switches, fuses are screw-in glass cylinders or cylindrical cartridges. To cut power to a circuit, you unscrew the fuse. To cut power to the whole house, pull the main fuse block (a large rectangular piece that pulls straight out).

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
A standard household circuit in the United States operates at 120 volts. Large appliances like electric dryers, ovens, and central air conditioning units use 240-volt circuits with double-sized breakers. The main breaker controls the full 240 volts coming into your home.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

{{< drg/checklist title="Emergency Power Shutdown" subtitle="Steps to follow in an electrical emergency" >}}
- Assess the situation: Determine whether you need to cut one circuit or all power.
- Check for water: Never touch the panel if the floor is wet or the panel is damaged.
- Use one hand: Keep the other hand at your side to reduce shock risk.
- Flip the breaker: Turn the individual breaker or main breaker to the OFF position.
- Verify power is off: Check that lights and devices on the circuit are no longer working.
- Call for help: If the situation involves fire, water, or injury, call 911.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}
