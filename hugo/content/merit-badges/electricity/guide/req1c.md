{{< drg/requirement number="1c" >}}
Describe how to safely get out of a car in an accident if you suspect a utility wire is on the car.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A car accident on a stormy night, a fallen tree dragging a power line across the road — and now a high-voltage wire is draped across the hood of the vehicle you are sitting in. This is one of the most dangerous electrical scenarios you can encounter, and the correct response is the opposite of what most people would do.

## Stay in the Car

If a power line is on or near your vehicle and you are not in immediate danger (the car is not on fire), **stay inside the car.** The tires insulate you from the ground. As long as you remain inside the vehicle and do not touch the metal frame while also touching the ground, the current has no path through your body.

- Turn off the engine.
- Keep your hands in your lap — do not touch the doors, the steering column, or anything connected to the car's body.
- Call 911 and tell them a power line is on your vehicle. Give them your exact location.
- Warn anyone approaching to stay at least 35 feet away. Shout through a closed window if you need to.
- Wait for the utility company to arrive and confirm the power is off.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
A downed high-voltage power line can energize the ground around it. The voltage spreads outward in rings, like ripples in a pond. The ground near the wire can be lethal even if the wire is not touching you directly. This is called **step potential** — the voltage difference between your two feet can be enough to kill you if you are standing near the wire.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## When You Must Exit: The Bunny Hop

There is one situation where you must leave the vehicle: **if it is on fire.** A fire that reaches the fuel tank can cause an explosion, and that threat overrides the electrical danger. But you cannot simply step out of the car. The moment one foot touches the ground while the other is still on the vehicle, you create a path for current to flow through your body.

Here is the correct exit technique:

1. **Open the door** without touching the metal frame with your bare skin (use your elbow or a cloth).
2. **Jump clear** of the vehicle — both feet leaving the car at the same time, landing with your feet together. Do not let any part of your body touch the car and the ground simultaneously.
3. **Shuffle or hop away** with your feet together, taking tiny bunny hops. Never take a normal stride — the voltage difference between your front foot and back foot (step potential) can electrocute you.
4. **Keep hopping** until you are at least 35 feet away from the vehicle and any downed wires.
5. Once clear, call 911 if you have not already.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Practice the bunny hop technique on dry ground so the motion feels natural. In a real emergency, you will not have time to think through the steps. Jump clear, feet together, tiny hops away. Repeat that in your head until it is automatic.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## What NOT to Do

- **Do not touch the wire.** Even if it looks dead, it may be energized or could become re-energized at any moment as utility workers try to restore power.
- **Do not try to move the wire** with a stick, branch, or any other object. Utility lines can carry thousands of volts — far more than household current.
- **Do not walk or run normally** near a downed wire. Use the shuffle or bunny hop to avoid step potential.
- **Do not drive over a downed line.** If you can drive away safely without going over the wire, do so. But if the wire is on the vehicle, driving could drag it further or cause the wire to arc.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
A typical residential power line carries 7,200 volts, and high-voltage transmission lines can carry 100,000 to 765,000 volts. Unlike household circuits, which have breakers that trip in milliseconds, a downed utility line can remain energized for hours because the utility company's recloser keeps trying to restore power automatically.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Wire on the Car After a Storm" >}}
You are a passenger in a car that has just been hit by a falling tree branch, and a power line has come down across the trunk.

- **Stay calm and stay inside.** The car's tires are insulating you from the ground.
- **Turn off the engine** and keep your hands in your lap.
- **Call 911.** Tell them a power line is on your vehicle and give your location.
- **Warn bystanders** to stay at least 35 feet away. Yell through the window.
- **If the car catches fire:** Open the door, jump with both feet clear, and bunny hop away at least 35 feet.
- **Wait** for the utility company to confirm the line is de-energized before approaching the vehicle.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}
