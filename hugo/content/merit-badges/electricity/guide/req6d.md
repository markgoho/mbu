{{< drg/requirement number="6d" >}}
Tell how to find a blown fuse and a tripped circuit breaker in your home. Show how to safely reset the circuit breaker.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The power goes out in one room but the rest of the house is fine. That is almost always a tripped breaker or a blown fuse. Knowing how to find the problem and restore power safely is a practical skill you will use for the rest of your life.

## Finding a Tripped Circuit Breaker

Open your electrical panel and look at the breakers. Most breakers have three positions:

- **ON** — The toggle is pushed firmly to one side (usually toward the center of the panel)
- **OFF** — The toggle is pushed firmly to the opposite side
- **Tripped** — The toggle sits in a middle position, between ON and OFF, and may feel loose

Some breakers have a small colored indicator that appears when the breaker has tripped — often an orange or red stripe visible through a window on the breaker face.

The tripped breaker will be the one that is not fully in the ON position. On a panel with 20 or more breakers, it can be subtle — look carefully at each one.

{{< drg/tip >}}
If your panel is well-labeled (as you may have done during your [Req 2](/merit-badges/electricity/guide/req2/) inspection), you can go straight to the breaker that serves the room where power was lost. If the panel is not labeled, you may need to check every breaker to find the tripped one.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Safely Resetting a Circuit Breaker

Before you reset the breaker, address the reason it tripped:

1. **Unplug or turn off** some of the devices on the affected circuit. If it tripped because of an overload, resetting it with the same load will just trip it again immediately.
2. **Check for obvious problems** — damaged cords, sparking outlets, or a burning smell. If you find any of these, do not reset the breaker. Call an electrician.

### Reset Procedure

1. Stand on a dry surface with dry hands.
2. Turn the tripped breaker fully to the **OFF** position first. You must push it past the tripped (middle) position to OFF before you can turn it back ON.
3. Then firmly push the breaker to the **ON** position.
4. Listen for the click — it should snap into place.
5. Check that the power has been restored in the affected room.

If the breaker trips again immediately after resetting, there may be a short circuit somewhere on the circuit. Do not keep resetting it — call a licensed electrician.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
If the breaker trips repeatedly, if you smell burning, or if you see scorch marks on the panel, stop. These are signs of a serious wiring problem — possibly a short circuit or a loose connection arcing inside a wall. Only a licensed electrician should diagnose and repair these issues.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Finding a Blown Fuse

In a home with a fuse box instead of breakers:

1. Open the fuse box and look at each fuse. Screw-in fuses have a small glass window on the face.
2. A **blown fuse from an overload** will show a broken metal strip visible through a cloudy window.
3. A **blown fuse from a short circuit** will show a blackened, discolored window — the intense arc carbonized the glass.
4. If you are not sure which fuse is blown, you can use a continuity tester or multimeter to check each one.

### Replacing a Blown Fuse

1. Turn the main fuse switch or disconnect to OFF.
2. Unscrew the blown fuse by turning it counterclockwise.
3. Replace it with a **new fuse of the exact same amperage rating.** A 15-amp fuse must be replaced with a 15-amp fuse. Never substitute a higher-rated fuse.
4. Screw the new fuse in firmly.
5. Turn the main switch back ON.
6. Check that power is restored.

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Power Out in the Bathroom" >}}
You are getting ready in the morning and the bathroom lights, fan, and outlet all go dead at the same time. The rest of the house has power.

- **Go to the panel.** Look for a breaker in the tripped (middle) position. It may be labeled "Bathroom" or "Bath/Hall."
- **Identify the cause.** Were you running a hair dryer and a curling iron at the same time? That is likely an overload — together they can draw 25+ amps on a 20-amp circuit.
- **Reduce the load.** Unplug the hair dryer and curling iron.
- **Reset the breaker.** Push it fully to OFF, then firmly to ON.
- **Avoid repeating the problem.** Run high-draw appliances one at a time.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}
