Show how to render first aid to a person who is unconscious from an apparent electrical shock.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A person who has been shocked badly enough to lose consciousness is in serious danger. Electrical current can disrupt the heart's rhythm, stop breathing, cause internal burns along the current's path, and damage muscles and organs. Every second counts.

## Before You Touch the Victim

Before you provide any first aid, make absolutely sure the victim is no longer in contact with the electrical source. As you learned in [Req 1b](/merit-badges/electricity/guide/req1b/), touching an energized victim will shock you too. Confirm that:

- The power has been shut off, **or**
- The victim has been separated from the source using a non-conducting object.

Once the victim is clear of the source, you can safely touch them.

## First Aid for an Unconscious Shock Victim

### 1. Call 911 Immediately

Every electrical shock that causes unconsciousness requires emergency medical care — even if the person wakes up quickly. Electrical current can cause delayed cardiac arrhythmias (irregular heartbeats) that appear normal at first but become life-threatening hours later.

### 2. Check for Breathing and Pulse

- **Look** at the chest — is it rising and falling?
- **Listen** near the mouth and nose for breath sounds.
- **Feel** for a pulse at the carotid artery (the side of the neck, just below the jawline).

### 3. Begin CPR If Needed

If the person is not breathing or has no pulse, begin CPR immediately if you are trained:

- **30 chest compressions** — Push hard and fast in the center of the chest, at least 2 inches deep, at a rate of 100–120 compressions per minute.
- **2 rescue breaths** — Tilt the head back, lift the chin, pinch the nose, and give two breaths. Watch for the chest to rise.
- **Continue** the cycle of 30 compressions and 2 breaths until emergency responders arrive or the person begins breathing on their own.

If an **AED (automated external defibrillator)** is available, use it as soon as possible. AEDs are designed for exactly this situation — a heart thrown into an abnormal rhythm by electrical shock. Follow the AED's voice prompts. It will analyze the heart rhythm and deliver a shock only if needed.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Do not move a shock victim unless they are in immediate danger (fire, gas leak, continuing electrical hazard). Electrical shock can cause spinal injuries from muscle contractions or falls. Moving the victim could worsen a spinal injury. Keep them still and let paramedics handle movement.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

### 4. Treat for Shock

Even if the person is breathing and has a pulse, they may be in medical shock (a dangerous drop in blood pressure). Signs include:

- Pale, cool, clammy skin
- Rapid, weak pulse
- Shallow, rapid breathing
- Confusion or anxiety
- Nausea

To treat for shock:

- Lay the person on their back on a flat surface.
- Elevate the legs about 12 inches (unless you suspect a spinal injury, head injury, or broken legs).
- Keep them warm with a blanket or coat.
- Do not give them anything to eat or drink.

### 5. Look for Burns

Electrical current enters the body at one point and exits at another, burning tissue along the entire path. Check for:

- **Entry wound** — often on the hands (where the person touched the source)
- **Exit wound** — often on the feet or the point of contact with the ground

These burns may look small on the surface but can involve severe damage to deeper tissues. Do not apply ice or butter. Cover the burns loosely with a dry, sterile bandage. You will learn more about treating electrical burns in the [next requirement](/merit-badges/electricity/guide/req1e/).

{{< drg/tip >}}
If you are not trained in CPR, consider taking a course through the American Red Cross or American Heart Association. CPR certification is valuable well beyond this merit badge — it is a life skill. Many Scout councils offer CPR training as part of their program.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

{{< drg/checklist title="Electrical Shock First Aid" subtitle="Steps in order of priority" >}}
- Ensure the victim is separated from the electrical source before touching them.
- Call 911 or have someone call immediately.
- Check for breathing and pulse.
- Begin CPR if the person is not breathing or has no pulse.
- Use an AED if one is available.
- Treat for medical shock — lay flat, elevate legs, keep warm.
- Check for entry and exit burn wounds.
- Do not move the victim unless they are in immediate danger.
- Stay with the victim until emergency responders arrive.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}
