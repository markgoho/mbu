{{< drg/requirement number="2" >}}
Complete an electrical home safety inspection of your home, using the checklist found in the Electricity merit badge pamphlet or one approved by your counselor. Discuss what you find with your counselor.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You have spent the previous seven requirements learning how to respond to electrical emergencies. Now you are going to become the expert who prevents them. A home electrical safety inspection walks you room by room through your house, looking for the hazards that cause electrical fires, shocks, and injuries.

## How to Conduct Your Inspection

Set aside about an hour and work methodically through your home. Bring a notebook or use a checklist to record what you find. Inspect every room, including the garage, basement, laundry room, and outdoor areas. Do not skip the rooms you rarely use — those are often where problems hide.

### What to Look For

#### Outlets and Switches

- **Warm or discolored outlet covers** — An outlet that feels warm to the touch or has brown/black marks may have a loose connection arcing behind the wall plate. This is a fire hazard.
- **Cracked or broken plates** — Missing or damaged cover plates expose live wiring.
- **Loose-fitting plugs** — If a plug wobbles or falls out easily, the outlet contacts are worn. This creates resistance, which produces heat.
- **Outlets near water** — Kitchens, bathrooms, garages, and outdoor areas should have GFCI (Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter) outlets. These outlets have "Test" and "Reset" buttons. Press "Test" — the power should cut off. Press "Reset" to restore it. If the GFCI does not trip when tested, it needs to be replaced.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Test every GFCI outlet in your home during your inspection. Press the "Test" button — a small lamp or phone charger plugged into the outlet should lose power immediately. Then press "Reset" to restore it. If a GFCI fails to trip, write it down as a finding for your counselor discussion.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

#### Cords and Plugs

- **Frayed, cracked, or damaged cords** — Inspect the power cords on appliances, lamps, and electronics. Damaged insulation exposes live conductors.
- **Cords under rugs or through doorways** — Walking on cords damages the insulation over time. Cords pinched by doors can short circuit.
- **Extension cords used as permanent wiring** — Extension cords are designed for temporary use. If one has been plugged in for months or years, that is a hazard.
- **Overloaded power strips** — A power strip with every outlet full and additional cords daisy-chained into it is a fire waiting to happen.

#### Appliances

- **Space heaters** — Must be at least 3 feet from anything flammable (curtains, bedding, furniture, paper).
- **Appliances near water** — Hair dryers, radios, and phone chargers should never rest on the edge of a sink or bathtub.
- **Appliance condition** — Look for cracked housings, exposed wiring, or burn marks on appliances.

#### Electrical Panel

- **Accessible** — The panel should not be blocked by storage, furniture, or clutter. Keep at least 3 feet clear.
- **Properly labeled** — Each breaker should be labeled with the area or circuit it controls.
- **No missing breakers** — Every breaker slot should be filled or properly covered. An open slot exposes live bus bars inside the panel.
- **No signs of overheating** — Look for melted plastic, burn marks, or a burning smell.

#### Light Bulbs

- **Correct wattage** — A 100-watt bulb in a fixture rated for 60 watts generates excess heat that can melt the socket or ignite nearby materials. Check the label inside each fixture.
- **Compact spaces** — Closet lights and recessed fixtures are especially vulnerable to overheating. Make sure bulbs are the correct type and wattage.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
According to the Electrical Safety Foundation International, electrical malfunctions are the second leading cause of house fires in the United States, causing approximately $1.5 billion in property damage annually. Many of these fires start with hazards that a simple home inspection could catch.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

## Recording Your Findings

For each room, note:
- What you inspected
- Any problems you found
- The severity (immediate hazard vs. something to fix soon)
- Recommended action (replace cord, install GFCI, move space heater, etc.)

When you discuss your findings with your counselor, be specific. Do not just say "everything looked fine." Your counselor will want to know what you checked, what you found, and what you would recommend to make your home safer.

{{< drg/image src="images/home-inspection-checklist.avif" alt="A Scout crouching beside a wall outlet, using a flashlight to inspect the outlet plate while holding a clipboard with a checklist" >}}

{{< drg/checklist title="Quick Inspection Checklist" subtitle="Key items to check in every room" >}}
- Outlet covers are intact and none feel warm to the touch.
- GFCI outlets are present near water and pass the Test/Reset check.
- No cords are frayed, cracked, under rugs, or through doorways.
- Extension cords are not used as permanent wiring.
- Power strips are not overloaded or daisy-chained.
- Space heaters are at least 3 feet from flammable materials.
- Light bulbs match the fixture's rated wattage.
- Electrical panel is accessible, labeled, and shows no signs of damage.
- Smoke detectors near the panel and bedrooms are working.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}
