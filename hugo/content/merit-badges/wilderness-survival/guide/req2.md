Priorities for Survival. Explain the importance of each of the seven priorities of survival in a wilderness location.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You're lost. The trail disappeared an hour ago, your phone has no signal, and the sun is dropping fast. You have your pack, your wits, and maybe four hours of daylight. What do you do *first*?

The seven priorities of survival answer that question. They give you a decision-making framework so you don't waste precious time and energy on the wrong things. Learn them in order — that order matters.

## The Seven Priorities

### 1. STOP (Sit, Think, Observe, Plan)

The first priority isn't an action — it's the *absence* of action. When you realize you're in a survival situation, your instinct is to do something immediately. Fight that instinct. Sit down. Breathe. Think.

**S** — Sit down. Stop moving.
**T** — Think about your situation calmly.
**O** — Observe your surroundings: landmarks, weather, available resources.
**P** — Plan your next steps based on facts, not fear.

Panicked people walk in circles, burn through energy, and make injuries worse. The STOP protocol breaks the panic cycle. You'll learn more about this in [Req 3](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req3/).

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Search and rescue teams report that lost hikers who stay put after realizing they're lost are found an average of three times faster than those who keep moving. Moving makes you a moving target — and you might walk away from rescuers heading toward your last known position.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

### 2. First Aid

Once you're calm and have a plan, take care of any injuries. An untreated wound, sprain, or case of dehydration will get worse over time and drain your ability to handle everything else. Apply the backcountry first aid skills you learned in [Req 1a](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req1a/).

Check everyone in your group. Adrenaline can mask injuries — someone might not realize they're bleeding or that an ankle is sprained until the adrenaline fades.

### 3. Shelter

Exposure kills faster than thirst or hunger. In extreme cold, you can become hypothermic in under three hours. In extreme heat, heatstroke can be fatal within minutes. Your third priority is getting out of the elements.

Shelter doesn't have to be elaborate. A space blanket draped over a low branch, a pile of leaves against a fallen log, or even huddling in a rock alcove can save your life. What matters is that it blocks wind, rain, and direct sun. You'll dive deep into shelter-building in [Req 5](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req5a/).

### 4. Fire

Fire addresses multiple survival needs at once:

- **Warmth** — prevents and treats hypothermia
- **Water purification** — boiling is the most reliable method
- **Signaling** — smoke is visible for miles during the day
- **Morale** — the psychological comfort of a fire is enormous
- **Protection** — deters most predators

Building fire is Priority 4 because it requires effort and resources. If you're injured or exposed, secure shelter first. But once you have shelter, fire multiplies your survival capabilities. You'll learn three matchless fire-starting methods in [Req 6](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req6/).

### 5. Signaling

Once you're sheltered and warm, help rescuers find you. Signaling is Priority 5 because it requires energy and planning — but it's what actually gets you *rescued*.

Three universal distress signals:
- **Three of anything** — three whistle blasts, three fires in a triangle, three piles of rocks
- **Mirror flashes** — a signal mirror can be seen for 10+ miles
- **Ground-to-air signals** — large symbols cleared in snow, dirt, or sand

You'll master signaling techniques in [Req 7](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req7/).

### 6. Water

Your body needs water to function — but you can survive about three days without it. That's why water is Priority 6, not Priority 1. Shelter and fire come first because dehydration takes days to become critical, while exposure can kill in hours.

When you do need water, natural sources (streams, lakes, rain, snow) must be treated before drinking. Untreated water can contain parasites and bacteria that cause vomiting and diarrhea — which accelerate dehydration and make your situation worse. You'll learn three water treatment methods in [Req 8](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req8/).

### 7. Food

Food is last on the list for a reason: you can survive for three weeks without it. In most survival situations, rescue arrives long before starvation becomes a concern. Spending energy hunting or foraging when you should be building shelter or signaling is a dangerous misallocation of your limited resources.

That said, if you're in a prolonged situation, small amounts of food — especially high-calorie items from your pack — help maintain energy and morale. You'll learn why foraging for wild plants is usually a bad idea in [Req 9](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req9/).

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
The priorities are ordered for a reason. Skipping ahead — like spending hours looking for water before building shelter — can be fatal. Follow the order. Handle each priority before moving to the next.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Why the Order Matters

The seven priorities are ranked by **how quickly each threat can kill you**:

| Priority | Threat | Approximate survival time |
|----------|--------|--------------------------|
| STOP | Panic-driven bad decisions | Immediate |
| First Aid | Untreated injuries | Hours to days |
| Shelter | Exposure (heat/cold) | 3 hours in extreme conditions |
| Fire | Cold, impure water, low morale | Ongoing |
| Signaling | Not being found | Days |
| Water | Dehydration | 3 days |
| Food | Starvation | 3 weeks |

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Priorities in Action" >}}
Your patrol gets separated from the group during a day hike in November. It's 3:00 PM, temperatures are dropping, and rain is starting. Here's how the priorities guide your response:

1. **STOP** — Everyone sits down. You take stock: four people, one has a minor cut, you have two packs with Scout Essentials.
2. **First Aid** — Clean and bandage the cut. Check everyone for other injuries.
3. **Shelter** — Find a natural windbreak (fallen tree, rock overhang). Use your rain gear and space blankets to create a dry, wind-protected area.
4. **Fire** — Gather dry tinder from under logs, build a fire using your ferro rod.
5. **Signal** — Build the fire in an open area where smoke is visible. Blow your whistle in sets of three.
6. **Water** — You have water bottles. Ration them. If needed, boil water from a nearby stream.
7. **Food** — Share trail food. Don't worry about finding more — rescue should come before food is critical.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
Memorize the seven priorities by creating a mnemonic. One popular version: **"S**top **F**reaking out, **S**eek **F**ire, **S**ignal, **W**ater, **F**ood" — SFSFSWF. Or create your own that sticks in your memory.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

The seven priorities give you a roadmap when your brain wants to shut down. When everything feels overwhelming, just ask: "What's the next priority on the list?" Then do that one thing. Then the next.
