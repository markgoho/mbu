This requirement asks you to choose **one** of the following options:

- **Option a:** Research a career related to wilderness survival
- **Option b:** Explore how wilderness survival skills apply to a hobby or volunteer opportunity

Both options require research, so pick the one that interests you most. Below is guidance for each.

## Option A: Wilderness Survival Careers

The skills you've learned in this merit badge — emergency response, shelter construction, fire management, navigation, water treatment — translate directly into real careers. Here are several worth researching:

### Search and Rescue (SAR) Professional

SAR teams find and rescue lost or injured people in wilderness settings. Many teams are volunteer-based, but professional positions exist with the National Park Service, Coast Guard, and state emergency management agencies.

- **Training:** Wilderness first responder (WFR) certification, technical rescue training, navigation skills, helicopter operations
- **Education:** Some positions require a degree in emergency management or related field; many prioritize certifications and field experience
- **Salary range:** $35,000–$70,000 for professional SAR; many positions are volunteer with paid coordinators

### Wilderness Guide / Outfitter

Guides lead groups through backcountry environments for recreation, education, or adventure tourism. They must be experts in wilderness survival, group management, and local ecology.

- **Training:** Wilderness first aid (minimum), guide certifications through organizations like NOLS or AMGA
- **Education:** Varies; many guides have degrees in outdoor recreation or environmental science
- **Salary range:** $25,000–$55,000 depending on location and season length

### Wildland Firefighter

Wildland firefighters combat forest and brush fires in remote terrain. The work demands extreme physical fitness, fire behavior knowledge, and survival skills for when conditions change rapidly.

- **Training:** S-130/S-190 fire training, physical fitness tests, chainsaw certification
- **Education:** High school diploma minimum; college degrees can accelerate advancement
- **Salary range:** $30,000–$60,000 (entry level); hotshot crew supervisors earn more

### Park Ranger / Conservation Officer

Rangers manage and protect public lands, enforce regulations, respond to emergencies, and educate visitors. The job combines law enforcement, wildlife management, and wilderness expertise.

- **Training:** Law enforcement academy (for commissioned rangers), EMT certification, extensive field training
- **Education:** Bachelor's degree in criminal justice, natural resources, or park management
- **Salary range:** $35,000–$65,000 for federal positions (GS-5 to GS-9)

### Survival Instructor

Survival instructors teach wilderness skills to military personnel, outdoor enthusiasts, corporate groups, and Scout troops. This career directly applies every skill from this merit badge.

- **Training:** Advanced survival training (military SERE school, civilian programs like BOSS or NOLS), instructor certifications
- **Education:** No specific degree required, but extensive practical experience is essential
- **Salary range:** $30,000–$60,000; top instructors with their own schools can earn more

### Research Checklist

When researching a career for your counselor discussion, cover these areas:

- Certifications or licenses needed
- Typical costs for education and training

## Option B: Hobbies & Volunteer Opportunities

Wilderness survival skills open doors to hobbies and volunteer work that keep you outdoors and make a difference.

### Hobby Ideas

**Bushcraft** — the practice of wilderness skills as a recreational pursuit. Bushcrafters regularly practice fire-starting, shelter-building, carving, and foraging (with proper training). It's a direct extension of everything in this merit badge.

**Backpacking and thru-hiking** — long-distance backpacking (like the Appalachian Trail or Pacific Crest Trail) applies survival skills daily. Lightweight gear selection, water treatment, shelter setup, and weather navigation are constant companions.

**Orienteering** — competitive navigation using map and compass. Orienteering combines wilderness travel with problem-solving and athleticism. Many clubs welcome beginners.

**Winter camping** — taking your shelter and fire skills into cold-weather environments. Building quinzhees, sleeping in snow trenches, and managing cold-weather layering systems are advanced applications of what you've learned.

### Volunteer Opportunities

**Search and Rescue volunteer teams** — many counties rely on volunteer SAR teams. Training is typically provided, and the skills you've developed in this merit badge give you a strong foundation. Minimum age varies by team (often 16–18).

**Trail maintenance crews** — organizations like the Appalachian Trail Conservancy, Pacific Crest Trail Association, and local hiking clubs organize volunteer trail maintenance. Crews spend days in the backcountry maintaining trails, bridges, and shelters.

**Disaster response** — organizations like the American Red Cross and CERT (Community Emergency Response Team) train volunteers in emergency response. Your wilderness first aid and survival skills transfer directly.

{{< drg/tip >}}
If you're interested in SAR volunteering, contact your county sheriff's office or local emergency management agency to ask about volunteer opportunities. Many teams accept junior members who train alongside experienced searchers.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### Research Checklist for Hobbies & Volunteering

{{< drg/checklist title="Hobby/Volunteer Research Points" subtitle="Gather information on each" >}}
- Training or skills needed to get started
- Costs (gear, training, travel, membership fees)
- Organizations that promote or support it
- Short-term goals (first 6 months)
- Long-term goals (1–3 years)
- How this connects to your wilderness survival skills
{{< /drg/checklist >}}
