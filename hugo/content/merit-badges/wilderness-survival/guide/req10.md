{{< drg/requirement number="10" >}}
Do ONE of the following:

**Option A:** Explore careers related to this merit badge. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. With permission of your parent or guardian, your research methods may include an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Discuss with your counselor both your findings and what about this profession might make it an interesting career.

**Option B:** Explore how you could use knowledge and skills from the Wilderness Survival merit badge to pursue a hobby or to serve as volunteer. Research any training needed, expenses, and organizations that promote or support it. Discuss with your counselor what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursue this.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This requirement is about **your future.** The wilderness survival skills you've learned aren't just for emergencies—they open doors to careers and hobbies that fulfill the desire many Scouts have: to live and work outdoors.

You'll choose one of two paths: explore a career in wilderness or outdoor work (Option A), or explore how to turn these skills into a hobby or volunteer service (Option B).

## Option A: Wilderness & Outdoor Careers

These careers directly use wilderness survival, outdoor skills, and the knowledge you're building in this badge:

### Search & Rescue (SAR)

**What they do:** SAR teams locate and rescue lost hikers, climbers, and stranded people. They operate in wilderness areas, sometimes in dangerous conditions.

**Training needed:**
- EMT (Emergency Medical Technician) certification
- Wilderness First Responder or Wilderness First Aid certification
- Extensive training in navigation, rope rescue, and specific environments
- Often require volunteer service before employment

**Job prospects:** Moderate; most SAR positions are volunteer, but some paid positions exist with government agencies

**Salary:** $30,000–$60,000 (varies widely; many positions are volunteer with no salary)

**Education:** High school diploma + certifications (no specific degree required, though degrees in outdoor recreation or emergency management help)

**Career advancement:**
- Start as volunteer SAR technician
- Progress to paid position with state/federal agency
- Specialize (rope rescue, avalanche rescue, swift-water rescue)
- Become a trainer or coordinator

### Park Ranger

**What they do:** Rangers manage public lands, enforce regulations, educate visitors, and conduct search and rescue operations.

**Training needed:**
- Strong wilderness skills
- Environmental knowledge
- Emergency response training
- Law enforcement training (if doing full ranger duties)

**Job prospects:** Moderate to good; many agencies hire seasonal rangers

**Salary:** $35,000–$55,000 (federal rangers); varies by state and level

**Education:** Bachelor's degree in forestry, environmental science, or outdoor recreation preferred; some positions available with high school diploma + certifications

**Career advancement:**
- Start as seasonal ranger
- Progress to permanent, year-round position
- Specialize (backcountry management, environmental education, SAR coordination)
- Move into management or administration

### Wilderness Outfitter/Guide

**What they do:** Guides lead backpacking trips, climbing expeditions, kayaking tours, or other wilderness adventures. They teach wilderness skills and keep groups safe.

**Training needed:**
- Wilderness First Responder certification
- Technical skills specific to your specialty (rock climbing, kayaking, mountaineering)
- Leave No Trace training
- Possibly additional certifications (avalanche, swift-water, etc.)

**Job prospects:** Competitive; depends on location and specialty

**Salary:** $25,000–$50,000 (often seasonal, with combined income from multiple trips)

**Education:** High school diploma; certifications matter more than formal education

**Career advancement:**
- Start as assistant guide
- Progress to lead guide
- Specialize in specific activities (expedition climbing, technical mountaineering)
- Become an instructor or business owner

### Wildland Firefighter

**What they do:** Firefighters combat wildfires, often working in remote wilderness areas. They're part of a team that builds firebreaks, protects structures, and ensures public safety.

**Training needed:**
- Wildland Firefighter Type 1 or Type 2 certification
- Physical fitness (very demanding)
- Emergency response training
- Equipment proficiency

**Job prospects:** Good; demand increases as wildfire seasons lengthen

**Salary:** $30,000–$55,000 (often seasonal, with overtime pay increasing total)

**Education:** High school diploma; certifications earned through training courses

**Career advancement:**
- Start as seasonal firefighter
- Progress to permanent position
- Specialize (incident management, crew leadership, training)
- Move into management

## Option B: Hobbies & Volunteer Service

If a full wilderness career doesn't appeal to you, consider how wilderness survival skills support hobbies and service:

### Backpacking & Hiking as a Hobby

**What it is:** Extended backcountry trips, multi-day expeditions, trail exploration

**Training needed:** Minimal beyond what you've learned in this badge; add expertise in specific areas (rock climbing, mountaineering)

**Expenses:** Equipment (backpack, tent, sleeping bag, stove): $500–$2,000 initial investment; trip costs vary

**Organizations:**
- American Hiking Society
- Appalachian Trail Conservancy
- Local hiking clubs
- Sierra Club

**Short-term goals:**
- Complete a multi-day backpacking trip
- Hike a long-distance trail section (10–50 miles)
- Master a specific skill (navigation, leave-no-trace practices)

**Long-term goals:**
- Complete a full long-distance trail (Appalachian Trail, Pacific Crest Trail)
- Climb significant peaks
- Develop expertise in a specific environment (winter, alpine, desert)

### Search & Rescue Volunteering

**What it is:** Volunteer with an SAR organization, assisting with training, logistics, and actual rescue operations

**Training needed:**
- Wilderness First Aid minimum
- Wilderness First Responder preferred
- SAR-specific training
- Physical fitness

**Expenses:** Minimal (organizations usually provide gear); mostly time

**Organizations:**
- National Association for Search and Rescue (NASAR)
- Local county SAR teams
- Mountain rescue clubs
- Ski patrol (winter rescue)

**Short-term goals:**
- Get Wilderness First Aid certification
- Join a local SAR team
- Participate in training exercises

**Long-term goals:**
- Become a SAR team leader
- Specialize (rope rescue, swift-water, avalanche)
- Contribute hundreds of hours to rescuing people

### Trail Maintenance & Conservation Volunteering

**What it is:** Volunteer with trail maintenance organizations to clear, repair, and maintain hiking trails

**Training needed:** Minimal; organizations provide on-the-job training

**Expenses:** Minimal (tool and training provided)

**Organizations:**
- American Hiking Society
- Appalachian Trail Conservancy
- Local trail clubs
- Bureau of Land Management (BLM) volunteer programs
- Forest Service volunteer programs

**Short-term goals:**
- Volunteer for one trail maintenance project
- Learn trail building techniques
- Help maintain local trails

**Long-term goals:**
- Lead trail maintenance crews
- Specialize in trail design or restoration
- Contribute to trail network expansion in your area

## How to Research Your Career or Hobby

**Internet search:**
- Search "(career name) requirements," "(hobby) organizations," "how to become a..."
- Look at job postings to see real requirements
- Read blogs or forums from people in the field

**Library research:**
- Career guides (Occupational Outlook Handbook, specific career books)
- Books about specific activities (mountaineering, wilderness medicine)

**Interviews (with parent permission):**
- Contact local park rangers, hiking guides, or SAR coordinators
- Ask about their path, training, salary, and challenges
- Offer to shadow them (spend a day watching their work)

**Visit locations:**
- Visit a ranger station and talk to staff
- Attend a training course or certification class
- Join a local hiking or outdoor club meeting

## Discussing with Your Counselor

Be ready to explain:

1. **Why this career or hobby interests you** — what appeals to you about it?
2. **What training or education is needed** — what steps would you take?
3. **The costs involved** — realistic expenses you'd expect
4. **Job prospects or availability** — realistic assessment of opportunities
5. **Short-term and long-term goals** — what would you do first, and where could this lead?

Your counselor wants to see that you've done genuine research and that you're thinking seriously about your future in the outdoors.
