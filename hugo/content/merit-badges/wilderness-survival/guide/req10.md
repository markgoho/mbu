## Option A: Careers in Wilderness Survival

The skills you have learned in this badge are not just for emergencies — they are the foundation of real careers. People get paid to work in wild places, lead others through challenging terrain, and keep people safe in the outdoors.

### Search and Rescue Professional

Search and rescue (SAR) teams are the people who find lost hikers, rescue stranded climbers, and respond to wilderness emergencies. Many SAR teams are volunteer-based, but professional positions exist with national parks, county sheriff departments, and the military.

- **Training:** Wilderness first responder (WFR) certification, technical rescue training, navigation, helicopter operations
- **Education:** Varies widely — some positions require only certifications, others prefer emergency management or related degrees
- **Salary:** $35,000–$65,000 for full-time positions; many start as volunteers
- **What makes it interesting:** Every call is different, and the work has immediate, life-saving impact

### Wilderness Guide / Outdoor Educator

Guides lead groups through backcountry trips — backpacking, kayaking, mountaineering, winter camping — and teach the skills to do it safely. Organizations like NOLS, Outward Bound, and REI Adventures employ guides year-round.

- **Training:** Wilderness First Aid (WFA) or WFR, Leave No Trace trainer, activity-specific certifications
- **Education:** Outdoor education or recreation degrees are helpful but not always required; experience matters most
- **Salary:** $25,000–$50,000 (seasonal positions are common, especially early in the career)
- **What makes it interesting:** Your office is the wilderness, and you get to share your passion with others

### Park Ranger / Wildlife Manager

Park rangers manage and protect public lands — from national parks to state forests. The job blends law enforcement, education, resource management, and emergency response. Wildlife managers focus specifically on animal populations and habitat health.

- **Training:** Law enforcement academy (for commissioned rangers), wilderness skills, natural resource management
- **Education:** Bachelor's degree in natural resource management, environmental science, biology, or forestry
- **Salary:** $40,000–$75,000 depending on agency and location
- **What makes it interesting:** You protect some of the most beautiful places on Earth and help visitors experience them safely

### Wildland Firefighter

Wildland firefighters combat forest fires using hand tools, chainsaws, and controlled burns. Hotshot crews and smokejumpers (who parachute into remote fire zones) represent the elite of this field. The work is physically demanding and seasonal, but deeply rewarding.

- **Training:** S-130/S-190 firefighter training, physical fitness test (pack test), chainsaw certification
- **Education:** No degree required to start; advancement may require fire science or forestry coursework
- **Salary:** $30,000–$55,000 (seasonal); hotshot crew supervisors earn more
- **What makes it interesting:** The teamwork is intense, the work is physically challenging, and you are directly protecting communities and wildlands

### Military Survival Instructor (SERE)

The military's Survival, Evasion, Resistance, and Escape (SERE) program trains service members to survive behind enemy lines. SERE instructors are among the most skilled survivalists in the world, teaching everything from shelter construction and water procurement to navigation and signaling.

- **Training:** Military service + SERE school + instructor qualification
- **Education:** Military enlistment or officer commission
- **Salary:** Military pay scale
- **What makes it interesting:** You teach the most advanced survival skills to people whose lives may depend on them

{{< drg/tip >}}
When researching your chosen career for your counselor, interview someone who actually does the work if possible. A 15-minute phone call or email exchange with a park ranger or SAR volunteer will give you insights that no website can.

## Option B: Hobbies and Volunteer Opportunities

### Bushcraft

Bushcraft takes survival skills and turns them into an art form. Practitioners build elaborate shelters, carve tools from wood, tan hides, make cordage from plant fibers, and master fire-by-friction techniques. The goal is not just to survive but to thrive in the wilderness using traditional skills.

- **Getting started:** Practice skills from this badge on weekend campouts; take a bushcraft course; watch reputable instructors online
- **Gear needed:** A quality fixed-blade knife, a ferro rod, cordage, and access to wooded areas
- **Organizations:** Bushcraft USA, various regional wilderness skills schools

### Backpacking and Thru-Hiking

Long-distance backpacking puts all your survival skills to use over days, weeks, or months. Trails like the Appalachian Trail (2,190 miles), Pacific Crest Trail (2,650 miles), and Continental Divide Trail (3,100 miles) challenge hikers with every condition you studied in this badge.

- **Getting started:** Start with weekend trips, gradually extend to multi-day and weeklong trips
- **Costs:** Initial gear investment of $500–$1,500 for pack, shelter, sleeping system, and clothing
- **Organizations:** American Hiking Society, Appalachian Trail Conservancy, Pacific Crest Trail Association

### Search and Rescue Volunteering

Many county and state SAR teams actively recruit and train volunteers. This is one of the most direct ways to use your wilderness survival skills to help others.

- **Getting started:** Contact your county sheriff's office or local SAR team
- **Training provided:** Most teams provide free training in navigation, first aid, search techniques, and radio communications
- **Time commitment:** Monthly training meetings plus callouts (which can happen any time)

### Wilderness First Aid

Taking a Wilderness First Aid (WFA) or Wilderness First Responder (WFR) course deepens the medical skills from this badge. These certifications are respected by outdoor organizations and are required for many volunteer and professional outdoor positions.

- **Getting started:** Take a WFA course (16 hours) through NOLS Wilderness Medicine, SOLO, or the Red Cross
- **Costs:** $200–$350 for WFA; $600–$900 for WFR
- **Benefits:** Better prepared for any outdoor emergency; a stepping stone to guiding and SAR careers

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
Volunteers make up the majority of search and rescue teams in the United States. These trained volunteers respond to thousands of calls per year and provide the same level of expertise as professional teams in many areas. Starting as a volunteer is one of the best ways to build wilderness survival skills while serving your community.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}
