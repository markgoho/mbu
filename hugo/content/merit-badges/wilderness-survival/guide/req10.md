Careers. Do ONE of the following:

**Option A:** Explore careers related to this merit badge. Research one career to learn about the training and education needed, costs, job prospects, salary, job duties, and career advancement. With permission of your parent or guardian, your research methods may include an internet or library search, an interview with a professional in the field, or a visit to a location where people in this career work. Discuss with your counselor both your findings and what about this profession might make it an interesting career.

**Option B:** Explore how you could use knowledge and skills from the Wilderness Survival merit badge to pursue a hobby or to serve as volunteer. Research any training needed, expenses, and organizations that promote or support it. Discuss with your counselor what short-term and long-term goals you might have if you pursue this.
{{</* /drg/requirement */>}}

This requirement is about connecting your wilderness survival skills to the real world. You've learned fire-building, shelter-making, first aid, and signaling. Now—does this point toward a career you're curious about? Or a hobby or volunteer opportunity? Choose ONE path and explore it deeply.

## Option A: Wilderness Careers

### **What Counts as a Wilderness Career?**

Careers that directly use wilderness survival skills include:

- **Search and Rescue (SAR) Coordinator**
- **Wilderness First Responder / EMT**
- **Park Ranger**
- **Outdoor Education Instructor**
- **Wilderness Guide**
- **Survival Skills Instructor**
- **Forest Service Worker**
- **Wildland Firefighter**
- **Mountaineering Guide**
- **Adventure Travel Guide**

These are just examples. The requirement is flexible—choose any career that connects to wilderness survival in some way.

### **What Research to Conduct**

For your chosen career, research:

1. **Training and Education**
   - What degrees or certifications are required?
   - How many years of education/training?
   - What subjects are studied?
   - Are wilderness survival skills taught, or learned on the job?

2. **Costs**
   - Tuition for degree programs?
   - Cost of certifications?
   - Gear or equipment you'd need to purchase?
   - Are there scholarships?

3. **Job Prospects**
   - How many jobs are available?
   - Is the field growing or shrinking?
   - What areas of the country have the most opportunities?
   - Is the job market competitive?

4. **Salary**
   - Entry-level pay?
   - Salary after 5 years of experience?
   - Top salary in the field?
   - Does it vary by region?

5. **Job Duties**
   - What do people in this career actually *do* day-to-day?
   - What's exciting about it?
   - What's challenging?
   - How much time in the wilderness vs. office work?

6. **Career Advancement**
   - How do people move up in this field?
   - From entry-level to management?
   - Related careers (lateral moves)?

### **Research Methods**

Choose at least one:

**Internet/Library Search:**
- Bureau of Labor Statistics (bls.gov) — salary, job outlook, requirements
- Professional organizations (e.g., National Park Service, Search & Rescue organizations)
- Job sites (LinkedIn, Indeed) — see actual job postings to understand requirements
- News and magazine articles about people in the field

**Interview with Professional:**
- Contact a park ranger, outdoor educator, or wilderness guide
- Ask about their career path, training, and experience
- Get their perspective on the field
- Ask what they wish they'd known starting out

**Visit a Workplace:**
- Visit a national or state park
- Attend a wilderness skills course or outdoor education program
- Observe professionals working
- Ask questions about the work

### **Discussion with Your Counselor**

Prepare a summary:
- **Career chosen:** Name and brief description
- **Training required:** Degrees, certifications, timeline
- **Costs:** Realistic expenses
- **Job market:** Availability and growth
- **Salary range:** Entry-level to experienced
- **Daily work:** What a typical day looks like
- **Appeal to you:** Why this career interests you, what excites you

Be ready to discuss:
- Would you pursue further training in this area?
- What skills you already have that would help?
- What skills you'd need to develop?
- Whether you'd want to pursue this long-term or if it's just interesting short-term?

## Option B: Wilderness Hobbies & Volunteering

### **What Counts?**

Hobbies and volunteer opportunities using wilderness survival skills:

**Outdoor Hobbies:**
- Backcountry hiking/backpacking
- Rock climbing
- Mountaineering
- Wilderness camping
- Orienteering or navigation sports
- Outdoor survival competitions
- Canyoneering
- Winter mountaineering

**Volunteer Opportunities:**
- Search and Rescue volunteer
- Trail maintenance (hiking club, Forest Service)
- Outdoor education (teaching Scouts, camp counseling)
- Environmental conservation work
- Wildland firefighting (seasonal)
- Disaster relief (Red Cross, etc.)

### **What Research to Conduct**

For your chosen hobby or volunteer path, research:

1. **Training Needed**
   - What skills or certifications are required?
   - Are there courses or classes?
   - How much practice before you're "ready"?
   - What's the learning curve?

2. **Expenses**
   - Equipment costs (backpack, tent, climbing gear, etc.)
   - Course costs?
   - Travel expenses?
   - Ongoing costs (permits, membership fees)?
   - What can you borrow vs. buy?

3. **Organizations That Support It**
   - Clubs or groups (hiking club, climbing gym, kayak club)?
   - Volunteer organizations (SAR team, conservation corps)?
   - Online communities?
   - Where do you start?

4. **Short-Term and Long-Term Goals**

### **Short-Term Goals (Next 1–2 Years)**
- What specific skills will you develop?
- What trips, events, or experiences will you pursue?
- What training will you complete?
- What equipment will you acquire?

### **Long-Term Goals (5+ Years)**
- Are you building toward a career in this area?
- Could this hobby lead to volunteer leadership?
- Could you teach others?
- What's your vision for 5–10 years of involvement?

### **Research Methods**

**Internet/Organization Research:**
- Search for local hiking clubs, climbing gyms, SAR teams
- Look up volunteer organizations (Sierra Club, American Alpine Club, etc.)
- Find online communities (forums, Reddit, Facebook groups)
- Search for courses or certifications

**Interviews:**
- Talk to people already doing this hobby/volunteering
- Ask about their entry story: How did they start?
- What equipment do they recommend?
- What are the biggest challenges?
- Is it worth the investment of time and money?

**Trial or Introductory Experience:**
- Take an introductory class
- Go on a club hike
- Attend an event
- Shadow someone in the field

### **Discussion with Your Counselor**

Prepare a summary:
- **Hobby/Volunteer activity:** Clear description
- **Training required:** Courses, certifications, self-teaching
- **Expenses:** Realistic costs to get started and ongoing
- **Organizations:** Where to get involved, who to contact
- **Short-term goals:** What you'll do in the next 1–2 years
- **Long-term goals:** Your vision 5+ years out
- **Appeal to you:** Why this excites you

Be ready to discuss:
- Why this hobby/volunteer path appeals to you?
- How wilderness survival skills fit into it?
- What's your timeline to get involved?
- Will you pursue this actively or just explore?

## My Recommendation: Option B (Most Scouts Choose It)

Most Scouts find Option B more engaging because:
- You're exploring something you actually plan to *do*, not just study
- The research is more personal and exciting
- You can take action immediately (join a club, sign up for a course)
- It connects the badge to your real life

But if you're seriously considering a wilderness-related career, Option A is valuable. You'll make an informed decision about whether that career path is right for you.

## Before You Move On

Whether you choose Option A or Option B:

- **Conduct thorough research.** 1–2 hours of internet research, or an actual interview/visit
- **Take notes.** Document what you find
- **Be ready to discuss.** Your counselor will ask follow-up questions
- **Show genuine interest.** Your enthusiasm matters more than having a "perfect" answer

This requirement is about opening doors—whether to a potential career or an exciting new hobby. The best answer is one that reflects *your* interests and excitement about wilderness skills.
