You just learned about thirteen backcountry hazards in [Req 1a](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req1a/). Now consider this: most of those situations become manageable — or avoidable entirely — if you have the right gear in your pack. That's exactly what the Scout Essentials are designed to do.

## The Scout Essentials

The Scout Essentials (sometimes called the "Scout Outdoor Essentials" or "Ten Essentials") are a baseline packing list for every outdoor trip. They aren't random items — each one directly addresses a specific survival hazard.

| Essential | Survival Hazards Addressed |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Pocketknife** | Shelter building, fire preparation, first aid (cutting bandages/tape), signaling |
| **First aid kit** | Every injury from [Req 1a](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req1a/) — blisters, bites, sprains, shock |
| **Extra clothing** | Hypothermia, wet conditions, sun exposure, wind chill |
| **Rain gear** | Hypothermia from wet cold, the single deadliest exposure combination |
| **Flashlight or headlamp** | Navigation after dark, signaling, avoiding hazards at night |
| **Trail food** | Maintaining energy and body heat, preventing poor decision-making from low blood sugar |
| **Water bottle** | Dehydration, heatstroke prevention |
| **Matches and fire starters** | Hypothermia prevention, water purification, signaling, morale |
| **Sun protection** | Sunburn, heatstroke, eye injuries from UV radiation |
| **Map and compass** | Preventing a survival situation in the first place by not getting lost |

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
The original "Ten Essentials" list was created in the 1930s by The Mountaineers, a Seattle-based climbing organization. Their members kept getting stranded on Pacific Northwest peaks, and the list was designed to answer one question: "What do you need to survive an unplanned night outdoors?"
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

## From Essentials to Survival Kit

The Scout Essentials are your *starting point*, not your finish line. A personal survival kit builds on these essentials by adding lightweight, compact items that multiply your options in an emergency.

Think of it as layers of preparedness:

- **Layer 1 — Scout Essentials:** The baseline you carry on every trip
- **Layer 2 — Survival additions:** Items like a signal mirror, emergency whistle, space blanket, water purification tablets, paracord, and a ferro rod
- **Layer 3 — Trip-specific gear:** Additions based on where you're going (bear spray for bear country, extra water for desert hikes, gaiters for snake territory)

The key principle is **redundancy for critical needs**. Matches are an essential — but a ferro rod gives you a backup fire-starting method that works when matches get wet. A water bottle keeps you hydrated — but purification tablets let you refill from natural sources when your bottle runs dry.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Build your survival kit inside a small waterproof container (like a hard-sided case or dry bag) that lives permanently in your pack. If it's always packed and ready, you'll never leave it behind.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## How Each Essential Buys You Time

In a survival situation, you're running a clock. Each essential buys you time on one or more survival priorities (which you'll learn about in [Req 2](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req2/)):

- **Rain gear and extra clothing** buy you hours against hypothermia
- **Water and trail food** buy you energy to build shelter and signal for help
- **Matches and fire starters** buy you warmth, the ability to purify water, and a visible signal
- **Map and compass** may prevent the survival situation entirely
- **First aid kit** keeps a minor injury from becoming a major crisis

None of these items weigh much. A full set of Scout Essentials adds roughly 3–5 pounds to your pack. That's a small price for a massive increase in your ability to handle whatever the backcountry throws at you.

{{< drg/checklist title="Scout Essentials Quick Check" subtitle="Verify before every outing" >}}
- Pocketknife: blade sharp, mechanism working
- First aid kit: stocked, medications not expired
- Extra clothing: appropriate for weather forecast *and* worst-case conditions
- Rain gear: no tears, seams intact
- Flashlight/headlamp: batteries fresh, backup batteries packed
- Trail food: high-calorie, non-perishable
- Water bottle: full, plus purification method for refills
- Matches/fire starters: stored in waterproof container
- Sun protection: sunscreen, hat, sunglasses
- Map and compass: correct map for the area, compass working
