{{< drg/requirement number="5e" >}}
Improvise a natural shelter. For the purpose of this demonstration, use techniques that have little negative impact on the environment. Spend a night in your shelter.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This is the hands-on culmination of everything in the shelter section. You've described the theory in Req 5d — now you build it for real and sleep in it. One night in a shelter you built yourself is worth a hundred hours of reading about it.

{{< drg/image src="images/scout-building-debris-hut.avif" alt="A Scout in work clothes gathering armfuls of leaves and branches to pile on a debris hut frame in a wooded area" >}}

## Low-Impact Techniques

The requirement specifically says to use techniques with **little negative impact on the environment.** This matters for two reasons: it aligns with Leave No Trace principles, and it demonstrates that survival skills don't require trashing the wilderness.

**What "low-impact" means in practice:**

- Gather dead, fallen wood for your ridgepole and ribbing — don't cut or break branches off living trees
- Use fallen leaf litter, dead pine needles, and dry debris — don't strip living plants
- Choose a build site that doesn't require clearing vegetation
- When you're done, scatter the debris and leave the site looking as natural as possible

{{< drg/tip >}}
You can typically gather all the materials for an effective debris hut without cutting a single branch. Look for naturally fallen branches near the base of trees, accumulations of dry leaves in hollow spots, and wind-down areas where debris has piled against obstacles.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Planning Your Build

Before picking up the first stick, walk your site and plan. Thirty minutes of planning saves three hours of rebuilding.

**Site selection checklist:**

{{< drg/checklist title="Shelter Site Selection" subtitle="Check these before you start building" >}}
- **Wind direction:** Orient the entrance away from the prevailing wind. The closed back should face into the wind.
- **Overhead hazards:** Look up — "widowmakers" (dead branches lodged overhead) can fall overnight. Don't camp directly under them.
- **Drainage:** On a slope or flat area? Water flows downhill — if it rains, you don't want to be in the path of runoff. Choose slightly elevated ground.
- **Debris availability:** Are there enough dead leaves and branches within easy carry distance? Collect materials before you start assembling.
- **Space:** Can you lie flat with a few inches of headroom? Bigger is colder. Measure yourself — shoulder width plus a few inches is the right interior width.
- **Insulation from ground:** Plan for 4–6 inches of debris beneath you as a mattress — the ground steals your heat.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Building the Debris Hut: Full Process

The debris hut is the recommended shelter for this requirement. It's the most effective natural insulation shelter you can build, and it doesn't require tools or manufactured materials.

**Materials to gather before building:**

1. One ridgepole, 9–12 feet long, wrist-thick, straight
2. 20–30 smaller ribbing branches, 2–4 feet long
3. Armfuls of dry sticks for lattice work
4. As much dry leaf litter and pine needles as possible — you'll need more than you think. Think "fill a small car" worth of debris.

**Build sequence:**

1. Set the ridgepole (prop in tree fork or Y-stick at hip height, far end on ground)
2. Lean ribbing branches on both sides at ~45° angles
3. Fill in the ribbing with crisscrossed smaller sticks (this keeps debris from falling through)
4. Pile debris from the ground up — start thick at the base, work toward the top
5. Target at least 2–3 feet of debris thickness everywhere — more in wet or cold conditions
6. Stuff the interior with dry debris (this becomes your "mattress" and additional insulation)
7. Create a door plug — a bundle of debris you can pull in behind you to seal the entrance

## What to Expect Your First Night

Your first night in a natural shelter is almost never comfortable in the way a tent is comfortable. That's okay. The standard is survival, not luxury. Things that are normal and expected:

- **It will feel cramped.** This is correct — a snug shelter is warmer than a roomy one.
- **You may hear rustling.** Small animals are often curious. They're not a concern.
- **The debris shifts.** Some settling happens naturally. Check your entrance seal if you feel a draft.
- **You'll probably sleep lighter than normal.** First-time shelter sleepers often sleep in shorter cycles. This is normal.
- **You may be surprised how warm it is.** A well-built debris hut retains body heat remarkably well.

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Middle of the Night — What If I Get Cold?" >}}
You wake at 2am and you're cold. Here's what to check:

- **Draft at the entrance?** Your door plug has shifted. Push it back firmly.
- **Cold from below?** You need more debris under you. You can add it — pull debris from less critical walls and place it beneath you.
- **Damp feeling?** Your shelter debris may have gotten wet. If possible, get up, move, and start generating body heat. Do jumping jacks, push-ups, anything to warm up. Then address the moisture issue.
- **Truly cold throughout?** Your debris layer is too thin. This is fixable if materials are available — at minimum, you can stuff more leaves into the interior. If you're in real distress, build a fire outside and warm yourself, then improve the shelter before going back in.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

## Counselor Demonstration

Your counselor needs to see the finished shelter and verify you spent a night in it. Prepare to discuss:

- The site selection process — why you chose this spot
- How you gathered materials with minimal environmental impact
- How the debris insulation works (principle of dead air space)
- What you learned about the shelter's performance overnight
- What you would do differently next time

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Always do your shelter-building practice with adult supervision present and know exactly where your troop leaders or adults are camping relative to your shelter. Even for practice, a Solo night in the woods requires responsible adults nearby. Never build and use a natural shelter in a situation where help isn't reasonably accessible.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}
