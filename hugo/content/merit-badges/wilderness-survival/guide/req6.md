{{< drg/requirement number="6" >}}
Fire Building. Using three different methods (other than matches), build and light three fires.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Building fire without matches isn't just a merit badge skill — it's what separates someone who can say "I know how to start a fire" from someone who actually can. In a survival situation, your match supply runs out. Your lighter leaks. Knowing two or three backup methods means fire is always within reach.

{{< drg/image src="images/ferro-rod-sparking-tinder.avif" alt="Close-up of a hand striking a ferro rod over a bird's nest of dry grass tinder, with bright orange sparks flying toward the tinder bundle" >}}

## The Fire Triangle

Every fire needs three things: **fuel**, **heat**, and **oxygen**. Remove any one and the fire dies. Understanding this triangle is the key to troubleshooting failed fire attempts — which you will have.

**Tinder** is fine, dry, flammable material that catches a spark or small flame: dry grass, birch bark, cedar bark fibers, cattail fluff, pine needles, or commercially prepared cotton balls in petroleum jelly. It lights easily but burns fast.

**Kindling** is small sticks, finger-width or smaller, that the burning tinder can ignite. Without kindling, tinder burns out before the larger fuel catches.

**Fuel** (or "firewood") is what sustains the fire once established — sticks, logs, and larger wood.

The layering principle: build your fire structure from smallest to largest, ensuring each stage is lit by the previous before adding the next.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Collect tinder, kindling, and fuel *before* you try to start a fire. Many beginners light a small flame and then scramble for wood. By the time they return, the tinder has burned out. Have everything staged in arm's reach before you strike a single spark.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Method 1: Ferro Rod (Ferrocerium Strike)

A ferro rod (also called a fire steel or ferrocerium rod) creates a shower of sparks by scraping a hardened rod with a sharp edge. It's one of the most reliable backup fire-starting tools.

**What you need:** A ferro rod, a sharp metal scraper (often included), and a dry tinder bundle.

**Technique:**
1. Prepare your tinder bundle on the ground or on a bark platform.
2. Hold the rod tip very close to or touching the tinder.
3. Hold the rod stationary and draw the scraper backward along the rod in a firm, fast stroke (don't jab the rod into the tinder).
4. Sparks will land in the tinder. When you see a glowing coal, blow gently and steadily.
5. Once the tinder is burning, place it under your prepared kindling structure.

**Common mistakes:** Holding the rod too far from the tinder (sparks cool before landing); using wet or fluffy-but-damp tinder; moving the rod rather than the scraper (scatters sparks erratically).

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
A ferro rod doesn't wear out from rain or humidity the way a lighter can. A single ferro rod typically provides 10,000–15,000 strikes before it's depleted — enough for a lifetime of camping.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

## Method 2: Friction Fire (Bow Drill)

The bow drill is the most reliable traditional friction fire method. It uses a wooden drill, a fireboard, a bow, and a handhold to generate heat through friction.

**Equipment:** Collect dry, soft-to-medium hardwood for both the spindle (drill) and fireboard. Good wood: cedar, willow, cottonwood, mullein, dry yucca stalk. Bad wood: green/wet wood, very hard woods like oak, or resinous woods like pine.

**Basic technique:**
1. Cut a small notch in the fireboard near the socket depression. The notch is where the coal forms.
2. Place a small piece of bark under the notch to catch the coal.
3. Wrap the bowstring once around the spindle. Apply downward pressure via the handhold.
4. Bow back and forth steadily, maintaining consistent pressure and pace.
5. Continue until smoke appears and persists after you stop. A glowing coal should be in the notch.
6. Tap the coal onto your tinder bundle and blow gently until it flames.

The bow drill requires practice before you need it. This is a skill you build over multiple sessions, not one you learn the day you need it.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Practice friction fire outdoors in a safe area with adult supervision. Have water or dirt available to extinguish any unintended fire. When practicing near dry grass or leaves, clear a circle of bare earth first.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Method 3: Flint and Steel (or Improvised Sparking)

The traditional flint-and-steel method predates ferro rods but uses the same principle: strike a hard material (flint, quartzite, or high-carbon steel) to produce sparks.

**Flint and steel:** Strike a piece of flint or quartz sharply against high-carbon steel (a carbon steel knife spine works). Direct sparks onto a char cloth or other fine tinder.

**Improvised method:** In a survival situation without purpose-made tools, a quartzite rock struck sharply against a carbon steel knife spine can generate sparks. This is harder than a purpose-made ferro rod but possible with practice.

## Method 4 (Bonus): Magnifying Lens

If you have a magnifying glass, clear glass lens, or even a water-filled balloon or transparent plastic bag, focused sunlight can start a fire on a sunny day.

**Technique:** Focus sunlight to the sharpest possible point on very dry tinder. Hold steady. Patience is the key — it may take 30–60 seconds of steady focus. As the tinder begins to smoke, maintain focus until a coal forms, then blow gently.

**Limitation:** Doesn't work at night, on cloudy days, or in winter at low sun angles. Always have a backup method.

## Fire-Lighting Tips for Challenging Conditions

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Starting a Fire in Rain" >}}
It's raining steadily and you need fire to stay warm overnight.

- **Step 1:** Find dry tinder under bark, inside split dead logs, or from dead branches still attached to standing trees — these stay drier than ground wood.
- **Step 2:** Carve into the dry interior of a dead log to access dry wood shavings. Dead standing wood is usually drier than fallen wood.
- **Step 3:** Build a small platform of bark or sticks to keep your tinder off the wet ground.
- **Step 4:** Use your body as a wind and rain shield during ignition — hover over the tinder bundle.
- **Step 5:** Shelter the small fire under an overhang, poncho, or lean-to until it builds enough heat to sustain itself in rain.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}
