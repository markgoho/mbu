Wilderness survival isn't about being lost in a remote forest with zero supplies. It's about staying calm, thinking clearly, and using practical knowledge and skills to handle unexpected situations—whether you're stranded on a trail for a few hours or facing a genuine emergency. This badge teaches you exactly what to do when things go wrong in the backcountry.

{{< drg/image src="images/scout-wilderness-overview.avif" alt="Scout standing at mountain overlook with map and compass, looking out over vast wilderness landscape of forests and peaks" >}}

## Then and Now

**Then:** Traditional wilderness survival was the domain of hunters, trappers, and mountain men who spent months in isolation with minimal gear. Survival meant building shelters from scratch, hunting and foraging for food, and relying on deep knowledge of local plants and animals.

**Now:** Modern wilderness survival builds on those time-tested principles but adds emergency preparedness, rescue awareness, and a shift in philosophy. Today's Scout is taught that the goal in a true emergency is usually to **stay put, stay calm, and signal for help**—not to try to survive indefinitely. You're equipped with first-aid knowledge, signaling devices, and the mental tools to avoid panic. You understand that rescue is often on the way; your job is to make yourself findable and prevent secondary injuries while you wait.

## Get Ready!

This badge covers a lot of ground—from recognizing the early signs of hypothermia to building a shelter that will actually keep you warm, to understanding why eating unknown plants can turn a bad day into a dangerous one. By the end, you'll have skills that genuinely could save your life or someone else's.

## Kinds of Wilderness Environments

Wilderness takes many forms, and each presents unique challenges:

{{< drg/image src="images/wilderness-hazards-summary.avif" alt="Four panels showing environmental hazards: thermometer for cold, sun for heat, raindrop for wet conditions, wind symbol for wind" >}}

**Forests & Woodlands**
Dense trees offer shelter and firewood but can disorient you quickly. Navigation is trickier, visibility is low, and finding water usually means listening for streams rather than scanning the horizon. Rain and wind are absorbed somewhat by the canopy, but a forest fire moves fast.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
In forests, familiarize yourself with the local wildlife. Know where bears or venomous snakes live in your region. Make noise while hiking to avoid surprising animals.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

**Mountains & High Elevations**
Mountains expose you to wind, cold, and rapid weather changes. The view is excellent for navigation and signaling, but altitude sickness is a real concern, and terrain is rugged. A slip becomes a fall. Weather can go from pleasant to dangerous in minutes.

**Deserts & Arid Regions**
Your enemy is heat and dehydration. Water is rare, shade is precious, and navigation is easier (open sight lines) but more challenging if landmarks all look alike. Sun exposure and sand storms are hazards. Night temperatures can plummet, creating a different survival challenge.

**Water Environments**
Lakes, rivers, and oceans present drowning and hypothermia risks. Currents and tides are invisible dangers. Rescue may take longer. Cold water shocks the system and can incapacitate you in minutes.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Always wear a life jacket in or near water, even if you're a strong swimmer. Cold water shock is real and affects even experienced swimmers.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

**Urban/Built Environments**
Being "lost" in a city is rare, but after a disaster (earthquake, flood, power failure), urban survival means finding clean water, avoiding hazards like downed power lines, and staying oriented without working phones.

## Get Started with the Seven Priorities

The skills in this badge rest on understanding what matters most in a survival situation. You'll learn all about it in Requirement 2, but here's the preview: **your mindset and quick decision-making are more important than any fancy gear**. Panic kills. Staying calm and working through the seven priorities—in order—gives you a strategy.
