{{< drg/requirement number="7a" >}}
Explain and show how lost or stranded Scouts could send signals to attract the attention of ground, airborne, or water search teams.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Signaling is your primary rescue method. If you're lost, your job is to **make yourself findable**. Search teams will come—your trip plan ensures that—but they need to spot you. This requirement covers signals for three types of rescuers.

## Signals for Ground Rescues

Ground rescuers (hikers, park rangers, search and rescue teams on foot) look for visual movement, sounds, and signs.

**Visual Signals:**
- **Bright colors:** Wear bright orange (the official lost person color), bright yellow, or reflective material. Wave your arms or a bright cloth.
- **Mirror or reflective surfaces:** Flash toward the searchers. Any reflective surface works.
- **Smoke:** A fire during the day produces visible smoke (at night, use fire for warmth and visibility of flames).
- **Ground markings:** Large X patterns made with rocks or logs, or messages spelled out with branches or rocks.

**Audible Signals:**
- **Three-blast whistle pattern:** Three short blasts is the universal distress signal. A whistle carries sound up to 1 mile and doesn't require lung power like your voice.
- **Voice:** Shouting "HELP!" periodically, though voices tire and don't carry as far as whistles.
- **Rhythmic signals:** Three 3-second blasts on a horn or rocks banged together.

**Movement Signals:**
- **Jumping or waving:** Anything that creates visible movement in a landscape.
- **Running short distances:** In open terrain, moving catches eyes better than staying still.

## Signals for Aircraft Rescue

Aircraft (helicopters, search planes) look for you from hundreds of feet or miles above. They need large, high-contrast signals visible from that distance.

**Visual Signals (made on ground, visible from above):**
- **Large X pattern:** Made with rocks, logs, or bright cloth, at least 10 feet across. This is the universal distress signal.
- **Bright colors:** A large orange emergency tarp, bright tent, or reflective material spread on the ground.
- **Ground-to-Air signals:** Standard patterns recognized by pilots:
  - **V pattern:** Need assistance
  - **X pattern:** Need medical attention / distress
  - **SOS pattern:** Extreme distress (made with rocks, logs, or your body position if visible from above)
  - **Triangle pattern:** Safe to land here
  - **Arrow pattern:** Direction of travel
- **Smoke:** A large fire producing dark smoke during the day. Add green branches for more smoke.
- **Mirror flashing:** A signal mirror flashed toward the sun is visible to aircraft up to 10 miles away.

**Audible Signals:**
- Aircraft don't hear you, but if you hear a plane or helicopter, prepare to signal visually (wave your arms, flash your mirror).

## Signals for Water Rescues

If you're at or on the water (stranded on a shore, in a life raft, or in water), rescue might come by boat or helicopter.

**Visual Signals:**
- **Bright colors and reflective clothing:** Wear or display orange, bright yellow, or reflective material.
- **Waves and arm movements:** Move to attract attention from a distance.
- **Large objects:** Use your life jacket, raft, or any large object to appear larger from a distance.
- **Fire or smoke:** On a shore, visible fire attracts attention.
- **Mirror flashing:** A signal mirror works over water as well as land.

**Audible Signals:**
- **Whistle:** Carries across water well.
- **Voice:** Sound carries differently over water. Project your voice outward.
- **Distress calls:** "HELP!" or "MAYDAY!" if you have a radio.

**Special Consideration: Cold Water**
If you're in water, getting rescued quickly is critical because cold water causes hypothermia rapidly. Signal aggressively. Don't assume you're seen—keep signaling until rescue arrives.

## The Universal Distress Signals

Rescuers know to look for these:
- **SOS pattern:** Three dots, three dashes, three dots (Morse code: • • • — — — • • •)
- **Three of anything:** Three whistle blasts, three smoke plumes, three mirror flashes = distress
- **X pattern on ground:** Visible from above, universal for "help me"
- **HELP written on ground:** Spelled with rocks, logs, or your body
- **Bright colors in unnatural places:** Orange, yellow, or reflective material stands out

## Demonstrating to Your Counselor

Be ready to show or explain:

1. **Three rescue scenarios:** Explain signals for ground (searcher nearby), air (helicopter overhead), and water (rescue boat approaching).

2. **At least three different signal methods:** For example:
   - Whistle (audible)
   - Mirror or reflective material (visual)
   - Ground marking (X or SOS pattern)
   - Smoke or fire (visual)
   - Body movement (visual)

3. **Why these work:** Explain why three blasts is the distress signal (universally recognized), why large X patterns work from aircraft, why audible signals work for ground rescue.

4. **What to do if you're lost:** Walk through the decision: "First, I stay calm. Second, I stay put (my trip plan means someone will search where I'm supposed to be). Third, I make myself findable with signals."
