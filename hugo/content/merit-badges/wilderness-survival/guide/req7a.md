If you're lost, the best outcome is rescue. And rescue only happens if rescuers know where you are. This requirement is about making yourself visible and audible to the people looking for you.

## Why Signaling Matters

The 7 priorities of survival (#2 on the list) is signaling for rescue. You can survive a night in the wilderness with the right mindset, but you'll be found only if rescuers can locate you. Effective signaling means rescue arrives in hours instead of days.

## Signaling Ground Rescue Teams

Ground rescue teams (people on foot, in vehicles) are searching trails and roads. Your job is to make yourself visible and audible.

### **Whistle (Audio Signal)**

A whistle is your most reliable ground-signaling tool.

**Why it works:**
- Carries far (up to 1 mile in open terrain, 0.5 mile in forest)
- Doesn't require batteries, matches, or fuel
- Works day and night
- Universally understood: three blasts = distress signal

{{</* drg/image src="images/ground-air-signals.avif" alt="Five ground-to-air signals shown from above: X, SOS, I line, arrow, V shape with labels" */>}}

**How to use it:**
1. **Blow three short, sharp blasts** (rest 5–10 seconds between sets).
2. **Repeat every few minutes.** Rescuers might not hear the first blast.
3. **Move to higher ground** if possible (sound travels better from elevation).
4. **Whistle in patterns:** Three blasts is distress. Two blasts might mean "I heard you" or "I'm here."

**Practice:** Blow a whistle now. Feel how loud it is and how far the sound carries. A quiet Scout can whistle louder than they can yell.

### **Visual Signals**

Rescuers often use dogs, helicopters, and ground teams. Making yourself visible is critical.

**Bright Clothing and Signals:**
- **Wear bright colors.** Orange, neon yellow, red—anything that contrasts with the environment.
- **Tie bright cloth or bandanas** to trees or rocks near your location.
- **Arrange debris into large shapes** visible from the air: SOS, X, or arrows pointing to your location.

**Smoke:**
- **Build a fire with green branches.** Green wood produces smoke (different from white smoke of a signal flare, but still visible).
- **Create contrast:** In snowy terrain, burn wood to create dark smoke. In dark forests, find light material to create white smoke.
- **Smoke is visible for miles** on a clear day.

**Reflective Materials:**
- **Signal mirror** (covered in detail in Req 7b).
- **Shiny objects:** Metal, foil, anything reflective can be seen by aircraft.
- **Water:** If you're stranded near water, the sun reflecting off water is visible from aircraft.

### **Noise Beyond Whistles**

- **Yelling in patterns:** Three yells = distress. But your voice tires quickly and doesn't carry as far as a whistle.
- **Banging:** Clanging rocks or metal (like a pot) creates rhythmic noise rescuers might recognize.
- **Gunshots:** If you're armed (which you shouldn't be on a Scout trip, but in a real wilderness situation), three

gunshots is a distress signal.

## Signaling Airborne Rescue (Helicopters, Airplanes)

Aircraft search for missing people, especially in mountainous or remote terrain. Making yourself visible from the air is critical.

### **Ground-to-Air Signals**

Aircraft pilots can see things from above that ground teams can't. Your goal is to create large, visible signals.

### **Effective Ground-to-Air Signals**

| Signal | What It Means | How to Make It |
|--------|---------------|----|
| **X** | Need assistance | Arrange rocks, branches, or lie down in an X shape. Make it as large as possible (50+ feet across). |
| **SOS** | Distress | Arrange rocks/branches in SOS letters. Each letter should be 10+ feet tall. |
| **→ Arrow** | Go this direction | Point an arrow (made of rocks/branches/debris) in the direction of help/civilization. |
| **Smoke** | Here I am | Build a fire with green branches to create visible smoke. |
| **Mirror flash** | Here I am | Reflect sunlight toward aircraft (covered in detail in Req 7b). |
| **Waving** | Here I am | Stand in an open area and wave arms/bright cloth. Works best in open terrain. |

### **Color Contrast**

Aircraft pilots look for *contrast*. A stranded Scout in dark green clothing in a dark forest is invisible from the air. But:
- Bright orange against green forest = visible
- Debris arranged in geometric shapes against natural terrain = visible
- Smoke against blue sky = visible

**Your rule:** If a search aircraft flies overhead, they need to immediately see something unusual that says "a human is here."

## Signaling Water Rescue

If you're stranded at or on water (capsized boat, stranded on an island), water rescue teams will search the area.

### **Water-Specific Signals**

- **Waving:** Stand up and wave arms. A moving human silhouette is visible for miles on water.
- **Bright clothing:** Bright colors are visible against blue water.
- **Smoke or fire:** Much more visible over water.
- **Reflective signals:** Signal mirror works well for water rescue.
- **Whistles and noise:** Sound carries well over water.

## Creating Permanent Signals at Your Location

If you're stranded and staying put (which you should), create signals that persist:

**At camp:**
- Arrange rocks in large X or SOS
- Tie bright cloth to trees in multiple directions
- Build a large fire pit ready to ignite (green branches piled nearby for smoke)
- Place reflective items where aircraft can see them

**Communication:**
- Mark the highest nearby ground with signals (aircraft search high-elevation areas)
- Point directional arrows toward your camp
- Use contrasting colors (bright, unnatural colors stand out)

## The Universal Principle of Signaling

**Contrast + Repetition + Visibility = Rescue**

- **Contrast:** Make something unusual appear in a natural landscape.
- **Repetition:** Signal multiple times. The first aircraft might not see you. The second or third will.
- **Visibility:** Choose signal methods based on terrain (whistle in forest, mirror in open, smoke for both).

## Before You Move On

Your counselor will ask: "You're stranded in a forest and a rescue helicopter is overhead. What signals do you use?" Be ready to describe ground-to-air signals (arrangement of debris, smoke, waving). For ground rescue: "How do you signal rescuers on foot?" (Whistle, bright cloth, noise patterns.) Explain *why* each signal works and when you'd use it.
