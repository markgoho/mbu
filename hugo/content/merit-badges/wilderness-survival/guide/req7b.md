A signal mirror is a small, lightweight tool that can be seen from *miles* away—even by aircraft flying at high altitude. A skilled Scout with a mirror can signal rescuers across a 10+ mile distance on a clear day. This requirement asks you to demonstrate the technique.

## How a Signal Mirror Works

A signal mirror (or "heliograph") reflects sunlight into a beam. Unlike other signals, it doesn't require fire, doesn't make noise, and works in daylight. A mirror no bigger than a credit card can be seen from aircraft 10+ miles away.

## Types of Signal Mirrors

### **Commercial Signal Mirror**

A signal mirror is a simple reflective tool with:
- A reflective surface (glass or polished metal)
- A sight hole (small hole to aim through)
- Often a lanyard (cord to attach it to your pack or belt)

**Cost:** $5–15. **Weight:** Ounces. It's a no-brainer to include in your survival kit.

### **Improvised Signal Mirror**

If you don't have a commercial mirror:
- **Shiny tin can** (reflective metal)
- **Polished metal object** (buckle, watch, piece of foil)
- **Water surface** (reflect sunlight off still water—less effective but works)
- **Glass or polished stone** (less reflective than metal but can work)

The principle is the same: reflect sunlight toward your target.

## Basic Signaling Technique

### **Aiming the Mirror**

1. **Identify your target.** Are you signaling an aircraft overhead? A distant mountain? A valley where rescue teams might be?
2. **Hold the mirror in front of your face** at about chest height.
3. **Look through the sight hole** (if your mirror has one) to aim at your target.
4. **Adjust the mirror** so the sunlight reflection passes through the sight hole and hits your target.
5. **Once aimed, flash the beam:** Tilt the mirror slightly back and forth so the reflection blinks on/off/on/off—creating a flashing signal visible from far away.

### **Key Principle**

The goal is not a continuous beam, but a *flashing* pattern. The flashing catches attention—a continuous beam might be mistaken for sunlight glinting off a rock. Three flashes (pause) three flashes (pause) three flashes = distress signal (SOS in Morse code).

## Technique: Aiming at Aircraft

This is the most common use—an aircraft is approaching and you want to signal it.

1. **Position yourself in an open area.** Dense forest blocks your view and the aircraft's view of your signal.
2. **Watch the aircraft's approach.** Predict its path and prepare to signal when it's overhead or close by.
3. **Hold the mirror to your face** with the sun behind you (so the reflection goes toward the aircraft).
4. **When the aircraft is close,** flash the mirror in the direction of the aircraft. Alternate between looking at the aircraft and looking through the sight hole to maintain aim.
5. **Flash in a pattern:** On, off, on, off, on, off (or three quick flashes, pause, three quick flashes).
6. **Keep flashing until the aircraft responds** (sometimes they rock wings or change course to acknowledge).

## Technique: Aiming at Distant Targets

If you're signaling rescuers on the ground at a distance:

1. **Identify the target location.** Where are rescuers likely to be? Roads, trails, valley floors?
2. **Hold the mirror at arm's length** in front of you.
3. **Tilt it so the sunlight reflection points toward the target** (you'll see the bright spot of reflected light).
4. **Flash in a pattern:** Regular flashes (every second) are better than a continuous beam.
5. **Sweep the mirror** slowly across the area—you might not know the exact location of rescuers, so cover a wide area.

## Practical Demonstration for Your Counselor

Your counselor will ask you to demonstrate mirror technique. Here's how to prepare:

### **Setup**

- Clear, sunny day (essential—mirror signaling requires sunlight)
- Open area with good visibility
- A target 100+ feet away (a tree, building, or person)

### **Demonstration**

1. **Show the mirror.** Let your counselor see it's a legitimate signal mirror (or the improvised one you're using).
2. **Explain the technique.** Describe how you'd aim and flash.
3. **Actually signal the target.** Use the mirror to create a visible reflection on the target (your counselor, a tree, a wall). Flash it on and off.
4. **Demonstrate steady control.** Show that you can maintain the beam, adjust aim, and create deliberate flashing patterns.

**What your counselor is looking for:**
- ✓ Understanding of how mirrors work
- ✓ Ability to actually aim and create a visible reflection
- ✓ Understanding of flashing patterns (distress vs. continuous)
- ✓ Knowledge of when to use mirror (daytime, clear skies, when aircraft/rescuers are visible)

## Advanced Technique: The "V" or "Notch" Method

Some signal mirrors have a notched sight system:

1. Place your eye at the back sight (rear notch).
2. Aim the front sight at your target.
3. Adjust the mirror so the sun reflects through both sights onto the target.

This takes practice but is more accurate than the simple sight-hole method.

## Important Limitations

**Signal mirrors work ONLY in:**
- Daylight
- Clear weather (fog, rain, thick clouds reduce effectiveness)
- When the sun is above the horizon (no signaling at night)

**In bad weather or darkness, use:**
- Whistle
- Flares
- Smoke signals
- Flashlight

A complete signaling toolkit includes multiple methods—mirror during day, whistle anytime, fire/smoke for visibility.

## Practice Before Emergencies

Before your merit badge review, practice:
- Aim the mirror at a target 100+ feet away
- Create a visible reflection
- Flash in controlled patterns
- Switch targets quickly
- Demonstrate that you understand the technique

Practice on a sunny day. Your hands will shake a bit—that's normal. Once you've practiced, the motion becomes automatic.
