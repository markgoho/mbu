A signal mirror produces a flash of reflected sunlight visible for up to 10 miles — farther than any handheld flare and without any fuel requirement. On a sunny day, it's one of the most powerful rescue signals you can carry, and it fits in a shirt pocket.

{{< drg/image src="images/signal-mirror-technique.avif" alt="Hands holding a small signal mirror at eye level outdoors, demonstrating the aiming technique with the reflective surface facing outward" >}}

## How a Signal Mirror Works

A purpose-built signal mirror has a small aiming hole in the center. The hole allows you to see a retroreflective spot when the mirror is correctly aimed — a visual confirmation that your flash is going where you want it.

When sunlight enters the aiming hole from the front, it reflects a spot of light on your face or chest. When you position the mirror so that retroreflective spot aligns with a target (an aircraft, a distant ridge where searchers might be), your reflected flash is traveling precisely toward that target.

## Technique with a Purpose-Built Signal Mirror

1. **Face the sun.** Hold the mirror at arm's length, tilted at roughly 45°.
2. **Look through the aiming hole.** You'll see a small bright dot — that's the reflection of the sun.
3. **Raise your other hand** until your target (aircraft, boat, distant hilltop) is framed between your fingers.
4. **Move the mirror** so the bright dot aligns with the gap between your fingers.
5. **Sweep slowly** across your target area. If you're not sure where the searchers are, sweep the entire horizon slowly.
6. **Flash in sets of three.** Tilt the mirror on and off the target three times, pause, repeat.

## Technique Without a Purpose-Built Mirror

If you don't have a signal mirror, improvise with any reflective surface: a CD or DVD, a piece of aluminum foil pressed flat, a polished metal lid, a phone screen, or a mylar emergency blanket.

The improvised technique is slightly less precise but still very effective:

1. Hold the reflective surface up so it's catching sunlight.
2. Extend your free hand and create a V between two fingers.
3. Aim the reflected light at the V first (you'll see it as a bright spot on your hand).
4. Slowly raise the mirror until the V and the target are in the same line, then move the bright spot from your hand toward the target.
5. Flash in sets of three.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
During World War II, downed pilots were found more often by signal mirror than by any other method in sunny terrain. A mirror flash in direct sun can be seen for over 100 miles in clear air from altitude — making it one of the most visible rescue signals that exists, despite fitting in a pocket.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

## Practicing Before You Need It

Signal mirror technique requires practice to get right. The key difficulty is aligning the retroreflective spot with the target while maintaining a consistent mirror angle. Practice at home:

1. Use a mirror or phone screen outdoors on a sunny day.
2. Try to "hit" a specific target (a door, a tree, a parked car) with the reflected sun spot.
3. Gradually increase the distance to your target.
4. Practice sweeping across a target area in a slow, deliberate pattern.

A few sessions of deliberate practice will make the technique intuitive when you actually need it.

{{< drg/checklist title="Signal Mirror Carry Checklist" subtitle="Ensure your mirror is ready to deploy" >}}
- Mirror present: Small commercial signal mirror or improvised reflective item.
- Mirror accessible: In an outer pocket or attached to the outside of your pack — not buried.
- Mirror clean: Fingerprints and scratches reduce reflectivity significantly.
- Practice done: You've practiced the aiming technique at least once in real conditions.
