{{< drg/requirement number="4" >}}
First Aid Kits. Put together a personal first aid kit and a personal survival kit. Show how items in the kits are used.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Two kits, two distinct purposes. Your first aid kit treats injuries and illness. Your survival kit keeps you alive in an extended emergency. Both fit in a daypack. Neither requires expensive gear. What matters is knowing why each item is there and how to use it — which is exactly what this requirement tests.

## Your Personal First Aid Kit

A backcountry first aid kit differs from a home medicine cabinet. It's designed for the specific hazards covered in Req 1a, must be lightweight, and must contain items you actually know how to use.

{{< drg/checklist title="Personal First Aid Kit" subtitle="Essential items for backcountry day hikes and overnight trips" >}}
- Adhesive bandages (assorted sizes): For minor cuts and abrasions.
- Sterile gauze pads (4x4 inch): For larger wounds that need pressure and coverage.
- Medical tape: To secure gauze and improvised splints.
- Elastic bandage (2-inch): For sprains — the R.I.C.E. compression step from Req 1a.
- Moleskin sheets: Blister prevention and treatment.
- Antiseptic wipes or betadine: Wound cleaning before bandaging.
- Triple antibiotic ointment (small tube): Reduces infection risk in minor wounds.
- Nitrile gloves (2 pairs): Protects you when treating others.
- Tweezers: Tick removal and splinter extraction.
- Safety pins: Improvisational uses (sling construction, etc.).
- Non-prescription pain reliever (ibuprofen or acetaminophen): Pain management, fever reduction.
- Antihistamine tablets: Allergic reactions and bug bite relief.
- Personal medications: Any prescription medications relevant to your condition or your group's.
- Emergency contact card: Your information, allergies, emergency contacts.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

**Key principle:** Every item in your kit should be something you can demonstrate using. If you pack it, know it.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Pre-cut moleskin into several donut shapes at home and store them flat between gauze pads. When a hot spot develops on the trail, you'll want to apply moleskin immediately — you won't want to dig through your kit and cut a shape while your boot is off.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Your Personal Survival Kit

A survival kit is built around the seven priorities from Req 2. Its core purpose is extending your ability to survive an unplanned night or two in the wilderness without your main pack.

{{< drg/checklist title="Personal Survival Kit" subtitle="Compact items that address the seven survival priorities" >}}
- Signal whistle: Three blasts is the universal distress signal. Audible for a mile or more. Far superior to shouting.
- Emergency mylar blanket: Reflects 90% of body heat. Weighs about an ounce. Works as shelter, signaling surface, and rain protection.
- Waterproof matches or lighter: Fire-starting is Priority 4. Store in a small waterproof container.
- Tinder (dry cotton balls in petroleum jelly): Takes a spark reliably even in damp conditions.
- Signal mirror: On a sunny day, a mirror flash is visible for miles from the air.
- Paracord (25 feet minimum): Shelter building, improvised lashing, clothesline, emergency rope.
- Small folding knife or multi-tool: Utility for cutting, preparing tinder, first aid assistance.
- Compass: Navigation when electronic devices fail.
- Emergency water purification tabs: Backup water treatment when you have no way to boil.
- High-energy food (2 bars or equivalent): 400–600 calories bridges hours of exertion.
- Small flashlight or headlamp with backup batteries: Navigation and signaling after dark.
- Duct tape (small roll or wrapped around water bottle): Infinite repair and improvisation uses.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
A cotton ball saturated with petroleum jelly burns for three to five minutes — long enough to ignite even damp kindling. It weighs almost nothing and costs pennies to prepare at home. Many experienced wilderness travelers consider it the most reliable tinder available.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

## How Items Connect to Survival Priorities

| Survival Priority | Survival Kit Items |
|---|---|
| Positive attitude | All items — having a kit means knowing you're prepared |
| First aid | First aid kit (separate) |
| Shelter | Mylar blanket, paracord, duct tape |
| Fire | Matches/lighter, tinder |
| Signaling | Whistle, signal mirror, flashlight |
| Water | Purification tabs |
| Food | Emergency food bars |

## Showing How Items Are Used

For your counselor demonstration, be ready to explain and demonstrate each item — not just say what it is, but show how it works:

- **Whistle:** Blow three short blasts (universal distress signal). Explain why it beats shouting (range, endurance, doesn't dry out your throat).
- **Mylar blanket:** Open it, wrap it reflective-side-out for signaling, or reflective-side-in for warmth. Explain the difference.
- **Fire-starting:** Demonstrate lighting a cotton-ball tinder with a match or lighter.
- **Signal mirror:** Aim a small light reflection across the room to simulate sighting on an aircraft.
- **Elastic bandage:** Show the R.I.C.E. wrapping technique from Req 1a.
