{{< drg/requirement number="4" >}}
First Aid Kits. Put together a personal first aid kit and a personal survival kit. Show how items in the kits are used.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

In [Req 1b](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req1b/), you learned how the Scout Essentials form the foundation of survival preparedness. Now you're building two specific kits: a personal first aid kit and a personal survival kit. These aren't the same thing — and knowing the difference matters.

## Personal First Aid Kit

Your first aid kit handles injuries and medical problems. It treats what's already gone wrong.

### Core Contents

| Item | Purpose | Quantity |
|------|---------|----------|
| Adhesive bandages (assorted) | Minor cuts, blisters | 10–15 |
| Gauze pads (4x4) | Wound coverage | 4–6 |
| Roller gauze | Securing dressings, compression | 1 roll |
| Elastic bandage | Sprains, compression wraps | 1 |
| Adhesive tape | Securing bandages, blister prevention | 1 roll |
| Moleskin | Blister treatment | 2 sheets |
| Antiseptic wipes | Wound cleaning | 10 |
| Antibiotic ointment | Infection prevention | 4–6 packets |
| Tweezers | Tick removal, splinters | 1 |
| Safety pins | Securing slings, gear repair | 4 |
| Nitrile gloves | Infection control | 2 pairs |
| Ibuprofen/acetaminophen | Pain and swelling | As needed |
| Antihistamine tablets | Allergic reactions, stings | 4–6 |
| Sting relief pads | Bee stings, bug bites | 4–6 |
| Irrigation syringe | Wound flushing | 1 |
| Emergency information card | Medical info, contacts | 1 |

{{< drg/tip >}}
Build your own kit rather than buying a pre-made one. You'll know exactly what's inside, how to use each item, and what needs replacing. Store everything in a waterproof bag or hard case labeled with your name.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

### Knowing Your Kit

Having a first aid kit does no good if you've never opened it. Practice with every item before you need it in the field:

- Can you open a gauze pad with cold, wet hands?
- Can you wrap an elastic bandage around your own ankle?
- Can you remove a tick with tweezers in low light?
- Do you know which medications are for pain vs. allergic reactions?

Your counselor will ask you to demonstrate how items are used. Practice until you can do it without thinking.

## Personal Survival Kit

Your survival kit is different from your first aid kit. It handles the *situation* — helping you meet the seven priorities of survival you learned in [Req 2](/merit-badges/wilderness-survival/guide/req2/).

### Core Contents

| Item | Survival Priority Addressed | Weight |
|------|------------------------------|--------|
| Emergency whistle | Signaling | 0.5 oz |
| Signal mirror | Signaling | 1 oz |
| Ferro rod (fire steel) | Fire | 1.5 oz |
| Waterproof matches | Fire (backup) | 0.5 oz |
| Cotton balls in petroleum jelly | Fire (tinder) | 1 oz |
| Space blanket (mylar) | Shelter, warmth | 2 oz |
| 50 ft paracord | Shelter, gear repair | 4 oz |
| Water purification tablets | Water | 1 oz |
| Small folding knife | Multiple | 3 oz |
| Duct tape (wrapped around pencil) | Repair, first aid, fire | 1 oz |
| Aluminum foil (folded flat) | Water container, signaling, cooking | 0.5 oz |
| Small LED flashlight | Navigation, signaling | 1 oz |
| Fishing line and hooks | Food (extended survival) | 0.5 oz |
| Trash bag (heavy duty) | Rain protection, water collection | 1 oz |

**Total weight: under 1 pound.**

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
A standard emergency space blanket reflects up to 90% of your body heat back to you. It weighs 2 ounces, costs about two dollars, and has saved countless lives. But it has a weakness — it tears easily and provides no insulation on its own. Use it as a heat reflector *combined* with other insulation, not as your only layer.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

### Multi-Use Items

The best survival kit items serve more than one purpose. Learning the many uses of each item makes your kit more versatile without adding weight:

**Paracord** — shelter rigging, clothesline, bootlace replacement, fishing line (inner strands), snare line, tourniquet, gear hanging (bear bags)

**Duct tape** — blister treatment, gear repair, splinting, fire starter (burns hot), waterproofing, marking trail

**Heavy-duty trash bag** — emergency rain poncho, water collection, ground cloth, wind barrier, gear waterproofing

**Aluminum foil** — improvised cup for boiling water, signal reflector, fire wind screen, food wrap

{{< drg/checklist title="Kit Assembly Checklist" subtitle="Build both kits and verify" >}}
- First aid kit: all items present and in date
- Survival kit: all items present and functional
- Both kits: stored in waterproof containers
- Ferro rod: tested — can you get sparks consistently?
- Whistle: tested — produces a loud, carrying blast
- Signal mirror: tested — can you aim the reflection?
- Water purification tablets: not expired
- Medications: not expired
- Emergency contact card: up to date
- Both kits fit in your pack without adding significant weight
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Demonstrating Your Kits

When you meet with your merit badge counselor, be prepared to:

1. **Lay out both kits** and explain why each item is included
2. **Show how each item is used** — actually demonstrate, don't just describe
3. **Explain the difference** between a first aid kit (treats injuries) and a survival kit (addresses survival priorities)
4. **Discuss multi-use items** and what survival priorities they serve
5. **Explain your storage method** — how you keep kits waterproof and organized

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Check your kits before every trip. Medications expire, batteries die, and items get used and not replaced. A survival kit that's missing its fire starter is just a bag of miscellaneous stuff.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

Your kits are only as good as your ability to use them. Practice with every item. The more familiar you are with your gear, the faster and more effectively you'll deploy it when it matters.
