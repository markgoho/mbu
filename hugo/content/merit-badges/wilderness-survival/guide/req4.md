You already learned what Scout Essentials are and why they matter. Now it's time to build two kits—**first aid** (for treating injuries that happen) and **survival** (for when you're stranded and need to last until rescue). These kits are practical: your merit badge counselor will ask you to actually assemble them and show how each item is used.

## Building Your Personal First Aid Kit

A personal first aid kit is different from a troop first aid kit. Yours is smaller, more portable, and focused on the injuries *you're* most likely to encounter. Aim for a kit that fits in a small pouch (roughly 6" x 4" x 2")—something you'll actually carry.

{{</* drg/image src="images/first-aid-kit-contents.avif" alt="First aid kit contents laid out neatly: bandages, gauze, pain relievers, antibiotic ointment, elastic wrap, tweezers organized by type" */>}}

### **Essential First Aid Items**

| Item | Quantity | Use |
|------|----------|-----|
| Adhesive bandages (various sizes) | 6–8 | Minor cuts, scrapes, blisters |
| Antibiotic ointment packets | 3–4 | Prevent infection in cuts |
| Gauze pads (sterile, various sizes) | 4–6 | Larger wounds, deep cuts |
| Elastic bandage (ACE wrap) | 1 | Ankle/knee sprains, compression |
| Triangular bandage or cloth | 1 | Sling, pressure dressing, tourniquet |
| Pain reliever (ibuprofen/acetaminophen) | 8–12 tablets | Headache, muscle pain, fever |
| Antihistamine (Benadryl) | 4–6 tablets | Allergic reactions, insect stings |
| Hydrocortisone cream packets | 3–4 | Insect bites, rashes, itching |
| Blister treatment (moleskin or gel pads) | 3–4 | Prevent and treat blisters |
| Tweezers | 1 small | Remove ticks, splinters |
| Antibiotic wipe packets | 4–6 | Clean wounds before bandaging |
| Instant cold pack | 1–2 | Ice for sprains and stings |
| Waterproof matches or lighter | 1 | Fire starting (doubles as fire starter for survival kit) |
| Elastic gloves (latex-free if possible) | 2–3 pairs | Protect yourself from bloodborne illness |
| Pain reliever with caffeine (optional) | 4 tablets | Headache relief, mental clarity |

### **Special Considerations**

- **Medications:** If you take a prescription medication, include it in your kit.
- **Allergies:** If you have a severe allergy, carry an EpiPen and make sure your counselor and hiking partners know.
- **Climate:** In hot climates, add electrolyte packets. In cold climates, add lip balm and extra hydrocortisone for chapped skin.

### **Demonstrating First Aid Kit Use**

Your counselor will ask you to explain how you'd use items in your kit for various injuries. Be ready to discuss:
- "Your friend has a blister. What do you do?" (Clean with antibiotic wipe, apply blister pad, monitor for infection.)
- "Someone has a minor cut that's bleeding. What's your process?" (Apply pressure with gauze, clean with antibiotic wipe, apply ointment, bandage, monitor for infection.)
- "A Scout has an ankle sprain. What's your first aid response?" (RICE: Rest, Ice, Compression with elastic bandage, Elevation.)

## Building Your Personal Survival Kit

A survival kit is backup. It's items you hope you won't need, but that make survival possible if Scout Essentials aren't enough. A well-packed survival kit fits in a small pouch or tin (roughly the size of a card deck) and weighs only a pound or two.

### **Essential Survival Items**

| Item | Purpose | Specific Notes |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| **Fire Starters** | | |
| Ferro rod (ferrocerium rod) | Striking to make sparks | Works when wet, most reliable backup |
| Cotton balls soaked in vaseline | Tinder that burns even when damp | Pack 8–10 |
| Waterproof matches | Fire starting | Backup to ferro rod |
| Small lighter | Fire starting | Backup |
| **Shelter & Insulation** | | |
| Emergency bivy sack or space blanket | Ultralight emergency shelter | Reflects body heat, fits in pocket |
| Lightweight tarp or large garbage bag | Shelter extension or rain protection | 5'x8' tarp or heavy-duty garbage bag |
| **Water** | | |
| Water purification tablets | Make found water safe | Iodine or chlorine dioxide |
| Portable water filter straw | Drink from streams safely | LifeStraw or equivalent |
| **Signaling** | | |
| Signal mirror | Day signaling to aircraft | Visible for miles if sun is out |
| Bright cloth or emergency whistle | Ground signaling or audio signal | Whistle works night and day |
| **High-Calorie Food** | | |
| Energy bars or hard candy | Quick calories when stranded | 2–3 bars of 200+ calories each |
| Peanut butter packets or trail mix | Fat and protein for sustained energy | More satisfying than candy |
| **Tools** | | |
| Small multi-tool or knife | Cutting, building, processing | Redundancy to your Scout Essentials knife |
| Paracord (50 feet) | Building, securing, lashing | Compact, high-strength |
| Duct tape | Repair anything | Wrap on a card to save weight |
| **Emergency Information** | | |
| Emergency contact card | Phone numbers to call for help | Include name, age, medications |

### **Survival Kit Assembly Tips**

- **Weight matters:** Pack it so you'll actually carry it. If it weighs more than a pound, you won't have it when you need it.
- **Waterproof container:** Use a waterproof bag or plastic container so items don't get soaked if you cross a stream.
- **Redundancy:** Include backup fire starters. What if your lighter gets wet? You still have a ferro rod.
- **Familiarity:** Don't pack items you've never used. Practice starting a fire with a ferro rod before you're stranded.

### **Demonstrating Survival Kit Use**

Your counselor will ask scenarios like:
- "You're stranded overnight. Your shelter is built. What's next?" (Use items in order: fire starter to make a fire, signal mirror or whistle to signal, water treatment to get safe water.)
- "You're soaked and cold. What survival kit items help?" (Space blanket for insulation, fire starter for warmth, emergency shelter for protection.)
- "Walk me through how you'd use each item in your survival kit." (Be ready to explain the purpose of every item and when you'd use it.)

## First Aid vs. Survival: Know the Difference

| Aspect | First Aid Kit | Survival Kit |
|--------|---------------|--------------|
| **Size/Weight** | Moderate (fits in palm-sized pouch) | Very compact (fits in pant pocket or small bag) |
| **Primary Use** | Treating injuries as they happen | Lasting until rescue when stranded |
| **What It Addresses** | Wounds, bites, stings, sprains, reactions | Exposure, dehydration, despair, signaling |
| **Permanence** | Stays in your pack on every trip | Backup—you hope you don't need it |
| **Items Focus** | Medical supplies (bandages, ointments) | Survival tools (fire, shelter, signaling) |

## Real-World Kit Assembly

Don't overthink this. Here's a realistic example:

**First Aid Kit (in a small zippered pouch):**
- 8 adhesive bandages
- 4 pain relievers
- 4 antihistamine tablets
- Antibiotic ointment and hydrocortisone cream (shared packets)
- Elastic bandage
- Tweezers
- 3 blister pads
- Antibiotic wipes

**Total weight:** About 4 ounces. Total cost: $15–20.

**Survival Kit (in a small plastic waterproof bag):**
- Ferro rod + cotton-vaseline tinder (6 pieces)
- Signal mirror
- Whistle
- Space blanket
- 2 energy bars
- Water purification tablets (6)
- 30 feet paracord
- Small knife or multi-tool
- Emergency contact card

**Total weight:** About 12 ounces. Total cost: $25–35.

## Testing Your Kits

Before your merit badge review:
- **First aid kit:** Practice bandaging a mock wound, wrapping an ankle, treating a blister on your own foot.
- **Survival kit:** Practice starting a fire with your ferro rod. Demonstrate how to use the signal mirror. Set up the emergency shelter.

Your counselor will ask you to show, not tell. Being able to actually *use* these items confidently makes all the difference.

{{</* drg/tip */>}}
Don't assemble your kits a week before your review. Build them now, carry them on hikes and camping trips, and use items as needed. A kit you've actually used is far more convincing than one you assembled just for the counselor.
{{</* /drg/tip */>}}

## Before You Move On

By the time you meet your counselor, you should have assembled both kits and practiced using them. Be ready to explain the purpose of every item, when you'd use it, and how it works. The strength of this requirement is that it's entirely practical—a counselor can't question your judgment, because you're showing actual competence with real tools.
