Put together a personal first aid kit and a personal survival kit. Show how items in the kits are used.

This is a hands-on requirement. You will physically assemble both kits and demonstrate to your counselor that you know what each item is for and how to use it. There's no "right" kit list — but there are smart choices versus random impulse buys.

## Your Personal First Aid Kit

A personal first aid kit is sized for one person doing day hikes or overnight trips. It handles the most likely backcountry injuries — not every possible medical emergency. Your goal is a kit that fits in a quart-size ziplock bag or a compact pouch, organized so you can find anything in the dark.

### Core Contents

{{< drg/checklist title="Personal First Aid Kit" subtitle="Build this kit and know how to use every item" >}}
- **Adhesive bandages (assorted sizes):** Multiple sizes for small cuts and abrasions. Include some large, square-format bandages for larger wounds.
- **Moleskin (4x4 inch sheets, 2–4):** Blister prevention and treatment. Cut to size and apply before a hot spot becomes a blister.
- **Gauze pads (2x2 and 4x4 inch):** Wound dressings for larger cuts and lacerations.
- **Medical tape (1 inch roll):** Secures gauze, wraps ankles, creates improvised support.
- **Elastic (ACE) bandage (3 inch):** RICE treatment for sprains. Double as ankle and knee support.
- **Antiseptic wipes (6–8):** Cleaning wounds before dressing. Prevent infection.
- **Antibiotic ointment (small tube or packets):** Apply to cleaned wounds to reduce infection risk.
- **Tweezers:** Splinter removal, tick removal, foreign object extraction.
- **Safety pins (4):** Securing slings, popping blisters, improvised repairs.
- **Nitrile gloves (2 pairs):** Protects you and the patient from cross-contamination.
- **Triangular bandage/sling:** Arm slings, pressure bandages, head coverings.
- **Pain reliever/fever reducer (ibuprofen or acetaminophen):** For pain management and fever reduction.
- **Antihistamine tablets:** For allergic reactions, insect bites, and exposure rashes.
- **Emergency contact card:** Your name, emergency contact, relevant allergies or medical conditions.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
Store everything in a waterproof bag inside your kit container. Heat, humidity, and water degrade adhesives and wipes faster than you'd expect. Check your kit before every trip and replace anything expired or compromised.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## Your Personal Survival Kit

A survival kit is different from a first aid kit in purpose and size. A survival kit is meant to help you get found, stay warm, and stay alive in an unexpected emergency. It should fit in a shirt pocket or attach to your belt — because it's most useful when you've lost everything else.

The concept is sometimes called an "altoids tin survival kit" — everything fits in a small tin that you keep on your person, not in your pack.

### Core Contents

{{< drg/checklist title="Personal Survival Kit" subtitle="Compact enough to keep in a pocket at all times" >}}
- **Waterproof matches or lighter:** Fire is the highest-value item in a survival situation. A BIC lighter works reliably in most conditions; waterproof matches are a good backup.
- **Fire starter/tinder:** Cotton balls rubbed with petroleum jelly, commercial fire cubes, or char cloth. Takes fire from "possible" to "reliable."
- **Emergency space blanket:** Weighs 1–2 oz. Reflects 90% of body heat. Doubles as a signaling device (the reflective surface is visible from aircraft).
- **Signal mirror:** Can signal rescuers up to 10 miles away in sunlight. Requires no battery and works better than most other visual signals.
- **Whistle:** Three blasts is the universal distress signal. A whistle can be heard farther than shouting and uses a fraction of the energy.
- **Water purification tablets:** Two tablets treat one liter. Removes most bacteria and protozoa. Chlorine dioxide tablets work against Cryptosporidium; iodine does not.
- **Paracord (10–15 feet):** Tripwire for shelter, clothesline for drying gear, cordage for lashing, emergency fishing line.
- **Small LED flashlight or headlamp:** Navigation at night and signaling. Replace batteries before each trip.
- **Compass:** Always works. Fits in any kit.
- **Duct tape (short length, wrapped around an old gift card):** Wound closure, gear repair, blister cover, structural fixes.
- **Folded paper and permanent marker:** Leave notes at your campsite for rescuers, create ground-to-air signals, mark a route.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Showing How Items Are Used

When your counselor asks you to "show how items in the kits are used," they want more than just a name. Prepare to demonstrate or explain:

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Demonstrating Your Kit" >}}
For the first aid kit, be ready to walk through a realistic scenario:

- **Blister:** Show how to clean the site, cut moleskin into a donut shape, apply around the blister without covering it.
- **Ankle sprain:** Show the RICE principle — compress with the elastic bandage, explain elevation and icing.
- **Wound:** Show how to clean with antiseptic wipe, apply ointment, cover with gauze and tape.
- **Tick:** Show proper removal with tweezers, close to the skin, straight pull.

For the survival kit:

- **Signal mirror:** Show how to angle it toward the sun and flash toward a target.
- **Whistle:** Demonstrate the international distress signal — three short blasts, pause, repeat.
- **Space blanket:** Unfold and show how to wrap around the body to retain heat (shiny side in for warmth, shiny side out for signaling).
- **Fire starter:** Describe how to use your waterproof matches and tinder together to prepare a fire lay.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
The space blanket was invented by NASA in 1964 for the space program. Designed to reflect radiant heat away from spacecraft equipment, engineers quickly realized that the same material works in reverse — reflecting body heat back to a human. It has been standard emergency gear ever since.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}
